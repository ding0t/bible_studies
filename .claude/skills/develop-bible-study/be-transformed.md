# Be Transformed: what makes Think, Attitude and Do good

[SKILL.md](SKILL.md)'s Key Takeaways table links here. The shape is set in
[key-takeaways.md](../../../docs/content/about/key-takeaways.md#be-transformed): three labeled
bullets, `**Think.**`, `**Attitude.**`, `**Do.**`, each naming the verse it stands on. This file
covers what goes in each bullet. A 2026-10-31 survey of 115 three-bullet sets found the shape
mostly right and the content weak in three repeated ways. About half the Do bullets (58 of 115)
cite no verse. Many Attitude bullets are really actions, observations about the text, or the
study's own method. A few Think bullets are instructions.

## Each label has one job, and a test

| Label | It is | The test | It drifts into |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Think** | a truth about God, and about you because of Him, to believe | Could it be true or false? Does it finish "this shows that God ___"? | an instruction ("Say it out loud once this week, to yourself, in the car", `be-prepared.md`); a reading method ("Before you quote a verse, read the paragraph", `verses-often-misquoted.md`) |
| **Attitude** | a posture you carry into every hour: how you come to God, hold a thing, or face a person | Could you finish it and tick it off? If so, it is a Do | a one-off act ("Read a prophet this month", `scribe-trained-for-the-kingdom.md`); an observation ("Job's repentance in 42:6 follows no new information about his losses", `behemoth-and-leviathan.md`); the study's method ("the honest move is to say which reading you hold", `three-days-and-three-nights.md`) |
| **Do** | one action, done once or on a set rhythm, this week | Could a group member say next week whether they did it? | a restatement ("'Stay awake' as Jesus frames it", `day-is-near.md`); an inward review with no next step ("Name one place ... and put it down"); a puzzle to work out |

Write the Attitude with a verb of posture: *come, hold, wait, rest, grieve, fear, receive, suspect*.
Write the Do with a verb someone could watch you do: *pray (this text), read aloud to, tell, give,
go to, write down, leave*.

## The Do is the passage's own action

The strongest Do does what the text does, or what it tells you to do, and puts Scripture in the
reader's hands to do it. The author's own favourite was:

> This week, pray Ephesians 3:16-19 by name for one person, and for yourself.

That is Paul's prayer, from the passage the study worked through, prayed over a named person. The
action needs no explanation, because the text supplies the words. Look for the Do in this order,
and use the first one the passage offers:

1. **The passage's own command, done once.** "Therefore encourage one another with these words"
   (1 Thessalonians 4:18) becomes "tell one grieving believer where their loved one is"
   (`at-home-with-the-lord.md`). "Leave the edge of your field" (Leviticus 23:22) becomes giving to
   someone who cannot repay (`weeks.md`).
2. **The passage's prayer, prayed.** If the text prays, or holds a prayer, pray it over a named
   person or a named thing. `immediately-after.md` prays Daniel's prayer from Daniel 9.
   `take-up-your-cross.md` prays "your will be done" (Matthew 26:42) over the step that costs
   something. `names-of-god.md` prays a name back to God, as Moses prayed Exodus 34:6-7 back in
   Numbers 14:17-19. A promise can be prayed the same way: turn it into a request and name the
   person.
3. **The passage's model, copied.** Do what someone in the text did. Joseph decided in advance and
   ran (Genesis 39:12, `sexual-immorality.md`). Jesus took a towel (John 13:4-5, `humility.md`).
4. **The text spoken to someone.** Answer a question the text itself asks: "What do you mean by
   this service?" (Exodus 12:26, `passover.md`). Or read the passage aloud to the person it was
   written for: Revelation 21:1-7 to someone grieving (`new-heaven-and-new-earth.md`).

Only when the passage offers none of these, write a good act the study's principle implies, and
cite the verse that commands that kind of act. The verse makes the action Scripture's.

**Rules for the Do:**

- **Cite the verse in the Do itself**, even when the Think already cited it. A reader with only the
  printed sheet should be able to open their Bible at the action.
- **Name a person, a time or a place.** "Pray for them this week by name" is a Do. "Live daily with
  the readiness Matthew 25's parable calls for" (`rapture.md`) is an Attitude.
- **One action.** Two only when they make one movement: pray for a person, then contact them.
  `prayer-as-communion.md` labels its two **(a)** and **(b)** inside one daily time of prayer:
  Ephesians 3:16-19 prayed for a named person, then the Lord's Prayer pattern that puts God, His
  will and His provision before the reader's own list.
  `olivet-discourse-parables.md` runs two actions across 150 words, and a group will remember
  neither.
- **An inward review needs a next step with a verse in it.** "Name one thing you are treating as a
  condition of God accepting you" is the right start. Say what to do with it once it is named, and
  with which words of Scripture.
- **A reading exercise is a Do only in a study about reading Scripture**, such as the chronology
  and `scripture/` sections. Even there, once the puzzle is worked out, end with a response to God:
  thanks, trust, a prayer.

## Think carries the theology

The Think is where AGENTS.md's "this shows that God ___" lands for the reader. State the truth
about God first, then what it makes true of you, and name the doctrine. `passover.md` is the
reference specimen for the whole set. Each bullet does its own job and stands on its own verse, and
its Do is the text's own question:

```markdown
- **Think.** You are safe from God's judgment for one reason: the blood of His Son Jesus. "Much
  more shall we be saved by him from the wrath of God" (Romans 5:9, ESV).
- **Attitude.** Live as Israel ate that night, "with your belt fastened, your sandals on your
  feet" (Exodus 12:11, ESV): rescued, and ready to leave the old life.
- **Do.** Answer the question Israel's children asked, "What do you mean by this service?"
  (Exodus 12:26, ESV). Tell someone this week, in your own words, what the blood of Jesus has done
  for you.
```

## Keep the three in step with the questions

The **Be transformed.** discussion question
([discussion-questions.md](discussion-questions.md)) and this Do should point the same way. When
they point the same way, the group sheet's closing question asks the reader to do the thing the
study has already asked of them.

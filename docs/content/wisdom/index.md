# Wisdom

**Scripture treats wisdom as a road you walk.** Proverbs says so in its own figure: wisdom calls out
in the street, two paths lie open, and a life ends on one of them. The Hebrew
<span dir="rtl">חׇכְמָה</span> (*ḥokmâ*) is skill in living rightly before God, and Proverbs uses the
word 70 times across its 31 chapters working out what that skill looks like at a market stall, in a
marriage, in an argument, and at the end of a life.

This section holds what wisdom literature teaches and the reflections that come out of reading it.

## Key takeaways

### Lessons about Jesus

**Proverbs says wisdom is found in a person, and the New Testament names Him.** Wisdom in Proverbs 8
speaks in the first person, was there before the world was made, and holds out life to whoever finds
her: "whoever finds me finds life and obtains favor from the LORD" (Proverbs 8:35, ESV). Paul takes
that language and puts it on Christ — He "became to us wisdom from God" (1 Corinthians 1:30, ESV),
He *is* "the wisdom of God" (1 Corinthians 1:24, ESV), and in Him "are hidden all the treasures of
wisdom and knowledge" (Colossians 2:3, ESV). Jesus made the claim about Himself against the wisest
king Israel ever had: the Queen of the South came from the ends of the earth to hear Solomon, "and
behold, something greater than Solomon is here" (Matthew 12:42, ESV).

That is the reason Proverbs can promise so much to the one who finds wisdom. God did not offer a
technique for living well and leave you to master it. He gave His Son Jesus, and everything the
book holds out — life, favour, a straight path, an understanding heart — is held out in Him.

**The fear of the LORD is where wisdom starts, and it is knowledge of a Person.** Proverbs states it
twice, at the head of the book and at the hinge of its first half: the fear of the LORD is the
beginning of knowledge (1:7) and the beginning of wisdom, "and the knowledge of the Holy One is
insight" (9:10, ESV). The second line of 9:10 defines the first. This reverence is not dread of an
unknown power; it is what knowing the Holy One produces in a creature who has understood who He is.
So wisdom begins where relationship begins, and no amount of accumulated cleverness substitutes for
it.

**God gives it to those who ask.** Wisdom in Proverbs is never withheld from those who seek it —
she calls in the street (1:20-21) — and James says the same thing without a figure of speech: "If
any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask God, who gives generously to all without reproach, and it will
be given him" (James 1:5, ESV). So you are not left to generate wisdom out of your own experience.
You may ask for it, and you will be given it.

### Be transformed

- **Think.** Wisdom begins with knowing God, so a mind stocked with Scripture and a heart that
  reveres Him is the foundation every practical decision rests on. Build there first.
- **Attitude.** Receive reproof. Proverbs ties correction to life so often that the willingness to
  be told you are wrong becomes the working test of whether you are on the road at all
  (10:17, 15:31).
- **Do.** Ask. James 1:5 makes wisdom a thing to request before a decision, not a thing to regret
  after one.

### Prayer

Father, You are the Holy One, and to know You is insight itself. You did not leave me to work life
out on my own — You gave Your Son Jesus, who became to me wisdom from God, and in Him You hid every
treasure of wisdom and knowledge. Teach me to fear You rightly, to take correction without
flinching, and to ask You before I act rather than after. Make my paths straight. In Jesus' name.
Amen.

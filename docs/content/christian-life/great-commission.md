# Go and Make Disciples: His Mission, His Timing, Your Part

You may have heard it from a pulpit or a missions conference: "Jesus can't come back until every
people group has heard the gospel, and that is on us." It is meant to stir you to obey. For many
believers it becomes a weight they carry for years, with the souls of the unreached and the delay of
Christ's return both resting on their shoulders. The command behind it is real. The slogan built on
it is a likely misreading of the Great Commission and of Matthew 24:14.

**In one sentence:** The risen Jesus, holding all authority, commands His church to make disciples of
all nations and promises to be with you as you do it; the day of His return is fixed by the Father,
Matthew 24:14 is His promise of what God will see done before the end, and the Lord Himself adds to
His church; so you share the gospel gladly, as a forgiven child, with no debt of guilt to pay.

> ✝️ Matthew 28:18-20 (ESV)
>
> 18 And Jesus came and said to them, "All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me.
> 19 Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of
> the Son and of the Holy Spirit, 20 teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you. And
> behold, I am with you always, to the end of the age."

## Key Takeaways

### Types & Prophecy

- **Prophecy.** "This gospel of the kingdom will be proclaimed throughout the whole world as a
  testimony to all nations, and then the end will come" (Matthew 24:14, ESV). Jesus states it as a
  certainty about the future. Revelation shows it done in the last days by an angel "with an eternal
  gospel to proclaim ... to every nation and tribe and language and people" (Revelation 14:6, ESV).

### Lessons about Jesus

- **The mission runs on His authority.** "All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me"
  comes before "Go" (Matthew 28:18).
- **He goes with you.** The commission ends with a promise: "I am with you always, to the end of the
  age" (Matthew 28:20, ESV).
- **He builds His own church.** "I will build my church, and the gates of hell shall not prevail
  against it" (Matthew 16:18, ESV).
- **He bought people from every nation with His blood** (Revelation 5:9). The outcome of the mission
  is already sung in heaven.

### Memory verses

> ✝️ Matthew 28:20 (ESV)
>
> 20 teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you. And behold, I am with you always, to the
> end of the age.

> ✝️ 1 Corinthians 3:6 (ESV)
>
> 6 I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the growth.

### Be Transformed

- **Think.** The day of Christ's return is fixed by the Father (Acts 1:7). Your evangelism serves a
  timetable God already holds.
- **Attitude.** Share the gospel as someone with good news and nothing to prove. "There is therefore
  now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus" (Romans 8:1, ESV).
- **Do.** Name one person you will pray for by name this week, one missionary you will support, and
  one conversation where you will be ready to give "a reason for the hope that is in you" (1 Peter
  3:15, ESV).

## The commission

### His authority, His presence

The eleven met the risen Jesus on a mountain in Galilee, "but some doubted" (Matthew 28:17, ESV).
Jesus did not wait for the doubts to clear. He spoke about His authority first: "All authority in
heaven and on earth has been given to me" (Matthew 28:18, ESV). Then "therefore": the mission
follows from who He is. He closes with a promise of His presence "to the end of the age" (Matthew
28:20, ESV). The commission opens on His authority and closes on His company, and the work sits
between them.

### One command

The sentence has one main verb. μαθητεύσατε (*mathēteusate*, G3100) is an aorist imperative, "make
disciples". The verb appears four times in the New Testament: of the scribe "trained for the kingdom"
(Matthew 13:52), of Joseph of Arimathea, who "also was a disciple of Jesus" (Matthew 27:57), here,
and of Paul and Barnabas, who "had made many disciples" at Derbe (Acts 14:21, ESV). "Go", "baptizing"
and "teaching" are participles that describe how the disciple-making happens (ESV Study Bible on
Matthew 28:19).

That does not soften "go". The participle πορευθέντες (*poreuthentes*, G4198) stands before the
imperative and shares its force, and every translation checked for this study renders it as a
command: ESV, NIV, CSB, NASB, LSB, NKJV and NLT. The church is sent. Jesus gave the commission to the
eleven (Matthew 28:16), and it runs "to the end of the age", so it is the church's for the whole of
this age.

**This shows that God sends His Son's people with His Son's authority, and never sends them
alone.** The commission is binding, and it carries its own promise.

## Matthew 24:14: a promise about the end

### The verse in its place

> ✝️ Matthew 24:14 (ESV)
>
> 14 And this gospel of the kingdom will be proclaimed throughout the whole world as a testimony to
> all nations, and then the end will come.

"Will be proclaimed" is κηρυχθήσεται (*kērychthēsetai*, G2784), a future passive. Jesus states what will happen, and leaves the agent unnamed. "World" is
οἰκουμένη (*oikoumenē*, G3625), the inhabited earth (the LSB and CSB footnotes give "inhabited
earth").

The verse belongs to a sequence. Jesus has named the "beginning of the birth pains" (Matthew 24:8),
then persecution, betrayal and false prophets (Matthew 24:9-13). Straight after verse 14 comes "the
abomination of desolation spoken of by the prophet Daniel" (Matthew 24:15, ESV). This site reads that
sequence as Daniel's seventieth week, with the birth pains at its opening and the abomination at its
midpoint (see [Immediately After](../last-things/immediately-after.md) and
[The Olivet Discourse](../last-things/olivet-discourse.md)). On that reading verse 14 describes the
gospel going out to every nation during those years, as Revelation 14:6 shows it going "to every
nation and tribe and language and people".

### What "the end" is

"The end" is τέλος (*telos*, G5056), the end of the age, which Jesus marks with His visible return
"immediately after the tribulation of those days" (Matthew 24:29, ESV). The rapture of the church is
a different event, and Scripture gives it no preceding sign (see
[The imminence of the rapture](../last-things/rapture.md#the-imminence-of-the-rapture)). So nothing
in Matthew 24:14 stands between you and the Lord's coming for His church.

### The other readings

Many read verse 14 as the church's mission through the whole present age. The ESV Study Bible calls
it an indicator of the nearness of Christ's return, a task that began at Matthew 28:19. The NIV
Biblical Theology Study Bible notes that some see it fulfilled before AD 70. Paul could already write
that the gospel "in the whole world ... is bearing fruit and increasing" (Colossians 1:6, ESV) and
"has been proclaimed in all creation under heaven" (Colossians 1:23, ESV). Mark has the same saying
with "must": "the gospel must first be proclaimed to all nations" (Mark 13:10, ESV).

None of these readings makes the church the party who decides when Jesus returns. On every one of
them God foretells what He will see accomplished.

**The verdict.** "The gospel will reach every nation before the end" is fair; Jesus promised it.
"Jesus can't come back until we have reached everyone" is a likely misreading. It turns a promise into
a quota, puts the timing of the return in human hands, and ignores both the Father's fixed day and
the signless rapture.

## Hastening the day?

### Acts 1: the times are His

The disciples asked the risen Jesus about timing: "Lord, will you at this time restore the kingdom to
Israel?" (Acts 1:6, ESV). He answered, "It is not for you to know times or seasons that the Father
has fixed by his own authority. But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you,
and you will be my witnesses" (Acts 1:7-8, ESV). Jesus set the timing and the witness side by side:
the Father keeps the first, and the Holy Spirit empowers the second. Paul told Athens that God "has
fixed a day" (Acts 17:31, ESV). "Concerning that day and hour no one knows ... but the Father only"
(Matthew 24:36, ESV).

### 2 Peter 3: "hastening"

The strongest text for the slogan is 2 Peter 3:12. Peter's readers ought to live "in lives of
holiness and godliness, waiting for and hastening the coming of the day of God" (2 Peter 3:11-12,
ESV). σπεύδω (*speudō*, G4692) means to hurry or to be eager for; the CSB footnotes "speed the
coming".

**This is contested, so here are both sides.** The ESV Study Bible argues that God ordained the day
to follow the completion of His saving purposes, so that Christians who share the gospel and pray do
"hasten" it from a human point of view, while the day itself stays foreordained. The NIV Cultural
Backgrounds Study Bible notes that Jewish teachers disputed whether the end was fixed or could be
hurried by Israel's repentance. On the other side, Peter ties hastening to "holiness and godliness"
(2 Peter 3:11), and he explains the delay as God's patience: "The Lord is not slow to fulfill his
promise ... but is patient toward you, not wishing that any should perish" (2 Peter 3:9, ESV). The
"day of God" here is the day the heavens are "set on fire and dissolved" (2 Peter 3:12), which in
the dispensational order follows the millennium.

Read either way, the verse calls you to eager, holy waiting. It does not make you answerable for the
delay. The delay is God's mercy, and He keeps it.

## Blood on your hands?

The heaviest guilt-lever in missions preaching comes from Ezekiel: "his blood I will require at your
hand" (Ezekiel 3:18; 33:8, ESV).

God spoke those words to Ezekiel: "Son of man, I have made you a watchman for the house of Israel.
Whenever you hear a word from my mouth, you shall give them warning from me" (Ezekiel 3:17, ESV). It
was a prophet's office, with a specific word, for a specific people facing a specific judgment. The
ESV Study Bible observes that the passage's main concern is the prophet's faithfulness in delivering
the warning; the wicked man dies for his own iniquity either way (Ezekiel 3:18-19).

Paul echoed it once, about his own ministry to the Ephesian church: "I am innocent of the blood of
all, for I did not shrink from declaring to you the whole counsel of God" (Acts 20:26-27, ESV). He
applied it to faithful teaching by an apostle. The New Testament never lays it on every believer for
every unbeliever they meet. Some apply the watchman principle more widely (the NLT Life Application
Study Bible does), and the principle that carries over is this: speak faithfully what God has given
you to speak. **Another person's eternity rests with God, who judges
justly. Jesus carried the weight of sin once, and His sprinkled blood "speaks a better word" for you
(Hebrews 12:24, ESV).**

## Whose mission is it?

### The Lord adds

The mission is God's from end to end. Jesus said, "I will build my church" (Matthew 16:18, ESV). In
Jerusalem "the Lord added to their number day by day those who were being saved" (Acts 2:47, ESV).
"No one can come to me unless the Father who sent me draws him" (John 6:44, ESV). Paul put his own
work in its place:

> ✝️ 1 Corinthians 3:5-7 (ESV)
>
> 5 What then is Apollos? What is Paul? Servants through whom you believed, as the Lord assigned to
> each. 6 I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the growth. 7 So neither he who plants nor he who
> waters is anything, but only God who gives the growth.

The ending is already sung in heaven: "by your blood you ransomed people for God from every tribe and
language and people and nation" (Revelation 5:9, ESV), and John saw "a great multitude that no one
could number, from every nation" (Revelation 7:9, ESV). **God will finish what He started, and He
lets you share in the work.**

### Your part

The need is real. "How are they to hear without someone preaching? And how are they to preach unless
they are sent?" (Romans 10:14-15, ESV). The church answers it with different people doing different
things, "as the Lord assigned to each" (1 Corinthians 3:5):

- **Some are sent far.** Christ gave "the evangelists" among His gifts to the church (Ephesians
  4:11). Paul's own call left him no choice: "necessity is laid upon me. Woe to me if I do not preach
  the gospel!" (1 Corinthians 9:16, ESV). That was an apostle's commission (Acts 9:15).
- **Some send.** John tells Gaius to send travelling workers on "in a manner worthy of God ... that
  we may be fellow workers for the truth" (3 John 6, 8, ESV). Paul thanked the Philippians for their
  "partnership in the gospel from the first day until now" (Philippians 1:5, ESV).
- **All speak where they are.** When persecution scattered the Jerusalem church, "those who were
  scattered went about preaching the word" (Acts 8:4, ESV). Paul told the Thessalonians "to aspire to
  live quietly ... so that you may walk properly before outsiders" (1 Thessalonians 4:11-12, ESV), and
  their faith became known "everywhere" (1 Thessalonians 1:8). "Let your speech always be gracious"
  (Colossians 4:6, ESV); be "prepared to make a defense to anyone who asks you for a reason for the
  hope that is in you" (1 Peter 3:15, ESV).

### Where the guilt goes

Missions guilt says you have never done enough, and someone's eternity may hang on what you left
undone. The gospel answers that voice the same way it answers every other accusation: "There is
therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus" (Romans 8:1, ESV). Jesus' yoke is
easy and His burden light (Matthew 11:30); [Run the Race](run-the-race.md#never-good-enough) treats
the weight of never being good enough, and
[Assurance of Salvation](../salvation/assurance-of-salvation.md) the ground you stand on.

The Thessalonians turned to God "to serve the living and true God, and to wait for his Son from
heaven" (1 Thessalonians 1:9-10, ESV). Serving and waiting belong together. **So you may go, give,
pray and speak as a child sent by the Father, with the Son beside you and the Holy Spirit's power, and
leave the day and the harvest with God.**

## Discussion questions

1. **The language.** "Make disciples" is the one command in Matthew 28:19, with "go", "baptizing" and
   "teaching" around it. What does it change to read the commission as making disciples, with
   baptism and lifelong teaching built in?
2. **The text in its context.** Matthew 24:14 comes between the birth pains (Matthew 24:8) and the
   abomination of desolation (Matthew 24:15). How does its place in that sequence affect the claim
   that it is the church's task to finish?
3. **Christ.** The commission opens with "All authority ... has been given to me" and closes with "I
   am with you always" (Matthew 28:18, 20). Which of the two do you most need to hear, and why?
4. **The hard part.** 2 Peter 3:12 speaks of "hastening the coming of the day of God". How do you hold
   that phrase together with Acts 1:7, where the times are fixed by the Father's authority?
5. **Ezekiel's watchman.** Ezekiel 3:18 is often applied to every believer. What changes when you read
   it as Ezekiel's own commission, and Paul's use of it in Acts 20:26-27 as his?
6. **Be transformed.** Of going, sending and speaking where you are (1 Corinthians 3:5), which has God
   given you now, and what would it look like to do it without guilt this week?

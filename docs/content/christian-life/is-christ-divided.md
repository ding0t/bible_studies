# Is Christ Divided?

A few years after Paul left Corinth, a report reached him in Ephesus. It came from "Chloe's people"
(1 Corinthians 1:11, ESV), and it gave him four slogans:

> ✝️ 1 Corinthians 1:12-13 (ESV)
>
> 12 What I mean is that each one of you says, "I follow Paul," or "I follow Apollos," or "I follow
> Cephas," or "I follow Christ." 13 Is Christ divided? Was Paul crucified for you? Or were you
> baptized in the name of Paul?

Paul answered with three questions, and each one turns the church back to Jesus. He spends nearly three chapters
on those questions, and then says why he wrote them. "I have applied all these things to
myself and Apollos for your benefit, brothers, ... that none of you may be puffed up in favor of one
against another" (1 Corinthians 4:6, ESV).

**In one sentence:** Paul and Apollos were servants, and only Christ was crucified for you, so take
your name from Christ alone and count every faithful teacher as His gift to you; and because God
chose the despised and every believer is the Lord's own servant, never hold another church in
contempt, while you still test every teacher by the gospel.

## Key Takeaways

### Lessons about Jesus

- **Christ is one, and so is His body.** Christ cannot be shared out among teachers (1 Corinthians
  1:13). The church is one because its Lord is one: "one Lord, one faith, one baptism" (Ephesians
  4:5, ESV).
- **Only Jesus was crucified for you.** Paul, Apollos and Cephas brought you the message. Jesus is
  the message (1 Corinthians 2:2), and you were baptized into His name.
- **Every servant belongs to Him.** Paul calls himself and Apollos "servants of Christ and stewards
  of the mysteries of God" (1 Corinthians 4:1, ESV). A believer in another church "stands or falls"
  before his own Master (Romans 14:4, ESV), and that Master is Jesus.

### Memory verses

> ✝️ 1 Corinthians 3:5 (ESV)
>
> 5 What then is Apollos? What is Paul? Servants through whom you believed, as the Lord assigned to
> each.

> ✝️ 1 Corinthians 3:21-23 (ESV)
>
> 21 So let no one boast in men. For all things are yours, 22 whether Paul or Apollos or Cephas or
> the world or life or death or the present or the future—all are yours, 23 and you are Christ's,
> and Christ is God's.

### Be Transformed

- **Think.** You belong to Christ, and every faithful teacher belongs to you (1 Corinthians
  3:21-23). Your church is where you serve. Christ is who you are.
- **Attitude.** Hold every other believer as someone "God has welcomed" (Romans 14:3, ESV). Come
  to their church, their worship and their preacher with that in mind.
- **Do.** This week, pray by name for one church in your town that is not your own. Thank God for
  it, and ask Him to bless its preaching of Christ (Philippians 1:18).

### Prayer

Father, you chose what the world despised, and you chose me (1 Corinthians 1:27-28). Thank you for
every servant who planted and watered in my life. You gave the growth. Keep my eyes on your Son
Jesus, who alone was crucified for me. Guard my mouth from mocking anyone you have welcomed. Make me
glad wherever Christ is proclaimed, and keep me faithful to the gospel I received. Make us one, as
your Son prayed, so that the world may believe you sent Him. In Jesus' name. Amen.

## Four names in Corinth

### Who planted and who watered

Paul founded the church in Corinth. He "stayed a year and six months, teaching the word of God among
them" (Acts 18:11, ESV). After he left, Apollos arrived from Ephesus with a letter of welcome:

> ✝️ Acts 18:24, 27 (ESV)
>
> 24 Now a Jew named Apollos, a native of Alexandria, came to Ephesus. He was an eloquent man,
> competent in the Scriptures. ... 27 And when he wished to cross to Achaia, the brothers encouraged
> him and wrote to the disciples to welcome him. When he arrived, he greatly helped those who through
> grace had believed,

Corinth was in Achaia, and Apollos was there by Acts 19:1. Cephas is Peter's Aramaic name. The
Corinthians knew of him and his wife (1 Corinthians 9:5), but no verse says whether he visited the
city. So Corinth had a founder, an eloquent second teacher and a famous apostle. Paul wrote back
from Ephesus (1 Corinthians 16:8).

### "I am of Paul"

The ESV's "I follow Paul" translates ἐγὼ μέν εἰμι Παύλου, "I am of Paul". The name
Παῦλος (*Paulos*, POW-los, G3972) is in the genitive, the case of belonging. The NASB 1995, LSB and NKJV keep the
bare "I am of Paul", and the CSB has "I belong to Paul." The Corinthians were naming who they
belonged to, and they took their identity from a teacher.

Greek cities prized eloquent speakers, and a man's standing rose with the teacher he attached
himself to. [Saved as Through Fire](../salvation/saved-as-through-fire.md#what-divided-corinth)
traces how that habit came into the church, and how Paul calls it being "of the flesh"
(1 Corinthians 3:3, ESV).

### The fourth slogan

Whether "I follow Christ" was a fourth party is contested, and the text gives reasons on both sides.

- **A fourth faction.** The four clauses are built the same way, ἐγὼ μέν ... ἐγὼ δέ, and Paul's
  next question, "Is Christ divided?", answers all four. Later, Paul writes to the same church, "If
  anyone is confident that he is Christ's, let him remind himself that just as he is Christ's, so
  also are we" (2 Corinthians 10:7, ESV). And Paul introduces all four with "each one of you says"
  (1 Corinthians 1:12, ESV), which puts the fourth slogan in the Corinthians' mouths with the other
  three.
- **Paul's own retort, or no party at all.** When Paul returns to the slogans he names only Paul and
  Apollos (1 Corinthians 3:4), then Paul, Apollos and Cephas (1 Corinthians 3:22). He never
  addresses a Christ party. And he ends with the same genitive as the right answer: "you are
  Christ's" (1 Corinthians 3:23, ESV).

Either way, the words "I am Christ's" are true of every believer. What makes them a slogan is saying
them *against* another believer. A church that calls itself simply Christian, or non-denominational,
can make Christ's name into one more badge.

## Three questions

### A tear to be mended

Paul's appeal comes first, "by the name of our Lord Jesus Christ":

> ✝️ 1 Corinthians 1:10 (ESV)
>
> 10 I appeal to you, brothers, by the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, that all of you agree, and that
> there be no divisions among you, but that you be united in the same mind and the same judgment.

"Divisions" is σχίσμα (*schisma*, SKHIZ-mah, G4978), a tear. Its first New Testament use is a patch on an old
garment: "the patch tears away from the garment, and a worse tear is made" (Matthew 9:16, ESV). It
occurs eight times. Two are a torn cloth (Matthew 9:16; Mark 2:21). Three are a crowd split over
Jesus (John 7:43; 9:16; 10:19). Three are the church in Corinth (1 Corinthians 1:10; 11:18; 12:25).

"United" is καταρτίζω (*katartizō*, kat-ar-TID-zoh, G2675), to put in order or make complete. Its thirteen New
Testament uses run from God equipping His people (Hebrews 13:21) to fishermen "mending their nets"
(Matthew 4:21, ESV) and restoring a brother "in a spirit of gentleness" (Galatians 6:1, ESV).
Beside a word for a tear, Paul's sense is the restoring one. He asks them to let what is torn be
put right.

### Divided, crucified, baptized

"Is Christ divided?" uses μερίζω (*merizō*, meh-RID-zoh, G3307), to divide into portions, in the perfect tense.
The LSB's footnote says it can also be read as a statement, "Christ has been divided!" Either
way, Paul names something impossible. A church cut into teachers' shares treats Christ as if He could
be shared out.

The next two questions carry the gospel. "Was Paul crucified for you?" Only Jesus died for your sins
(1 Corinthians 15:3). "Were you baptized in the name of Paul?" Every believer was baptized into the
name of Jesus. Paul is glad he baptized only a few of them, "so that no one may say that you were
baptized in my name" (1 Corinthians 1:15, ESV).

### The cross emptied

> ✝️ 1 Corinthians 1:17-18 (ESV)
>
> 17 For Christ did not send me to baptize but to preach the gospel, and not with words of eloquent
> wisdom, lest the cross of Christ be emptied of its power. 18 For the word of the cross is folly to
> those who are perishing, but to us who are being saved it is the power of God.

When the church gathers around a teacher's eloquence, the cross loses its place. So when Paul first
came to Corinth he "decided to know nothing among you except Jesus Christ and him crucified"
(1 Corinthians 2:2, ESV).

This shows that God puts the power in the message and none of it in the messenger. You were saved
by the cross of your Lord Jesus, and nothing a teacher adds to it saves you.

## What then is Apollos?

### Servants, attendants, stewards

> ✝️ 1 Corinthians 3:4-7 (ESV)
>
> 4 For when one says, "I follow Paul," and another, "I follow Apollos," are you not being merely
> human? 5 What then is Apollos? What is Paul? Servants through whom you believed, as the Lord
> assigned to each. 6 I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the growth. 7 So neither he who plants
> nor he who waters is anything, but only God who gives the growth.

Paul's question uses the neuter τί (*ti*, TEE, G5101), "what". He asks what they are, and the answer is
a job. They are διάκονοι (*diakonoi*, dee-AH-koh-noy, G1249), servants. In 1 Corinthians 4:1 he
adds two more words. They are ὑπηρέται (*hypēretai*, hoo-pay-REH-tai, G5257), attendants: the same word names the
synagogue attendant who took the scroll back from Jesus (Luke 4:20). And they are οἰκονόμοι
(*oikonomoi*, oy-koh-NOH-moy, G3623), stewards, managing a house that belongs to someone else. "It is required of
stewards that they be found faithful" (1 Corinthians 4:2, ESV).

Each word describes someone who works for a master. None of them describes someone who gathers
followers of his own.

### The teachers were one

"He who plants and he who waters are one" (1 Corinthians 3:8, ESV). At the end of the letter Paul
writes of "our brother Apollos" and says, "I strongly urged him to visit you"
(1 Corinthians 16:12, ESV). Paul wanted the man whose name was on a rival slogan to go back to
Corinth. The fans fought over their teachers, and the two teachers worked together.

### You are God's temple

Between the builders and the boasting, Paul gives the whole church a warning:

> ✝️ 1 Corinthians 3:16-17 (ESV)
>
> 16 Do you not know that you are God's temple and that God's Spirit dwells in you? 17 If anyone
> destroys God's temple, God will destroy him. For God's temple is holy, and you are that temple.

Every "you" here is plural, ὑμεῖς, and "temple" is singular, ναός (*naos*, nah-OSS, G3485). The
church together is one temple, and God's Spirit lives in it. (In 1 Corinthians 6:19 the same word
names each believer's body.) Paul uses one verb, φθείρω (*phtheirō*, FTHAY-roh, G5351), for both
halves of verse 17: whoever ruins the temple, God will ruin. In this chapter, the danger to the
temple is the "jealousy and strife" Paul has just named (1 Corinthians 3:3, ESV). Splitting a
church over its teachers pulls down the house God lives in, and God defends His house.

## All things are yours

> ✝️ 1 Corinthians 3:21-23 (ESV)
>
> 21 So let no one boast in men. For all things are yours, 22 whether Paul or Apollos or Cephas or
> the world or life or death or the present or the future—all are yours, 23 and you are Christ's,
> and Christ is God's.

Paul turns the slogan around. The Corinthians said, "I am of Paul." Paul says Paul is yours. They
had made themselves the property of one teacher. He makes every teacher theirs, a gift from God to
the whole church. Then he gives them the genitive back in its one right place: "you are Christ's"
(Χριστοῦ, *Christou*, khris-TOO, G5547). And Christ is God's.

### Denominations, movements and famous preachers

**What this means now** (this is application). Corinth's factions were inside one city's church.
The nearest things to them today are denominations, movements and the followers of a famous
preacher. A Baptist, an Anglican, a Pentecostal and a Calvary Chapel member who all trust the
crucified and risen Jesus are one body: "Now you are the body of Christ and individually members of
it" (1 Corinthians 12:27, ESV). Paul wrote this letter "together with all those who in every place
call upon the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, both their Lord and ours" (1 Corinthians 1:2, ESV).

Every faithful teacher in that wider church is yours. Learn from a commentator outside your stream.
Sing the hymn written in a church you would never join. Thank God for the preacher in another
denomination who brought your friend to Christ.

None of this makes a local church optional. Scripture tells you to keep meeting with believers
(Hebrews 10:24-25). The [FAQ](../faq.md#what-religion-are-you-what-denomination) puts it this way:
your church answers *where you fellowship*, and Christ answers *what you are*.

### Puffed up, one against another

Paul names the root of the factions in 1 Corinthians 4:6. He wants "that none of you may be puffed
up in favor of one against another" (ESV). "Puffed up" is φυσιόω (*physioō*, foo-see-OH-oh, G5448), and six of its
seven New Testament uses are in this letter. [In Humility](humility.md) traces them. Then he asks
the question that ends every faction:

> ✝️ 1 Corinthians 4:7 (ESV)
>
> 7 For who sees anything different in you? What do you have that you did not receive? If then you
> received it, why do you boast as if you did not receive it?

Your church's sound doctrine, good music and faithful preaching were all received. A gift gives you
nothing to boast about over someone who received a different one.

## Do not despise

### God chose the despised

Paul has already told the Corinthians who they were when God called them:

> ✝️ 1 Corinthians 1:27-29 (ESV)
>
> 27 But God chose what is foolish in the world to shame the wise; God chose what is weak in the
> world to shame the strong; 28 God chose what is low and despised in the world, even things that
> are not, to bring to nothing things that are, 29 so that no human being might boast in the
> presence of God.

"Despised" is ἐξουθενέω (*exoutheneō*, ex-oo-theh-NEH-oh, G1848), to treat as nothing. It occurs eleven times in the
New Testament. Two of them show what the word looks like:

- Jesus told a parable "to some who trusted in themselves that they were righteous, and treated
  others with contempt" (Luke 18:9, ESV). The Pharisee prayed, "God, I thank you that I am not like
  other men ... or even like this tax collector" (Luke 18:11, ESV).
- On the morning He was crucified, "Herod with his soldiers treated him with contempt and mocked him"
  (Luke 23:11, ESV). Herod's contempt for Jesus is this word.

Paul uses it twice for believers who look down on each other's practice: "Let not the one who eats
despise the one who abstains ... for God has welcomed him" (Romans 14:3, ESV), and "why do you
despise your brother? For we will all stand before the judgment seat of God" (Romans 14:10, ESV).

### Mocking another church

Mocking another church does what this word describes. A joke about another congregation's worship,
size or preacher treats as nothing people God chose when they were nothing. A believer who laughs
at another church takes the Pharisee's place in the parable: trusting in himself and treating others
with contempt (Luke 18:9). Jesus' verdict went the other way. The tax collector "went down to his
house justified, rather than the other" (Luke 18:14, ESV).

### "I have no need of you"

Paul makes the same point with the body:

> ✝️ 1 Corinthians 12:21, 25 (ESV)
>
> 21 The eye cannot say to the hand, "I have no need of you," nor again the head to the feet, "I have
> no need of you." ... 25 that there may be no division in the body, but that the members may have
> the same care for one another.

Verse 25 uses σχίσμα again. The letter's first appeal, "no divisions among you" (1 Corinthians
1:10), comes back here as "no division in the body". Mocking a church is the eye telling the hand it
is not needed.

### Reviled, we bless

Paul lists the "reviler", λοίδορος (*loidoros*, LOY-doh-ros, G3060), among those the church must not keep company
with: "anyone who bears the name of brother if he is ... [a] reviler" (1 Corinthians 5:11, ESV).
The word occurs twice in the New Testament, both in this letter (1 Corinthians 5:11; 6:10). In the
chapter where he calls himself and Apollos stewards, he describes how he and the other apostles answered
the reviling verb: "When reviled, we bless" (1 Corinthians 4:12, ESV). That is how Jesus answered:

> ✝️ 1 Peter 2:23 (ESV)
>
> 23 When he was reviled, he did not revile in return; when he suffered, he did not threaten, but
> continued entrusting himself to him who judges justly.

So Scripture puts the reviler in the same list as the idolater and the drunkard, and the apostles
answered reviling with blessing. James gives the same command to every church: "Do not speak evil
against one another, brothers" (James 4:11, ESV). Paul tells the Galatians where it ends: "If you
bite and devour one another, watch out that you are not consumed by one another" (Galatians 5:15,
ESV). Believers who tear at each other are tearing at God's temple (1 Corinthians 3:17).

This is what it means for you. Never be the one who reviles another church, from the pulpit, on
the drive home or online. And when another church speaks against yours, answer the way Jesus did:
bless them, and leave the judging to "him who judges justly" (1 Peter 2:23, ESV).

### "He was not following us"

Jesus met the same thing in His own disciples:

> ✝️ Mark 9:38-40 (ESV)
>
> 38 John said to him, "Teacher, we saw someone casting out demons in your name, and we tried to stop
> him, because he was not following us." 39 But Jesus said, "Do not stop him, for no one who does a
> mighty work in my name will be able soon afterward to speak evil of me. 40 For the one who is not
> against us is for us."

John's complaint was that the man "was not following *us*". The man was acting in Jesus' name. Moses
heard the same complaint about Eldad and Medad prophesying in the camp, and he answered, "Are you
jealous for my sake? Would that all the LORD's people were prophets" (Numbers 11:29, ESV). So the
other church down the road is no rival. Whatever it does in Jesus' name is for you.

## Where acceptance stops

### The gospel is the line

Paul, who wrote "let no one boast in men," also wrote this:

> ✝️ Galatians 1:8 (ESV)
>
> 8 But even if we or an angel from heaven should preach to you a gospel contrary to the one we
> preached to you, let him be accursed.

Jude tells believers "to contend for the faith that was once for all delivered to the saints" (Jude
3, ESV). John says that whoever "does not abide in the teaching of Christ, does not have God" (2 John
9, ESV). So not mocking a church is a different thing from accepting everything it teaches.

Paul himself shows where the line runs. In prison, he heard of rivals who preached "from envy and
rivalry", hoping to make his life harder. His verdict:

> ✝️ Philippians 1:18 (ESV)
>
> 18 What then? Only that in every way, whether in pretense or in truth, Christ is proclaimed, and in
> that I rejoice. Yes, and I will rejoice,

Their motives were wrong and their message was Christ, so Paul rejoiced. The Galatian teachers
preached "a different gospel" (Galatians 1:6, ESV), and Paul cursed it. The difference is the
content: what Paul calls "of first importance", "that Christ died for our sins in accordance with
the Scriptures, that he was buried, that he was raised on the third day" (1 Corinthians 15:3-4,
ESV). A church's music, its government, its way of baptising, or its view of food and days (Romans
14:1-6) are opinions Paul tells you not to quarrel over.

Two more things hold that gospel up. It happened "in accordance with the Scriptures", which Paul
says twice in two verses, so Scripture, "breathed out by God" (2 Timothy 3:16, ESV), is its
authority. And the name every believer was baptized into, where Paul would not let his own name
stand, is "the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit" (Matthew 28:19, ESV): one
name, ὄνομα (*onoma*, OH-noh-mah, G3686), and three persons. A church that denies the authority of
Scripture, the Trinity, the deity of Jesus ("the Word was God", John 1:1, ESV), His atoning death
or His bodily resurrection is preaching another gospel.

Two other studies here do the testing in detail. [Test the Spirits](test-the-spirits.md) gives
Scripture's three marks of a false teacher, and [First Take the Log Out](first-take-the-log-out.md)
shows when Scripture tells believers to judge, and how.

### The hard part: Paul's own sharp words

Paul was not always gentle. He wrote to this same church, "Already you have all you want! Already
you have become rich! Without us you have become kings!" (1 Corinthians 4:8, ESV). Of the false
teachers in Galatia he wrote, "I wish those who unsettle you would emasculate themselves!"
(Galatians 5:12, ESV). So the question is fair: is that mockery?

1 Corinthians 4:8 is irony, and it is sharp. "You have all you want" is κορέννυμι (*korennymi*,
ko-REN-noo-mee, G2880), to eat your fill; its only other New Testament use is the sailors who "had
eaten enough" (Acts 27:38, ESV). "Become rich" echoes Paul's thanksgiving that "in every way you
were enriched in him" (1 Corinthians 1:5, ESV). The Corinthians talked as if they already reigned,
when the saints' judging of the world is still to come (1 Corinthians 6:2), after "the Lord comes"
(1 Corinthians 4:5, ESV). Three things in the paragraph show what kind of irony it is.

- **Paul wishes it were true.** "And would that you did reign, so that we might share the rule with
  you!" (1 Corinthians 4:8, ESV). ὄφελον (*ophelon*, OH-feh-lon, G3785) is a wish. Paul wants their
  reign to be real, and wants to share it with them.
- **Paul puts himself on the despised side.** "We are fools for Christ's sake, but you are wise in
  Christ. We are weak, but you are strong" (1 Corinthians 4:10, ESV). "Fools", "weak" and "strong"
  are μωρός, ἀσθενής and ἰσχυρός, the three words of 1 Corinthians 1:27, where God "chose what is
  foolish ... what is weak ... to shame the strong" (ESV). Paul stands with what God chose, and
  lets the Corinthians see where their boasting has put them.
- **Paul says it to them, and says why.** Every "you" in the paragraph is the church itself, and he
  explains: "I do not write these things to make you ashamed, but to admonish you as my beloved
  children" (1 Corinthians 4:14, ESV). "Admonish" is νουθετέω (*noutheteō*, noo-theh-TEH-oh,
  G3560), to warn someone so they will change. In Ephesus, Paul admonished "every one with tears"
  (Acts 20:31, ESV).

Mockery is a different act. The New Testament's word for it, ἐμπαίζω (*empaizō*, em-PIDE-zoh,
G1702), occurs thirteen times, and eleven of them are men mocking Jesus. Luke joins it to contempt
in one sentence: Herod "treated him with contempt and mocked him" (Luke 23:11, ESV). Paul never
uses the word. Mockery wants a laugh at someone it counts as nothing. Paul's irony wants
his children back.

Galatians 5:12 uses the same ὄφελον, but there the wish is against men who were preaching
circumcision for justification to Paul's converts (Galatians 5:2-4). Galatians 1:8 has already put
those men on the far side of the gospel line, and Paul aims the wish at them for the sake of the
people they were troubling.

So before you say something sharp about another church, ask the questions this paragraph answers.
Do I want their good? Am I standing with them among what God chose, or above them? Would I say it to
them, as a father to his children? A joke about another church's worship, told to your own people,
fails all three.

## What this shows about God

The Father has one Son, and the Son has one body. "There is one body and one Spirit ... one Lord,
one faith, one baptism, one God and Father of all" (Ephesians 4:4-6, ESV). On the night before He
died, Jesus prayed for everyone who would believe through the apostles' word, "that they may all be
one, just as you, Father, are in me, and I in you ... so that the world may believe that you have
sent me" (John 17:21, ESV).

This shows that God owns every servant and gives every faithful teacher to all His children. He
chose the despised so that no one could boast, and He welcomed the believer you might look down on.
So you can hold your own church's convictions firmly and still love the church down the road. You
belong to Christ, and so do they.

## Discussion questions

1. **The language.** The Corinthians said ἐγὼ μέν εἰμι Παύλου, "I am of Paul" (1 Corinthians 1:12),
   and Paul answered "you are Christ's" with the same genitive (1 Corinthians 3:23). What changes
   when you say who you *belong to* in place of whom you *follow*?
2. **The text in its context.** Paul and Apollos were "one" (1 Corinthians 3:8), and Paul urged
   Apollos to go back to Corinth (1 Corinthians 16:12). Why might the followers fight when their
   teachers did not?
3. **Christ.** "Was Paul crucified for you?" (1 Corinthians 1:13). How does the cross answer
   every boast in a teacher or a church?
4. **The hard part.** Paul rejoiced when rivals preached Christ (Philippians 1:18), and cursed a
   different gospel (Galatians 1:8). Where do you put a teaching you disagree with, and what in
   1 Corinthians 15:3-4 helps you decide?
5. **Be transformed.** Jesus said, "the one who is not against us is for us" (Mark 9:40). Which
   church or teacher have you spoken about with contempt, and what would it look like to bless them
   this week (1 Corinthians 4:12)?

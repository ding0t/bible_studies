# Through a Glass, Darkly

Paul's best-known picture of the Christian life is a man looking into a mirror. He wrote it to a
church in Corinth that was proud of how much it knew.

> ✝️ 1 Corinthians 13:9-12 (ESV)
>
> 9 For we know in part and we prophesy in part, 10 but when the perfect comes, the partial will
> pass away. 11 When I was a child, I spoke like a child, I thought like a child, I reasoned like a
> child. When I became a man, I gave up childish ways. 12 For now we see in a mirror dimly, but then
> face to face. Now I know in part; then I shall know fully, even as I have been fully known.

The King James Version has "now we see through a glass, darkly" (1 Corinthians 13:12, KJV), and
most English speakers still remember it that way. It describes something every believer knows. You
are sure God knows you, and you struggle to see Him. Much of life feels like a haze. Now and then
you glimpse His glory and His plan, and the rest of the time the world in front of your eyes fills
the view.

**In one sentence:** God has known you fully from the start; now He shows Himself to you in His Son
Jesus as in a mirror and in riddles, enough to love Him by while sleep and the world dull your
sight; and when Christ appears you will see the face of Jesus and know Him as He knows you.

## Key Takeaways

### Types & Prophecy

- **The promise still ahead.** "They will see his face, and his name will be on their foreheads"
  (Revelation 22:4, ESV). John says it of the church too: "when he appears we shall be like him,
  because we shall see him as he is" (1 John 3:2, ESV). Centuries earlier David hoped for the same
  thing: "when I awake, I shall be satisfied with your likeness" (Psalm 17:15, ESV).

### Lessons about Jesus

- **Jesus is the face in the mirror.** God has shone in our hearts "to give the light of the
  knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ" (2 Corinthians 4:6, ESV). "Whoever has
  seen me has seen the Father" (John 14:9, ESV).
- **Jesus showed His glory to sleepy men.** Peter, James and John "were heavy with sleep, but when
  they became fully awake they saw his glory" (Luke 9:32, ESV).
- **Jesus wants you to see it.** He prayed that His people "may be with me where I am, to see my
  glory" (John 17:24, ESV).

### Memory verses

>
> 12 For now we see in a mirror dimly, but then face to face. Now I know in part; then I shall know
> fully, even as I have been fully known.

> ✝️ 1 John 3:2 (ESV)
>

> when he appears we shall be like him, because we shall see him as he is.

### Be Transformed

- **Think.** God knew you fully before you knew anything of Him, and His knowing holds on the days
  your sight fades. "If anyone loves God, he is known by God" (1 Corinthians 8:3, ESV).
- **Attitude.** Live awake, with your mind on Christ. "Seek the things that are above, where Christ
  is" (Colossians 3:1, ESV), and come to the gospel expecting to see His glory there
  (2 Corinthians 4:6).
- **Do.** Each morning this week, pray Paul's prayer from Ephesians 1:17-18 by name, for yourself
  and for one other believer: that God would give "the Spirit of wisdom and of revelation in the
  knowledge of him, having the eyes of your hearts enlightened" (ESV).

### Prayer

Father, you knew me before I ever looked for you. Thank you that I am fully known, and loved, and
yours. I see so little of you. My eyes are heavy, and the things in front of me crowd you out. Shine
in my heart the light of your glory in the face of your Son Jesus. Wake me when I sleep, open my eyes
to your word, and keep me from being led astray from Him. Teach me to love Him whom I have not seen,
and to wait with hope for the day I see Jesus face to face.

In Jesus' name. Amen.

## A church that thought it saw clearly

Paul wrote 1 Corinthians from Ephesus (1 Corinthians 16:8), probably in AD 53, 54 or 55 (*ESV Study
Bible*, introduction). He had planted the church, and it was richly gifted. He thanks God that they
were "enriched in him in all speech and all knowledge" and "not lacking in any gift" (1 Corinthians
1:5, 7, ESV).

It was also proud. "Already you have all you want! Already you have become rich!" (1 Corinthians
4:8, ESV) is Paul mocking a church that talked as if the age to come had already arrived.

Much of the pride ran through knowledge, γνῶσις (*gnōsis*, G1108). In chapter 8 Paul quotes their
slogan, "all of us possess knowledge," and answers it:

> ✝️ 1 Corinthians 8:1-3 (ESV)
>
> 1 Now concerning food offered to idols: we know that "all of us possess knowledge." This
> "knowledge" puffs up, but love builds up. 2 If anyone imagines that he knows something, he does
> not yet know as he ought to know. 3 But if anyone loves God, he is known by God.

Chapter 13 comes back to the same three things: knowing, being known, and love.

### Every gift is partial

Chapter 13 sits inside Paul's answer about spiritual gifts (1 Corinthians 12-14). He has just told
them to "earnestly desire the higher gifts" and promised "a still more excellent way" (1 Corinthians
12:31, ESV). The way is love. Tongues, prophecy, "all mysteries and all knowledge" and mountain-moving
faith are nothing without it (1 Corinthians 13:1-2).

Then he gives the reason love outlasts them. "Love never ends," and prophecy, tongues and knowledge
will pass (1 Corinthians 13:8, ESV), "for we know in part and we prophesy in part" (1 Corinthians
13:9, ESV). The gifts are pieces of a whole that has not yet come.

Paul puts himself in the sentence. "Now I know in part." This is the man who was caught up into
paradise and "heard things that cannot be told" (2 Corinthians 12:4, ESV). His knowledge of God is
partial too.

The child in verse 11 makes the same point. Νήπιος (*nēpios*, G3516, "a child, an infant") is the
word Paul used of the Corinthians in chapter 3: "infants in Christ" (1 Corinthians 3:1, ESV). There
it was a rebuke. In chapter 13 he includes himself. Measured against what is coming, every believer
is a child, the apostle as much as anyone.

This shows that God gives His people real knowledge of Himself now and keeps the full knowledge for
the day of Christ. So you can hold what you know of Him with confidence, because He gave it, and
with humility, because it is in part.

## A mirror and a riddle

"In a mirror dimly" (1 Corinthians 13:12, ESV) translates four Greek words: διʼ
ἐσόπτρου ἐν αἰνίγματι, "through a mirror, in a riddle."

### The mirror

Ἔσοπτρον (*esoptron*, G2072) is a mirror, the polished metal kind a person held in the hand. The New
Testament uses it twice, here and James 1:23. Corinth was famous for its bronze, and the *NIV
Cultural Backgrounds Study Bible* says many mirrors were made of it. The study Bibles say these gave
a dim image (*ESV Study Bible*; *NIV Cultural Backgrounds Study Bible*). unfoldingWord's translators
say many ancient mirrors reflected well (*unfoldingWord Translation Notes*, 1 Corinthians 13:12).
How good Corinthian mirrors were is disputed.

The verse rests on what every mirror shares. A mirror shows a face at one remove. You see it truly,
and you see a reflection.

### Mouth to mouth with Moses

Αἴνιγμα (*ainigma*, G135) is a riddle, a saying whose meaning is veiled. This is its only use in the
New Testament. The Greek Old Testament uses it nine times, and one of them is the passage where God
defines how He speaks to Moses:

> ✝️ Numbers 12:6-8 (ESV)
>
> 6 And he said, "Hear my words: If there is a prophet among you, I the LORD make myself known to him
> in a vision; I speak with him in a dream. 7 Not so with my servant Moses. He is faithful in all my
> house. 8 With him I speak mouth to mouth, clearly, and not in riddles, and he beholds the form of
> the LORD. Why then were you not afraid to speak against my servant Moses?"

The Hebrew "riddles" is <span dir="rtl">חִידֹת</span> (*chidot*, H2420), and the Greek translators
rendered "not in riddles" as οὐ διʼ αἰνιγμάτων (Numbers 12:8, Brenton). Paul writes διʼ ἐσόπτρου
ἐν αἰνίγματι. The word is rare, and the subject is the same one: how God lets a person see Him. That
makes an echo of Numbers 12 likely, and the *NIV Cultural Backgrounds Study Bible* reads the verse
the same way. Paul does not quote Numbers, so the link rests on the shared word.

### "Dimly", "darkly", "in a riddle": the translations

ESV and NASB print "dimly", the NIV "only a reflection", the BSB "but a dim reflection". Each
describes the image. The KJV's "darkly" and the Legacy Standard Bible's footnote, "Lit in a riddle",
keep the riddle, and with it the road back to Numbers 12. Read 1 Corinthians 13:12 in one of those
alongside your usual translation.

### From Horeb to face to face

Read with Numbers 12 behind it, the verse puts every believer in a line that Scripture has already
drawn:

| Who | What they had | Text |
|---|---|---|
| Israel at Horeb | "the sound of words, but saw no form" | Deuteronomy 4:12 |
| A prophet | a vision, a dream, a riddle | Numbers 12:6, 8 |
| Moses | "mouth to mouth", "the form of the LORD"; God's back, and His face withheld | Numbers 12:8; Exodus 33:20-23 |
| A believer now | a mirror and a riddle, with the glory seen "in the face of Jesus Christ" | 1 Corinthians 13:12; 2 Corinthians 4:6 |
| A believer then | "face to face"; "I shall know fully" | 1 Corinthians 13:12 |

Moses asked, "Please show me your glory," and God answered, "you cannot see my face, for man shall
not see me and live" (Exodus 33:18, 20, ESV). He saw God's back from a cleft in the rock (Exodus
33:22-23). Exodus can also say that "the LORD used to speak to Moses face to face, as a man speaks to
his friend" (Exodus 33:11, ESV). The two sentences sit nine verses apart. Verse 11 describes how
close the speaking was; verse 20 marks where the seeing stopped.

Paul promises you the seeing. God has always come to His people in a measured revelation, and in
Christ He has promised you a sight of Himself He did not give Moses. With the Scriptures and the Holy
Spirit you stand now where the prophets stood. Then you will stand further in than Moses did.

## The face in the mirror is Jesus

In his next letter Paul used the mirror again, to the same church:

> ✝️ 2 Corinthians 3:18 (ESV)
>
> 18 And we all, with unveiled face, beholding the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the
> same image from one degree of glory to another. For this comes from the Lord who is the Spirit.

"Beholding" is κατοπτριζόμενοι, from κατοπτρίζομαι (*katoptrizomai*, G2734), "to look as in a
mirror." It appears nowhere else in the New Testament. It can also mean "reflect" (the CSB
footnote reads "Or are reflecting"), and the *ESV Study Bible* reports commentators defending
"behold", "reflect" and "look at in a mirror". The *ESV Study Bible* itself prefers a plain "behold".
The word is built on κάτοπτρον, "mirror", and Paul has used the mirror image to this church before,
so the mirror is likely still in view.

The face doing the beholding is unveiled. A veil "lies over their hearts" when Moses is read, but
"when one turns to the Lord, the veil is removed" (2 Corinthians 3:15-16, ESV). [The Names of the
Holy Spirit](../holy-spirit/names-of-the-holy-spirit.md) treats that verse and "the Lord is the
Spirit". So the sight is indirect, and it is real, and it changes the one who looks "from one degree
of glory to another." That change is sanctification, the Spirit's work in you now.

### The glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ

What the mirror shows, Paul says a few lines later:

> ✝️ 2 Corinthians 4:6 (ESV)
>
> 6 For God, who said, "Let light shine out of darkness," has shone in our hearts to give the light
> of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ.

"No one has ever seen God; the only God, who is at the Father's side, he has made him known" (John
1:18, ESV). Jesus told Philip, "Whoever has seen me has seen the Father" (John 14:9, ESV). Hebrews
calls Him "the radiance of the glory of God" (Hebrews 1:3, ESV). A Jewish wisdom book written before
Paul (its exact date is disputed) had called Wisdom "a spotless mirror", ἔσοπτρον ἀκηλίδωτον, "of the working of God",
beside the words for "radiance" and "image" (Wisdom 7:26, Brenton's Greek). The New Testament gives
both of those words to Jesus (Hebrews 1:3; 2 Corinthians 4:4). Whether Paul had that book in mind is
uncertain.

Paul does not name the mirror in 1 Corinthians 13. In 2 Corinthians the glory is seen in "the gospel
of the glory of Christ" (2 Corinthians 4:4, ESV), so the likeliest reading is the gospel: Jesus as
the Scriptures present Him, preached and believed, and lit by the Holy Spirit, who illumines what God
has given (1 Corinthians 2:10, 12).

God lets Himself be seen now, in His Son. The glass is dark, and the face in it is Jesus.

### Eyes God opened: the mountain, Emmaus, Dothan

Scripture records the glimpses, and each one is given.

On the mountain Jesus' face was altered and His clothing became "dazzling white" (Luke 9:29, ESV).
"Peter and those who were with him were heavy with sleep, but when they became fully awake they saw
his glory and the two men who stood with him" (Luke 9:32, ESV). Luke's "fully awake" is
διαγρηγορήσαντες, from διαγρηγορέω (*diagrēgoreō*, G1235), its only use in the New Testament. Only
Luke mentions the sleep (compare Matthew 17:1-8; Mark 9:2-8). Then a
cloud came, the Father's voice said "listen to him!", and "Jesus was found alone" (Luke 9:35-36,
ESV).

Peter wrote about the mountain decades later. "We were eyewitnesses of his majesty," he said, and then
sent his readers to the Scriptures:

> ✝️ 2 Peter 1:19 (ESV)
>
> 19 And we have the prophetic word more fully confirmed, to which you will do well to pay attention
> as to a lamp shining in a dark place, until the day dawns and the morning star rises in your
> hearts,

The man who saw the glory points you to the word as your lamp, until the day dawns.

On the Emmaus road "their eyes were kept from recognizing him" (Luke 24:16, ESV). Jesus opened the
Scriptures to them and broke the bread, "and their eyes were opened, and they recognized him" (Luke
24:31, ESV). Elisha prayed for his servant, "O LORD, please open his eyes that he may see," and the
servant saw that "the mountain was full of horses and chariots of fire all around Elisha" (2 Kings
6:17, ESV). Job, after his ordeal, said: "I had heard of you by the hearing of the ear, but now my eye sees you" (Job 42:5, ESV).

Every one of those openings is God's doing. "Their eyes were opened." "The LORD opened the eyes of
the young man." So you can ask Him for it, as Paul asked for the Ephesians: "having the eyes of your
hearts enlightened" (Ephesians 1:18, ESV).

## Why the glass seems darker than it is

The dimness Paul describes belongs to this age. Scripture also names things on our side that make
it darker.

### Heavy with sleep

Luke's word for the disciples at the Transfiguration is βεβαρημένοι, from βαρέω (*bareō*, G916,
"to weigh down"). Matthew uses the same participle in Gethsemane, of the same three men: Jesus "found
them sleeping, for their eyes were heavy" (Matthew 26:43, ESV). At the glory and at the agony they
slept. "The spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh is weak" (Matthew 26:41, ESV).

Luke uses the verb again when Jesus warns His disciples about the end:

> ✝️ Luke 21:34, 36 (ESV)
>
> 34 "But watch yourselves lest your hearts be weighed down with dissipation and drunkenness and
> cares of this life, and that day come upon you suddenly like a trap. 36 But stay awake at all
> times, praying that you may have strength to escape all these things that are going to take place,
> and to stand before the Son of Man."

The letters say the same thing to believers. "The hour has come for you to wake from sleep" (Romans
13:11, ESV). "Awake, O sleeper, and arise from the dead, and Christ will shine on you" (Ephesians
5:14, ESV). "Let us not sleep, as others do, but let us keep awake and be sober" (1 Thessalonians
5:6, ESV).

### The world in front of your eyes

"We look not to the things that are seen but to the things that are unseen. For the things that are
seen are transient, but the things that are unseen are eternal" (2 Corinthians 4:18, ESV). Paul's
"transient" is πρόσκαιρα (*proskaira*, G4340), "for a season".

The seen is close and loud. John names what it works on: "the desires of the flesh and the desires of
the eyes and pride of life," and then its end: "the world is passing away along with its desires"
(1 John 2:16-17, ESV). Paul's answer is to set the mind somewhere: "Set your minds on things that are
above, not on things that are on earth" (Colossians 3:2, ESV).

### Led astray

The worst of it is deception. Unbelievers are blinded: "the god of this world has blinded the minds
of the unbelievers, to keep them from seeing the light of the gospel of the glory of Christ"
(2 Corinthians 4:4, ESV). Believers are led astray. Paul feared for the Corinthians, "as the serpent
deceived Eve by his cunning, your thoughts will be led astray from a sincere and pure devotion to
Christ" (2 Corinthians 11:3, ESV), because "even Satan disguises himself as an angel of light"
(2 Corinthians 11:14, ESV). Hebrews names the danger and the guard together: "exhort one another every
day... that none of you may be hardened by the deceitfulness of sin" (Hebrews 3:13, ESV).
[Know the Truth](know-the-truth.md) treats the father of lies and how abiding in Jesus' word frees you
from him.

### "A spirit of stupor": Romans 11:8 in its context

Romans 11:8 has the strongest word for a dulled mind: "God gave them a spirit of stupor, eyes that
would not see and ears that would not hear, down to this very day" (Romans 11:8, ESV). Paul joins two
texts here: Deuteronomy 29:4 ("to this day the LORD has not given you a heart to understand or eyes
to see or ears to hear", ESV) and Isaiah 29:10. His κατάνυξις (*katanyxis*, G2659, "stupor") comes
from the Greek of Isaiah 29:10, where the Hebrew is <span dir="rtl">רוּחַ תַּרְדֵּמָה</span>
(*ruach tardemah*, H7307, H8639), "a spirit of deep sleep".

A reader who feels stupefied may take that verse as a description of themselves, so its context
matters. In both places it is God's judgment on people who refused Him. Isaiah's hearers "honor me
with their lips, while their hearts are far from me" (Isaiah 29:13, ESV). In Romans it is the part of
Israel that "failed to obtain what it was seeking", while "the rest were hardened" (Romans 11:7,
ESV). That hardening is "partial" and lasts "until the fullness of the Gentiles has come in", and
"in this way all Israel will be saved" (Romans 11:25-26, ESV).

Your haze is heavy eyes, the pull of the seen and the enemy's lies, and God has answered each: wake,
set your mind above, hold to Christ. Romans 11:8 warns where a heart ends up that keeps refusing to
look. Your face has been unveiled (2 Corinthians 3:16, 18).

This shows that God wakes His children. "Awake, O sleeper... and Christ will shine on you" (Ephesians
5:14, ESV) is a command with a promise inside it.

## Fully known

The last line of 1 Corinthians 13:12 turns on three verbs:

| Greek | Form | ESV |
|---|---|---|
| γινώσκω ἐκ μέρους | present active, "I know in part" | "Now I know in part" |
| ἐπιγνώσομαι | future middle, of ἐπιγινώσκω (*epiginōskō*, G1921) | "then I shall know fully" |
| ἐπεγνώσθην | aorist passive, of the same verb | "even as I have been fully known" |

Set against "in part", ἐπιγινώσκω here means "know fully, know thoroughly". The last verb is
passive, and the one who did the knowing is God (*unfoldingWord Translation Notes*, 1 Corinthians
13:12). The ESV reads "have been fully known"; the NIV and BSB "am fully known". The aorist states
God's knowing as a settled fact.

Paul writes the same pattern elsewhere. "If anyone loves God, he is known by God" (1 Corinthians 8:3,
ESV). "Now that you have come to know God, or rather to be known by God" (Galatians 4:9, ESV), where
he corrects himself in mid-sentence. "I press on to make it my own, because Christ Jesus has made me
his own" (Philippians 3:12, ESV), where the Greek has the same active-then-passive turn, καταλάβω and
κατελήμφθην. In each, God's grasp of you comes first and yours of Him follows.

In Scripture, for God to know a people is for Him to set His love on them: "You only have I known of
all the families of the earth" (Amos 3:2, ESV). Paul's "those whom he foreknew he also predestined"
(Romans 8:29, ESV) is that knowing before time. This is election, God's choosing love, and it is the
ground under your feet when the glass is dark.

God's knowledge of you is complete, settled and loving. So when you cannot see Him,
you can rest on the fact that He sees you, and has from before you looked.

## Face to face

"Then" in 1 Corinthians 13:12 is the time "when the perfect comes" (1 Corinthians 13:10, ESV). In
this letter that is the coming of Christ. The Corinthians "wait for the revealing of our Lord Jesus
Christ" (1 Corinthians 1:7, ESV). Judgment waits "before the Lord comes, who will bring to light the
things now hidden in darkness" (1 Corinthians 4:5, ESV). The dead are raised "at his coming"
(1 Corinthians 15:23, ESV). The [Annex](#annex-when-the-perfect-comes) sets out the debate over "the
perfect" and the gifts.

What you will see is the face of Jesus. He asked the Father for it: "that they also, whom you have
given me, may be with me where I am, to see my glory" (John 17:24, ESV). Now the glory of God shines
"in the face of Jesus Christ" (2 Corinthians 4:6, ESV); then you will see that face with nothing
between. This is glorification: "when he appears we shall be like him, because we shall see him as
he is" (1 John 3:2, ESV). In Him you see God: "Whoever has seen me has seen the Father" (John 14:9,
ESV). So "the pure in heart … shall see God" (Matthew 5:8, ESV), and in the city where "the throne
of God and of the Lamb" stands, "they will see his face" (Revelation 22:3-4, ESV).

"Even as" is καθώς, and it compares the manner of knowing. God's knowledge of you is direct and
personal, and yours of Him will be too: you will know Him as His creature, by sight, close to Him,
with nothing between.

### When I awake

David tied the seeing to waking:

> ✝️ Psalm 17:15 (ESV)
>
> 15 As for me, I shall behold your face in righteousness; when I awake, I shall be satisfied with
> your likeness.

"Likeness" is <span dir="rtl">תְּמוּנָה</span> (*temunah*, H8544, "form"), the word for what Moses
beheld (Numbers 12:8) and what Israel at Horeb did not see (Deuteronomy 4:12). The *ESV Study Bible*
and *NIV Biblical Theology Study Bible* both take "when I awake" as waking from death. The Greek
translators rendered the line "I shall be satisfied when your glory is seen" (Psalm 16:15, Brenton's
Greek). Job had the same hope: "in my flesh I shall see God, whom I shall see for myself, and my eyes
shall behold, and not another" (Job 19:26-27, ESV).

For the church that day comes when "the Lord himself will descend from heaven" and the living and the
dead in Christ are caught up "to meet the Lord in the air, and so we will always be with the Lord"
(1 Thessalonians 4:16-17, ESV). A believer who dies before then is "at home with the Lord"
(2 Corinthians 5:8, ESV) at once; [At Home with the Lord](../last-things/at-home-with-the-lord.md)
works that out. What your body becomes at His coming is the subject of
[We Shall All Be Changed](../last-things/we-shall-all-be-changed.md).

This shows that God means you to see the face of His Son Jesus. Jesus asked it of the Father
(John 17:24), and God has promised it (Revelation 22:4).

## What remains while we wait

> ✝️ 1 Corinthians 13:13 (ESV)
>
> 13 So now faith, hope, and love abide, these three; but the greatest of these is love.

"Abide" is μένει (*menei*, G3306): faith, hope and love last while the gifts pass. Whether faith
and hope continue in the age to come is contested. Paul says elsewhere that "hope that is seen is not hope" (Romans 8:24, ESV) and that "we walk by faith,
not by sight" (2 Corinthians 5:7, ESV), which suggests both give way to sight. Others read "abide" as
lasting forever, with trust and expectation in God never ending. Love is greatest on either reading,
because "love never ends" (1 Corinthians 13:8, ESV) and because love is the bond in which God knows
His own (1 Corinthians 8:3). Paul's next words make it a command: "Pursue love" (1 Corinthians 14:1,
ESV).

### Loving Him whom you have not seen

So the haze is where faith, hope and love do their work. Peter wrote to believers who had never seen
Jesus at all:

> ✝️ 1 Peter 1:8 (ESV)
>
> 8 Though you have not seen him, you love him. Though you do not now see him, you believe in him
> and rejoice with joy that is inexpressible and filled with glory,

Moses "endured as seeing him who is invisible" (Hebrews 11:27, ESV). Paul counted everything loss
"because of the surpassing worth of knowing Christ Jesus my Lord" and pressed on toward the goal
(Philippians 3:8, 14, ESV). Hosea called Israel to the same pursuit: "Let us press on to know the
LORD; his going out is sure as the dawn" (Hosea 6:3, ESV).

And the hope works backward into today. "Everyone who thus hopes in him purifies himself as he is
pure" (1 John 3:3, ESV). God keeps you by faith, hope and love until He gives you sight. You will
see Him as He is. Wake up, look to Him in the gospel, and love Him
now, until the day the glass is gone.

## Discussion questions

1. **The language.** Paul's "dimly" is αἴνιγμα (*ainigma*), "a riddle", the word the Greek Old Testament uses
   where God says He speaks with Moses "not in riddles" (Numbers 12:8). What changes in 1 Corinthians
   13:12 when you hear "in a riddle" where the ESV prints "dimly"?
2. **The text in its context.** The Corinthians said, "all of us possess knowledge" (1 Corinthians
   8:1). Why would Paul tell that church that he himself knows only "in part" (1 Corinthians 13:12)?
3. **Christ.** Peter saw Jesus' glory on the mountain (Luke 9:32) and later sent his readers to "the
   prophetic word" as "a lamp shining in a dark place" (2 Peter 1:19). What does it say to you that
   the eyewitness points you to Scripture?
4. **The hard part.** Exodus says the LORD spoke to Moses "face to face, as a man speaks to his
   friend" (Exodus 33:11), and nine verses later God tells him "you cannot see my face" (Exodus
   33:20). How do you hold those two sentences together?
5. **Be transformed.** Jesus warned His disciples to watch "lest your hearts be weighed down with...
   cares of this life" (Luke 21:34). Which care of this life is weighing your eyes down this month?

## Annex: when "the perfect" comes

"When the perfect comes, the partial will pass away" (1 Corinthians 13:10, ESV) is read two ways.

- **The gifts ceased with the apostles.** On the cessationist reading, prophecy, tongues and
  revelatory knowledge authenticated the apostles and their writings and ended when the New Testament
  was complete and the apostles died. "The perfect" is then the completed Scriptures, or the church's
  maturity; the Legacy Standard Bible footnotes τέλειον (*teleion*, G5046) "Or mature".
- **The gifts continue until Christ comes.** On this reading "the perfect" arrives with Christ's
  return, because 1 Corinthians 13:12 defines "then" as seeing "face to face", the Old Testament's
  phrase for a person seeing God (Genesis 32:30; Judges 6:22).

The *ESV Study Bible* sets out both and notes that 1 Corinthians 13:12 supports the second. The passage's own
"then" is the return of Christ: knowing in part ends when you see Him. Whether a particular gift could
cease earlier, on other grounds, is a question 1 Corinthians 13 does not address, and this site's
[statement of faith](../about/statement-of-faith.md) takes no position on it.

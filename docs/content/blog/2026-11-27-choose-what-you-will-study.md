# Choose this day what you will study

This week I took a page down. It surveyed today's deliverance ministries and what each one teaches.
The work was careful, but a page about a teaching gives that teaching the floor. The studies here
already teach the truth on every point it raised, and the truth is where the time belongs.

Joshua set a choice before Israel: "choose this day whom you will serve" (Joshua 24:15, ESV), the
LORD or the gods their fathers served beyond the River. He gave his own answer: "as for me and my
house, we will serve the LORD." What we choose to study is a smaller choice of the same kind.

## Have nothing to do with myths

Paul wrote 1 Timothy to a young pastor in a church troubled by false teachers. Chapter 4 opens with
the warning: "some will depart from the faith by devoting themselves to deceitful spirits and
teachings of demons" (1 Timothy 4:1, ESV). He names the error in three verses and answers it with
what is true: "everything created by God is good" (1 Timothy 4:4, ESV). Then he tells Timothy where
to put his attention:

- "Have nothing to do with irreverent, silly myths. Rather train yourself for godliness" (1 Timothy
  4:7, ESV).
- Paul tells him to "devote yourself to the public reading of Scripture, to exhortation, to
  teaching" (1 Timothy 4:13, ESV).
- "Practice these things, immerse yourself in them, so that all may see your progress" (1 Timothy
  4:15, ESV).
- "Keep a close watch on yourself and on the teaching. Persist in this, for by so doing you will save
  both yourself and your hearers" (1 Timothy 4:16, ESV).

The rest of the chapter is training in the truth.

## Be wise about what is good

- "I want you to be wise as to what is good and innocent as to what is evil" (Romans 16:19, ESV).
- Jesus commended the believers in Thyatira "who have not learned what some call the deep things of
  Satan", and told them, "Only hold fast what you have until I come" (Revelation 2:24-25, ESV).

Holding fast is enough.

## How the New Testament answers false teaching

The apostles did answer it. Jude urged his readers "to contend for the faith that was once for all
delivered to the saints" (Jude 3, ESV). Their answers share four marks:

- **It answers an error already in the church.** Galatians was written because "there are some who
  trouble you and want to distort the gospel of Christ" (Galatians 1:7, ESV).
- **It falls to those who care for the flock.** An elder must be able "to give instruction in sound
  doctrine and also to rebuke those who contradict it" (Titus 1:9, ESV; see Acts 20:28-30).
- **It aims to bring the person back.** The Lord's servant is "correcting his opponents with
  gentleness. God may perhaps grant them repentance leading to a knowledge of the truth" (2 Timothy
  2:25, ESV).
- **It is brief, and then it teaches Christ.** Colossians warns in one sentence, "See to it that no
  one takes you captive by philosophy and empty deceit" (Colossians 2:8, ESV), and the verses on
  either side are about Christ (Colossians 2:6-7, 9-10).

## Teaching the truth is the daily bread

- Paul gave the church at Ephesus "the whole counsel of God" for three years (Acts 20:27, 31, ESV).
- Christ gave teachers to His church "so that we may no longer be children, tossed to and fro by the
  waves and carried about by every wind of doctrine" (Ephesians 4:14, ESV).
- "Sanctify them in the truth; your word is truth" (John 17:17, ESV).
- Paul's list of what to think about begins with "whatever is true" (Philippians 4:8, ESV).

Know the truth well, and you will know the counterfeit when it comes.

## The way this site will go

Each study here teaches what its passage says. Where a verse has been bent to carry a burden,
[Verses Often Misquoted](../scripture/verses-often-misquoted.md) reads it back in its paragraph.

Keep a close watch on the teaching, and keep your eyes on Jesus, "the founder and perfecter of our
faith, who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross" (Hebrews 12:2, ESV).

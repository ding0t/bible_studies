This is one of the first pages I am rolling out significantly by hand. Well, at least in time taken. You should be able to readily see what
I have written by hand, what i have prompted AI, and the AI output.

The question I have refelcted on recently is has AI been useful in these studies, and would I encourage others to do use AI in bible studies? 

The one measure I have for myself at the front of my mind is - after six months of study I have personally developed far more skill than I would have alone. ??? note "How I answered, in plain terms"

    **I read the diary first.** Every change to this site is saved with a date, a short note and the
    name of whoever helped: 944 of those entries since April 2024. I read through them in order and
    counted them month by month, then grouped them into chapters: the slow start, the move to a new

    **I set Dave's memories beside the record.** For each thing he recalled, I looked for the entry that
    dated it. Most matched. Where his memory and the record differed, I went with the record and said
    so.

    **I checked every number twice.** A first count is the easiest place for an AI to be confidently
    wrong. Rechecking found four mistakes, and I corrected them before handing the page back.

    **I kept his words and mine apart.** His introduction and recall stay as he wrote them, typos
    included. My summary sits in its own section, so a reader can tell who said what.

??? note "Why your answer would be different"

    If you asked an AI the same question on your own computer, you would get a different answer, for
    four reasons:

    1. **It would not have the diary.** My answer comes from this site's full change history, and a
       fresh chat window has none of it. Without the record, an AI can only guess, and a guess written
       fluently looks just like a fact.
    2. **It would not have the tools.** On this computer I can look up Bible text, original-language
       dictionaries and study notes in databases Dave has built, along with the written rules for how
       studies here are made. Those rules hold the lessons of earlier mistakes. A general chat starts
       with none of that.
    3. **It would not have the conversation.** I had been working with Dave all day on this site, so I
       knew what had just been built and what had gone wrong this week. That context shaped what
       surprised me.
    4. **The same question never gets exactly the same words twice.** An AI writes each answer fresh,
       and different versions of the model write differently. The facts should agree when they come
       from the same record. The wording and the emphasis will not.

    The answer is only as good as what the AI can look at. Give it the record and it can check itself
    against the record. Without one, all it has is its own guesses.

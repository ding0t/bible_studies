# The Names of the Holy Spirit

> ✝️ John 14:16-18 (ESV)
>
> 16 And I will ask the Father, and he will give you another Helper, to be with you forever, 17 even
> the Spirit of truth, whom the world cannot receive, because it neither sees him nor knows him. You
> know him, for he dwells with you and will be in you. 18 "I will not leave you as orphans; I will
> come to you.

**In one sentence:** The night before the cross Jesus promised "another Helper", and every name the
Bible gives the Holy Spirit says whose He is and what He brings: He is the Spirit of God and of the
LORD, of the Father and of the Son, God Himself present with His people, promised by the prophets
and given to live in you forever, and He is the one by whom you call God "Abba! Father!" and say
"Jesus is Lord", so the God whose names you have learned now lives in you.

This is the third of three studies. [The Names of God](../god/names-of-god.md) follows the names God
gave Himself, and [The Names of Jesus](../jesus/names-of-jesus.md) the names given to God the Son.
This one follows the names of the Spirit, by whom both are known. The names are also printable word
cards, in [Hebrew](../hebrew/names-of-the-holy-spirit-cards.md) and in
[Greek](../greek/names-of-the-holy-spirit-cards.md), ordered to teach each alphabet a few letters at a
time.

## Key Takeaways

### Types & Prophecy

- **Prophecy.** The prophets promised a day when God would give His Spirit to all His people: "I
  will pour out my Spirit on all flesh" (Joel 2:28, ESV); "I will put my Spirit within you"
  (Ezekiel 36:27, ESV). Jesus promised the same Spirit in the upper room (John 14:16-17), and
  poured Him out at Pentecost (Acts 2:33).
- **Prophecy.** Isaiah saw "the Spirit of the LORD" resting on the Branch from Jesse's stump
  (Isaiah 11:1-2) and on the Anointed sent to the poor (Isaiah 61:1). In Nazareth Jesus read the
  second and said, "Today this Scripture has been fulfilled in your hearing" (Luke 4:21, ESV).
- **Picture.** *Ruach* and *pneuma* both mean breath and wind. The breath that stood the slain
  army on its feet (Ezekiel 37:10) pictures the new birth Jesus described with the same word
  (John 3:8), and He breathed on His disciples when He said "Receive the Holy Spirit" (John 20:22,
  ESV).

### Lessons about Jesus

- Jesus calls the Spirit "another Helper" (John 14:16). The word, <span lang="grc">παράκλητος</span> (*paraklētos*, G3875), is
  used once more in the New Testament, of Jesus Himself: "we have an advocate with the Father,
  Jesus Christ the righteous" (1 John 2:1, ESV). The Spirit does for His people on earth what the
  Son does for them before the Father.
- The Spirit rested on Jesus without measure, as Isaiah promised, and the Spirit is named after
  Him: "the Spirit of Christ" (Romans 8:9), "the Spirit of Jesus" (Acts 16:7), "the Spirit of his
  Son" (Galatians 4:6).
- The Spirit's work is to make Jesus known: "He will glorify me, for he will take what is mine and
  declare it to you" (John 16:14, ESV).

### Memory verses

> ✝️ John 14:16-17 (ESV)
>
> 16 And I will ask the Father, and he will give you another Helper, to be with you forever, 17 even
> the Spirit of truth, whom the world cannot receive, because it neither sees him nor knows him. You
> know him, for he dwells with you and will be in you.

> ✝️ Romans 8:15 (ESV)
>
> 15 For you did not receive the spirit of slavery to fall back into fear, but you have received
> the Spirit of adoption as sons, by whom we cry, "Abba! Father!"

### Be Transformed

- **Think.** The Holy Spirit is a person and He is God. Lying to Him is lying to God (Acts 5:3-4),
  and He can be grieved (Ephesians 4:30). Speak of Him as "He", the way Jesus did.
- **Attitude.** You are not an orphan. The Spirit of adoption lives in you, and He is given
  "forever" (John 14:16, ESV).
- **Do.** Ask. "How much more will the heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to those who ask him!"
  (Luke 11:13, ESV). When you pray and do not know what to say, the Spirit "intercedes for us with
  groanings too deep for words" (Romans 8:26, ESV).

### Prayer

Abba, Father, You promised Your Spirit by the prophets and gave Him through Your Son. Thank You that
I am not left an orphan, that the Spirit of truth lives in me and will not leave. Holy Spirit,
teach me what Jesus said, show me His glory, and lead me in Your good way. Keep me from grieving
You. Let me call God Father and Jesus Lord with all my heart. In Jesus' name. Amen.

## "I will not leave you as orphans"

[![Paraklētos, ὁ παράκλητος, “the Helper: one called alongside to help”, in large Greek with its uncial capitals. In the scene: a large upper room, furnished (Mark 14:15); and it was night (John 13:30); the basin and the towel (13:5). Not stated, drawn for the scene: the disciples, drawn without faces; Jesus, who is speaking, is not drawn. The plate quotes John 14:16 (ESV).](../assets/img/names-of-the-holy-spirit/parakletos.svg)](../assets/img/names-of-the-holy-spirit/parakletos.svg)

It was the last night before the cross. Jesus had washed His disciples' feet, Judas had gone out,
and Jesus had told the rest that He was going where they could not follow (John 13:33). Thomas
asked how they could know the way (John 14:5), and Philip asked to see the Father (John 14:8). Then
Jesus made a promise, in the verses that open this study, and named the one He would send three
ways in a single sentence.

- **"Another Helper."** <span lang="grc">ἄλλον παράκλητον</span> (*allon paraklēton*, G243, G3875).
  A *paraklētos* is one called alongside to help: the *Legacy Standard Bible* footnotes it "one
  called alongside as a helper", and the CSB offers "advocate" or "comforter". Jesus had been that
  for three years. The Spirit is "another" of the same kind.
- **"The Spirit of truth."** He comes from the God who cannot lie, and He leads into "all the
  truth" (John 16:13, ESV).
- **"He dwells with you and will be in you."** God had lived among His people in a tent and a
  temple. Now He would live in them, and "forever".

Then Jesus said why it mattered: "I will not leave you as orphans; I will come to you" (John 14:18,
ESV). In the Spirit, Jesus Himself comes to His people.

Moses asked God, "What is his name?" (Exodus 3:13), and [The Names of
God](../god/names-of-god.md#what-is-his-name) follows God's answer. At Caesarea Philippi Jesus asked,
"Who do you say that I am?", and [The Names of
Jesus](../jesus/names-of-jesus.md#who-do-you-say-that-i-am) follows the answers. In the upper room
nobody asked about the Spirit. Jesus named Him unasked, to frightened men about to lose their
teacher.

## Breath, wind and Spirit

The Hebrew word is <span dir="rtl">רוּחַ</span> (*ruach*, ROO-akh, H7307), and it occurs 378 times
in the Hebrew Old Testament. It means breath, wind and spirit, and a reader often has to choose.
Ezekiel's vision of the valley of dry bones uses it in all three senses in one scene: "Come from the
four winds, O breath, and breathe on these slain, that they may live" (Ezekiel 37:9, ESV), "and the
breath came into them, and they lived" (Ezekiel 37:10, ESV), and then God explains, "I will put my
Spirit within you, and you shall live" (Ezekiel 37:14, ESV).

The Greek word is <span lang="grc">πνεῦμα</span> (*pneuma*, PNYOO-mah, G4151), 379 times in the
Greek New Testament, and it carries the same range. Jesus used both senses together when He told
Nicodemus about the new birth: "The wind blows where it wishes, and you hear its sound, but you do
not know where it comes from or where it goes. So it is with everyone who is born of the Spirit"
(John 3:8, ESV). "Wind" and "Spirit" in that verse are the one word.

Most of the Spirit's names are built the same way: "the Spirit of" something. Some say whose He is:
the Spirit of God, of the LORD, of Christ. Others say what He brings: wisdom, truth, life, adoption,
grace, glory. Read the names below in those two groups and they answer two questions, *whose is He?*
and *what does He bring?*

## Names in the Old Testament

### The Spirit of God

[![Ruach Elohim, רוּחַ אֱלֹהִים, “the Spirit of God”, in large Hebrew with its Paleo-Hebrew form. In the scene: darkness over the face of the deep (Genesis 1:2); the face of the waters (1:2); the Spirit of God hovering over them (1:2). Not stated, drawn for the scene: the light and moving air that picture Him. The plate quotes Genesis 1:2 (ESV).](../assets/img/names-of-the-holy-spirit/ruach-elohim.svg)](../assets/img/names-of-the-holy-spirit/ruach-elohim.svg)

The Spirit is in the Bible's second verse: "And the Spirit of God was hovering over the face of the
waters" (Genesis 1:2, ESV). <span dir="rtl">רוּחַ אֱלֹהִים</span> (*ruach Elohim*, H7307, H430)
was present before light, over a world "without form and void". Elihu said it of himself: "The
Spirit of God has made me, and the breath of the Almighty gives me life" (Job 33:4, ESV). The God
who made the world by His word made it in the presence of His Spirit, and every breath you take is
His gift.

### The Spirit of the LORD

[![Ruach YHWH, רוּחַ יְהוָה, “the Spirit of the LORD”, in large Hebrew with its Paleo-Hebrew form. In the scene: Samuel anoints David with the horn of oil (1 Samuel 16:13); in the midst of his brothers, the seven Jesse had brought before Samuel (16:10, 13); the sheep the youngest was keeping (16:11); the Spirit of the LORD rushing upon David, drawn as light (16:13). Not stated, drawn for the scene: David kneeling. The plate quotes 1 Samuel 16:13 (ESV).](../assets/img/names-of-the-holy-spirit/ruach-yhwh.svg)](../assets/img/names-of-the-holy-spirit/ruach-yhwh.svg)

<span dir="rtl">רוּחַ יְהוָה</span> (*ruach YHWH*, H7307, H3068) joins the Spirit to God's own
name. In the Old Testament the Spirit of the LORD came on particular people for a particular task:
on judges, on prophets, and on the king. When Samuel anointed David, "the Spirit of the LORD rushed
upon David from that day forward" (1 Samuel 16:13, ESV). The next verse is the sorrow of it: "Now
the Spirit of the LORD departed from Saul" (1 Samuel 16:14, ESV).

Isaiah saw the Spirit of the LORD resting on one King for good. From the stump of Jesse's cut-down
line a Branch would grow (Isaiah 11:1), the Branch of [The Names of
Jesus](../jesus/names-of-jesus.md#the-branch), and:

> ✝️ Isaiah 11:2 (ESV)
>
> 2 And the Spirit of the LORD shall rest upon him, the Spirit of wisdom and understanding, the
> Spirit of counsel and might, the Spirit of knowledge and the fear of the LORD.

The verse names Him four times, and the last three come in pairs:
<span dir="rtl">רוּחַ חָכְמָה וּבִינָה</span> (*ruach chokhmah uvinah*, H2451, H998), "wisdom and
understanding"; <span dir="rtl">רוּחַ עֵצָה וּגְבוּרָה</span> (*ruach ʿetsah ugevurah*, H6098,
H1369), "counsel and might"; and <span dir="rtl">רוּחַ דַּעַת וְיִרְאַת יְהוָה</span>
(*ruach daʿat veyirʾat YHWH*, H1847, H3374), "knowledge and the fear of the LORD". The verb is
<span dir="rtl">נָחָה</span> (*nachah*, H5117), "rest", the word for settling and staying. The
Septuagint renders the last "fear" as <span lang="grc">εὐσεβεία</span> (*eusebeia*, G2150),
"godliness", then has "the spirit of the fear of God" in Isaiah 11:3, so the Greek counts seven
gifts where the Hebrew counts six. That Greek count is one source of the church's phrase "the
sevenfold Spirit".

Isaiah heard the same King speak: "The Spirit of the Lord GOD is upon me, because the LORD has
anointed me to bring good news to the poor" (Isaiah 61:1, ESV). <span dir="rtl">רוּחַ אֲדֹנָי
יְהוִה</span> (*ruach Adonai YHWH*, H136, H3069) is on Him, and the anointing is the name
*Mashiach*, [the Anointed](../jesus/names-of-jesus.md#the-anointed). In the synagogue at Nazareth
Jesus read this passage, "The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he has anointed me" (Luke 4:18,
ESV), and told the room it was fulfilled that day. The Spirit who came and went on Israel's leaders
rested on Jesus and stayed.

### Your Holy Spirit

[![Ruach Qodshekha, רוּחַ קָדְשְׁךָ, “your Holy Spirit”, in large Hebrew with its Paleo-Hebrew form. In the scene: Nathan sent to David, and gone (2 Samuel 12:1, 15). Not stated, drawn for the scene: the night, the room, the lamp, and David on his knees. The plate quotes Psalm 51:11 (ESV).](../assets/img/names-of-the-holy-spirit/ruach-qodshekha.svg)](../assets/img/names-of-the-holy-spirit/ruach-qodshekha.svg)

The name "Holy Spirit" occurs three times in the whole Old Testament, and two of them are about
losing Him. David prayed after his sin with Bathsheba: "Cast me not away from your presence, and
take not your Holy Spirit from me" (Psalm 51:11, ESV). He had seen it happen to Saul.
<span dir="rtl">רוּחַ קָדְשְׁךָ</span> (*ruach qodshekha*, H7307, H6944) is literally "the Spirit of
your holiness", which the NLT gives as its footnote. The other two are in Isaiah's prayer, looking
back on the wilderness:

> ✝️ Isaiah 63:10-11 (ESV)
>
> 10 But they rebelled and grieved his Holy Spirit; therefore he turned to be their enemy, and
> himself fought against them. 11 Then he remembered the days of old, of Moses and his people.
> Where is he who brought them up out of the sea with the shepherds of his flock? Where is he who
> put in the midst of them his Holy Spirit,

"Grieved" is <span dir="rtl">עִצְּבוּ</span> (*ʿitsevu*, from H6087), to hurt or pain. Only a
person can be grieved. Paul wrote, "do not grieve the Holy Spirit of God" (Ephesians 4:30, ESV), and
his verb, <span lang="grc">λυπέω</span> (*lypeō*, G3076), is the Hebrew sense of Isaiah 63:10. The
Septuagint has "provoked" (<span lang="grc">παροξύνω</span>, *paroxynō*, G3947) at that verse, so
Paul is following the Hebrew. David asked, "Where shall I go from your Spirit? Or where shall I flee
from your presence?" (Psalm 139:7, ESV). The Holy Spirit is God present, and the parallel lines of
the psalm say so.

### Your good Spirit

[![Ruchakha HaTovah, וְרוּחֲךָ הַטּוֹבָה, “and your good Spirit”, in large Hebrew with its Paleo-Hebrew form. In the scene: the pillar of cloud to lead them by day (Nehemiah 9:19); water out of the rock (9:15, 20); manna on the ground (9:20). Not stated, drawn for the scene: the camp's tents. The plate quotes Nehemiah 9:20 (ESV).](../assets/img/names-of-the-holy-spirit/ruchakha-hatovah.svg)](../assets/img/names-of-the-holy-spirit/ruchakha-hatovah.svg)

Nehemiah's Levites prayed through Israel's history and remembered the wilderness: "You gave your
good Spirit to instruct them and did not withhold your manna from their mouth" (Nehemiah 9:20, ESV).
<span dir="rtl">רוּחֲךָ הַטּוֹבָה</span> (*ruchakha hattovah*, H7307, H2896). The Spirit was given
with the manna and the water from the rock, as daily as bread. David prayed for the same: "Teach me
to do your will, for you are my God! Let your good Spirit lead me on level ground!"
(Psalm 143:10, ESV). God's Spirit is good, and He teaches and leads.

### My Spirit, poured out

[![Ruchi, רוּחִי, “my Spirit”, in large Hebrew with its Paleo-Hebrew form. In the scene: the valley full of bones, very dry (Ezekiel 37:1-2); breath from the four winds (37:9); they lived and stood on their feet, an exceedingly great army (37:10). The plate quotes Ezekiel 37:14 (ESV).](../assets/img/names-of-the-holy-spirit/ruchi.svg)](../assets/img/names-of-the-holy-spirit/ruchi.svg)

The prophets promised more than a Spirit who came on a few. God would pour Him out on everyone who
belongs to Him:

> ✝️ Joel 2:28-29 (ESV)
>
> 28 "And it shall come to pass afterward, that I will pour out my Spirit on all flesh; your sons
> and your daughters shall prophesy, your old men shall dream dreams, and your young men shall see
> visions. 29 Even on the male and female servants in those days I will pour out my Spirit.

<span dir="rtl">רוּחִי</span> (*ruchi*, H7307), "my Spirit", is God speaking of His own Spirit.
Joel names sons and daughters, old and young, slaves male and female: no category of Israel is left
out. Peter stood up at Pentecost and said, "this is what was uttered through the prophet Joel"
(Acts 2:16, ESV). [Weeks](../feasts/weeks.md#the-day-of-pentecost) follows the feast it fell on,
and [Dreams and Visions](dreams-and-visions/index.md) the dreams Joel names.

Ezekiel heard the promise as a new heart: "I will put my Spirit within you, and cause you to walk in
my statutes" (Ezekiel 36:27, ESV). The Spirit within does what the law written on stone could not.
To Zerubbabel, rebuilding the temple with a handful of returned exiles, God said, "Not by might, nor
by power, but by my Spirit, says the LORD of hosts" (Zechariah 4:6, ESV). Both prophets spoke to
Israel first, and both promises still stand for Israel; [Israel's Regathering and
Refining](../israel-and-church/israels-regathering-and-refining.md) follows Ezekiel 36-37 for the
nation.

Zechariah's last promise is a Spirit poured out on Jerusalem in mourning: "I will pour out on the
house of David and the inhabitants of Jerusalem a spirit of grace and pleas for mercy, so that, when
they look on me, on him whom they have pierced, they shall mourn for him" (Zechariah 12:10, ESV).
<span dir="rtl">רוּחַ חֵן</span> (*ruach chen*, H7307, H2580) is "a spirit of grace". The ESV
prints a small *s*, and the NLT and CSB footnote "Or *the Spirit*". The Hebrew has no capital
letters, so the case rests on the verse. It is God who pours, and in Joel and Ezekiel what God pours
out and puts within is His Spirit. Against that, "a spirit of grace and pleas for mercy" can be read
as a disposition He gives, a heart that pleads. Hebrews points the New Testament reader to the first reading. The
Septuagint renders the phrase <span lang="grc">πνεῦμα χάριτος</span> (*pneuma charitos*, G4151, G5485), and
Hebrews 10:29 warns against having "outraged the Spirit of grace", <span lang="grc">τὸ πνεῦμα τῆς
χάριτος</span>, the same words.

## Names Jesus gave Him

[![To Pneuma to Hagion, τὸ πνεῦμα τὸ ἅγιον, “the Holy Spirit; literally, the Spirit, the holy one”, in large Greek with its uncial capitals. In the scene: all together in one place, sitting (Acts 2:1-2); a sound from heaven like a mighty rushing wind, filling the house (2:2); divided tongues as of fire, resting on each one (2:3). Not stated, drawn for the scene: the house drawn open to show them. The plate quotes Acts 2:4 (ESV).](../assets/img/names-of-the-holy-spirit/to-pneuma-to-hagion.svg)](../assets/img/names-of-the-holy-spirit/to-pneuma-to-hagion.svg)

In the upper room Jesus named the Spirit more fully than any prophet had. He called Him the Helper
four times (John 14:16, 26; 15:26; 16:7), and the Spirit of truth three times (John 14:17; 15:26;
16:13). He said where the Spirit comes from and what He does:

- **The Holy Spirit, sent in Jesus' name.** "The Helper, the Holy Spirit, whom the Father will send
  in my name, he will teach you all things and bring to your remembrance all that I have said to
  you" (John 14:26, ESV). The Gospels you read are the disciples' memory of Jesus, kept by the
  Spirit.
- **Who proceeds from the Father.** "When the Helper comes, whom I will send to you from the Father,
  the Spirit of truth, who proceeds from the Father, he will bear witness about me" (John 15:26,
  ESV). The Father sends Him (John 14:26) and the Son sends Him (John 16:7).
- **Who convicts.** "When he comes, he will convict the world concerning sin and righteousness and
  judgment" (John 16:8, ESV). [The Restrainer](../last-things/the-restrainer.md#the-spirit-dwells-in-the-church)
  follows that work through the church.
- **Who glorifies Jesus.** "He will glorify me, for he will take what is mine and declare it to you"
  (John 16:14, ESV). [Know the Truth](../christian-life/know-the-truth.md) studies the Spirit who
  guides "into all the truth".

John's Greek shows a person in the grammar. *Pneuma* is a neuter noun, so John 14:26 says "the Holy
Spirit, <span lang="grc">ὃ</span>" (*ho*, G3739), with a neuter relative pronoun, as the grammar requires. Then
the next word is <span lang="grc">ἐκεῖνος</span> (*ekeinos*, G1565), masculine: "*he* will teach
you". John uses the masculine again with "the Spirit of truth" in John 16:13. [The
Restrainer](../last-things/the-restrainer.md#from-what-to-he) follows the same shift in
2 Thessalonians 2.

After the resurrection Jesus called Him **the promise of the Father**: "wait for the promise of the
Father, which, he said, 'you heard from me; for John baptized with water, but you will be baptized
with the Holy Spirit not many days from now'" (Acts 1:4-5, ESV). About ten days after the ascension, Peter
explained what Jerusalem was seeing: Jesus, "having received from the Father the promise of the Holy
Spirit, he has poured out this that you yourselves are seeing and hearing" (Acts 2:33, ESV). The
Father promised, the Son received and poured, and the Spirit came.

## Names that say He is God

The New Testament gives the Spirit names that join Him to the Father and to the Son, and it uses
them interchangeably.

- **The Spirit of God and the Spirit of Christ.** Paul uses both in one verse for the same Spirit:
  "You, however, are not in the flesh but in the Spirit, if in fact the Spirit of God dwells in you.
  Anyone who does not have the Spirit of Christ does not belong to him" (Romans 8:9, ESV). He is
  also "the Spirit of Jesus" (Acts 16:7), "the Spirit of his Son" (Galatians 4:6), "the Spirit of
  Jesus Christ" (Philippians 1:19), and "the Spirit of Christ" in the prophets who foretold His
  sufferings (1 Peter 1:11).
- **The Lord.** "Now the Lord is the Spirit, and where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is freedom"
  (2 Corinthians 3:17, ESV). The verse before, "when one turns to the Lord, the veil is removed"
  (2 Corinthians 3:16, ESV), echoes Moses, who removed his veil "whenever Moses went in before the
  LORD" (Exodus 34:34, ESV). "The Lord" here is the God of Sinai, and the Spirit is that Lord.
- **God.** Ananias kept back part of the price of his land and claimed to give it all. Peter said
  to him, "why has Satan filled your heart to lie to the Holy Spirit?", and then, "You have not lied
  to man but to God" (Acts 5:3-4, ESV).
- **One name.** "Baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit"
  (Matthew 28:19, ESV). "Name" is singular, <span lang="grc">ὄνομα</span> (*onoma*, G3686), and the
  Spirit shares it with the Father and the Son. Paul's blessing names the three together: "The grace
  of the Lord Jesus Christ and the love of God and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit be with you all"
  (2 Corinthians 13:14, ESV).

These verses are why the church confesses one God in three persons. The Spirit is a person, and He
is God, the third person, equal with the Father and the Son, as the [Statement of
Faith](../about/statement-of-faith.md#god-the-holy-spirit) sets out.

## Names for what He does in you

The second group of names says what the Spirit brings. Paul gives most of them, and he gives them to
ordinary believers.

### The Spirit of life

"The law of the Spirit of life has set you free in Christ Jesus from the law of sin and death"
(Romans 8:2, ESV). The breath that raised Ezekiel's army raises you. "If the Spirit of him who
raised Jesus from the dead dwells in you, he who raised Christ Jesus from the dead will also give
life to your mortal bodies through his Spirit who dwells in you" (Romans 8:11, ESV).

### The Spirit of adoption

"You have received the Spirit of adoption as sons, by whom we cry, 'Abba! Father!'"
(Romans 8:15, ESV). <span lang="grc">πνεῦμα υἱοθεσίας</span> (*pneuma huiothesias*, G4151, G5206)
is "the Spirit of son-placing", the legal act that made someone a son and heir. "The Spirit himself
bears witness with our spirit that we are children of God" (Romans 8:16, ESV). This is the doctrine
of **adoption**: God the Father takes you as His child, and the Spirit inside you is the proof and
the voice of it. Jesus promised it in the upper room: no orphans.

### The seal and the guarantee

> ✝️ Ephesians 1:13-14 (ESV)
>
> 13 In him you also, when you heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation, and believed
> in him, were sealed with the promised Holy Spirit, 14 who is the guarantee of our inheritance until
> we acquire possession of it, to the praise of his glory.

"The promised Holy Spirit" is in Greek "the Holy Spirit of promise", the promise of the Father
again. A seal (<span lang="grc">σφραγίζω</span>, *sphragizō*, G4972) marked ownership and protected
what it closed. The guarantee is <span lang="grc">ἀρραβών</span> (*arrabōn*, G728), a first payment
that binds the payer to pay the rest. The New Testament uses the word three times, and all three
are of the Spirit (2 Corinthians 1:22; 5:5; Ephesians 1:14). [Assurance of
Salvation](../salvation/assurance-of-salvation.md) and [The Bride of
Christ](../israel-and-church/bride-of-christ.md) study the word; in modern Greek
<span lang="el">αρραβώνας</span> is an engagement, and the ring that pledges a marriage.

### The Spirit of wisdom and of revelation

Paul prayed "that the God of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of glory, may give you the Spirit of
wisdom and of revelation in the knowledge of him" (Ephesians 1:17, ESV). The Spirit of wisdom who
rested on the Branch (Isaiah 11:2) is given to those who belong to the Branch.

### The Spirit of glory

"If you are insulted for the name of Christ, you are blessed, because the Spirit of glory and of God
rests upon you" (1 Peter 4:14, ESV). Peter's verb is <span lang="grc">ἀναπαύεται</span>
(*anapauetai*, G373), "rests", and it is the Septuagint's verb in Isaiah 11:2, "the Spirit of God
shall rest upon him". The Spirit who rested on Jesus rests on the people insulted for His name.

### The eternal Spirit

Christ, "who through the eternal Spirit offered himself without blemish to God", purifies "our
conscience from dead works to serve the living God" (Hebrews 9:14, ESV). <span lang="grc">πνεῦμα
αἰώνιον</span> (*pneuma aiōnion*, G4151, G166). The Spirit was there at the cross, as He was over
the waters at creation.

### Upon, and within forever

Read the Old Testament names beside the New and one difference stands out. The Spirit of the LORD
came upon Israel's judges and kings for a task, and could depart, as He did from Saul
(1 Samuel 16:14); David prayed that He would not be taken from him (Psalm 51:11). Jesus promised a
Helper "to be with you forever" who "dwells with you and will be in you" (John 14:16-17, ESV), and
John says why the change waited: "as yet the Spirit had not been given, because Jesus was not yet
glorified" (John 7:39, ESV). Since Pentecost the Spirit indwells every believer and seals each one
"for the day of redemption" (Ephesians 4:30, ESV), and "God's Spirit dwells in you"
(1 Corinthians 3:16, ESV). This is the dispensational reading of these verses, which takes the
indwelling of John 14 as new with the church. Others read David's prayer as showing the Spirit
already indwelt Old Testament believers, and take Psalm 51:11 as a king afraid of losing the anointing
for office. Both readings agree on the promise you hold: He is given to you forever.

## Names at the end

### The seven spirits before the throne

John greets the seven churches with grace "from him who is and who was and who is to come, and from
the seven spirits who are before his throne, and from Jesus Christ the faithful witness"
(Revelation 1:4-5, ESV). The CSB footnotes "Or *the sevenfold Spirit*". Placed between the Father
and Jesus Christ as a source of grace, the seven spirits are best read as the Holy Spirit in His
fullness, with the "seven torches of fire, which are the seven spirits of God" (Revelation 4:5, ESV) and Isaiah 11:2's
names behind it. Some read them as seven angels of the presence. This one is contested, and [The
Lampstand](../jesus/the-heavenly-pattern/lampstand.md#seven-torches-before-the-throne) sets out the
case.

### The Spirit and the Bride

The Bible's last invitation is spoken by the Spirit: "The Spirit and the Bride say, 'Come.' And let
the one who hears say, 'Come.' And let the one who is thirsty come; let the one who desires take the
water of life without price" (Revelation 22:17, ESV). Jesus had cried in the temple, "If anyone
thirsts, let him come to me and drink", and John explained, "this he said about the Spirit"
(John 7:37-39, ESV). The Spirit's last word in Scripture is the invitation He has been speaking
through the church since Pentecost.

## The Spirit teaches you the other names

The first study in this series ended with the name Jesus, and the second with the names He will
wear when He returns. This one ends where the Spirit's work does: in your mouth.

> ✝️ Galatians 4:6 (ESV)
>
> 6 And because you are sons, God has sent the Spirit of his Son into our hearts, crying, "Abba!
> Father!"

<span lang="grc">Αββα</span> (*Abba*, G5) is the Aramaic word Jesus prayed in Gethsemane
(Mark 14:36), kept untranslated. The Spirit cries it in you. And "no one can say 'Jesus is Lord'
except in the Holy Spirit" (1 Corinthians 12:3, ESV). Every name you learned in [The Names of
God](../god/names-of-god.md) and [The Names of Jesus](../jesus/names-of-jesus.md) you speak by the
Spirit. The Father gave His names, the Son came with His, and the Spirit lives in you to say them:
so you may call God "Abba! Father!", and call Jesus "Lord", and know that God Himself is with you.

## Discussion questions

1. **The language.** *Ruach* and *pneuma* each mean breath, wind and spirit. Read Ezekiel 37:9-14
   and John 3:8. What does the choice of one word for all three teach you about the Spirit's work?
2. **The text in its context.** Jesus named the Spirit "another Helper" on the night before the
   cross (John 14:16-18). What were the disciples afraid of, and how does each of the three names
   answer it?
3. **Christ.** Isaiah saw the Spirit resting on the Branch (Isaiah 11:2), and Peter says the Spirit
   of glory rests on you (1 Peter 4:14). What does it mean that the Spirit who rested on Jesus is
   the Spirit who lives in you?
4. **The hard part.** In the Old Testament the Spirit could depart (1 Samuel 16:14; Psalm 51:11).
   Since Pentecost He is given "forever" (John 14:16). What changed at Pentecost, and how should
   Ephesians 4:30 shape the way you live, if He will not leave?
5. **Be transformed.** Romans 8:15 says the Spirit is the one "by whom we cry, 'Abba! Father!'" When
   did you last pray to God as Father? What would change this week if you believed you were never
   an orphan?

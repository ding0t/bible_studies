# The Restrainer

Paul wrote to a frightened church that thought the day of the Lord had already come. He told them it
had not, and that something was holding back the man who must come first: "And you know what is
restraining him now so that he may be revealed in his time" (2 Thessalonians 2:6, ESV). They knew. We
have to read it back out of what Paul wrote.

**In one sentence:** The restrainer of 2 Thessalonians 2:6-7 is the Holy Spirit dwelling in the
church, the body of believers; while the church stands in the midst of the world, God holds back the
full revelation of lawlessness, and when the church is caught up, His restraining presence comes to be
out of the midst and the lawless one is revealed.

## Key Takeaways

### Types & Prophecy

Paul prophesies an order of events: the rebellion, the revealing of "the man of lawlessness," his
seat "in the temple of God," and his end, when "the Lord Jesus will kill [him] with the breath of his
mouth and bring to nothing by the appearance of his coming" (2 Thessalonians 2:3-4, 8, ESV). The
restrainer stands between the church's present and that revealing, which falls
within Daniel's seventieth week, the seven years of the tribulation (Daniel 9:27).

### Lessons about Jesus

- **Jesus sent the restrainer.** "If I go, I will send him to you" (John 16:7, ESV). The Spirit who
  holds back lawlessness came because Jesus went to the Father.
- **Jesus ends the lawless one.** The man Satan empowers is brought "to nothing by the appearance of
  his coming" (2 Thessalonians 2:8, ESV).
- **Jesus gathers His people first.** Paul opens the chapter with "the coming of our Lord Jesus
  Christ and our being gathered together to him" (2:1, ESV).

### Be Transformed

- **Think.** The Holy Spirit lives in the church, and His presence in her holds back the full tide
  of lawlessness now. The church matters to the world in ways the world cannot see.
- **Attitude.** Live as salt and light "in the midst of a crooked and twisted generation"
  (Philippians 2:15, ESV), unafraid of the day of wrath: "God has not destined us for wrath" (1
  Thessalonians 5:9, ESV).
- **Do.** Name one place this week where your presence can hold back wrong — at work, online, in
  your family — and be there as someone the Spirit lives in.

## The passage

The Thessalonians had been told, "either by a spirit or a spoken word, or a letter seeming to be from
us," that "the day of the Lord has come" (2 Thessalonians 2:2, ESV). Paul answers with an order that
has not yet happened: "that day will not come, unless the rebellion comes first, and the man of
lawlessness is revealed, the son of destruction" (2:3, ESV), who "takes his seat in the temple of God,
proclaiming himself to be God" (2:4, ESV).

Then Paul reminds them of what he taught them in person (2:5) and names the reason the lawless one has
not yet appeared:

> ✝️ 2 Thessalonians 2:6-8 (ESV)
>
> 6 And you know what is restraining him now so that he may be revealed in his time. 7 For the mystery
> of lawlessness is already at work. Only he who now restrains it will do so until he is out of the
> way. 8 And then the lawless one will be revealed, whom the Lord Jesus will kill with the breath of
> his mouth and bring to nothing by the appearance of his coming.

The verb is κατέχω (*katechō*), to hold back or hold fast. Lawlessness is "already at work" as a
mystery, hidden. Its full form, a man, waits until the restrainer is out of the way. And Paul begins
the whole chapter with "our being gathered together to him" (2:1, ESV), the event he described in 1
Thessalonians 4:17.

## What the restrainer must be

The passage itself sets three requirements.

**Personal.** In verse 7 the restrainer is ὁ κατέχων (*ho katechōn*), "he who restrains," a
masculine participle.

**Greater than Satan.** "The coming of the lawless one is by the activity of Satan with all power and
false signs and wonders" (2:9, ESV). Whatever holds that back holds back Satan's own work. John says
who is greater: "he who is in you is greater than he who is in the world" (1 John 4:4, ESV).

**Present now, until a set end.** The restrainer is at work "now" (2:6, 7) and will do so "until" he
is out of the way. His restraint belongs to this age and ends at a point God has fixed, "in his time"
(2:6).

A person, stronger than Satan, present in the world through this whole age and then out of the way:
that describes God. Which Person of God is the next question, and the answer this study gives is the
One who dwells in the church in this age, the Holy Spirit. Interpreters divide over that
identification (see [Other readings](#other-readings)); what follows is the case for it.

## From "what" to "he"

Paul names the restrainer twice, in two genders. Verse 6 has τὸ κατέχον (*to katechon*), neuter, "what
is restraining." Verse 7 has ὁ κατέχων (*ho katechōn*), masculine, "he who restrains." The *ESV Study
Bible* notes the same shift (note on 2:5-7).

The Holy Spirit carries both kinds of name in John's Gospel. His name πνεῦμα (*pneuma*), "Spirit," is
neuter. His title παράκλητος (*paraklētos*), "Helper," is masculine. John 14:26 uses both in one
sentence: "But the Helper, the Holy Spirit, whom the Father will send in my name, he will teach you
all things" (ESV). There the neuter "whom" (ὅ) follows "Spirit," and the masculine "he" (ἐκεῖνος)
takes up "Helper." John 16:13 sets the two side by side: ἐκεῖνος, τὸ πνεῦμα τῆς ἀληθείας, "he, the
Spirit of truth" (ESV "When the Spirit of truth comes"). One Person is spoken of in the neuter and in
the masculine. Paul's neuter τὸ κατέχον and masculine ὁ κατέχων fit a restrainer who is that Person;
the correspondence is this study's reading, and the grammar leaves room for it.

## The Spirit dwells in the church

### His temple

Jesus promised the Spirit to His disciples as a lasting presence: "he will give you another Helper, to
be with you forever, even the Spirit of truth, whom the world cannot receive ... he dwells with you and
will be in you" (John 14:16-17, ESV). Paul tells a whole congregation, "you are God's temple and ...
God's Spirit dwells in you" (1 Corinthians 3:16, ESV), where "you" is plural. The church is "being
built together into a dwelling place for God by the Spirit" (Ephesians 2:22, ESV).

So in this age the Holy Spirit's dwelling on earth is the church, the body of believers. When Paul
says "he who now restrains," he describes the Spirit present in the world in a particular way: in
the people He indwells.

### Convicting the world through them

Jesus said the Spirit's coming would reach the world through the disciples: "if I go, I will send him
to you. And when he comes, he will convict the world concerning sin and righteousness and judgment"
(John 16:7-8, ESV). He comes *to you*, and from there He convicts *the world*. Jesus called His people
"the salt of the earth" and "the light of the world" (Matthew 5:13-14, ESV).

The Old Testament may show the Spirit holding back evil before. Before the flood the LORD said, "My
Spirit shall not abide in man forever" (Genesis 6:3, ESV). The Hebrew verb there is uncertain: the CSB
footnotes "strive," and the LSB "rule in," adding that some ancient versions read "abide in." On any rendering, God's Spirit was engaged
with a corrupt world until a set time, and then judgment fell.

**This shows that God holds back evil through the people His Spirit lives in.** The church's presence
in the world is God's restraint on lawlessness in this age.

## Out of the midst

### Paul's phrase

"Until he is out of the way" is ἕως ἐκ μέσου γένηται (*heōs ek mesou genētai*), literally "until he
comes to be out of the midst." The verb γίνομαι (*ginomai*) means "become, come to be." The clause
names no one who removes him; it says he comes to be out of the midst. [The Rapture of the
Church](rapture.md#what-verse-7-actually-says-about-the-going) sets out that grammar in full.

Paul uses "out of the midst," ἐκ μέσου (*ek mesou*), of people and things taken out from among
others. The man in Corinth is to "be removed from among you" (1 Corinthians 5:2, ESV). The record of
debt was "set aside," literally taken "out of the midst" (ἐκ τοῦ μέσου), at the cross (Colossians
2:14). And the
church herself is called out, in words Paul takes from Isaiah 52:11: "Therefore go out from their
midst, and be separate from them" (2 Corinthians 6:17, ESV).

### The church in the midst now

Paul also tells the church where she stands now. Believers are "children of God without blemish in the
midst of a crooked and twisted generation, among whom you shine as lights in the world" (Philippians
2:15, ESV). "In the midst" is μέσον (*meson*), the same word. The church stands in the midst of the
world now. 2 Thessalonians 2:7 does not say what the restrainer comes out of the midst of; read with
Philippians 2:15, this study takes it as the world where the church now stands.

### How Hebrew translators heard it

Two nineteenth-century translations of the New Testament into Hebrew show how Hebraists heard Paul's
phrase: Franz Delitzsch's and the Salkinson–Ginsburg version. Both were translated from the Greek,
so they record a Hebraist's judgement of what Paul meant.

Delitzsch renders "out of the midst" with <span dir="rtl">מתוך</span> (*mitokh*, "from the midst").
He uses the same word at 2 Corinthians 6:17, "go out from their midst"
(<span dir="rtl">מתוכם</span>, *mitokham*), and he places the church
<span dir="rtl">בתוך</span> (*betokh*), "in the midst of," a crooked generation at Philippians 2:15.
In his Hebrew the three verses share one word, as they share μέσος in Paul's Greek.

Salkinson–Ginsburg carries the same word through all three verses:
<span dir="rtl">מִתּוֹךְ</span> at 2 Thessalonians 2:7, <span dir="rtl">בְּתוֹךְ</span> at
Philippians 2:15 and <span dir="rtl">מִתּוֹכָם</span> at 2 Corinthians 6:17. It names the restrainer
<span dir="rtl">הָעֹצֵר</span> (*ha-otser*), "the one who holds back," from
<span dir="rtl">עָצַר</span> (*atsar*), and adds <span dir="rtl">הַמְּסִילָּה</span> (*ha-mesillah*),
"the road," which has no counterpart in the Greek; whether it is the translator supplying the idiom
behind English "out of the way" is an open question. Both translations use a passive verb —
Delitzsch <span dir="rtl">שיוסר</span> (*she-yusar*), Salkinson–Ginsburg
<span dir="rtl">יוּסַר</span> (*yusar*), "he will be removed" — which supplies an agent the Greek
leaves unstated.

### Caught up

On this reading, the rapture is that moment. "We who are alive, who are left, will be caught up together with them in
the clouds to meet the Lord in the air" (1 Thessalonians 4:17, ESV). "Caught up" is ἁρπαγησόμεθα, from
ἁρπάζω (*harpazō*), to snatch. The church is taken out of the midst, and the Spirit's restraining
presence in her goes with her. Then "the lawless one will be revealed" (2 Thessalonians 2:8, ESV).

**This shows that God keeps His people out of the wrath to come.** The same event that ends the
restraint gathers the church to her Lord: "God has not destined us for wrath, but to obtain salvation
through our Lord Jesus Christ" (1 Thessalonians 5:9, ESV).

## Where this falls in the seven years

The tribulation that follows the rapture is Daniel's seventieth week: "he shall make a strong covenant
with many for one week, and for half of the week he shall put an end to sacrifice and offering. And on
the wing of abominations shall come one who makes desolate" (Daniel 9:27, ESV). This site reads that
week as seven literal years, beginning after the church is caught up ([The Rapture of the
Church](rapture.md#the-tribulation)).

Dispensational readers identify Paul's lawless one with Daniel's covenant-maker. He comes into the
open during the seven years. At their midpoint, the "half of the week" of Daniel 9:27, he "takes his
seat in the temple of God, proclaiming himself to be God" (2 Thessalonians 2:4, ESV). On that
reading this is the "abomination of desolation
spoken of by the prophet Daniel, standing in the holy place" (Matthew 24:15, ESV), and it opens what
Jesus called "great tribulation, such as has not been from the beginning of the world until now"
(24:21, ESV). Paul does not say how long passes between the restrainer's going and the covenant; he
says only that the revealing cannot come first.

So the order is this: the church in the midst, restraining; the church caught up; the seven years,
with the lawless one revealed and seated in the temple at their midpoint; and his end "by the
appearance of his coming" (2 Thessalonians 2:8, ESV).

## The Spirit in the tribulation

The Holy Spirit does not leave the world. He is God: "Where shall I go from your Spirit?" (Psalm
139:7, ESV). After the church is caught
up, people still come to Christ: John sees "a great multitude that no one could number, from every
nation," who "are the ones coming out of the great tribulation" and "have washed their robes and made
them white in the blood of the Lamb" (Revelation 7:9, 14, ESV). An angel proclaims "an eternal gospel
... to every nation and tribe and language and people" (14:6, ESV). No one is saved apart from the
Spirit.

What ends is His restraining presence in the church, the body He indwells in this age. On this reading the
Spirit works in the tribulation as He worked before Pentecost, among people He draws to God, and the full
tide of lawlessness is let loose.

## Other readings

Interpreters have proposed other restrainers, and the identification is contested. The *ESV Study
Bible* lists the Roman Empire or emperor, the Holy Spirit and the archangel Michael (note on 2:5-7).
The *NIV Biblical Theology Study Bible* lists seven, among them "the presence of the church and the
Holy Spirit" (note on 2:6-7). The case against each alternative rests on the passage's own
requirements.

### The objection from verses 1-4

The sharpest objection to this study's reading comes from the *NIV Cultural Backgrounds Study Bible*:
"Some suggest the church in the world, but this would contradict the context (vv. 1–4)" (note on
2:6-7). The answer lies in that context. Paul's subject in verse 1 is "the coming of our Lord Jesus
Christ and our being gathered together to him." The error he corrects in verse 2 is that "the day of
the Lord has come." Verses 3-4 list what must come before *that day*: the rebellion and the revealing
of the man of lawlessness. They set the conditions for the day of the Lord, and the gathering of
verse 1 is not among them. A church gathered before that day, and a restrainer who comes out of the
midst when she is gathered, fits verses 1-4. [The Rapture of the Church](rapture.md) makes the same
case at length.

### Rome, Michael and the gospel

- **Rome, or human government.** Government does restrain evil: the ruler is "the servant of God, an
  avenger who carries out God's wrath on the wrongdoer" (Romans 13:4, ESV). But the third
  requirement fails. Rome fell, and the lawless one did not appear. Government does not come out of
  the midst when he is revealed; its authority passes to him, as the beast is given "authority ...
  over every tribe and people and language and nation" (Revelation 13:7, ESV). Victorinus of Pettau read the restrainer as Rome; [The
  Rapture of the Church](rapture.md#what-victorinus-actually-says) sets out what he wrote.
- **The archangel Michael.** This is the strongest alternative, and the *NIV Biblical Theology Study
  Bible* favours it (note on 2:6-7). Michael resists evil powers: he "came to help" against "the
  prince of the kingdom of Persia" (Daniel 10:13, ESV), and "Michael and his angels" fight the dragon
  (Revelation 12:7, ESV). The *ESV Study Bible* cites a Septuagint reading and rabbinic traditions in
  which Michael "passes away" before the great tribulation (note on 2:5-7). That Greek text is not in
  this project, whose Greek Daniel is Theodotion's; it reads ἀναστήσεται, "shall arise." The Hebrew
  verb <span dir="rtl">עָמַד</span> (*amad*) means "stand," and it can mean "stop" ("she ceased
  bearing," Genesis 29:35, ESV), so the verb alone does not settle it. The verse's context does:
  Michael is "the great prince who has charge of your people," and "at that time your people shall be
  delivered" (Daniel 12:1, ESV). He stands for Israel in the time of trouble. And he fights under
  God's authority: contending with the devil, he "did not presume to pronounce a blasphemous
  judgment, but said, 'The Lord rebuke you'" (Jude 9, ESV). On the Michael reading the restraint is
  still God's, which leads back to the one Person of God who dwells in the church.
- **The preaching of the gospel, and Paul.** The gospel is proclaimed in the tribulation (Revelation
  14:6), so it does not come out of the midst when the lawless one is revealed, and Paul's death did
  not bring the lawless one.

### The reading that meets all three

The Holy Spirit dwelling in the church meets all three requirements: He is a person, He is greater
than Satan, and His presence in the church lasts through this age and ends when she is caught up.

## Discussion Questions

1. John 14:26 names the Spirit "the Helper" (masculine) and "the Holy Spirit" (neuter) in one sentence.
   How does that help you read Paul's "what is restraining" and "he who restrains" (2:6-7)?
2. The Thessalonians thought the day of the Lord had come (2:2). What fears does the order of events
   in 2:1-8 answer?
3. Jesus sent the Spirit to His disciples, and the Spirit convicts the world (John 16:7-8). How does
   the church's presence hold back evil where you live?
4. Michael is the strongest alternative reading. What in Daniel 12:1 and Jude 9 weighs against it?
5. Philippians 2:15 places the church "in the midst" of a crooked generation, and 2 Thessalonians 2:7
   has the restrainer come "out of the midst." How does the rapture connect the two?
6. Jesus sent the Spirit (John 16:7) and will end the lawless one by His coming (2:8). Trace the order
   of events in "Where this falls in the seven years": where is the church at each point?
7. "God has not destined us for wrath" (1 Thessalonians 5:9). How does that change the way you wait?

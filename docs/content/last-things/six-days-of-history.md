# Six days of history

Israel learned its week from Genesis 1. "For in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, the sea,
and all that is in them, and rested on the seventh day" (Exodus 20:11, ESV). Every Israelite worked
six days and rested on the seventh because God had. Within a century of the apostles, Christians
were reading the same week as a map of history: six thousand years of work, then a thousand years
of rest. This study walks the six working days one at a time and asks what each of them looks like
laid over the millennium it names.

> ✝️ Genesis 1:31 (ESV)
>
> 31 And God saw everything that he had made, and behold, it was very good. And there was evening
> and there was morning, the sixth day.

**In one sentence:** Read as a framework, the six working days of Genesis 1 match the shape of the
six thousand years that follow them, from light divided from darkness to man's counterfeit image,
and the pattern shows a God who orders history as He ordered creation, sends His Son Jesus at the
appointed time as the true image of God, and brings the week to His rest.

### Types & Prophecy

- **Six days, then rest, is Scripture's own pattern.** Genesis 2:1-3 blesses the seventh day,
  Hebrews 4:9 says a Sabbath rest "remains," and Revelation 20 numbers the coming reign at a thousand
  years. Barnabas and Irenaeus read the six days as six thousand years.
- **The day-by-day match is a framework.** Matching each creation day to a particular millennium is
  this site's construction, built on those witnesses. It shows the shape of history and gives no
  date (Acts 1:7).
- **Day six ends in a counterfeit.** Man was made in God's image on the sixth day. Revelation 13
  ends man's dominion with an image of the beast given breath and a number made of sixes.

### Lessons about Jesus

- **Jesus is the image of God that Adam bore and the beast counterfeits.** "He is the image of the
  invisible God" (Colossians 1:15, ESV), and God is conforming His people "to the image of his Son"
  (Romans 8:29, ESV).
- **Jesus came "when the fullness of time had come"** (Galatians 4:4, ESV). On this framework that
  fullness falls at the end of the fourth day, when the sun of righteousness rises (Malachi 4:2).
- **The last Adam died on the day the first Adam was made.** Irenaeus saw Christ's death on the
  sixth day of the week as "a second creation" (*Against Heresies* 5.23.2).

### Be Transformed

- **Think.** History has a shape because God gave it one. He declares "the end from the beginning"
  (Isaiah 46:10), and the week you live in is His pattern for the whole.
- **Attitude.** Hold the framework loosely and the Lord tightly. The shape is His gift to you; the
  date He keeps (Acts 1:7).
- **Do.** Let your own week preach to you. Work six days as someone made in God's image, and rest
  on the seventh looking forward to His rest.

## Genesis 1 in its setting

Genesis stands at the head of the five books of Moses, and Jews and Christians alike received it as
his until the nineteenth century (*ESV Study Bible*, introduction to Genesis). Its first hearers
were Israel, the people God brought out of Egypt and gave a Sabbath. They heard Genesis 1 as the
account of the God who had just delivered them.

The unit is Genesis 1:1-2:3. The *ESV Study Bible* describes it as seven sections marked by set
phrases (note on 1:1-2:3). Each working day opens "And God said" and closes "And there was evening
and there was morning," followed by the day's number. Then comes the seventh day, which has no
evening at all. The sixth day is the longest section by far, which the *ESV Study Bible* reads as
marking it the peak of the passage (note on 1:24-31).

The chapter's own point is God. He speaks and the world comes into being. He separates and names
and fills. He sees that it is good, six times, and at the end "very good" (1:31). **This shows that
God is the sovereign Creator who orders everything by His word, and that the world He made answers
to His purpose.** Israel's week preached that every seventh day (Exodus 20:11).

Everything that follows in this study stands on that ground. The creation week is first of all an
account of creation. Reading it as a pattern for history is a second step, and it has to be marked
as one.

## Reading the week as history

### The ancient witnesses

The church read the six days as six thousand years almost at once. The *Epistle of Barnabas*,
early in the second century, is the first: "in six days, that is, in six thousand years, all
things will be finished" (*Barnabas* 15). Irenaeus, late in the second century, made it a rule:
"in as many days as this world was made, in so many thousand years shall it be concluded"
(*Against Heresies* 5.28.3). Both lean on the ratio of Psalm 90:4 and 2 Peter 3:8, and Jewish
readers had used the same ratio before them. [A Day Is a Thousand Years](day-is-a-thousand-years.md)
traces that history and the text's own reasons for the reading. Psalm 90 itself is studied in
[A Thousand Years in Your Sight](a-thousand-years-in-your-sight.md).

### What kind of claim this is

The witnesses establish the frame: six days of work, then rest. Matching each creation day to the
content of a particular millennium is a further step, and it is this site's construction. It has
precedent. The Temple's daily psalms were tied each to a day of creation (b. *Rosh HaShanah* 31a,
discussed in the [parent study](day-is-a-thousand-years.md#the-temple-the-creation-week-sung-every-week)).
But no ancient writer walks the six days through history the way the sections below do. **Treat
what follows as typology: a pattern observed in the text, offered for reflection, and answerable to
Scripture at every point.**

It is also no calendar. The anchors below use the site's working epoch, creation at 3959 BC. On
that epoch the six thousand years run out in AD 2042; on Ussher's 4004 BC, which this site used
until 2026-10-01, they ran out in AD 1997. [The Day Is Near](day-is-near.md#when-is-the-year-6000)
works from a different epoch and reaches AD 2075, and the parent study sets out
[why nobody has got the date right](day-is-a-thousand-years.md#why-nobody-has-got-the-date-right).
Jesus said, "It is not for you to know times or seasons that the Father has fixed by his own
authority" (Acts 1:7, ESV). The framework gives the shape of history. The Father keeps the date.

### The days and the millennia

The anchors use the site's working convention — creation at 3959 BC, Adam at year 0 of the world
(AM), consistent with [Bible Chronology & Genealogical Time](genealogy-times.md) — and the dates
inside the first two millennia come straight from the age-data in Genesis 5 and 11.

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Light divided from darkness (1:3-5) | AM 0-1000 | Adam; the fall; the two lines; Adam dies AM 930, Enoch taken AM 987 |
| 2 | Waters divided above and below (1:6-8) | AM 1000-2000 | The Flood at AM 1656 — that division undone and remade; Babel |
| 3 | Dry land; seed and fruit (1:9-13) | AM 2000-3000 | Abraham, the land, the *seed*; Israel through the sea onto dry ground |
| 4 | Sun, moon, stars for appointed times (1:14-19) | AM 3000-4000 | Temple, throne, feasts, prophets; closes with the Sun of Righteousness |
| 5 | Waters swarm; birds fly (1:20-23) | AM 4000-5000 | The gospel over the sea of the nations; the church multiplying |
| 6 | Beasts; man in God's image, given dominion (1:24-31) | AM 5000-6000 | Man's dominion at full stretch — and its counterfeit image |
| 7 | God rests, blesses, sanctifies (2:1-3) | AM 6000-7000 | Revelation 20's thousand years |

## The first three days

### Day one — light out of darkness

The first day divides light from darkness, and the first millennium divides the human race the
same way: Cain's line and Seth's, running in parallel through Genesis 4-5. Genesis 4:26 marks the
turn — "at that time people began to call upon the name of the LORD" — and places it at the birth
of Seth's son Enosh. The verse does not say that *only* Seth's line called on the LORD, and the
*NKJV Cultural Backgrounds Study Bible* is right to warn against reading it that way. What Genesis
gives is two genealogies on two trajectories.

The day closes with two different ends. Adam dies at AM 930 (Genesis 5:5), inside the thousand-year
day. The book of Jubilees read that as the fulfilment of "in the day that you eat of it you shall
surely die" (Genesis 2:17): Adam died within the "day" of a thousand years (*Jubilees* 4:29-30,
set out in the [parent study](day-is-a-thousand-years.md#jubilees-adam-died-inside-the-day)). Enoch,
seventh from Adam, is taken at AM 987 without dying at all (Genesis 5:23-24). The first day of
history ends with the first man under the sentence and one man exempted from it, both inside the
same thousand years.

### Day two — the waters divided

Day two is the only day of the week God does not call good. Genesis 1:8 has no
<span dir="rtl">כִּי־טוֹב</span> (*ki tov*, "that it was good"); day three has it twice (1:10,
1:12). What day two makes is a separation — an expanse holding waters above apart from waters
below.

That separation is undone in the second millennium. "On that day all the fountains of the great
deep burst forth, and the windows of the heavens were opened" (Genesis 7:11, ESV) — the waters
below and the waters above rejoin, and the world of day two is uncreated. Then it is remade:
"the fountains of the deep and the windows of the heavens were closed" (Genesis 8:2). The Flood
falls at AM 1656 on the Masoretic figures. The millennium ends with a second division, of tongues
and nations, at Babel.

### Day three — dry land and seed

Two words from day three carry the third millennium. The land appears —
<span dir="rtl">יַבָּשָׁה</span> (*yabbashah*, H3004, "dry land," Genesis 1:9-10) — and the earth
brings forth vegetation "yielding seed," <span dir="rtl">זֶרַע</span> (*zera*, H2233, Genesis
1:11-12).

Both reappear as the substance of the promise to Abraham. "To your **offspring** I will give this
land" (Genesis 12:7) is *zera*; "I will surely multiply your **offspring**… and in your
**offspring** shall all the nations of the earth be blessed" (Genesis 22:17-18) is *zera* twice
more. And when God brings that seed out of Egypt, "the people of Israel went into the midst of the
sea on **dry ground**" (Exodus 14:22) — *yabbashah*, the same word as Genesis 1:9, land emerging
from divided waters so that life can stand on it.

**These three days show that God keeps His word through judgment.** He called light out of
darkness, He brought eight people through the waters (Genesis 7:13), and He promised a land and a seed and then
walked His people onto dry ground to receive them.

## The last three days

### Day four — lights for the appointed times

> ✝️ Genesis 1:14 (ESV)
>
> 14 And God said, "Let there be lights in the expanse of the heavens to separate the day from the
> night. And let them be for signs and for seasons, and for days and years,

"Seasons" is <span dir="rtl">מוֹעֲדִים</span> (*mo'adim*, H4150), *appointed times*, the word
Leviticus 23 uses six times across four verses for the feasts of the LORD (23:2, 4, 37, 44). Day
four is when God installs the machinery the festival calendar runs on.

The fourth millennium is when Israel starts running on it. The Temple is founded in its opening
decades (Solomon's fourth year, c. 967 BC, AM ~3037), and with the Temple come the *mo'adim* kept in
their appointed order. The throne established in the same period is described in exactly day four's
terms — sun, moon and stars together. Of David's line: "His offspring shall endure forever, his
throne as long as the sun before me… Like the moon it shall be established forever, a faithful
witness in the skies" (Psalm 89:36-37). Abraham's offspring had already been numbered against the
stars: "Look toward heaven, and number the stars… So shall your offspring be" (Genesis 15:5). And
the millennium closes with the greater light: "the sun of righteousness shall rise with healing in
its wings" (Malachi 4:2) — in the closing chapter of Malachi, the last book of the Christian Old
Testament, at the end of the fourth day.

On the working epoch, AM 4000 falls at 4 BC, close to the traditional date of Jesus' birth. The
parent study explains why that fit is partly an artifact of how Ussher set his epoch. Scripture's
own statement is the firmer one: "when the fullness of time had come, God sent forth his Son"
(Galatians 4:4, ESV). God appointed the time as He appointed the lights.

### Day five — the swarming sea

Day five fills the waters and the sky, and it is the first day God blesses anything: "And God
blessed them, saying, 'Be fruitful and multiply and fill the waters in the seas…'" (Genesis
1:22).

In Revelation's own vocabulary, "the waters that you saw… are peoples and multitudes and nations
and languages" (17:15). The fifth millennium is the gospel going out over that sea — men called to
be "fishers of men" (Matthew 4:19), a kingdom "like a net that was thrown into the sea and gathered
fish of every kind" (Matthew 13:47), a commission whose verbs are day five's verbs. The church is
the one thing in this scheme that begins with a blessing and a command to multiply.

### Day six — the image, and the counterfeit

Day six makes man in God's image and gives him dominion over everything already made.

> ✝️ Genesis 1:26-27 (ESV)
>
> 26 Then God said, "Let us make man in our image, after our likeness. And let them have dominion
> over the fish of the sea and over the birds of the heavens and over the livestock and over all
> the earth and over every creeping thing that creeps on the earth." 27 So God created man in his
> own image, in the image of God he created him; male and female he created them.

The sixth millennium is man's dominion pressed to its limit, and Revelation's account of the end
of that dominion reads like day six run backwards. The second beast tells the earth "to make an
**image** for the beast" and is "allowed to give breath to the image of the beast, so that the
image of the beast might even speak" (Revelation 13:14-15). The word is **εἰκών** (*eikōn*, G1504)
— the same word the Septuagint uses at Genesis 1:26-27, κατ' εἰκόνα. On the day whose own act was
God making man in His image and breathing life into him, man makes an image, gives it breath, and
demands worship for it. The breath words differ — Genesis 2:7 LXX has πνοή (*pnoē*, G4157), Revelation 13:15 has
πνεῦμα (*pneuma*, G4151) — so the parallel rests on the act and on *eikōn*.

Then the number: "let the one who has understanding calculate the number of the beast, for it is
the number of a man, and his number is 666" (Revelation 13:18). Irenaeus already read this as
belonging to the sixth day — "six times a hundred, six times ten, and six units… as a summing up
of the whole of that apostasy which has taken place during six thousand years" (*Against Heresies*
5.28.2). A number of a man, made of sixes, on the day man was made.

Day six also holds the counter-move. Irenaeus notes that Christ "underwent His sufferings upon the
day preceding the Sabbath, that is, the sixth day of the creation, on which day man was created;
thus granting him a second creation by means of His passion" (*Against Heresies* 5.23.2). The
last Adam dies on the day the first Adam was made.

## The true image

Day six is the peak of Genesis 1, and it turns on one word. The man and woman are made in the image
of God. The beast's image is the same word used against God. And the New Testament gives the word
its fullest sense in a person: "He is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of all
creation" (Colossians 1:15, ESV). Paul's word is **εἰκών** again (ὅς ἐστιν εἰκὼν τοῦ θεοῦ).

So the week's sixth day ends with two images standing against each other. One is made by man,
given a borrowed breath, and worshipped under compulsion (Revelation 13:15). The other is the eternal
Son, who took flesh and died on the sixth day of the week. Among those who reign with Christ for the
thousand years are "those who had not worshiped the beast or its image" (Revelation 20:4, ESV). **This shows that God restores His
image in fallen people through His Son Jesus.** He "predestined" His people "to be conformed to the
image of his Son" (Romans 8:29, ESV), and that work is under way now: "we all… are being transformed
into the same image from one degree of glory to another" (2 Corinthians 3:18, ESV; *eikona* again).
That is sanctification, and its end is glorification.

The week does not end on the sixth day. God rested on the seventh, blessed it and made it holy
(Genesis 2:3), and "there remains a Sabbath rest for the people of God" (Hebrews 4:9, ESV). The
parent study follows that rest into
[Revelation 20's thousand years](day-is-a-thousand-years.md#day-seven-the-rest-that-is-numbered).
**This shows that God works history toward His own rest.** He is "declaring the end from the
beginning… saying, 'My counsel shall stand, and I will accomplish all my purpose'" (Isaiah 46:10,
ESV). You live somewhere in His week. Bear His image, refuse the counterfeit, and wait for His rest.

## Reading Genesis 1 in English

Genesis marks two of its days in a way English Bibles often erase. Day one is a cardinal —
<span dir="rtl">יוֹם אֶחָד</span> (*yom echad*), "day one" — the day the others are counted from.
Days two to five carry bare ordinals with no article. The definite article first appears at day
six, <span dir="rtl">יוֹם הַשִּׁשִּׁי</span> (*yom hashishi*), "**the** sixth day," and stays for
the seventh. The parent study sets out the full pattern in
[The week's own grammar](day-is-a-thousand-years.md#the-weeks-own-grammar).

Checked directly against the Hebrew:

| Version | Genesis 1:5 | Genesis 1:23 | Genesis 1:31 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hebrew (WLC) | day one | a fifth day | **the** sixth day |
| ESV | the first day | the fifth day | the sixth day |
| BSB | the first day | the fifth day | the sixth day |
| WEB | the first day | a fifth day | a sixth day |
| ASV | one day | a fifth day | **the** sixth day |
| NASB 2020 | one day | a fifth day | **the** sixth day |
| Rotherham | one day | a fifth day | **the** sixth day |

ESV and BSB supply "the" throughout and erase the distinction entirely. WEB keeps the indefinite
ordinals but loses the article at day six. ASV, NASB and Rotherham preserve both. If you read
Genesis 1 with this framework in view, keep a second, more literal version open at this chapter.
The article at day six is the Hebrew's own mark on the day of the image, and the most widely read
translations do not show it.

## Discussion Questions

1. Genesis 1:31 has "**the** sixth day" where days two to five have no article. What does the
   Hebrew draw attention to by marking the day man was made?
2. Israel first heard Genesis 1 as a people given a Sabbath (Exodus 20:11). What would the six
   days of work and the seventh of rest have told them about the God who brought them out of Egypt?
3. Revelation 13:14-15 has an image made, given breath and worshipped; Colossians 1:15 calls Jesus
   "the image of the invisible God." What does it mean for you that the true image is a person?
4. Barnabas and Irenaeus read the six days as six thousand years, and Jesus said the times are the
   Father's to fix (Acts 1:7). How do you hold a pattern for history and a hidden date together?
5. Paul says believers "are being transformed into the same image" (2 Corinthians 3:18). Where in
   your week is that transformation meeting resistance?

# The End of the Age

The disciples sat with Jesus on the Mount of Olives and asked Him about "the end of the age"
(Matthew 24:3, ESV). Matthew's Gospel closes on the same words. "I am with you always, to the end of
the age" (Matthew 28:20, ESV). Between those two sentences sit a harvest, a net and the coming of the
Son of Man. Then Hebrews says the end of the ages has already come.

**In one sentence:** Jesus took Daniel's word for the end and named three things by it: the harvest
that separates the righteous from the wicked, His own coming, and the limit of His promise to be with
you every day; the apostles say that end began at the cross, so you live between its opening and its
close.

This study covers the line between the two ages: what the New Testament calls its close, and when.
The age on the far side of the line is in [A New Heaven and a New Earth](new-heaven-and-new-earth.md#the-age-to-come).
How long the ages were reckoned to run, six thousand-year days and the Talmud's three ages of two
thousand years, is in [A Day Is a Thousand Years](day-is-a-thousand-years.md).

## Key Takeaways

### Types & Prophecy

**Prophecy.** Daniel was told to seal his book "until the time of the end" (Daniel 12:4, ESV). Both
Greek versions of Daniel call that end the συντέλεια (*synteleia*), and Jesus uses the same word for
the close of this age (Matthew 24:3). The close comes when the Son of Man sends His angels to gather
and to separate (Matthew 13:41; 24:31).

**Prophecy fulfilled.** Christ "has appeared once for all at the end of the ages to put away sin by
the sacrifice of himself" (Hebrews 9:26, ESV). The end Daniel waited for opened at the cross.

### Lessons about Jesus

- **Jesus is the Lord of the harvest.** He sows the good seed, He owns the field, and at the end of
  the age He sends His angels to gather "out of his kingdom" everything that offends (Matthew 13:37,
  41).
- **Jesus is God with you.** He promises His presence "all the days" (Matthew 28:20). Only God is
  everywhere at once, and Matthew opened his Gospel calling Jesus "Immanuel … God with us" (Matthew
  1:23, ESV).
- **Jesus ended one thing and will end another.** His sacrifice opened the end of the ages (Hebrews
  9:26). His second appearing will close it, "to save those who are eagerly waiting for him" (Hebrews
  9:28, ESV).

### Memory verses

> ✝️ Matthew 28:20 (ESV)
>
> 20 teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you. And behold, I am with you always, to the
> end of the age.

> ✝️ Hebrews 9:26 (ESV)
>
> 26 for then he would have had to suffer repeatedly since the foundation of the world. But as it is,
> he has appeared once for all at the end of the ages to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself.

### Be Transformed

- **Think.** This age has a date on it. God fixed its end, began it at the cross, and will finish it
  when Jesus comes. You belong to the age that is coming.
- **Attitude.** Live unhurried and unafraid. The Lord of the harvest is with you every day until He
  returns, and He sorts the field Himself.
- **Do.** Pick one habit of this age you have taken on without thinking. Name it to God this week and
  ask Him to change your mind about it (Romans 12:2).

### Prayer

of the present evil age. Keep us faithful in the field until the harvest. Let us live as people of the
age to come, with your Son beside us every day.

In Jesus' name. Amen.

## Two ages

Jewish teaching in Jesus' day divided time into "this age" and "the age to come". Jesus used the frame
without explaining it. Blasphemy against the Holy Spirit "will not be forgiven, either in this age or
in the age to come" (Matthew 12:32, ESV). [A New Heaven and a New Earth](new-heaven-and-new-earth.md#the-age-to-come)
works through the age to come: the Mishnah's "world to come", the texts that promise it, and where
the thousand years fits. This study is about the line between the two ages.

The Jewish writings drew that line as a single judgment. The Book of the Watchers, now part of 1 Enoch
and written before the time of Christ, speaks of "the day of the consummation, the great judgement in
which the age shall be consummated" (1 Enoch 16:1, trans. R. H. Charles). 2 Esdras, a Jewish
apocalypse from about AD 100, says "the day of doom shall be the end of this time, and the beginning
of the immortality for to come" (2 Esdras 7:43, KJV Apocrypha; 7:113 in editions that restore the
missing fragment of chapter 7). It also says "The most High hath made this world for many, but the
world to come for few" (2 Esdras 8:1, KJV Apocrypha). 2 Esdras was written after Jesus, and it shows
the frame was still standard Jewish teaching two generations later.

The rabbis also measured the ages. The Talmud divides six thousand years into two thousand of chaos,
two thousand of Torah and two thousand of the days of Messiah (b. *Sanhedrin* 97a). [A Day Is a
Thousand Years](day-is-a-thousand-years.md#the-talmud-the-world-runs-on-a-sabbatical-cycle) works
through that count, and [The Zadok Calendar](../feasts/zadok-calendar.md#the-enoch-wheels-140-jubilees)
the jubilees it is reckoned in. This study needs only the line itself.

One line, one break: that is the top half of the chart below. The New Testament keeps the two ages
and finds that the break between them has a beginning and a close.

## Daniel's word for the end

Matthew's phrase is συντέλεια τοῦ αἰῶνος (*synteleia tou aiōnos*). συντέλεια (*synteleia*,
syn-TEL-ay-ah, G4930) means a completion, the point where something is brought to its finish. The
Legacy Standard Bible footnotes Matthew 13:39 and 24:3 "Or consummation". αἰών (*aiōn*, G165) is an
age, a span of time, and the SBLGNT uses it 122 times.

**The phrase belongs to Matthew.** συντέλεια occurs six times in the New Testament: Matthew 13:39,
40, 49; 24:3; 28:20, and Hebrews 9:26. Mark and Luke never use it.

**It is Daniel's word.** The angel told Daniel, "shut up the words and seal the book, until the time of
the end" (Daniel 12:4, ESV). The Hebrew is <span dir="rtl">עַד־עֵת קֵץ</span> (*ad-et qets*), "until
the time of the end", from קֵץ (*qets*, H7093), "end". Both Greek translations of Daniel render it
ἕως καιροῦ συντελείας, "until the time of the *synteleia*". The Old Greek, made before Christ, uses
συντέλεια 23 times in the book. Theodotion's later version keeps it at the points that matter here:
the end of the seventieth week (Daniel 9:27), the time of the end (Daniel 12:4), and Daniel's own
rising "at the end of the days" (Daniel 12:13, ESV).

So when the disciples ask about the συντέλεια of the age, they use the word Greek-reading Jews had
read in Daniel for more than a century. The word link is in the text. That the disciples were thinking of
Daniel is an inference, and a strong one, because twelve verses later Jesus sends them there by name:
"when you see the abomination of desolation spoken of by the prophet Daniel" (Matthew 24:15, ESV).

**This shows that God set the end of this age before He told anyone about it.** Daniel was told to
seal his book and "go your way till the end" (Daniel 12:13, ESV). Five centuries later His Son told
His disciples what that end would bring. You read Daniel's last chapter from the other side of the
cross.

## The harvest at the close of the age

The first three uses come together, in the two parables Jesus explained to His disciples in private
(Matthew 13:36).

### The weeds and the wheat

An enemy sowed weeds among a farmer's wheat. The servants offered to pull them up, and the master
said, "Let both grow together until the harvest, and at harvest time I will tell the reapers, 'Gather
the weeds first and bind them in bundles to be burned, but gather the wheat into my barn'" (Matthew
13:30, ESV). Jesus explained it:

> ✝️ Matthew 13:39-43 (ESV)
>
> 39 and the enemy who sowed them is the devil. The harvest is the end of the age, and the reapers
> are angels. 40 Just as the weeds are gathered and burned with fire, so will it be at the end of the
> age. 41 The Son of Man will send his angels, and they will gather out of his kingdom all causes of
> sin and all law-breakers, 42 and throw them into the fiery furnace. In that place there will be
> weeping and gnashing of teeth. 43 Then the righteous will shine like the sun in the kingdom of their
> Father. He who has ears, let him hear.

Four things are fixed. The field is the world (Matthew 13:38). Both grow in it together until the end.
The angels do the separating, and the Son of Man sends them. The wicked are removed first, and the
righteous are left to shine. "Shine like the sun" echoes Daniel again: "those who are wise shall shine
like the brightness of the sky above" (Daniel 12:3, ESV), one verse before the sealed book and the time
of the end.

### The net

> ✝️ Matthew 13:47-50 (ESV)
>
> 47 "Again, the kingdom of heaven is like a net that was thrown into the sea and gathered fish of
> every kind. 48 When it was full, men drew it ashore and sat down and sorted the good into containers
> but threw away the bad. 49 So it will be at the end of the age. The angels will come out and
> separate the evil from the righteous 50 and throw them into the fiery furnace. In that place there
> will be weeping and gnashing of teeth.

The net says the same thing from the sea. Nobody sorts the catch while the net is in the water. The
sorting happens once, when the net is full and drawn ashore.

The Cultural Backgrounds Study Bible notes that other Jewish writings also picture the end of the age
as a harvest, citing 2 Baruch 70:2 (*NIV Cultural Backgrounds Study Bible*, on Matthew 13:39). Jesus
gives the picture its owner. The harvest is His, the angels are His, and the kingdom they clear is
"his kingdom" (Matthew 13:41).

[One Taken, One Left](one-taken-one-left.md) follows this order into Matthew 24:40-41: at the Son of
Man's return the wicked are taken in judgment and the righteous are left, as Noah was left. "Gather the
weeds first" is the same order in a field.

**This shows that God is patient now and exact at the end.** He lets the wheat and the weeds grow side
by side, so that no wheat is torn up with a weed (Matthew 13:29). The separation belongs to the Son of
Man, and He makes no mistakes. So you can leave the sorting to Him and keep sowing.

## "Your coming and the end of the age"

On the Mount of Olives the disciples asked, "Tell us, when will these things be, and what will be the
sign of your coming and of the end of the age?" (Matthew 24:3, ESV).

The Greek puts the two nouns under one article: τῆς σῆς παρουσίας καὶ συντελείας τοῦ αἰῶνος, "the
your-coming-and-close-of-the-age". παρουσία (*parousia*, G3952) is an arrival, the word for a king's
visit. The grammar pairs the coming and the close as one subject of one question. It does not by
itself prove they are a single event, since the rule that would require that applies to persons, and
these are things. The rest of the chapter does the joining.

Jesus answers in order. First come wars, famines and false messiahs, which are "the beginning of the
birth pains" (Matthew 24:8, ESV). Then "this gospel of the kingdom will be proclaimed throughout the
whole world as a testimony to all nations, and then the end will come" (Matthew 24:14, ESV). Then
Daniel's abomination (Matthew 24:15) and a tribulation without equal (Matthew 24:21). Then:

> ✝️ Matthew 24:29-31 (ESV)
>
> 29 "Immediately after the tribulation of those days the sun will be darkened, and the moon will not
> give its light, and the stars will fall from heaven, and the powers of the heavens will be shaken.
> 30 Then will appear in heaven the sign of the Son of Man, and then all the tribes of the earth will
> mourn, and they will see the Son of Man coming on the clouds of heaven with power and great glory.
> 31 And he will send out his angels with a loud trumpet call, and they will gather his elect from
> the four winds, from one end of heaven to the other.

The angels who gather in Matthew 24:31 are the reapers of Matthew 13:39. On the dispensational reading
this site holds, the close of the age is the close of Daniel's seventieth week, the seven years of
Daniel 9:27, and the coming is Christ's visible return at its end (Revelation 19:11-16). That
placement rests on three things in the text: Daniel's word in the disciples' question, Daniel's
abomination in Jesus' answer, and "immediately after the tribulation" before the coming. Readers who
see no future seventieth week place the close at the same return, with the tribulation spread across
the whole church age. Both put the end of the age at Christ's coming in glory.

[The Olivet Discourse](olivet-discourse.md) works through the whole answer, and
[The Tribulation](tribulation.md) charts the week.

## "All the days"

Matthew ends with a command and a promise:

> ✝️ Matthew 28:18-20 (ESV)
>
> 18 And Jesus came and said to them, "All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me.
> 19 Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of
> the Son and of the Holy Spirit, 20 teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you. And
> behold, I am with you always, to the end of the age."

"Always" in the ESV is πάσας τὰς ἡμέρας (*pasas tas hēmeras*), and the LSB and CSB footnotes give it
literally: "all the days". Jesus promises His presence one day at a time, every day, until the
συντέλεια. The Cultural Backgrounds Study Bible observes that Jews held only God to be present
everywhere, so the promise presents Jesus as divine (*NIV Cultural Backgrounds Study Bible*, on
Matthew 28:20). Matthew began with "Immanuel" (Matthew 1:23) and ends with Immanuel keeping His name.

**This shows that God the Son goes with the people He sends.** The One with "all authority in
heaven and on earth" is the One beside you while you obey Him. So you can make disciples in an age that
does not want them, because every one of your days is covered.

### Where the church is at the close

Here two of this site's convictions meet, and the meeting is contested.

The [statement of faith](../about/statement-of-faith.md#end-times) holds that the church is caught up
to meet Christ before the seven-year tribulation begins. Matthew 28:20 runs "to the end of the age",
and Matthew 13 and 24 place that end at the return after the tribulation. A reader can fairly ask how
a promise that runs to the close of the age fits a church removed seven years before it.

**The dispensational answer has two parts.** First, the promise attaches to the task, and the task
continues after the church is gone. The gospel of the kingdom goes to all nations before the end
(Matthew 24:14), and John sees "a great multitude that no one could number, from every nation" come
"out of the great tribulation" (Revelation 7:9, 14, ESV). Jesus is with the disciples who make
disciples, through those seven years too. Second, the church caught up has the promise in a stronger
form: "and so we will always be with the Lord" (1 Thessalonians 4:17, ESV). The presence promised by
faith becomes presence face to face. The case for the timing itself rests on other texts, chiefly
1 Thessalonians 4:13-18, Revelation 3:10 and 2 Thessalonians 2:6-7, set out in
[The Rapture of the Church](rapture.md) and [The Restrainer](the-restrainer.md).

**The other case.** Post-tribulational and amillennial readers take Matthew 28:20 at its plainest: the
apostles stand for the church, "all the days" has no break in it, so the church is on earth until the
end of the age. They count this verse among their strongest.

This study holds the dispensational reading with moderate confidence, because Matthew 28:20 neither
names a rapture nor rules one out. Both readings agree on the part you need for today. Jesus is with
you this day, and every day after it, until He comes.

## The end has already come

Matthew puts the end of the age at the harvest. Two letters put it in the past.

> ✝️ Hebrews 9:26-28 (ESV)
>
> 26 for then he would have had to suffer repeatedly since the foundation of the world. But as it is,
> he has appeared once for all at the end of the ages to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself.
> 27 And just as it is appointed for man to die once, and after that comes judgment, 28 so Christ,
> having been offered once to bear the sins of many, will appear a second time, not to deal with sin
> but to save those who are eagerly waiting for him.

"At the end of the ages" is ἐπὶ συντελείᾳ τῶν αἰώνων (*epi synteleia tōn aiōnōn*), Matthew's word with
the ages in the plural. Paul says the same to Corinth. Israel's wilderness history was "written down
for our instruction, on whom the end of the ages has come" (1 Corinthians 10:11, ESV). The Greek is
τὰ τέλη τῶν αἰώνων (*ta telē tōn aiōnōn*), from τέλος (*telos*, G5056), and the verb κατήντηκεν
(*katēntēken*, G2658) is a perfect: it has arrived and it stays arrived. The CSB Ancient Faith Study Bible
footnotes the phrase "Or goals of the ages". Hebrews opens the same way: "in these last days he has
spoken to us by his Son" (Hebrews 1:2, ESV). That phrase, the prophets' *acharit hayamim*, has its own
study in [A Day Is a Thousand Years](day-is-a-thousand-years.md#the-last-days-are-the-last-of-the-days),
which places the last days in the week of history. This section follows the other phrase, "the end of
the ages".

So the end of the ages has two points in the New Testament. It opened when Christ "appeared once for
all … by the sacrifice of himself" (Hebrews 9:26). It closes when He "will appear a second time"
(Hebrews 9:28). Hebrews counts them: ἅπαξ (*hapax*, G530), "once", for the first, and ἐκ δευτέρου
(*ek deuterou*), "a second time", for the next.
The ESV Study Bible reads Hebrews 9:26 this way: the end of the ages was inaugurated by Christ's death
and awaits its consummation at His return (*ESV Study Bible*, on Hebrews 9:25-26). The NIV Biblical
Theology Study Bible says the same of 1 Corinthians 10:11.

This is settled teaching across the traditions. The disagreement is over what fills the span between
the two points, and the dispensational answer is the church age followed by Daniel's seventieth week.

[![A Larkin-style chart in two parts. Above, the Jewish two-age frame: this age and the age to come, divided by a single judgment, from 1 Enoch 16:1 and 2 Esdras 7:43. **This shows that God has already begun to finish this age, and He began with the cross.** The sin
that would have had to be dealt with "repeatedly since the foundation of the world" was put away once
(Hebrews 9:26). That is the atonement, and it is the first act of the end. So the second act is
certain. You wait for Christ "not to deal with sin but to save" (Hebrews 9:28), because the sin was
dealt with when the end began.

## Living in this age

If the end of the ages has come, you live in an age that is passing and belong to one that is coming.
Paul writes that Christ "gave himself for our sins to deliver us from the present evil age" (Galatians
1:4, ESV), ἐκ τοῦ αἰῶνος τοῦ ἐνεστῶτος πονηροῦ. Deliverance from an age is redemption: Christ paid for
your sins and moved you out from under the age that produced them.

You still live in it. Titus 2:12 calls this "the present age", where grace trains you "to live
self-controlled, upright, and godly lives" while "waiting for our blessed hope, the appearing of the
glory of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ" (Titus 2:12-13, ESV).

### A word most English versions translate "world"

Romans 12:2 uses the same noun:

> ✝️ Romans 12:2 (ESV)
>
> 2 Do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewal of your mind, that by testing
> you may discern what is the will of God, what is good and acceptable and perfect.

"This world" is τῷ αἰῶνι τούτῳ (*tō aiōni toutō*), "this age". The ESV, NIV, NASB, LSB, NKJV, WEB and
ASV all translate it "world". The CSB alone keeps "Do not be conformed to this age" (Romans 12:2, CSB).
"World" is a fair translation, and αἰών does sometimes mean the world as it now runs. But "age" keeps
the time in it. Paul's command assumes the two ages: do not take the shape of an age that is ending,
when you belong to the one that is coming. If you read Romans 12 in the ESV or NIV, read "age" where
it says "world" and the command carries its date.

**This shows that God transforms you ahead of time.** The renewing of your mind is the age to come
arriving in you before it arrives in the world (Hebrews 6:5). So every time you refuse the shape of
this age, you are living the end of the ages that Christ opened at the cross.

## Discussion Questions

1. συντέλεια means a completion, the point where a thing is finished. Daniel's angel sealed the book
   "until the time of the end" (Daniel 12:4). What did Daniel have to do while he waited (Daniel
   12:13), and what does that ask of you?
2. In Matthew 13:30 the master refuses to let his servants pull up the weeds. Why does he wait, and
   what does that tell you about the work Jesus gives His people now?
3. The disciples put "your coming" and "the end of the age" in one question (Matthew 24:3). Where in
   Matthew 24 does Jesus join the two, and what comes between the question and the answer?
4. Matthew 28:20 runs "to the end of the age", and this site holds that the church is caught up before
   the tribulation. Read the two parts of the dispensational answer and the case against it. Which
   points does the text settle, and which does it leave open?
5. Hebrews says Christ appeared "at the end of the ages" (Hebrews 9:26). What changes in how you read
   the news if the end has already begun?
6. Romans 12:2 says "Do not be conformed to this age." Name one way this age has shaped your habits.
   What would it look like this week to live as someone who belongs to the age to come?

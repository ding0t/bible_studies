# Ezekiel's Temple

*The glory comes back to stay*

In 573 BC Ezekiel had lived in Babylon for twenty-five years. The temple in Jerusalem had been ashes
for fourteen. Then "the hand of the LORD was upon me," he writes, "and he brought me to the city…
and set me down on a very high mountain" (Ezekiel 40:1-2, ESV). What he saw there fills nine
chapters: a temple measured gate by gate, the glory of God coming home, a river that heals the Dead
Sea, a land shared among twelve tribes, and a city whose name is "The LORD Is There" (Ezekiel 48:35,
ESV).

**In one sentence:** Ezekiel's temple is God's promise that the glory which left Jerusalem will come
back to stay: no building yet raised matches it, and its own details (death, marriage, a sea with
fishermen, twelve tribes on the land, a prince with sons) place it in the kingdom Christ will reign
over on this earth, where its offerings cleanse the place He dwells, before the city whose temple is
God and the Lamb.

### Types & Prophecy

- **Ezekiel 40-48 is a prophecy still waiting.** Ten of its details are tested below against four
  candidate fulfilments. Only the thousand years of Revelation 20 meets all ten.
- **One prophecy can span two comings.** Jesus read Isaiah 61 and stopped before "the day of
  vengeance" (Luke 4:19). Ezekiel's glory has the same shape: it came to the temple in person in
  Jesus and left the house "desolate" (Matthew 23:38). The return of Ezekiel 43 is still ahead.
- **The new Jerusalem reuses Ezekiel's pictures and changes them.** It has the high mountain, the
  twelve gates named for the tribes, the river and the trees for healing. It has no temple, no sea and
  no death (Revelation 21:1, 4, 22).

### Lessons about Jesus

- **The returning glory speaks with the voice of the risen Christ.** Ezekiel heard "the sound of
  many waters" (Ezekiel 43:2, ESV). John heard Jesus' voice "like the roar of many waters"
  (Revelation 1:15, ESV).
- **Jesus is both shepherds of Ezekiel 34.** The LORD says "I myself will be the shepherd of my
  sheep" (Ezekiel 34:15, ESV), and then sets over them "one shepherd, my servant David" (Ezekiel
  34:23, ESV). Jesus claims both roles: "I am the good shepherd" (John 10:11, ESV).
- **The King on the throne is the LORD, and the prince is someone else.** "This is the place of my
  throne" (Ezekiel 43:7, ESV). The prince offers a sin offering for himself (Ezekiel 45:22), so he is
  not Christ.
- **Jesus walked the glory's road.** He left the temple for the Mount of Olives (Matthew 24:1-3),
  went up to heaven from it (Acts 1:12), and the LORD's feet will stand on it (Zechariah 14:4). This
  is a pattern. No New Testament writer joins it to Ezekiel.

### Be Transformed

- **Think.** The point of the house is the One who lives in it (Ezekiel 43:7). He lives in His people
  now: "we are the temple of the living God" (2 Corinthians 6:16, ESV), where Paul quotes Ezekiel
  37:27.
- **Attitude.** Let grace make you ashamed in the way Ezekiel means. The house was shown "that they
  may be ashamed of their iniquities" (Ezekiel 43:10, ESV), and the shame follows forgiveness: "when

- **Do.** "Let us cleanse ourselves from every defilement of body and spirit" (2 Corinthians 7:1,
  ESV). Name one thing you have let into His house, and put it out this week.

## A vision for people with no temple

Ezekiel dates the vision "in the twenty-fifth year of our exile… in the fourteenth year after the
city was struck down" (Ezekiel 40:1, ESV). The exile counts from Jehoiachin's deportation in 597 BC,
so the year is 573 BC ([Chronology Anchors](../chronology/chronology-anchors.md), anchor 13).
Nebuchadnezzar's captain "burned the house of the LORD" in 586 BC (2 Kings 25:9, ESV).

The exiles had no altar and no house. God had already told them what He was to them in the meantime:
"yet I have been a sanctuary to them for a while in the countries where they have gone" (Ezekiel
11:16, ESV). The Hebrew is <span dir="rtl">מִקְדָּשׁ מְעַט</span> (*miqdash me'at*, H4720), "a
sanctuary in small measure" or "for a little while". Either way it was a sanctuary for the meantime.

The vision tells them what comes after the meantime. Ezekiel is told, "Declare all that you see to
the house of Israel" (Ezekiel 40:4, ESV), and later why: "that they may be ashamed of their
iniquities; and they shall measure the plan" (Ezekiel 43:10, ESV).

## The glory leaves, the glory returns

### Ezekiel 8-11: the glory goes out

Nineteen years earlier, in a vision dated to 592 BC (Ezekiel 8:1), Ezekiel watched the glory leave
the first temple. The people's abominations were "to drive me far from my sanctuary" (Ezekiel 8:6, ESV), and
the glory went in stages:

| Stage | Where the glory moves | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | From the cherub to the threshold of the house | Ezekiel 9:3; 10:4 |
| 2 | From the threshold to the cherubim, and out to the east gate | Ezekiel 10:18-19 |
| 3 | From the city to "the mountain that is on the east side of the city" | Ezekiel 11:22-23 |

### Ezekiel 43-44: the glory comes back

Nineteen years later Ezekiel is taken to "the gate facing east" (Ezekiel 43:1, ESV).

> ✝️ Ezekiel 43:2-5 (ESV)
>
> 2 And behold, the glory of the God of Israel was coming from the east. And the sound of his coming
> was like the sound of many waters, and the earth shone with his glory. 3 And the vision I saw was
> just like the vision that I had seen when he came to destroy the city, and just like the vision
> that I had seen by the Chebar canal. And I fell on my face. 4 As the glory of the LORD entered the
> temple by the gate facing east, 5 the Spirit lifted me up and brought me into the inner court; and
> behold, the glory of the LORD filled the temple.

Ezekiel draws the line himself: this is the vision of chapters 8-11 and of chapter 1. The glory left
by the east and comes back by the east. Then the LORD says what the return means:

> ✝️ Ezekiel 43:7 (ESV)
>
> 7 and he said to me, "Son of man, this is the place of my throne and the place of the soles of my
> feet, where I will dwell in the midst of the people of Israel forever. And the house of Israel
> shall no more defile my holy name, neither they, nor their kings, by their whoring and by the dead
> bodies of their kings at their high places,

The east gate is then shut: "it shall not be opened, and no one shall enter by it, for the LORD, the
God of Israel, has entered by it" (Ezekiel 44:2, ESV). The road the glory left by is closed behind
it. He is not leaving again.

### Ezekiel 33-39: the road to the house

Between the leaving and the return, the book builds toward the house in order:

1. **A shepherd.** God will seek His scattered sheep Himself, and set over them "one shepherd, my
   servant David" (Ezekiel 34:23, ESV).
2. **A people gathered, cleansed and given the Spirit.** "I will take you from the nations… I will
   sprinkle clean water on you… And I will put my Spirit within you" (Ezekiel 36:24-27, ESV). God
   does it "for the sake of my holy name" (Ezekiel 36:22, ESV).
3. **A nation raised and joined.** The dry bones live, Judah and Joseph become one stick, and "one
   king shall be king over them all" (Ezekiel 37:22, ESV).
4. **A sanctuary promised.** "I… will set my sanctuary in their midst forevermore. My dwelling place
   shall be with them" (Ezekiel 37:26-27, ESV).
5. **An enemy destroyed.** Gog comes against the restored land and falls (Ezekiel 38-39). When
   Ezekiel 38-39 happens beside the two Gog passages in Revelation (Revelation 19:17-18; 20:8) is a
   question for its own study.
6. **Every exile home, and God's face no longer hidden.** "I will leave none of them remaining among
   the nations anymore. And I will not hide my face anymore from them, when I pour out my Spirit upon
   the house of Israel" (Ezekiel 39:28-29, ESV).

Chapters 40-48 then show the sanctuary of Ezekiel 37:26. They come after the gathering, the
cleansing, the outpoured Spirit and the defeat of the last enemy. Ezekiel had said the same thing
earlier: "on my holy mountain… there I will accept them… when I bring you out from the peoples"
(Ezekiel 20:40-41, ESV). [Israel's Regathering and Refining](../israel-and-church/israels-regathering-and-refining.md)
follows that order through Ezekiel 36-38.

### The same road, walked by Jesus

Ezekiel 11:23 does not name the mountain east of the city. Zechariah does, and he uses Ezekiel's
words:

| Ezekiel 11:23 | Zechariah 14:4 |
|---|---|
| <span dir="rtl">וַיַּעֲמֹד עַל־הָהָר אֲשֶׁר מִקֶּדֶם לָעִיר</span> | <span dir="rtl">וְעָמְדוּ רַגְלָיו… עַל־הַר הַזֵּתִים אֲשֶׁר עַל־פְּנֵי יְרוּשָׁלִַם מִקֶּדֶם</span> |
| "and stood on the mountain that is on the east side of the city" | "his feet shall stand on the Mount of Olives that lies before Jerusalem on the east" |

Both use "stand" (<span dir="rtl">עָמַד</span>, *amad*, H5975), "on the mountain", and "on the east"
(<span dir="rtl">מִקֶּדֶם</span>, *miqqedem*, H6924). The glory stood on that mountain as it left.
The LORD's feet will stand on it when He comes back, and Ezekiel 43:7 calls the temple "the place of
the soles of my feet."

Jesus walked that road in both directions. He came into Jerusalem from the Mount of Olives (Luke
19:37), and when He saw the city "he wept over it… because you did not know the time of your
visitation" (Luke 19:41-44, ESV). In the temple He told its leaders, "See, your house is left to you
desolate. For I tell you, you will not see me again, until you say, 'Blessed is he who comes in the
name of the Lord'" (Matthew 23:38-39, ESV). Then "Jesus left the temple and was going away," and He
sat "on the Mount of Olives" (Matthew 24:1, 3, ESV). Forty days after He rose, He was taken up from
that mountain, and the angels said, "This Jesus… will come in the same way as you saw him go into
heaven" (Acts 1:11-12, ESV).

Hosea gives the same shape: "I will return again to my place, until they acknowledge their guilt and
seek my face" (Hosea 5:15, ESV). Jesus' "until you say" (Matthew 23:39) gives the condition of His
return in Israel's own welcome.

Jewish readers told of the Presence waiting on that mountain. *Lamentations Rabbah*
(compiled about AD 500) traces the Divine Presence from Ezekiel 9-11 to the Mount of Olives, and
then: "Rabbi Yonatan said: For three and a half years the Divine Presence stayed resting on the Mount
of Olives, believing that perhaps Israel would repent, but they did not." A voice called, "Return
wayward children" (Jeremiah 3:14) (*Lamentations Rabbah*, Petichta 25, Sefaria Midrash Rabbah, CC-BY). The rabbis
were describing the first temple's fall, and the parallel with Jesus' ministry is this site's
observation.

**The confidence:** a pattern. No New Testament writer quotes Ezekiel 11:23 or 43:2
of Jesus' departure or His return. The shared words and the shared mountain are in the text. The
link between them is an inference.

## The tour

### The house, the altar and the shut gate

| Chapters | What Ezekiel is shown |
|---|---|
| 40-42 | The house measured: the outer court and its three gates, the inner court, the temple, and a wall 500 cubits square "to make a separation between the holy and the common" (Ezekiel 42:20, ESV) |
| 43 | The glory's return, "the law of the temple" (the whole mountaintop "most holy", Ezekiel 43:12), and the altar's seven-day consecration |
| 44 | The east gate shut, the prince's place in it, Levites demoted, and the sons of Zadok as priests (Ezekiel 44:15) |
| 45-46 | The holy district, the prince's land, just weights, the offerings, Passover and the seventh-month feast |
| 47:1-12 | The river |
| 47:13-48:35 | The boundaries, the twelve tribes in strips, and the city with twelve gates named for the tribes |

Several things in Moses' tabernacle and Solomon's temple are missing. The concordance finds no ark
(H727), no lampstand (H4501), no veil (H6532) and no mercy seat (H3727) anywhere in Ezekiel, and no
"high priest". There is a wooden altar that the guide calls "the table that is before the LORD"
(Ezekiel 41:22, ESV), the subject of [The Table](../jesus/the-heavenly-pattern/table.md). Light
comes from the glory itself: "the earth shone with his glory" (Ezekiel 43:2, ESV). In John's city,
"its lamp is the Lamb" (Revelation 21:23, ESV), which is where
[The Lampstand](../jesus/the-heavenly-pattern/lampstand.md#ezekiels-temple) left the question.

### The river

> ✝️ Ezekiel 47:3-5 (ESV)
>
> 3 Going on eastward with a measuring line in his hand, the man measured a thousand cubits, and then
> led me through the water, and it was ankle-deep. 4 Again he measured a thousand, and led me through
> the water, and it was knee-deep. Again he measured a thousand, and led me through the water, and it
> was waist-deep. 5 Again he measured a thousand, and it was a river that I could not pass through,
> for the water had risen. It was deep enough to swim in, a river that could not be passed through.

The water starts as a trickle from under the threshold (Ezekiel 47:1-2). It has no tributary, and it
deepens anyway. It flows down to the Arabah and into the Dead Sea, and the sea comes alive: "Fishermen
will stand beside the sea. From Engedi to Eneglaim it will be a place for the spreading of nets"
(Ezekiel 47:10, ESV). Trees on both banks "bear fresh fruit every month… and their leaves for
healing" (Ezekiel 47:12, ESV).

Not all of it is healed: "its swamps and marshes will not become fresh; they are to be left for
salt" (Ezekiel 47:11, ESV). People in that land will still need salt. Ezekiel's priests salt the
offerings (Ezekiel 43:24).

Zechariah saw the same water from Jerusalem: "living waters shall flow out from Jerusalem, half of
them to the eastern sea and half of them to the western sea" (Zechariah 14:8, ESV). In the next verse
"the LORD will be king over all the earth" (Zechariah 14:9, ESV).

## Has it been fulfilled?

A prophecy is fulfilled when something meets its details. A candidate has to meet each one, or show
from the text why that detail is a figure. Ezekiel's details include things a reader can check: a
river, fishermen, priests who marry, tribes on strips of land. Here are ten of them against the four
places this vision has been said to be fulfilled.

| Detail | Reference | Second temple | Christ and the church | The thousand years | The new Jerusalem |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Built to Ezekiel's plan | Ezekiel 43:10-11 | fails (Ezra 6:3, 18) | fails | meets | fails (Revelation 21:22) |
| The glory enters and fills it | Ezekiel 43:2-5 | fails | partly (John 1:14) | meets | meets (Revelation 21:11) |
| On a very high mountain | Ezekiel 40:2 | fails | fails | meets (Isaiah 2:2) | meets (Revelation 21:10) |
| No exile left among the nations | Ezekiel 39:28 | fails | fails | meets | meets |
| A river freshens the Dead Sea, marshes left for salt | Ezekiel 47:8-11 | fails | partly (John 7:38) | meets | fails (Revelation 21:1) |
| Priests marry and mourn their dead | Ezekiel 44:22, 25 | meets | fails | meets (Isaiah 65:20) | fails (Revelation 21:4) |
| Offerings for one who sins by error | Ezekiel 45:20 | meets | fails | meets | fails (Revelation 21:27) |
| A prince with sons and an inheritance | Ezekiel 46:16-18 | fails | fails | meets | fails |
| Twelve tribes in strips, sojourners inheriting | Ezekiel 47:13-48:29 | fails | fails | meets | partly (Revelation 21:12) |
| The LORD dwells there forever | Ezekiel 43:7; 48:35 | fails | partly (2 Corinthians 6:16) | meets | meets (Revelation 21:3) |

### The second temple

The exiles who came back under Cyrus built a house, and it fails the vision on its own record. Cyrus's
decree gave its measures (Ezra 6:3), and the builders ordered its service "as it is written in the
Book of Moses" (Ezra 6:18, ESV). Ezekiel is never named. Old men who had seen Solomon's temple "wept
with a loud voice when they saw the foundation of this house being laid" (Ezra 3:12, ESV). Haggai
asked, "Is it not as nothing in your eyes?" (Haggai 2:3, ESV), and promised a glory still to come:
"I will fill this house with glory" (Haggai 2:7, ESV).

No verse says the glory filled the second temple, as it had filled the tabernacle (Exodus 40:34-35)
and Solomon's house (2 Chronicles 7:1-2). The Talmud lists five things the second temple lacked,
and "the Divine Presence" is one of them (*b. Yoma* 21b, William Davidson Edition, CC-BY-NC). Most of
the exiles stayed in the nations (Esther lives in Susa), so Ezekiel 39:28 was not met either.

### Christ and the church

Christ is the glory in person: "the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen his glory"
(John 1:14, ESV). He called His body the temple (John 2:19-21), and Paul calls the church "the temple
of the living God" (2 Corinthians 6:16, ESV). These are real fulfilments of the presence promise.
They leave the rest untouched: the land, the tribes, the river, the prince and his sons, the priests'
marriages. None of those has a figurative signal in the text, and the man with the measuring reed
measures to the cubit (Ezekiel 40:5).

### The new Jerusalem

John's city borrows Ezekiel's mountain, gates and river. It also excludes Ezekiel's world. "Death
shall be no more, neither shall there be mourning" (Revelation 21:4, ESV), and Ezekiel's priests may
defile themselves for a dead father or mother (Ezekiel 44:25). "The sea was no more" (Revelation
21:1, ESV), and Ezekiel's fishermen spread nets by one. "Nothing unclean will ever enter it"
(Revelation 21:27, ESV), and Ezekiel's house is cleansed "for anyone who has sinned through error or
ignorance" (Ezekiel 45:20, ESV).

### The thousand years

The kingdom of Revelation 20 has all of this. Christ reigns on the earth (Revelation 20:4-6). Death
still comes, though late: "the young man shall die a hundred years old" (Isaiah 65:20, ESV). The
nations "go up year after year to worship the King" (Zechariah 14:16, ESV). "The mountain of the
house of the LORD shall be established as the highest of the mountains" (Isaiah 2:2, ESV). This is
the reading the site holds, set out in [The Thousand Years](the-thousand-years.md#the-temple-ezekiel-saw).

**The confidence:** not yet fulfilled. No candidate meets every detail except a kingdom that has not
yet come.

## How the New Testament reads it

The New Testament never says Ezekiel 40-48 "is fulfilled". It uses the chapters in four ways, and
each is labelled here by what the writer claims.

| New Testament text | What it takes from Ezekiel | Kind of use |
|---|---|---|
| Revelation 21:10-22:2 | The high mountain (Ezekiel 40:2), twelve gates named for the tribes (Ezekiel 48:31), the measuring (Ezekiel 40:3), the river and trees for healing (Ezekiel 47:1-12) | Imagery reused, with changes: no temple, no sea, no death |
| 2 Corinthians 6:16 | "I will be their God, and they shall be my people" (Ezekiel 37:27) | Application to the church, with no fulfilment formula |
| Revelation 1:15 | "Like the sound of many waters" (Ezekiel 43:2) | Imagery: the voice of the glory is the voice of Christ |
| Revelation 3:12 | The name of the city (Ezekiel 48:35) | Imagery reused for the new Jerusalem |
| Revelation 11:1-2 | Measuring the temple (Ezekiel 40:3) | Imagery, for a temple in the tribulation |
| John 7:37-39 | "Rivers of living water" | Contested: Ezekiel 47 is one candidate among Isaiah 44:3; 58:11 and Zechariah 14:8 |

The Greek shows two things. In Revelation 1:15, "his voice was like the roar of many
waters" is φωνὴ ὑδάτων πολλῶν (*phōnē hydatōn pollōn*). That follows the Hebrew of Ezekiel 43:2,
<span dir="rtl">כְּקוֹל מַיִם רַבִּים</span> (*keqol mayim rabbim*). The Septuagint of Ezekiel 43:2
has "the voice of the camp, like the voice of many doubling" and no waters. John is reading
Ezekiel's own words and hearing them in Jesus' voice. And in 2 Corinthians 6:16 Paul applies Ezekiel
37:27 to the church. Then he draws the consequence: "let us cleanse ourselves from every defilement"
(2 Corinthians 7:1, ESV). Paul does not say Ezekiel's sanctuary will never be built.

An application or a reused image does not close a promise. Only a stated fulfilment does, and only
for the words it quotes. "The gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable" (Romans 11:29, ESV).
That is the dispensational rule this site reads by, and the New Testament gives no stated fulfilment
of Ezekiel 40-48 to set against it.

## Near, far, and the two stages

### The join in a single sentence

In the synagogue at Nazareth Jesus read Isaiah 61, and stopped mid-sentence:

> ✝️ Luke 4:18-19, 21 (ESV)
>
> 18 "The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he has anointed me to proclaim good news to the poor.
> He has sent me to proclaim liberty to the captives and recovering of sight to the blind, to set at
> liberty those who are oppressed, 19 to proclaim the year of the Lord's favor." … 21 And he began to
> say to them, "Today this Scripture has been fulfilled in your hearing."

Isaiah's sentence goes on: "and the day of vengeance of our God" (Isaiah 61:2, ESV). Jesus fulfilled
the first half and left the second for His return. Two thousand years sit inside one line.
[Two Mountain Peaks](immediately-after.md#two-mountain-peaks) sets this out with Larkin's chart.

Ezekiel has a join too, in the glory. The LORD whom Malachi promised "will suddenly come to his
temple" (Malachi 3:1, ESV) came in Jesus, and He left the house desolate (Matthew 23:38). That much
is fulfilled. The glory that fills the house forever (Ezekiel 43:5-7) is left over.

The prince's gift to a servant returns to
him "to the year of liberty" (Ezekiel 46:17, ESV). "Liberty" is <span dir="rtl">דְּרוֹר</span>
(*deror*, H1865), the word of Isaiah 61:1 that Jesus read, "to proclaim liberty to the captives".
It occurs seven times in the Hebrew Bible ([Melchizedek in Second Temple
Judaism](../scripture/melchizedek-in-second-temple-judaism.md) counts them). The liberty Jesus
proclaimed is kept as a calendar in Ezekiel's land.

### The offer to the exiles

Ezekiel 43:11 carries a condition: "if they are ashamed of all that they have done, make known to
them the design of the temple" (ESV). Some readers conclude that the plan was offered to the returning
exiles, who never built it. The condition governs whether the exiles were shown the design. It
does not govern whether the LORD returns. That rests on God alone: "It is not for your sake that I
will act… Be ashamed and confounded for your ways" (Ezekiel 36:32, ESV). In Ezekiel the shame comes
after the gift (Ezekiel 16:63). And the setting the vision requires, with every exile home and the
Spirit poured out (Ezekiel 39:28-29), did not exist in 538 BC.

A Jewish book from before Christ already held two temples apart. Tobit expects the exiles to "build
a temple, but not like to the first, until the time of that age be fulfilled", after which "the house
of God shall be built in it for ever with a glorious building, as the prophets have spoken thereof"
(Tobit 14:5, KJV Apocrypha). Tobit is not Scripture, and it shows how the prophets were heard.

### A temple in between

Paul expects "the man of lawlessness" to take "his seat in the temple of God" (2 Thessalonians
2:3-4, ESV), and John measures a temple whose outer court is trampled for forty-two months (Revelation
11:1-2). That house stands in the tribulation and is defiled. Ezekiel's house is entered by the glory
and never defiled again (Ezekiel 43:7). They are two buildings.

### The thousand years, then the new creation

Revelation gives the order: Christ's return (Revelation 19), the thousand years (Revelation 20), and
then "a new heaven and a new earth" (Revelation 21:1). Ezekiel's temple belongs to the second of
those. John's city, which has no temple, belongs to the third.

God says He will dwell in Ezekiel's house "forever" (Ezekiel 43:7), and John sees no temple in
the final city. "Forever" is <span dir="rtl">עוֹלָם</span> (*olam*, H5769), and TWOT's gloss is
"long duration". Ezekiel uses it 18 times. It looks back to "the people of old" (Ezekiel 26:20,
ESV) and "the ancient heights" (Ezekiel 36:2, ESV) as readily as it looks ahead. He uses the same
word for the daily grain offering, "a perpetual statute" (Ezekiel 46:14, ESV). Moses used it of the
Day of Atonement, "a statute forever" (Leviticus 16:34, ESV), and Hebrews says that covenant is
"ready to vanish away" (Hebrews 8:13, ESV). *Olam* lasts as long as the order it belongs to.

The dwelling outlasts the building. Ezekiel 37:27's promise, "my dwelling place shall be with them",
is the one Revelation 21:3 announces from the throne: "Behold, the dwelling place of God is with
man." The throne of Ezekiel 43:7 becomes "the throne of God and of the Lamb" (Revelation 22:3, ESV).
The house gives way because the One it housed fills the whole city: "its temple is the Lord God the
Almighty and the Lamb" (Revelation 21:22, ESV). The word "forever" is kept. The LORD never leaves
again.

Irenaeus, about AD 180, read the same two stages. In the kingdom, "Jerusalem rebuilt after the
pattern of the Jerusalem above"; then "after the times of the kingdom" the great white throne and
the new Jerusalem "descending upon the new earth" (*Against Heresies* 5.35.2, ANF vol. 1). [The New
Jerusalem](new-jerusalem.md#the-beloved-city) reads Ezekiel's city and John's as two cities, and
[A New Heaven and a New Earth](new-heaven-and-new-earth.md#god-pitches-his-tent) follows God's tent
into the new creation.

## The offerings

### What Ezekiel prescribes

The altar is cleansed and consecrated for seven days, and "from the eighth day onward the priests
shall offer on the altar your burnt offerings and your peace offerings, and I will accept you"
(Ezekiel 43:27, ESV). The prince provides "the sin offerings, grain offerings, burnt offerings, and
peace offerings, to make atonement on behalf of the house of Israel" (Ezekiel 45:17, ESV). The
sanctuary is purified on the first day of the first month and again on the seventh (Ezekiel
45:18-20). Passover is kept (Ezekiel 45:21), and so is the seven-day feast of the seventh month
(Ezekiel 45:25). Other prophets expect the same: Isaiah 56:7; 60:7; 66:20-23; Jeremiah 33:18;
Zechariah 14:16-21; Malachi 3:3-4.

Hebrews says, "Where there is forgiveness of these, there is no longer any offering for sin"
(Hebrews 10:18, ESV). [True Worship](../christian-life/true-worship.md) names that as the hard part,
and [The Thousand Years](the-thousand-years.md#the-temple-ezekiel-saw) gives the four answers held.

### "Make atonement" in Ezekiel

The verb is <span dir="rtl">כָּפַר</span> (*kaphar*, kah-FAR, H3722). Ezekiel uses it six times:

| Reference | Who acts | What is atoned for |
|---|---|---|
| Ezekiel 16:63 | God: "when I atone for you" | The people, "for all that you have done" |
| Ezekiel 43:20 | The priests | The altar: "Thus you shall purify the altar and make atonement for it" |
| Ezekiel 43:26 | The priests | The altar, for seven days |
| Ezekiel 45:15 | The offerings | "for them", the people |
| Ezekiel 45:17 | The prince's offerings | "on behalf of the house of Israel" |
| Ezekiel 45:20 | The priests | The house: "so you shall make atonement for the temple" |

The first use belongs to God, and it is about sin itself: "when I atone for you for all that you have
done" (Ezekiel 16:63, ESV). Three of the five temple uses name a place, the altar and the house. The
verb beside it in Ezekiel 43:20 and 45:18 is <span dir="rtl">חִטֵּא</span> (*chitte*, H2398),
"purify", to cleanse a thing of sin's defilement.

### What Ezekiel leaves out

Hebrews builds its argument on four things: the high priest, the veil, the mercy seat and the yearly
day of blood. "Into the second only the high priest goes, and he but once a year, and not without
taking blood, which he offers for himself and for the unintentional sins of the people" (Hebrews
9:7, ESV). Ezekiel 40-48 has none of the four. There is no high priest, no veil, no mercy seat and no
Day of Atonement; the concordance finds <span dir="rtl">כִּפֻּרִים</span> (*kippurim*, H3725)
nowhere in Ezekiel.

### What Hebrews closes, and what it grants

Hebrews closes one thing: offerings that take away sin. "It is impossible for the blood of bulls and
goats to take away sins" (Hebrews 10:4, ESV). That was never true, before the cross or after it.
Hebrews also grants the old offerings a real effect: "the blood of goats and bulls… sanctify for the
purification of the flesh" (Hebrews 9:13, ESV). Cleansing the flesh and the place is what Ezekiel's
"purify" and "make atonement for the altar" describe.

### What the apostles did after the cross

Some twenty-five years after the cross, James and the elders asked Paul to "purify yourself along with
them and pay their expenses, so that they may shave their heads" (Acts 21:24, ESV). Shaving the head
ended a Nazirite vow, and the law for it includes "one ewe lamb a year old without blemish as a sin
offering" (Numbers 6:14, ESV). Paul did it: he "purified himself along with them and went into the
temple, giving notice when the days of purification would be fulfilled and the offering presented for
each one of them" (Acts 21:26, ESV). The apostle who wrote that Christ's sacrifice was once for all
paid for offerings in the temple. Luke calls them *purification*, ἁγνισμός (*hagnismos*, G49).

### Cleansing, and remembering

The text leans to cleansing. Ezekiel's offerings purify the place where the LORD dwells among mortal
people, people who still marry, sin through error and die. The house is holy, its whole mountain is
"most holy" (Ezekiel 43:12, ESV), and the priests teach "the difference between the holy and the
common" (Ezekiel 44:23, ESV). Forgiveness rests where Ezekiel 16:63 puts it, with God: "whom God put
forward as a propitiation by his blood" (Romans 3:25, ESV).

The offerings may also look back. The Lord's Supper proclaims His death "until he comes" (1
Corinthians 11:26, ESV). Jesus said of the Passover, "I will not eat it until it is fulfilled in the
kingdom of God" (Luke 22:16, ESV), and Ezekiel's kingdom keeps the Passover (Ezekiel 45:21). The
*Bible Knowledge Commentary* (Charles Dyer, on Ezekiel 40:38-43) takes the offerings as memorials and
cites Acts 21:26. That verse names purification. Remembering and cleansing need not compete; the
words Ezekiel chose are the words of cleansing.

**The confidence:** the site leans to ceremonial cleansing, and a memorial meaning remains open.

## The prince and the King

### One Hebrew word, several men

"Prince" is <span dir="rtl">נָשִׂיא</span> (*nasi*, nah-SEE, H5387), "one lifted up". Ezekiel uses
it 37 times. It names Zedekiah, "the prince in Jerusalem" (Ezekiel 12:10, ESV), the princes of
Israel and of the nations, and Gog, "the chief prince of Meshech and Tubal" (Ezekiel 38:2, ESV). Twice
it names the coming David:

> ✝️ Ezekiel 34:23-24 (ESV)
>
> 23 And I will set up over them one shepherd, my servant David, and he shall feed them: he shall
> feed them and be their shepherd. 24 And I, the LORD, will be their God, and my servant David shall
> be prince among them. I am the LORD; I have spoken.

"David my servant shall be their prince forever" (Ezekiel 37:25, ESV), and in the same passage "my
servant David shall be king over them" (Ezekiel 37:24, ESV). Ezekiel 34 has two shepherds. The LORD
says "I, I myself will search for my sheep" and "I myself will be the shepherd of my sheep" (Ezekiel
34:11, 15, ESV), and then appoints David. Jesus claims both. He is "the good shepherd" with "one
flock, one shepherd" (John 10:11, 16, ESV), and He "came to seek and to save the lost" (Luke 19:10,
ESV), the LORD's own words, "I will seek the lost" (Ezekiel 34:16, ESV). This David is Christ, the
"righteous Branch" raised up for David (Jeremiah 23:5, ESV).

### Is Christ called "prince"?

Yes, with other words.

| Text | Hebrew or Greek | ESV | KJV |
|---|---|---|---|
| Isaiah 9:6 | <span dir="rtl">שַׂר־שָׁלוֹם</span> (*sar shalom*, H8269) | Prince of Peace | Prince of Peace |
| Daniel 9:25 | <span dir="rtl">מָשִׁיחַ נָגִיד</span> (*mashiach nagid*, H5057) | an anointed one, a prince | the Messiah the Prince |
| Daniel 8:25 | <span dir="rtl">שַׂר־שָׂרִים</span> (*sar sarim*) | the Prince of princes | Prince of princes |
| Acts 5:31 | ἀρχηγός (*archēgos*, G747) | Leader | a Prince |
| Acts 3:15 | ἀρχηγός (*archēgos*) | Author of life | the Prince of life |
| Revelation 1:5 | ἄρχων (*archōn*, G758) | the ruler of kings on earth | the prince of the kings of the earth |

Outside Ezekiel 34 and 37, Scripture never calls the Messiah *nasi*.

### The prince of chapters 44-48

*Nasi* occurs 20 times in Ezekiel 44-48. In those chapters the prince is never named David and never
called king. The word "king" appears in Ezekiel 40-48 only for the old kings' dead bodies (Ezekiel
43:7, 9), and the only throne is the LORD's: "this is the place of my throne" (Ezekiel 43:7, ESV).
What the prince does:

- He alone may "sit in it to eat bread before the LORD" in the gate the LORD entered, coming in by
  the vestibule (Ezekiel 44:3, ESV). The elders of Israel once "beheld God, and ate and drank"
  (Exodus 24:11, ESV).
- He receives the people's offering and provides the offerings for the feasts (Ezekiel 45:16-17).
- "The prince shall provide for himself and all the people of the land a young bull for a sin
  offering" (Ezekiel 45:22, ESV). "For himself" is <span dir="rtl">בַּעֲדוֹ</span> (*ba'ado*), "on
  his own behalf".
- He worships "at the threshold of the gate" while "the priests shall offer his burnt offering"
  (Ezekiel 46:2, ESV). He does not enter the inner court.
- He goes in and out with the people (Ezekiel 46:10).
- He has sons, and their inheritance is protected (Ezekiel 46:16-18).
- He is warned: "my princes shall no more oppress my people" (Ezekiel 45:8, ESV), and he "shall not
  take any of the inheritance of the people" (Ezekiel 46:18, ESV).

**He is not Christ.** Christ needs no sin offering, worships at no threshold, and is not warned
against oppression. The *Bible Knowledge Commentary* draws the same conclusion from Ezekiel 45:22.

### Who he is

This is contested, and the text gives reasons on both sides.

- **David raised from the dead.** Ezekiel names David as prince (Ezekiel 34:24; 37:25), and Jeremiah
  and Hosea name David as Israel's coming king (Jeremiah 30:9; Hosea 3:5). The *Bible Knowledge
  Commentary* holds this view. Against it, risen saints "neither marry nor are given in marriage"
  (Luke 20:35, ESV), and this prince has sons who inherit. A sin offering "for himself" also sits
  awkwardly with a body raised in glory. And Jeremiah's "David their king, whom I will raise up for
  them" (Jeremiah 30:9, ESV) uses the ordinary verb for appointing, <span dir="rtl">אָקִים</span>
  (*aqim*, from H6965), the verb of "the LORD your God will raise up for you a prophet" (Deuteronomy
  18:15, ESV) and of the judges (Judges 2:16).
- **A mortal ruler of Israel under the King.** The sons, the warning, the plural "my princes"
  (Ezekiel 45:8) and the sin offering all fit a man in a natural body who governs Israel's land and
  worship under Christ. His portion lies "between the territory of Judah and the territory of
  Benjamin" (Ezekiel 48:22, ESV). Against it, Ezekiel 37:25 uses the same title for David, and the
  text never names this prince's family.

The text leans to the second. The David of Ezekiel 34 and 37 is Christ, the King. The prince of
Ezekiel 44-48 is a man who rules under Him and eats in the gate His glory came through. Zechariah adds
who builds the house: "Behold, the man whose name is the Branch… It is he who shall build the temple of
the LORD and shall bear royal honor, and shall sit and rule on his throne. And there shall be a
priest on his throne" (Zechariah 6:12-13, ESV). The throne and the priesthood belong to the Branch. The prince has a seat
in the gate.

## The LORD is there

Every house God has filled shows the same thing: He wants to live with His people, and holiness is
the price of His nearness.

| When | Where God dwells | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Sinai | The glory fills the tabernacle | Exodus 40:34-35 |
| Solomon | The glory fills the temple | 2 Chronicles 7:1-2 |
| 592 BC | The glory leaves for the mountain east of the city | Ezekiel 10-11 |
| The exile | "A sanctuary to them for a while" | Ezekiel 11:16 |
| The Word made flesh | "Dwelt among us, and we have seen his glory" | John 1:14 |
| His last week | "Your house is left to you desolate" | Matthew 23:38 |
| Now | "You are God's temple… God's Spirit dwells in you" | 1 Corinthians 3:16 |
| The thousand years | "The place of my throne… where I will dwell" | Ezekiel 43:7 |
| The new creation | "Its temple is the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb" | Revelation 21:22 |

The last line of Ezekiel names the city: "The LORD Is There" (Ezekiel 48:35, ESV), <span
dir="rtl">יְהוָה שָׁמָּה</span> (*YHWH shammah*). The last chapter of the Bible finishes the same
thought: "They will see his face, and his name will be on their foreheads" (Revelation 22:4, ESV).

**This shows that God keeps His word for His own name's sake.** "It is not for your sake, O house of
Israel, that I am about to act, but for the sake of my holy name" (Ezekiel 36:22, ESV). He promised
Israel a land, a shepherd and a sanctuary, and He will give all three on this earth. **It shows that
God atones.** The first "make atonement" in Ezekiel is His own act, and He did it at the cross in His
Son Jesus. **And it shows that God comes back.** The glory that left Jerusalem by the east came back
in Jesus, wept on the mountain, went up from it, and will stand on it again.

So you can trust Him with what He has not yet done. He has already come to live in you by His Spirit.
Keep His house clean, and wait for the day His name is true of the whole earth.

## Discussion questions

1. **The language.** Ezekiel uses <span dir="rtl">עוֹלָם</span> (*olam*) for the LORD's dwelling
   "forever" (Ezekiel 43:7) and for the daily offering's "perpetual statute" (Ezekiel 46:14). How does
   that change the way you read "forever" in a promise?
2. **The text in its context.** The exiles had no temple when Ezekiel was told to describe this one
   "that they may be ashamed of their iniquities" (Ezekiel 43:10). Why would a picture of a future
   house make people in Babylon ashamed, and what would it give them hope for?
3. **Christ.** The glory left for the mountain east of the city (Ezekiel 11:23), and Jesus left the
   temple for the Mount of Olives (Matthew 24:1-3) saying "you will not see me again, until…" (Matthew
   23:39). What does the "until" tell you about His return?
4. **The hard part.** Hebrews says "there is no longer any offering for sin" (Hebrews 10:18), and
   Ezekiel's prince provides "the sin offerings… to make atonement on behalf of the house of Israel"
   (Ezekiel 45:17). Paul paid for purification offerings after the cross (Acts 21:26). How do you
   hold these texts together?
5. **Be transformed.** Paul read Ezekiel 37:27 and concluded, "let us cleanse ourselves from every
   defilement of body and spirit" (2 Corinthians 7:1). What would it look like for you to treat your
   life as the house God lives in this week?

## Annex: Ezekiel beside Moses, and the older readers

!!! warning "Extra-biblical sources: useful, and not Scripture"

    This annex quotes Tobit, the Talmud, *Lamentations Rabbah*, Justin Martyr, Irenaeus and the
    *Epistle of Barnabas*. None of them is Scripture. They show how the prophecy was heard, and when.
    Where they agree with the text they corroborate it; where they conflict with it, the text
    governs. Nothing the study concludes rests on them.

### Where Ezekiel's ordinances differ from Moses'

| Matter | Moses | Ezekiel |
|---|---|---|
| New moon offering | Two bulls, one ram, seven lambs (Numbers 28:11) | One bull, six lambs, a ram (Ezekiel 46:6) |
| Daily offering | A lamb morning and twilight (Numbers 28:3-4) | A lamb "morning by morning" (Ezekiel 46:13) |
| Whom a priest may marry | Ordinary priests: not a divorced woman (Leviticus 21:7); the high priest: a virgin only (Leviticus 21:13-14) | Every priest: a virgin of Israel or a priest's widow (Ezekiel 44:22) |
| The first month | No purification of the sanctuary | Purified on the first and seventh days (Ezekiel 45:18-20) |
| The high priest and the Day of Atonement | Leviticus 16 | Absent |
| The Feast of Weeks | Leviticus 23:15-21 | Absent |

The rabbis saw these differences. The Talmud remembers Hananiah ben Hezekiah, without whom "the book
of Ezekiel would have been suppressed because its contents, in many details, contradict matters of
Torah". He took three hundred jugs of oil to an upper room and stayed there until he had reconciled
them (*b. Shabbat* 13b; *b. Menachot* 45a). Of Ezekiel 45:18, Rabbi Yohanan said, "This passage is
indeed difficult, and in the future Elijah the prophet will interpret it." Rabbi Yosei read it of the
offerings at the inauguration "in the days of Ezra" (*b. Menachot* 45a). Both readings were offered,
and neither treated the vision as already built.

### Jewish readers

- **Tobit 14:5** (before Christ): a lesser temple after the exile, then a glorious house "for ever…
  as the prophets have spoken thereof" (KJV Apocrypha).
- ***b. Yoma* 21b**: the Divine Presence among the five things the second temple lacked.
- ***b. Rosh Hashanah* 31a**: the Divine Presence left in ten stages, read from Ezekiel 9-11 and
  other verses, through the city to the mountain of Ezekiel 11:23 and on to the wilderness.
  *Lamentations Rabbah*, Petichta 25, names that mountain the Mount of Olives.

### Early Christian readers

- **Justin Martyr** (about AD 155): "there will be a resurrection of the dead, and a thousand years in
  Jerusalem, which will then be built, adorned, and enlarged, [as] the prophets Ezekiel and Isaiah
  and others declare" (*Dialogue with Trypho* 80, ANF vol. 1).
- **Irenaeus** (about AD 180): Jerusalem rebuilt in the kingdom, then the new Jerusalem after it, and
  of the promises, "nothing is capable of being allegorized, but all things are stedfast, and true,
  and substantial" (*Against Heresies* 5.35.2, ANF vol. 1).
- ***Epistle of Barnabas* 16** (early second century) makes the temple the believer's heart, in a
  chapter that calls the Jews "wretched". The figurative reading of the temple arrives alongside
  contempt for the people the promise was made to.

# The Harlot and the Bride

Near the end of Revelation an angel shows John two women. One is a prostitute who rides a beast and
drinks the blood of the saints. The other is the Bride, the wife of the Lamb. Scripture has been
setting these two women before God's people since Proverbs, and Revelation shows where each of them
ends.

**In one sentence:** Scripture sets two women before every person, the harlot, who seduces with
luxury and sorcery and is devoured by the beast she rides, and the Bride, whom the Lamb washes,
clothes and marries, and God calls His people to come out of the one and make ready for the other.

## Key Takeaways

*(This section follows the [Key Takeaways](../about/key-takeaways.md) format — see that page for what each part is for.)*

### Types & Prophecy

- Babylon in Revelation carries the Old Testament's harlot cities forward: the golden cup of
  Jeremiah 51:7, the boast of Isaiah 47:8 and the silenced wedding of Jeremiah 25:10 all reappear
  in Revelation 17-18.
- The harlot falls in the last of the bowl judgments (Revelation 16:19), and the marriage of the
  Lamb follows (Revelation 19:7).

### Lessons about Jesus

- The Lamb conquers the beast and the kings who serve it, "for he is Lord of lords and King of
  kings, and those with him are called and chosen and faithful" (Revelation 17:14, ESV).
- He nourishes and cherishes His bride (Ephesians 5:29). The beast hates the woman it carries and
  devours her (Revelation 17:16).
- He gives His people His name and His Father's name on their foreheads (Revelation 14:1; 22:4).

### Be Transformed

- **Think.** Every invitation in your life comes from one of two women. Learn to tell the harlot's
  voice from the Bride's: luxury, flattery and secrecy on one side; the Spirit's plain "Come" on
  the other (Revelation 22:17).
- **Attitude.** Hold the world's glamour lightly. The harlot's purple and gold are on loan from the
  beast, and the beast will strip them off (Revelation 17:16).
- **Do.** Name one place where you share Babylon's table, and come out of it this week.

### Prayer

Father, you have called us out of the darkness to be the Bride of your Son Jesus. Open our eyes to
the harlot's cup, however golden it looks, and give us the courage to come out of her. Wash us,
clothe us in what you grant, and keep us ready for the marriage of the Lamb. Let our lives say
"Come" with your Spirit to everyone who is thirsty.

In Jesus' name. Amen.

## The angel's two invitations

> ✝️ Revelation 17:1-6 (ESV)
>
> 1 Then one of the seven angels who had the seven bowls came and said to me, "Come, I will show you
> the judgment of the great prostitute who is seated on many waters, 2 with whom the kings of the
> earth have committed sexual immorality, and with the wine of whose sexual immorality the dwellers
> on earth have become drunk." 3 And he carried me away in the Spirit into a wilderness, and I saw a
> woman sitting on a scarlet beast that was full of blasphemous names, and it had seven heads and
> ten horns. 4 The woman was arrayed in purple and scarlet, and adorned with gold and jewels and
> pearls, holding in her hand a golden cup full of abominations and the impurities of her sexual
> immorality. 5 And on her forehead was written a name of mystery: "Babylon the great, mother of
> prostitutes and of earth's abominations." 6 And I saw the woman, drunk with the blood of the
> saints, the blood of the martyrs of Jesus. When I saw her, I marveled greatly.

Four chapters later "one of the seven angels who had the seven bowls" comes again with the same
words: "Come, I will show you the Bride, the wife of the Lamb" (Revelation 21:9, ESV). In Greek the
two invitations are identical up to the woman: δεῦρο, δείξω σοι, "Come, I will show you," then τῆς
πόρνης, "the prostitute" (17:1), or τὴν νύμφην, "the Bride" (21:9). John is carried "in the Spirit"
both times, into a wilderness to see the one and to "a great, high mountain" to see the other
(Revelation 17:3; 21:10). The study [The New Jerusalem](new-jerusalem.md) sets the two visions side
by side in a table. This study follows the pattern behind them.

"Prostitute" is πόρνη (*pornē*, POR-nay, G4204), and Revelation uses it five times, all of Babylon
(Revelation 17:1, 5, 15, 16; 19:2). "Bride" is νύμφη (*nymphē*, NOOM-fay, G3565), and it occurs
four times in the book. The first is at Babylon's fall: "the voice of bridegroom and bride will be
heard in you no more" (Revelation 18:23, ESV). The other three are the New Jerusalem (Revelation
21:2, 9; 22:17). The bride's voice is silenced in the one city and heard in the other.

## A harlot, never a wife

The harlot is never called anyone's wife. She has lovers: "the kings of the earth have committed
sexual immorality" with her (Revelation 17:2), and "the merchants of the earth have grown rich from
the power of her luxurious living" (Revelation 18:3, ESV). She belongs to no one, and she is joined
to many.

She sits on the beast (Revelation 17:3, 7), and the beast's power comes from the dragon: "to it the
dragon gave his power and his throne and great authority" (Revelation 13:2, ESV). Revelation names
the dragon plainly: "that ancient serpent, who is called the devil and Satan, the deceiver of the
whole world" (Revelation 12:9, ESV). So Satan stands behind the harlot as the power that carries
her. Scripture never makes him her bridegroom, and no covenant joins them.

The end of the harlot shows it. "The ten horns that you saw, they and the beast will hate the
prostitute. They will make her desolate and naked, and devour her flesh and burn her up with fire"
(Revelation 17:16, ESV). The power that carried her turns and eats her. Set that beside what Paul
says of Christ and His bride: "no one ever hated his own flesh, but nourishes and cherishes it, just
as Christ does the church" (Ephesians 5:29, ESV). The beast "will hate" μισήσουσιν, the woman it
carried. Christ never hates His own.

Even this destruction serves God: "for God has put it into their hearts to carry out his purpose ...
until the words of God are fulfilled" (Revelation 17:17, ESV). This shows that God rules over the
harlot and the beast alike. Evil devours its own, and God uses even that to bring His word to pass.

## Two women in Proverbs

Proverbs has already drawn the two women. Wisdom builds a house, sets her table, and sends out her
young women "to call from the highest places in the town": "Whoever is simple, let him turn in
here!" (Proverbs 9:3-4, ESV). Her offer is life: "Leave your simple ways, and live" (9:6).

Folly sits at the door of her house, "on the highest places of the town," and calls with Wisdom's
very words: "Whoever is simple, let him turn in here!" (Proverbs 9:14, 16, ESV). Her offer is
secrecy: "Stolen water is sweet, and bread eaten in secret is pleasant." And her house has a
cellar the guest cannot see: "he does not know that the dead are there, that her guests are in the
depths of Sheol" (9:17-18, ESV).

Proverbs 7 shows the same woman at work. She is "dressed as a prostitute, wily of heart"; she
"seizes him and kisses him"; she speaks of sacrifices paid and a husband away; and "with much
seductive speech she persuades him" (Proverbs 7:10, 13-14, 19, 21, ESV). The young man follows "as
an ox goes to the slaughter" (7:22), and the father's verdict closes the chapter: "Her house is the
way to Sheol, going down to the chambers of death" (7:27, ESV).

The two women of Proverbs are personifications of wisdom and folly. Revelation does not quote them,
and this study does not make Folly a figure for Babylon. What Proverbs gives is the shape the
pattern keeps: two women, two invitations in the same words, and two houses, one of life and one of
death.

## Harlot cities

The prophets call cities harlots, and the same marks recur.

- **Jezebel.** Ahab "took for his wife Jezebel the daughter of Ethbaal king of the Sidonians, and
  went and served Baal" (1 Kings 16:31, ESV), and "there was none who sold himself to do what was
  evil ... like Ahab, whom Jezebel his wife incited" (1 Kings 21:25, ESV). Jehu named her sins:
  "the whorings and the sorceries of your mother Jezebel" (2 Kings 9:22, ESV).
- **Nineveh.** Nahum calls the Assyrian capital "the prostitute, graceful and of deadly charms, who
  betrays nations with her whorings, and peoples with her charms" (Nahum 3:4, ESV).
- **Tyre.** Isaiah pictures the merchant city as a "forgotten prostitute" singing to be remembered, who "will
  prostitute herself with all the kingdoms of the world" (Isaiah 23:17, ESV).
- **Babylon.** Jeremiah: "Babylon was a golden cup in the LORD's hand, making all the earth drunken;
  the nations drank of her wine" (Jeremiah 51:7, ESV). Isaiah gives her boast: "I shall not sit as a
  widow" (Isaiah 47:8, ESV), and calls her "the mistress of kingdoms" (47:5).

Two marks run through the list. The first is sorcery beside harlotry. The Septuagint of 2 Kings
9:22 pairs Jezebel's πορνεῖαι, "whorings," with her φάρμακα, "sorceries," and the Septuagint of
Nahum 3:4 pairs the same two words for Nineveh. Revelation gives Babylon both: she is the πόρνη, and
"all nations were deceived by your sorcery," φαρμακείᾳ (*pharmakeia*, far-mah-KAY-ah, G5331;
Revelation 18:23, ESV). The second is commerce: Tyre's trade with the kingdoms, and Babylon's
merchants grown rich (Revelation 18:3).

Revelation also reuses Babylon's own Old Testament words. The "golden cup" in the harlot's hand
(Revelation 17:4) is Jeremiah's: the Septuagint reads ποτήριον χρυσοῦν (Jeremiah 28:7 in its
numbering), and Revelation has ποτήριον χρυσοῦν. Her boast, "I sit as a queen, I am no widow"
(Revelation 18:7, ESV), is Isaiah 47:8's boast of Babylon. The name itself goes back to Babel, where
humanity built a tower to make a name for itself (Genesis 11:4, 9): the Hebrew <span dir="rtl">בָּבֶל</span>
(*Bāḇel*, bah-VEL, H894) is the same word for both.

## The wife who played the harlot

One woman in the prophets is a wife: Israel, married to the LORD. When the prophets call her a
harlot, the charge is unfaithfulness. "How the faithful city has become a whore, she who was full of
justice!" (Isaiah 1:21, ESV). "You trusted in your beauty and played the whore because of your
renown" (Ezekiel 16:15, ESV). "Adulterous wife, who receives strangers instead of her husband!"
(Ezekiel 16:32, ESV). Hosea's marriage was a living sermon on it: "the land commits great whoredom
by forsaking the LORD" (Hosea 1:2, ESV).

The difference between Israel and Babylon is the covenant. Babylon has lovers and no husband.
Israel has a Husband who comes after her: "Therefore, behold, I will allure her, and bring her into
the wilderness, and speak tenderly to her" (Hosea 2:14, ESV). The study [The Bride of
Christ](../israel-and-church/bride-of-christ.md) follows that restoration, and the reading this site
holds: Israel remains the wife of the LORD, restored, and the church is the Bride of Christ. Neither
is Babylon. The harlot of Revelation is the woman who was never anyone's wife.

This shows that God does not leave His covenant people in their unfaithfulness. He judges harlotry,
and He goes after His own.

## The serpent and the betrothed virgin

The harlot's pull works inside the church. Paul writes to the Corinthians:

> ✝️ 2 Corinthians 11:2-3 (ESV)
>
> 2 For I feel a divine jealousy for you, since I betrothed you to one husband, to present you as a
> pure virgin to Christ. 3 But I am afraid that as the serpent deceived Eve by his cunning, your
> thoughts will be led astray from a sincere and pure devotion to Christ.

The church is betrothed, and the danger Paul names is seduction: the serpent's cunning leading the
bride's thoughts away from her husband. He adds that the deception comes dressed in light: "even
Satan disguises himself as an angel of light" (2 Corinthians 11:14, ESV).

The risen Jesus said the same to the church at Thyatira: "you tolerate that woman Jezebel, who calls
herself a prophetess and is teaching and seducing my servants to practice sexual immorality and to
eat food sacrificed to idols" (Revelation 2:20, ESV). The name is Jezebel's, and so is the pattern:
a woman inside God's house teaching His servants the harlot's ways. James uses the prophets' word
for believers who love the world: "You adulterous people! Do you not know that friendship with the
world is enmity with God?" (James 4:4, ESV).

So the harlot is not only a city at the end of history. Her voice reaches the bride now, and the
bride is warned to know it. The study [Sin and Sexual Immorality](../sin/sexual-immorality.md) traces
the same seduction in the body.

## Two names on the forehead

Revelation divides humanity by whom they follow, and it marks the division on the forehead.

| | The harlot's people | The Bride's people |
|---|---|---|
| The name | the beast's mark "on the right hand or the forehead" (Revelation 13:16); the harlot's own name on hers (Revelation 17:5) | "his name and his Father's name written on their foreheads" (Revelation 14:1); "his name will be on their foreheads" (Revelation 22:4) |
| Who they are | "the dwellers on earth whose names have not been written in the book of life" (Revelation 17:8) | "called and chosen and faithful" (Revelation 17:14); "those who are written in the Lamb's book of life" (Revelation 21:27) |
| What they did | "nor did they repent of their murders or their sorceries or their sexual immorality or their thefts" (Revelation 9:21) | "those who wash their robes" (Revelation 22:14) |
| Where they end | "Outside are the dogs and sorcerers and the sexually immoral and murderers and idolaters" (Revelation 22:15) | they "enter the city by the gates" (Revelation 22:14) |

The harlot's two marks follow her people to the end. Revelation 22:15 puts φάρμακοι, "sorcerers"
(*pharmakoi*, G5333), and πόρνοι, "the sexually immoral," outside the Bride's city, the same two
sins that marked Jezebel, Nineveh and Babylon.

The people of the beast follow the harlot. Scripture never calls them her bride or Satan's. They are
her customers and her drinkers: "the dwellers on earth have become drunk" with her wine (Revelation
17:2). The Lamb's people are joined to Him by name and by covenant.

## Come out of her

> ✝️ Revelation 18:4-5 (ESV)
>
> 4 Then I heard another voice from heaven saying, "Come out of her, my people, lest you take part in
> her sins, lest you share in her plagues; 5 for her sins are heaped high as heaven, and God has
> remembered her iniquities.

God's people are inside Babylon when the voice calls them out. The words are the prophets'. To the
exiles in the first Babylon, Jeremiah said, "Go out of the midst of her, my people!" (Jeremiah
51:45, ESV), and Isaiah, "Depart, depart, go out from there; touch no unclean thing; go out from the
midst of her" (Isaiah 52:11, ESV). The Septuagint of Isaiah 52:11 reads ἐξέλθατε ἐκ μέσου αὐτῆς,
"go out from the midst of her," and Revelation 18:4 has ἐξέλθατε ... ἐξ αὐτῆς, "come out of her."

When Babylon falls is a question of the prophetic timeline. Revelation places it in the seventh
bowl: "God remembered Babylon the great, to make her drain the cup of the wine of the fury of his
wrath" (Revelation 16:19, ESV). This site reads the bowls as part of the coming tribulation and
holds that the church is caught up before it begins (see the [Statement of
Faith](../about/statement-of-faith.md)), so the voice of 18:4 speaks to God's people on earth in
those days. The call itself is already the church's. Paul quotes Isaiah 52:11 to the Corinthians:
"Therefore go out from their midst, and be separate from them, says the Lord, and touch no unclean
thing; then I will welcome you, and I will be a father to you, and you shall be sons and daughters
to me" (2 Corinthians 6:17-18, ESV).

Who Babylon is in Revelation is contested. The angel calls her "the great city that has dominion
over the kings of the earth" (Revelation 17:18) and places her on "seven mountains" (17:9), which
many read as Rome. The ESV Study Bible lists the futurist readings: a great religious entity
supporting the Antichrist, a rebuilt Babylon, or a revived Roman Empire (note on Revelation
17:1-19:10). The call to come out does not wait on the answer. Whatever city carries her name, God's
people are to leave her sins before her plagues fall.

## The end of each woman

The harlot's end is silence. "The light of a lamp will shine in you no more, and the voice of
bridegroom and bride will be heard in you no more" (Revelation 18:23, ESV). Jeremiah had spoken the
same sentence over Judah: "I will banish from them the voice of mirth and the voice of gladness, the
voice of the bridegroom and the voice of the bride, the grinding of the millstones and the light of
the lamp" (Jeremiah 25:10, ESV). Revelation keeps the mill and the lamp too (Revelation 18:22-23).
Heaven answers her fall with praise: "he has judged the great prostitute who corrupted the earth
with her immorality" (Revelation 19:2, ESV).

The Bride's end is a wedding:

> ✝️ Revelation 19:7-9 (ESV)
>
> 7 Let us rejoice and exult and give him the glory, for the marriage of the Lamb has come, and his
> Bride has made herself ready; 8 it was granted her to clothe herself with fine linen, bright and
> pure"— for the fine linen is the righteous deeds of the saints. 9 And the angel said to me,
> "Write this: Blessed are those who are invited to the marriage supper of the Lamb." And he said to
> me, "These are the true words of God."

The two women are dressed for what they are. The harlot wears purple and scarlet, gold and jewels,
and holds a cup "full of abominations" (Revelation 17:4). The Bride wears linen "bright and pure,"
and "it was granted her" (Revelation 19:8): her clothing is a gift. The harlot's finery is stripped
by the beast that gave it (Revelation 17:16). The Bride's is given by the Lamb and never taken.

The book closes with the Bride's own voice: "The Spirit and the Bride say, 'Come.' And let the one
who hears say, 'Come.' And let the one who is thirsty come; let the one who desires take the water of
life without price" (Revelation 22:17, ESV). The harlot made the nations drink her wine (Revelation
14:8). The Bride offers the water of life free.

This shows that God finishes what He began. He judges the harlot who corrupted the earth, He clothes
the Bride with what He grants, and He sends her out with His Spirit to call the thirsty. So you may
leave Babylon's table without fear, because a better one is set.

## Discussion questions

1. **The language.** Revelation introduces both women with the same words, δεῦρο, δείξω σοι, "Come,
   I will show you" (Revelation 17:1; 21:9). What does the matching introduction ask the reader to
   do with the two visions?
2. **The text in its context.** The harlot is never called a wife, and the beast that carries her
   ends by devouring her (Revelation 17:16). What does that say about what Satan offers those who
   follow him?
3. **Christ.** The Lamb "nourishes and cherishes" His bride (Ephesians 5:29) and grants her linen
   "bright and pure" (Revelation 19:8). How does what Christ gives His bride differ from what the
   harlot gives her lovers?
4. **The hard part.** Revelation 18:4 calls God's people out of Babylon, and Babylon's identity is
   contested. Why might the call be clearer than the identification, and what does Paul's use of
   Isaiah 52:11 (2 Corinthians 6:17) add?
5. **The language.** Harlotry and sorcery appear together for Jezebel, Nineveh and Babylon (2 Kings
   9:22; Nahum 3:4; Revelation 18:23), and both kinds of people are outside the city in Revelation
   22:15. What do the two sins have in common?
6. **Be transformed.** Folly calls with Wisdom's very words (Proverbs 9:4, 16). Where does the
   harlot's invitation sound like the Bride's in your own life, and how will you tell them apart?

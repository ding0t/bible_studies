# The Thousand Years

*What the saints do when Christ reigns*

The Lord's Prayer asks for the Father's will to be done "on earth as it is in heaven" (Matthew 6:10,
ESV). Revelation 20 shows the earth where that prayer is answered. Satan is shut in a pit. Thrones
are set. The martyrs come to life, and they reign with Christ for a thousand years.

**In one sentence:** When Jesus returns He will reign on this earth for a thousand years, with Satan
bound and His people raised to reign beside Him, so that every promise God made about this world is
kept on this world; the faithfulness He asks of you now is the first part of the work He will give
you then.

### Types & Prophecy

- **Psalm 2 is fulfilled in two stages.** The apostles read its raging nations of the cross
  (Acts 4:25-27) and its "You are my Son" of the resurrection (Acts 13:33). Revelation gives its rod
  of iron to Jesus at His return (Revelation 19:15) and to those who conquer with Him
  (Revelation 2:26-27).
- **The prophets saw a King reigning over an earth not yet made new.** Death still comes
  (Isaiah 65:20), and a nation can still refuse to worship (Zechariah 14:17). Revelation 20 gives
  that reign its place: after His return, before the new creation.

### Lessons about Jesus

- **Jesus will reign where He was rejected.** The King of Psalm 2 sits on Zion, and the law goes out
  from Jerusalem (Isaiah 2:3).
- **He shares His reign.** "I will grant him to sit with me on my throne" (Revelation 3:21, ESV). He
  is the One who conquered death, "who died and came to life" (Revelation 2:8, ESV), and His people's
  rising takes the same verb (Revelation 20:4).

### Be Transformed

- **Think.** God keeps His word to this earth. He promised His Son the nations (Psalm 2:8), and He
  will give Him them, with you beside Him: "if we endure, we will also reign with him"
  (2 Timothy 2:12, ESV).
- **Attitude.** Hold what you have been given as your King's trust, to be put to work "until I come"
  (Luke 19:13, ESV).
- **Do.** Name one "very little" task (Luke 19:17) you will do for Jesus this week, and each day as
  you do it pray "your kingdom come" (Matthew 6:10), asking Him to come back and reign.

## Revelation 20, in its order

### After the rider on the white horse

John wrote to seven churches in Asia under pressure (Revelation 1:4, 9). Just before Revelation 20,
Christ rides out of heaven (Revelation 19:11). The beast and the false prophet
"were thrown alive into the lake of fire" (Revelation 19:20, ESV), and "the rest were slain by the
sword that came from the mouth of him who was sitting on the horse" (Revelation 19:21, ESV). Revelation
20 deals with the third enemy, the dragon. [A Day Is a Thousand
Years](day-is-a-thousand-years.md#revelation-20-the-rest-counted-in-thousands) quotes Revelation
20:2-7 in full and counts the six "thousand years"; this study reads the chapter for what happens in
them.

### The dragon bound

> ✝️ Revelation 20:1-3 (ESV)
>
> 1 Then I saw an angel coming down from heaven, holding in his hand the key to the bottomless pit
> and a great chain. 2 And he seized the dragon, that ancient serpent, who is the devil and Satan, and
> bound him for a thousand years, 3 and threw him into the pit, and shut it and sealed it over him, so
> that he might not deceive the nations any longer, until the thousand years were ended. After that
> he must be released for a little while.

Five verbs close him in: seized, bound, threw, shut, sealed. The pit is the ἄβυσσος (*abyssos*,
AH-bus-sos, G12), seven times in Revelation the place where powers of evil are held (Revelation
9:1-2, 11; 11:7; 17:8; 20:1, 3). The purpose is ἵνα μὴ πλανήσῃ ἔτι τὰ ἔθνη, "so that he might not
deceive the nations any longer." "That ancient serpent" names him from Genesis 3. The deceiver of Eden
is silenced for the length of the reign.

### Thrones and the first resurrection

> ✝️ Revelation 20:4-5 (ESV)
>
> 4 Then I saw thrones, and seated on them were those to whom the authority to judge was committed.
> Also I saw the souls of those who had been beheaded for the testimony of Jesus and for the word of
> God, and those who had not worshiped the beast or its image and had not received its mark on their
> foreheads or their hands. They came to life and reigned with Christ for a thousand years. 5 The rest
> of the dead did not come to life until the thousand years were ended. This is the first
> resurrection.

The thrones echo Daniel: "thrones were placed" (Daniel 7:9, ESV), and "judgment was given for
the saints of the Most High, and the time came when the saints possessed the kingdom" (Daniel 7:22,
ESV). John does not name who sits on them. He does name the martyrs of the beast's reign, the people
Revelation 13:15 had seen killed for refusing the image.

"They came to life" is one Greek word, ἔζησαν (*ezēsan*, EDZ-ay-sahn, G2198), the aorist of "live."
The same form comes back in the next sentence: "The rest of the dead did not come to life" (οὐκ
ἔζησαν). Elsewhere in Revelation the aorist of this verb names a return from death. Jesus is the One
"who died and came to life" (Revelation 2:8, ESV), and the beast's wound is mortal "and yet lived"
(Revelation 13:14, ESV). John calls it "the first resurrection," ἀνάστασις (*anastasis*,
ah-NAH-stah-sis, G386). The noun occurs forty-two times in the New Testament. Outside Revelation
20:5-6 it is a rising from death in every place but one, Simeon's "fall and rising of many in Israel"
(Luke 2:34, ESV).

Verse 6 says what the risen do:

> ✝️ Revelation 20:6 (ESV)
>
> 6 Blessed and holy is the one who shares in the first resurrection! Over such the second death has
> no power, but they will be priests of God and of Christ, and they will reign with him for a
> thousand years.

Its people are risen. They serve as ἱερεῖς (*hiereis*, hee-er-ACE,
G2409), priests. And they reign "with him," which is Christ's own reign shared.

### The release and the last rebellion

At the end Satan is "released from his prison" and comes out "to deceive the nations that are at the
four corners of the earth, Gog and Magog, to gather them for battle; their number is like the sand of
the sea" (Revelation 20:7-8, ESV). They surround "the camp of the saints and the beloved city," and
"fire came down from heaven and consumed them" (Revelation 20:9, ESV). The devil is thrown "into the
lake of fire and sulfur where the beast and the false prophet were" (Revelation 20:10, ESV). Then
comes the great white throne (Revelation 20:11-15). What follows, and why the old earth ends there, is
worked through in [A New Heaven and a New Earth](new-heaven-and-new-earth.md#after-the-thousand-years).

After a thousand years under a righteous King, with the tempter shut away, a multitude still follows
him the moment he is let out. **This shows that
God's judgment of the nations is just.** No one at the last battle can say they never saw a good King.

## Three readings of the thousand years

Christians have read this chapter three ways, and each reading has textual reasons.

### The amillennial reading

The thousand years are the present age, from Christ's first coming to His second.

- **Revelation repeats itself.** The seals, trumpets and bowls each run to the end. "The battle," with
  the article, is gathered at Revelation 16:14, 19:19 and 20:8, and both 19:17-21 and 20:8 draw on
  Ezekiel 38-39. So Revelation 20:1 can open a new vision that goes back to the cross.
- **Jesus has bound the strong man.** "How can someone enter a strong man's house and plunder his
  goods, unless he first binds the strong man?" (Matthew 12:29, ESV). The binding stops one thing,
  deceiving "the nations," so the gospel now goes to them.
- **John sees souls, on thrones.** He saw "souls" under the altar too (Revelation 6:9), and thrones in
  Revelation are usually in heaven (Revelation 4:4; 11:16). So the martyrs reign with Christ now, in
  heaven.
- **Scripture pairs a spiritual and a bodily rising.** "The dead will hear the voice of the Son of
  God, and those who hear will live" is "now here" (John 5:25, ESV); the tombs open later
  (John 5:28-29). Believers are already "raised... with him and seated" with Him (Ephesians 2:6, ESV).
- **No other passage names an interim reign.** Outside Revelation 20 the New Testament sets the
  resurrection and the judgment at the one return (John 5:28-29; Matthew 25:31-46).

### The postmillennial reading

The gospel will so prevail that the world enjoys a long age of righteousness, and Christ returns
after it.

- **Christ rules now among His enemies.** "Rule in the midst of your enemies!" (Psalm 110:2, ESV).
  "He must reign until he has put all his enemies under his feet" (1 Corinthians 15:25, ESV).
- **The kingdom grows until it fills the world,** like leaven "till it was all leavened" (Matthew 13:33,
  ESV), and the prophets' peace among the nations comes through the preaching Jesus sent to "all
  nations" (Matthew 28:19).

### The premillennial reading

Christ returns, then reigns on the earth for a thousand years, then the last judgment comes. This is
the site's position ([statement of faith](../about/statement-of-faith.md#end-times)).

- **Chapter 20 assumes chapter 19 has happened.** The devil goes "where the beast and the false
  prophet were" (Revelation 20:10, ESV). They were thrown there at the return (Revelation 19:20).
- **The binding ends what Satan does now.** In this age he is "the deceiver of the whole world"
  thrown down "to the earth" (Revelation 12:9, ESV), prowling "like a roaring lion" (1 Peter 5:8,
  ESV), the god who "has blinded the minds of the unbelievers" (2 Corinthians 4:4, ESV). Revelation 20
  shuts and seals him away so that he deceives the nations no longer.
- **One verb, two verses.** "They came to life" and "the rest of the dead did not come to life" are the
  same word, ἔζησαν, in one context. If the second is the bodily rising of Revelation 20:12-13, the
  first is too. In John 5 Jesus marks the spiritual life with "is now here" and keeps ἀνάστασις for
  the tombs (John 5:29). Revelation 20 has no such marker.
- **The prophets need it.** Their King reigns over an earth with death in it and nations that can still
  refuse Him (Isaiah 65:20; Zechariah 14:17). That is an earth between His return and the new
  creation.

If "the rest were slain" at the return (Revelation 19:21), who are the nations Satan can no longer
deceive? This site's answer is that "the rest" are the armies gathered against the Lord (Revelation 19:19), and survivors of those
nations live on into the kingdom: "everyone who survives of all the nations that have come against
Jerusalem shall go up year after year to worship the King" (Zechariah 14:16, ESV).

### Where this study stands

**Christ returns, then reigns on the earth, then the dead are judged: Revelation 20 gives that
order, and this site holds it.** Revelation 20:10 looks back to Revelation 19:20, the binding takes
five verbs, and ἔζησαν comes twice in two verses. Revelation does repeat itself, and the amillennial
case rests on that, so faithful readers still differ here. The site reads the thousand years as a
literal length, as it reads Revelation's other periods (1,260 days, forty-two months).

Paul's own order leaves room for the reign: "Christ the firstfruits, then at his coming those who
belong to Christ. Then comes the end, when he delivers the kingdom to God the Father," for "he must
reign until he has put all his enemies under his feet" (1 Corinthians 15:23-25, ESV). [Your Kingdom
Come](your-kingdom-come.md) sets out how that kingdom is already here and not yet complete.

## What the prophets saw

John gave a length to a kingdom on the earth that the prophets had already promised.

### A King on Zion

> ✝️ Psalm 2:6-9 (ESV)
>
> 6 "As for me, I have set my King on Zion, my holy hill." 7 I will tell of the decree: The LORD said
> to me, "You are my Son; today I have begotten you. 8 Ask of me, and I will make the nations your
> heritage, and the ends of the earth your possession. 9 You shall break them with a rod of iron and
> dash them in pieces like a potter's vessel."

The Hebrew for "break them," <span dir="rtl">תְּרֹעֵם</span> (*tero'em*, teh-ro-EM, H7489), comes from
a verb meaning to shatter. The same consonants can be read as "shepherd them," from <span
dir="rtl">רָעָה</span> (*ra'ah*, H7462), and the Septuagint read them so: Ποιμανεῖς αὐτοὺς ἐν ῥάβδῳ
σιδηρᾷ. Revelation follows the Greek. Its verb for the rod of iron is ποιμαίνω (*poimainō*, poy-MY-noh,
G4165), "shepherd," in the hand of the Messiah (Revelation 12:5; 19:15) and of the one who conquers
(Revelation 2:27). The rod breaks rebellion and shepherds the nations that remain.

### Psalm 72: dominion from sea to sea

Psalm 72 is a prayer "Of Solomon" (Psalm 72:1, ESV) that asks for more than Solomon ever ruled:
"dominion from sea to sea" (Psalm 72:8, ESV), "all nations serve him" (Psalm 72:11, ESV), "all
nations call him blessed" (Psalm 72:17, ESV). It asks for a King who "delivers the needy when he
calls" and to whom "precious is their blood" (Psalm 72:12, 14, ESV). No New Testament writer applies
the psalm to Jesus by name, so reading it of His reign is an inference from its scope, which no king
of Israel ever filled.

### The nations come to be taught

Isaiah and Micah record the same oracle of "the latter days" (Isaiah 2:2-4; Micah 4:1-5). The nations
stream to Zion and say, "Come, let us go up to the mountain of the LORD, to the house of the God of
Jacob, that he may teach us his ways and that we may walk in his paths" (Isaiah 2:3, ESV). "Teach us"
is <span dir="rtl">יֹרֵנוּ</span> (*yorenu*, yo-RAY-noo, H3384), and the next clause uses the noun from
the same root: "out of Zion shall go forth the law," <span dir="rtl">תּוֹרָה</span> (*torah*,
toh-RAH, H8451). The LORD Himself is the teacher. Then "he shall judge between the nations," and
"they shall beat their swords into plowshares" (Isaiah 2:4, ESV). Micah adds, "they shall sit every man under his vine and under his fig tree, and no one shall make them
afraid" (Micah 4:4, ESV).

### The Branch and the peace of the earth

The Spirit of the LORD rests on the shoot from Jesse's stump
(Isaiah 11:1-2). "With righteousness he shall judge the poor" (Isaiah 11:4, ESV). The wolf dwells
with the lamb, "and a little child shall lead them" (Isaiah 11:6, ESV), "for the earth shall be full
of the knowledge of the LORD as the waters cover the sea" (Isaiah 11:9, ESV). Paul hears the chapter's
hope already reaching the Gentiles who trust Christ (Romans 15:12, quoting Isaiah 11:10); the peace
of the whole earth waits for the King's reign.

### Death still present

Isaiah 65:17 opens with "new heavens and a new earth" (ESV), then speaks of a world where "the young
man shall die a hundred years old" (Isaiah 65:20, ESV). Revelation 21:4 says death "shall be no
more." The dispensational answer, that Isaiah sees the kingdom and the new creation together, is set
out in [A New Heaven and a New Earth](new-heaven-and-new-earth.md#isaiah-6520-and-the-telescope), and
the passage is contested.

### The nations keep Booths

Zechariah 14 is set on the day "the LORD will be king over all the earth" (Zechariah 14:9, ESV). The
survivors of the nations "shall go up year after year to worship the King, the LORD of hosts, and to
keep the Feast of Booths," <span dir="rtl">סֻכּוֹת</span> (*sukkot*, soo-KOHT, H5521), and a family
that does not go up gets "no rain" (Zechariah 14:16-17, ESV). [Tabernacles](../feasts/tabernacles.md#zechariah-14)
works through the chapter.

### A prison, then a reckoning

Isaiah saw the shape of Revelation 20 in miniature. The LORD will punish "the host of heaven, in
heaven, and the kings of the earth, on the earth. They will be gathered together as prisoners in a
pit; they will be shut up in a prison, and after many days they will be punished," and "the LORD of
hosts reigns on Mount Zion" (Isaiah 24:21-23, ESV). Revelation gives the "many days" their number.

Put together, these passages describe a King reigning from Jerusalem over nations that still have
weather, harvests, death and the freedom to refuse Him. **This shows that God is faithful to the
world He made.** He will rule it in righteousness before He makes it new.

## After the tribulation: clearing and rebuilding

The King returns to a ruined world. Before the prophets' pictures of peace come true, someone has to
clear the wreckage, bury the dead and rebuild the cities.

### The damage left behind

The last bowl leaves the world broken: "The great city was split into three parts, and the cities of
the nations fell... And every island fled away, and no mountains were to be found" (Revelation
16:19-20, ESV). Isaiah saw the same: "the LORD will empty the earth and make it desolate, and he
will twist its surface and scatter its inhabitants" (Isaiah 24:1, ESV).

### Clearing the land

Ezekiel describes the clean-up after God destroys the army of Gog:

> ✝️ Ezekiel 39:9, 12 (ESV)
>
> 9 Then those who dwell in the cities of Israel will go out and make fires of the weapons and burn
> them, shields and bucklers, bow and arrows, clubs and spears; and they will make fires of them for
> seven years, ... 12 For seven months the house of Israel will be burying them, in order to cleanse
> the land.

The burying is organised work. "They will set apart men to travel through the land regularly and
bury those travelers remaining on the face of the land, so as to cleanse it" (Ezekiel 39:14, ESV).
"Cleanse" is טָהֵר (*taher*, tah-HER, H2891), the word for ritual purity: the land is made fit again
for God's people to live in. The weapons serve as fuel, "so that they will not need to take wood out
of the field or cut down any out of the forests" (Ezekiel 39:10, ESV).

When that war happens is contested. Many dispensational writers place Ezekiel 38-39 before the
tribulation or early in it, which puts most or all of the seven years of burning before the King
returns. Others place it in the middle of the tribulation or
at its end, at His return, which carries the burning into the kingdom's first years. Revelation 20:8
uses the names "Gog and Magog" for the rebellion after the thousand years, and some read Ezekiel
there.

### The days after the 1,260

Daniel counts from the day "the abomination that makes desolate is set up": "there shall be 1,290
days," and "Blessed is he who waits and arrives at the 1,335 days" (Daniel 12:11-12, ESV). Set
against the 1,260 days of the tribulation's second half, and with the 1,335 counted from the same
day, that is thirty days past the end and then forty-five more. Daniel does not say what they hold.
Many dispensational interpreters suggest a time of transition between the return and the settled reign: the judgment of
the nations, when "the Son of Man comes in his glory... Before him will be gathered all the nations"
(Matthew 25:31-32, ESV), and the setting in order of the land. That is an inference, and [the
Tribulation study](tribulation.md#one-span-counted-three-ways) sets out how the three spans are
counted.

### Rebuilding the ruins

- **Isaiah 61:4.** "They shall build up the ancient ruins; they shall raise up the former
  devastations; they shall repair the ruined cities, the devastations of many generations" (ESV).
  In the synagogue at Nazareth Jesus read Isaiah 61 as far as "to proclaim the year of the Lord's
  favor" (Luke 4:19, ESV), rolled up the scroll and said, "Today this Scripture has been fulfilled in
  your hearing" (Luke 4:21, ESV). He stopped in the middle of Isaiah 61:2, before "the day of
  vengeance of our God." The rebuilding of Isaiah 61:4 follows the vengeance in Isaiah's order, so on
  this reading it waits for His return.
- **Amos 9:11, 14.** "I will raise up the booth of David that is fallen and repair its breaches...
  they shall rebuild the ruined cities and inhabit them; they shall plant vineyards and drink their
  wine" (ESV). James quotes the first promise at the Jerusalem council, of the Gentiles now being
  called (Acts 15:14): "After this I will return, and I will rebuild the tent of David that has
  fallen" (Acts 15:16, ESV). Whether the rebuilding is that calling or follows Christ's return is
  contested, and [Israel's Regathering and
  Refining](../israel-and-church/israels-regathering-and-refining.md#after-this-james-at-the-jerusalem-council)
  sets out the readings.
- **Ezekiel 36:33, 35.** "The waste places shall be rebuilt," and people will say, "This land that
  was desolate has become like the garden of Eden" (ESV).
- **Joel 2:25.** "I will restore to you the years that the swarming locust has eaten" (ESV), said
  first of the locust plague of Joel's own day (Joel 1:4).

Who does the building? Isaiah 61:4's "they" are the people the anointed one comforts, and Amos 9:14's
are Israel restored to the land. On the dispensational reading the mortal survivors do the labour;
that the risen saints direct it, as servants set "over ten cities" (Luke 19:17, ESV), is inferred.

### What God Himself repairs

Some of the repair no human hand could do. When His feet stand on the Mount of Olives, it "shall be
split in two from east to west by a very wide valley" (Zechariah 14:4, ESV); "living waters shall
flow out from Jerusalem" (Zechariah 14:8, ESV); the land around becomes a plain while "Jerusalem
shall remain aloft on its site" (Zechariah 14:10, ESV). Ezekiel's river turns the Dead Sea fresh:
"everything will live where the river goes" (Ezekiel 47:9, ESV); the river flows from his temple, which this site places in the
thousand years ([the temple Ezekiel saw](#the-temple-ezekiel-saw)).
"The desert shall rejoice and blossom like the crocus" (Isaiah 35:1, ESV).

### The restoring of all things and the new world

Peter names the whole work. Heaven must receive Jesus "until the time for restoring all the things
about which God spoke by the mouth of his holy prophets long ago" (Acts 3:21, ESV). "Restoring" is
ἀποκατάστασις (*apokatastasis*, ah-po-kah-TAH-stah-sis, G605), used only here in the New Testament;
its verb is the disciples' "will you at this time restore the kingdom to Israel?" (Acts 1:6, ESV).
Jesus' "new world" (Matthew 19:28, ESV) is παλιγγενεσία (*palingenesia*, pah-ling-geh-neh-SEE-ah,
G3824), "regeneration," a word Paul uses once more for the new birth (Titus 3:5). Peter ties the
restoring to the time God will "send the Christ appointed for you" (Acts 3:20, ESV), and Jesus dates
the new world to His throne and the Twelve judging Israel's tribes. So both begin at His return, in
the thousand years, and are completed when He says, "Behold, I am making all things new" (Revelation
21:5, ESV); [Heaven and Earth Will Pass Away](heaven-and-earth-will-pass-away.md) follows them to that
completion.

**This shows that God finishes what He starts.** The earth that sin ruined and judgment broke, He
restores, and He gives His people work to do in the restoring. So the faithfulness you show now in
small things, mending what is broken and serving where you are set, is training for the work He has
ready for you then (Luke 19:17).

## What the saints do

The table marks each task: what Scripture states, what is inferred, and whom the text says it of.

| Task | Text | Stated or inferred | Said of |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reign with Christ | Revelation 20:4, 6; 5:10; 2 Timothy 2:12 | Stated | The risen; the redeemed; those who endure |
| Judge | Revelation 20:4; 1 Corinthians 6:2-3; Matthew 19:28 | Stated that they judge; what cases, not stated | The saints; the Twelve, over Israel's tribes |
| Rule the nations | Revelation 2:26-27; 3:21 | Stated | The one who conquers |
| Govern cities | Luke 19:17-19 | Stated in a parable; that the cities belong to the thousand years is inferred | The faithful servants |
| Serve as priests | Revelation 20:6; 1:6 | Stated | Those in the first resurrection |
| Worship the King and keep Booths | Zechariah 14:16 | Stated of the surviving nations; of the risen saints, inferred | The nations |
| Teach the nations | Isaiah 2:3 | The LORD teaches, stated; the saints sharing it, inferred | The LORD |
| Build and plant | Isaiah 65:21-22 | Stated of "my people" in a world where death remains; of the risen saints, inferred | Israel in the land |

### Reign and judge

The elders and the living creatures sing it to the Lamb: "you have made them a kingdom and priests to our God, and
they shall reign on the earth" (Revelation 5:10, ESV). The manuscripts differ on the tense. NA28
prints βασιλεύσουσιν (*basileusousin*, bah-see-LEW-soo-sin, G936), "they shall reign," which the ESV
follows; the Westcott-Hort text and the SBLGNT print βασιλεύουσιν, "they reign." Both put the reign
"on the earth."

Jesus promised the Twelve "twelve thrones, judging the twelve tribes of Israel," "when the Son of Man
will sit on his glorious throne" (Matthew 19:28, ESV; compare Luke 22:29-30). "The saints will judge
the world" (1 Corinthians 6:2, ESV). [First Take the Log
Out](../christian-life/first-take-the-log-out.md#the-saints-who-will-judge-the-world-and-angels)
works through that judging and the κρίμα (*krima*, KREE-mah, G2917) of Revelation 20:4. To the one
who conquers Jesus gives "authority over the nations" (Revelation 2:26, ESV).

### Govern what He gives you

In the parable of the minas a nobleman goes away "to receive for himself a kingdom and then return"
(Luke 19:12, ESV). "When he returned, having received the kingdom," he calls his servants to account
(Luke 19:15, ESV). The first hears, "Well done, good servant! Because you have been faithful in a
very little, you shall have authority over ten cities," and the second is put "over five cities" (Luke 19:17, 19, ESV).

**The King rewards faithfulness with responsibility,** in proportion to what each did with what he
was given; "I will set you over much" (Matthew 25:21, ESV) says the same. That
the cities belong to the thousand years is inferred from the timing: the reward comes when the King
returns with His kingdom. It is handed out at [the judgment seat of Christ](judgment-seat-of-christ.md).

### Serve as priests, and worship

"They will be priests of God and of Christ" (Revelation 20:6, ESV) is stated of everyone in the first
resurrection. Israel was called to be "a kingdom of priests" (Exodus 19:6, ESV), and Jesus "made us a
kingdom, priests to his God and Father" (Revelation 1:6, ESV). Zechariah states that the nations will
come up to worship the King (Zechariah 14:16). That His risen people worship with them is inferred; a
multitude "from every nation" already worships before the throne (Revelation 7:9-10, ESV).

### Teach, build and plant

Isaiah 2:3 makes the LORD the teacher of the nations. That the saints teach with Him is inferred from
their priesthood: "people should seek instruction from his mouth" is said of a priest (Malachi 2:7,
ESV), and "instruction" there is *torah*, the word that goes out from Zion.

Isaiah 65:21-22 is stated: "They shall build houses and inhabit them; they shall plant vineyards and
eat their fruit... my chosen shall long enjoy the work of their hands" (ESV). The verses speak of "my
people" in a passage where they still bear children (Isaiah 65:23) and death still comes
(Isaiah 65:20). On the dispensational reading these are Israel's mortal survivors in the land, and
the risen saints' share in the building is an inference.

### Who lives in the kingdom

On the dispensational reading the kingdom has two kinds of people. The risen and the raptured reign,
judge and serve as priests with Christ, and the second death "has no power" over them (Revelation
20:6, ESV). Mortal survivors of the tribulation, from Israel and the nations, enter the kingdom (Matthew
25:31-34; Zechariah 14:16), bear children, plant and die. That is why Isaiah 65 promises long life,
and why Satan finds a multitude to deceive when he is released. No single passage sets this out; it
is how this reading fits the texts together.

## The temple Ezekiel saw

Ezekiel 40-48 describes a temple, a prince, Zadokite priests and offerings. The altar is consecrated
with a sin offering: "Thus you shall purify the altar and make atonement for it" (Ezekiel 43:20, ESV).
The prince provides offerings "to make atonement on behalf of the house of Israel" (Ezekiel 45:17,
ESV). Hebrews says, "Where there is forgiveness of these, there is no longer any offering for sin"
(Hebrews 10:18, ESV). This site reads Ezekiel 40-48 of the thousand years, as
[Tabernacles](../feasts/tabernacles.md), [True Worship](../christian-life/true-worship.md) and [The New
Jerusalem](new-jerusalem.md) do. What the offerings mean beside the cross is the open part. Four
answers are held:

1. **Memorials.** A temple built in the thousand years, whose offerings look back to the cross as the
   Lord's Supper does now. This answer has to account for "make atonement."
2. **Ceremonial cleansing.** A temple built in the thousand years, whose offerings cleanse mortal
   worshippers and the place where the LORD dwells among them, the "purification of the flesh"
   Hebrews 9:13 (ESV) assigns to the old offerings, with forgiveness resting on the cross alone.
3. **Fulfilled in Christ.** A vision drawn in the terms of Ezekiel's own worship and fulfilled in
   Christ, His people and the new Jerusalem: "I saw no temple in the city, for its temple is the Lord
   God the Almighty and the Lamb" (Revelation 21:22, ESV).
4. **A conditional plan.** A design offered to the returning exiles "if they are ashamed of all that
   they have done" (Ezekiel 43:11, ESV), which they never built.

The first two belong to this site's reading, and the text leans to the second. Ezekiel's "make
atonement" (כָּפַר, *kaphar*, kah-FAR, H3722) is said of the altar (Ezekiel 43:20) and of the house,
"for anyone who has sinned through error or ignorance" (Ezekiel 45:20, ESV): a cleansing of the place
where the LORD dwells among mortal people. That is a lean, and a memorial reading remains open.

The priesthood of Revelation 20:6 stands on its own text, whichever of these is right.

## What the reign shows about God

**God keeps His word to the earth.** He promised His Son the nations as His heritage (Psalm 2:8), and
the earth that crucified Jesus will see Him reign over it. **God keeps His word to Israel and the
nations.** The law goes out from Zion, the swords are beaten into plowshares, and the nations come up
to worship the King (Isaiah 2:3-4; Zechariah 14:16). **God keeps His word to you.** The One who
conquered sat down with His Father on His throne, and He will grant you to sit with Him on His
(Revelation 3:21).

So you may treat the "very little" in your hands now as the beginning of that trust (Luke 19:17),
"knowing that in the Lord your labor is not in vain" (1 Corinthians 15:58, ESV).

## Discussion questions

1. **The language.** Revelation 2:27's "he will rule them" (ESV) is ποιμανεῖ, the Septuagint's verb
   "shepherd," where the Hebrew of Psalm 2:9 reads "break them" (<span dir="rtl">תְּרֹעֵם</span>). What does each verb say
   about how Jesus will rule the nations?
2. **The text in its context.** John uses ἔζησαν, "came to life," in Revelation 20:4 and again in
   Revelation 20:5. How does reading the two side by side shape what you think the first resurrection
   is?
3. **Christ.** Jesus says, "I will grant him to sit with me on my throne, as I also conquered and sat
   down with my Father on his throne" (Revelation 3:21, ESV). What does it tell you about Him that He
   shares His throne?
4. **The hard part.** Jesus speaks of binding the strong man (Matthew 12:29), Revelation 20:3 binds
   Satan "so that he might not deceive the nations any longer" (ESV), and Peter says he "prowls around
   like a roaring lion" (1 Peter 5:8, ESV). How do you hold those three texts together?
5. **Be transformed.** The nobleman gives cities to the servant who was "faithful in a very little"
   (Luke 19:17, ESV). What is the very little in your hands this week, and what would faithfulness with it
   look like?

## Annex: what Jews and early Christians expected

!!! warning "Extra-biblical sources: useful, and not Scripture"

    This section quotes 1 Enoch, 2 Baruch, Irenaeus's report of Papias, Justin Martyr and
    Lactantius, and cites 2 Enoch. None
    of them is Scripture. "All Scripture is breathed out by God" (2 Timothy 3:16, ESV) is said of the
    canon and of nothing else — not of the pseudepigraphal books, and not of the church fathers,
    however early or however many of them agree.

    What these sources establish is what people believed, and when. That 1 Enoch expected the
    righteous to judge sinners and acquire houses in an eighth "week" is a fact about Judaism before
    Christ, and it bears on how John's first readers heard Revelation 20. It is not evidence of what
    the thousand years will be.

    So treat them as you would any human commentary: useful, capable of being wrong, and always
    answerable to Scripture rather than the reverse. Where they agree with the text they
    corroborate; where they conflict with it, the text governs and they are simply mistaken. Nothing
    this study says about the reign rests on them.

### 1 Enoch

1 Enoch's Book of the Watchers has the fallen angels bound and confined "till the day of their
judgement" (*1 Enoch* 10:12), and then the earth flourishes:

> And then shall the whole earth be tilled in righteousness, and shall all be planted with trees and
> be full of blessing. And all desirable trees shall be planted on it, and they shall plant vines on
> it: and the vine which they plant thereon shall yield wine in abundance, and as for all the seed
> which is sown thereon each measure (of it) shall bear a thousand.
> — *1 Enoch* 10:18-19, trans. R. H. Charles

"All nations shall offer adoration and shall praise Me, and all shall worship Me" (*1 Enoch* 10:21).
The Apocalypse of Weeks (*1 Enoch* 93:3-10; 91:12-17) divides history into ten weeks. In the eighth,
"a sword shall be given to it that a righteous judgement may be executed on the oppressors, And
sinners shall be delivered into the hands of the righteous. And at its close they shall acquire houses
through their righteousness, And a house shall be built for the Great King in glory for evermore"
(*1 Enoch* 91:12-13). The new heaven comes only in the tenth week (*1 Enoch* 91:15-16), which [Heaven
and Earth Will Pass Away](heaven-and-earth-will-pass-away.md#what-1-enoch-and-josephus-expected)
quotes.

### 2 Baruch and 2 Enoch

2 Baruch, usually dated after the temple fell in AD 70, expects the Messiah to "begin to be revealed"
(*2 Baruch* 29:3) and the earth to bear wildly:

> The earth also shall yield its fruit ten thousandfold, and one vine there shall be a thousand
> branches, and each branch shall produce a thousand clusters, and each cluster produce a thousand
> grapes, and each grape produce a cor of wine.
> — *2 Baruch* 29:5, trans. R. H. Charles

Then, "when the time of the advent of the Messiah is fulfilled, that He will return in glory, then all
who have fallen asleep in hope of Him shall rise again" (*2 Baruch* 30:1). 2 Enoch counts history in
seven thousands and an endless eighth (*2 Enoch* 33:1), quoted in [A Day Is a Thousand
Years](day-is-a-thousand-years.md).

### Papias, as Irenaeus reports him

Irenaeus of Lyons, writing late in the second century, read the promises of the kingdom as "the times
of the kingdom, when the righteous shall bear rule upon their rising from the dead" (*Against
Heresies* 5.33.3). He passes on a saying from "the elders who saw John, the disciple of the Lord":

> The days will come, in which vines shall grow, each having ten thousand branches, and in each branch
> ten thousand twigs, and in each true twig ten thousand shoots, and in each one of the shoots ten
> thousand clusters, and on every one of the clusters ten thousand grapes.
> — Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 5.33.3, *Ante-Nicene Fathers* vol. 1

His source is written: "Papias, the hearer of John, and a companion of Polycarp, in his fourth book"
(*Against Heresies* 5.33.4). Irenaeus then quotes Isaiah 11:6-9 and Isaiah 65:25, and records that
"some persons endeavour to refer these words to the case of savage men... who come to believe." He
allows that for converts now and applies the words to the animals as well "in the resurrection of
the just." So the figurative and the literal readings of the prophets were both alive in the second
century.

### Justin Martyr and Lactantius: a city rebuilt

Justin Martyr, writing in the middle of the second century, put the rebuilding at the centre: "I and
others, who are right-minded Christians on all points, are assured that there will be a resurrection
of the dead, and a thousand years in Jerusalem, which will then be built, adorned, and enlarged, [as]
the prophets Ezekiel and Isaiah and others declare" (*Dialogue with Trypho* 80, *Ante-Nicene
Fathers* vol. 1). Early in the fourth century Lactantius had the risen "preside over the living as
judges" through the thousand years, while "the earth will open its fruitfulness, and bring forth most
abundant fruits of its own accord" (*Divine Institutes* 7.24, *Ante-Nicene Fathers* vol. 7).

Set beside Revelation, these sources show how restrained John is. The multiplying vine of Papias is
close to 2 Baruch's. John gives no harvest figures at all. He gives thrones, a resurrection, priests
and a reign with Christ.

# Pride

Nebuchadnezzar was walking on the roof of his palace, looking out over the greatest city in the world.
"Is not this great Babylon, which I have built by my mighty power as a royal residence and for the
glory of my majesty?" (Daniel 4:30, ESV). The words were still in his mouth when a voice came from
heaven, and he lost his kingdom and his mind.

When his reason came back, he had learned one thing, and he wrote it down for the whole empire:
"those who walk in pride he is able to humble" (Daniel 4:37, ESV).

**In one sentence:** Pride is self-sufficiency, living as though everything you have is your own
doing, and at its root it wants God's place; it comes out of the heart, it deceives the one who has
it, and God sets Himself against it, so the way down is to humble yourself before Him and boast in
the cross of Jesus.

## Key Takeaways

*(This section follows the [Key Takeaways](../about/key-takeaways.md) format — see that page for what each part is for.)*

### Types & Prophecy

- **The day of the LORD.** Isaiah foresees a day when "the lofty pride of men shall be humbled, and
  the LORD alone will be exalted in that day" (Isaiah 2:11, ESV). Every proud thing ends there.
- **The man of lawlessness.** Pride's last and fullest form is a man "proclaiming himself to be God"
  (2 Thessalonians 2:4, ESV), whom the Lord Jesus will "bring to nothing by the appearance of his
  coming" (2 Thessalonians 2:8, ESV).
- **Babylon.** Nebuchadnezzar's fall (Daniel 4) and Isaiah's taunt over the king of Babylon (Isaiah
  14:4-21) set a pattern Scripture carries to the end: Babylon the great, who says in her heart
  "I sit as a queen" and falls in a single day (Revelation 18:7-8, ESV).

### Lessons about Jesus

- **Jesus reverses pride.** Mary sang it before He was born: God "has scattered the proud in the
  thoughts of their hearts; he has brought down the mighty from their thrones and exalted those of
  humble estate" (Luke 1:51-52, ESV).
- **Jesus' cross leaves pride nothing to stand on.** We are "justified by his grace as a gift,
  through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus" (Romans 3:24, ESV), so boasting "is excluded"
  (Romans 3:27, ESV).

> ✝️ Jeremiah 9:23-24 (ESV)

### Be Transformed

- **Think.** Your wisdom, strength and money are all received (1 Corinthians 4:7). Boast in knowing
  the LORD (Jeremiah 9:24).
- **Attitude.** Suspect yourself. Pride deceives the heart it lives in (Obadiah 1:3), so you will be
  the last to see it.
- **Do.** Ask someone close to you where they see pride in you. Thank them, and do not defend
  yourself.

### Prayer

just. Forgive us for the glory we have taken for ourselves, and for living as though we did not need
you. Search our hearts, and show us the pride we cannot see. Humble us under your mighty hand, and
teach us to boast only in the cross of your Son Jesus, who humbled Himself and died for us.

In Jesus' name. Amen.

## What pride is

Humility is dependence on God (see [In Humility](../christian-life/humility.md)). Pride is the
reverse. Moses warned Israel what it would sound like once they were settled and full:

> ✝️ Deuteronomy 8:14, 17 (ESV)
>
> 14 then your heart be lifted up, and you forget the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land
> of Egypt, out of the house of slavery, ... 17 Beware lest you say in your heart, 'My power and the
> might of my hand have gotten me this wealth.'

Nebuchadnezzar said exactly that on his roof: "which I have built by my mighty power" (Daniel 4:30,
ESV). Pride forgets the Giver and credits the self.

At its root, pride wants God's place. The serpent's offer to Eve was "you will be like God, knowing
good and evil" (Genesis 3:5, ESV). Eve was already made in God's image. The temptation was to have
God's place without God. Every later form of pride has that shape.

## What the words mean

### Height and presumption

The Hebrew word most often translated "pride" is <span dir="rtl">גָּאוֹן</span> (*gaʾon*, gah-OHN,
H1347), from a root meaning "to rise up." It occurs 49 times with several senses. Used of God, it is
His majesty: Isaiah 2:10 tells the proud to hide "from the splendor of his majesty," and the next verse
says "the lofty pride of men shall be humbled" (Isaiah 2:11, ESV). Used of a person set against God,
it is pride: "Pride goes before destruction" (Proverbs 16:18, ESV), and the king of Babylon's "pomp is
brought down to Sheol" (Isaiah 14:11, ESV). It can also be a good pride God gives, "the pride of Jacob
whom he loves" (Psalm 47:4, ESV), and even the thick growth along the Jordan (Jeremiah 12:5).

So the word itself describes something lifted high. Whether that is good depends on who lifted it.
God's majesty is His own. A person who lifts himself up takes a height that was never given.

Two more words fill out the picture. The second half of Proverbs 16:18 has
<span dir="rtl">גֹּבַהּ רוּחַ</span> (*govah ruach*, H1363), "height of spirit." And
<span dir="rtl">זָדוֹן</span> (*zadon*, H2087) is presumption, the pride that takes liberties. Obadiah
uses it for Edom: "The pride of your heart has deceived you" (Obadiah 1:3, ESV).

### Showing off, smoke and swelling

The New Testament has four pictures:

- **Showing yourself above.** ὑπερήφανος (*hyperēphanos*, G5244) is built from "above" and "to
  appear." Abbott-Smith glosses it "showing oneself above others." Its noun, ὑπερηφανία
  (*hyperēphania*, G5243), is the Septuagint's usual word for *gaʾon*, and it is in Jesus' list of
  what comes out of the heart (Mark 7:22), its only New Testament use.
- **Smoke.** τυφόω (*typhoō*, G5187) is built on the word for smoke. In use it means "to puff up,
  becloud with pride" (Abbott-Smith). Paul uses it of the new convert who should not be made an
  overseer (1 Timothy 3:6).
- **Swelling.** φυσιόω (*physioō*, G5448), "to puff up," from the word for a bellows. "Knowledge puffs
  up, but love builds up" (1 Corinthians 8:1, ESV).
- **Pretension.** ἀλαζονεία (*alazoneia*, G212), boastful self-assurance. James uses it of people who
  plan their year without God (James 4:16). John calls it "pride of life" (1 John 2:16, ESV).

## Where pride comes from

### The heart

Jesus puts pride inside:

> ✝️ Mark 7:21-23 (ESV)
>
> 21 For from within, out of the heart of man, come evil thoughts, sexual immorality, theft, murder,
> adultery, 22 coveting, wickedness, deceit, sensuality, envy, slander, pride, foolishness. 23 All
> these evil things come from within, and they defile a person."

Pride sits in the same list as theft and murder, and it comes from the same heart.

### Success

Moses expected pride to arrive with prosperity: "when you have eaten and are full and have built good
houses" (Deuteronomy 8:12, ESV). King Uzziah is the example. God helped him, and he grew strong. "But
when he was strong, he grew proud, to his destruction" (2 Chronicles 26:16, ESV). He walked into the
temple to burn incense, a priest's work, and left it a leper (2 Chronicles 26:19-21).

### Gifts and knowledge

Spiritual gifts and Bible knowledge are no protection. Corinth was rich in both, and Paul calls some
there "arrogant" (1 Corinthians 4:18, ESV), using *physioō*. He would not have a new convert made an
overseer, "or he may become puffed up with conceit and fall into the condemnation of the devil"
(1 Timothy 3:6, ESV).

## What pride looks like

### Six faces

Scripture gives pride faces you can recognise.

- **Comparison.** "God, I thank you that I am not like other men" (Luke 18:11, ESV). Jesus told the
  parable "to some who trusted in themselves that they were righteous, and treated others with
  contempt" (Luke 18:9, ESV). Pride needs someone below it.
- **Planning without God.** "Today or tomorrow we will go into such and such a town ... As it is, you
  boast in your arrogance" (James 4:13, 16, ESV). The fix James gives is one sentence: "If the Lord
  wills" (James 4:15, ESV).
- **Putting yourself first.** John names a church leader, "Diotrephes, who likes to put himself first"
  (3 John 1:9, ESV).
- **Taking God's glory.** The crowd shouted that Herod spoke with "the voice of a god," and he let them.
  "An angel of the Lord struck him down, because he did not give God the glory" (Acts 12:22-23, ESV).
- **Contempt in the eyes.** The first of seven things the LORD hates is "haughty eyes" (Proverbs
  6:16-17, ESV). Pride looks down.
- **Religious show.** Pride can dress as humility. At Colossae, a man practising self-denial was
  "puffed up without reason" (Colossians 2:18, ESV). See [In Humility](../christian-life/humility.md#humility-for-show).

### Pride deceives its owner

And pride deceives its owner. Edom lived in the high rocks and said, "Who will bring me down to the
ground?" (Obadiah 1:3, ESV). God answered: "from there I will bring you down" (Obadiah 1:4, ESV).
Edom's pride was the reason Edom could not see what was coming.

## "You are gods"

### Men who claimed to be gods

Scripture records people claiming to be divine, or letting others call them so, and it answers every
one.

- **The prince of Tyre** said, "I am a god." God's reply: "yet you are but a man, and no god"
  (Ezekiel 28:2, ESV).
- **Herod** accepted "the voice of a god" and was struck down (Acts 12:22-23).
- **The man of lawlessness** will take "his seat in the temple of God, proclaiming himself to be God"
  (2 Thessalonians 2:4, ESV).

The apostles show the humble answer. At Lystra the crowd cried, "The gods have come down to us in the
likeness of men!" and brought oxen to sacrifice. Paul and Barnabas tore their clothes and ran into the
crowd: "Men, why are you doing these things? We also are men, of like nature with you" (Acts
14:11, 15, ESV).

### Three verses read as "little gods"

Some teach today that believers are "little gods," usually from three verses. Read in context, none
of them says so.

- **Psalm 82:6.** "I said, 'You are gods, sons of the Most High, all of you'" (ESV). The next verse is
  a sentence of death: "nevertheless, like men you shall die" (Psalm 82:7, ESV). The "gods" are judged
  for judging unjustly (Psalm 82:2-4). **Who they are is contested:** the *ESV Study Bible* and the
  *NIV Biblical Theology Study Bible* take them as human rulers; the *NIV Cultural Backgrounds Study
  Bible* sees heavenly beings with human rulers as their agents. On either reading the psalm condemns
  them.
- **John 10:34.** Jesus quoted Psalm 82:6 when He was accused, "you, being a man, make yourself God"
  (John 10:33, ESV). He argued from the lesser to the greater: if Scripture could call those judges
  "gods," He was not blaspheming when He said, "I am the Son of God" (John 10:36, ESV). He was
  defending His own unique sonship.
- **2 Peter 1:4.** Believers "become partakers of the divine nature" (ESV). The verse goes on to say
  how: "having escaped from the corruption that is in the world." The *ESV Study Bible* says believers
  "never become part of God" but share His character as they become like Him (note on 2 Peter 1:4).

The believer's glory is real. You are God's child and will be like Christ (1 John 3:2). It is given,
and it is always creature-glory. A teaching that makes you a god has returned to the serpent's offer
in Genesis 3:5.

## Did Satan fall through pride?

Many Christians have read two Old Testament poems as the story of Satan's fall: Isaiah 14:12-15 and
Ezekiel 28:12-17. **This reading is contested.** It is old and widely held, and the passages
themselves name human kings.

### The two passages

Isaiah 14 is "this taunt against the king of Babylon" (Isaiah 14:4, ESV). Its centre is a boast:

> ✝️ Isaiah 14:12-14 (ESV)
>
> 12 "How you are fallen from heaven, O Day Star, son of Dawn! How you are cut down to the ground, you
> who laid the nations low! 13 You said in your heart, 'I will ascend to heaven; above the stars of
> God I will set my throne on high; I will sit on the mount of assembly in the far reaches of the
> north; 14 I will ascend above the heights of the clouds; I will make myself like the Most High.'

"Day Star" is <span dir="rtl">הֵילֵל</span> (*heilel*, H1966), "shining one," found only here. The
Latin Vulgate rendered it *lucifer*, "light-bringer," the old name for the morning star, and the KJV
kept "Lucifer."

Ezekiel 28 has two oracles about Tyre. The first is to "the prince of Tyre," who says, "I am a god"
(Ezekiel 28:2, ESV). The second is a lament over "the king of Tyre" (Ezekiel 28:12), who was "in Eden,
the garden of God," was "an anointed guardian cherub," and was "blameless in your ways from the day
you were created, till unrighteousness was found in you" (Ezekiel 28:13-15, ESV). Then: "Your heart
was proud because of your beauty" (Ezekiel 28:17, ESV). The verb is <span dir="rtl">גָּבַהּ</span>
(*gavah*, H1361), "to be high."

### The case for reading them of Satan

- **The language goes beyond a man.** A human king was not in Eden, was not a cherub, and was not
  blameless from the day he was created. Origen made this argument about Ezekiel 28: "what fiery
  stones can he imagine in the midst of which any man could live?" (*De Principiis* 1.5.4, ANF 4,
  from Rufinus's Latin). He concluded it spoke of an angel set over Tyre, a fallen power. Of Isaiah 14
  he went further and read it of the devil, pairing it with Luke 10:18 (*De Principiis* 1.5.5).
- **It is old.** Tertullian, early in the third century, read Ezekiel 28 of the devil: "in the person
  of the prince of Tyre it is said in reference to the devil" (*Against Marcion* 2.10, ANF 3).
- **Modern commentators hold it too.** The *NLT Life Application Study Bible* sets out a reading in
  which both a human king and Satan are in view, and says "Pride was Satan's sin as well as Babylon's"
  (note on Isaiah 14:12-14; compare its note on Ezekiel 28:12-19).
- **Jesus used similar words.** "I saw Satan fall like lightning from heaven" (Luke 10:18, ESV). The
  Greek Isaiah 14:12 also has a fall "from heaven," and the *ESV Study Bible* points to Isaiah 14:12
  at Luke 10:18. Three verses earlier Jesus said of Capernaum, "will you be exalted to heaven? You
  shall be brought down to Hades" (Luke 10:15, ESV), the two places of Isaiah 14:13 and 14:15. So
  Isaiah 14 is likely in view in Luke 10, and Jesus applies it both to a city and to Satan. This
  study's cross-reference tools find no quotation, so treat it as an echo.
- **Scripture does join conceit and the devil.** The new convert "puffed up with conceit" falls "into
  the condemnation of the devil" (1 Timothy 3:6, ESV). The most natural reading is the condemnation
  the devil himself fell under, which implies his sin was conceit. The *NIV Biblical Theology Study
  Bible* notes it could instead mean being handed over to Satan (note on 1 Timothy 3:6).

### The case for reading them of human kings

- **Each passage names a man.** Isaiah 14 is addressed to the king of Babylon, and the onlookers say,
  "Is this the man who made the earth tremble?" (Isaiah 14:16, ESV). The first Tyre oracle tells the
  prince, "you are but a man, and no god" (Ezekiel 28:2, ESV).
- **Eden may point to Adam.** The *ESV Study Bible* says the king of Tyre is likened to "a second
  Adam, clearly a created being," set in the garden and fallen from it (note on Ezekiel 28:11-19).
  That answers "in Eden" and "blameless from the day you were created" without an angel.
- **"Cherub" is a vowel question.** The Hebrew consonants of Ezekiel 28:14 can be read "you were an
  anointed cherub" or "with an anointed cherub." The Septuagint reads "with the cherub" (μετὰ τοῦ
  χερουβ), and the CSB's footnote gives "Or *With* an anointed guardian cherub." On that reading the
  king is placed beside a cherub.
- **The imagery was familiar.** The *NIV Cultural Backgrounds Study Bible* notes that *heilel* is
  probably Venus, the morning star, which appears in Ugaritic myth. The *ESV Study Bible* notes that
  in Canaanite myth the gods met on a mountain in the north (note on Isaiah 14:12-15). Prophets used
  their neighbours' pictures to mock their neighbours' kings.
- **Commentators hold this too.** The *NIV Biblical Theology Study Bible* says Isaiah 14 "argues for a
  continued reference to creaturely pride," while allowing that since Satan is a creature, applying
  the words to him "is not inappropriate" (note on Isaiah 14:12-15). The *ESV Study Bible* reports
  that "some" see an allusion to Satan in each passage, and commits only to the king's own arrogance
  (notes on Isaiah 14:12-15 and Ezekiel 28:11-19).

### Where that leaves the reader

The passages are first about two human kings, and the words are theirs. The language reaches past
them, Jesus seems to echo Isaiah 14 of Satan's fall, and the church has read a spiritual power
behind them since the early third century. That reading is possible, and the texts themselves do not
establish it, so no doctrine should rest on it alone. What Scripture says plainly is enough: the
serpent's temptation was "you will be like God" (Genesis 3:5), the conceited man falls into "the
condemnation of the devil" (1 Timothy 3:6), and "that ancient serpent, who is called the devil and
Satan" will be thrown down (Revelation 12:9, ESV). The proud person walks the devil's road.

## Why God opposes the proud

### His glory, and pride's end

"God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble" (James 4:6, ESV), quoting Proverbs 3:34.
"Everyone who is arrogant in heart is an abomination to the LORD" (Proverbs 16:5, ESV).

The proud person claims a height that belongs to God, and so he claims something untrue. God guards
His glory, and in guarding it He guards the truth. It is also His kindness, because pride destroys
the one who holds it: "One's pride will bring him low" (Proverbs 29:23, ESV). The day of the LORD will
finish the work: "the LORD alone will be exalted in that day" (Isaiah 2:11, ESV).

### Mercy to the proud who turn

God opposes the proud, and He also receives them when they come down. Hezekiah's heart grew proud
after God healed him, "But Hezekiah humbled himself for the pride of his heart ... so that the wrath of
the LORD did not come upon them in the days of Hezekiah" (2 Chronicles 32:26, ESV). Nebuchadnezzar
lifted his eyes to heaven, and God gave him back his reason and his kingdom (Daniel 4:34, 36). The
tax collector who beat his breast "went down to his house justified" (Luke 18:14, ESV).

## The way down

The answer to pride is a different thing to boast in.

> ✝️ 1 Corinthians 1:28-31 (ESV)
>
> 28 God chose what is low and despised in the world, even things that are not, to bring to nothing
> things that are, 29 so that no human being might boast in the presence of God. 30 And because of
> him you are in Christ Jesus, who became to us wisdom from God, righteousness and sanctification and
> redemption, 31 so that, as it is written, "Let the one who boasts, boast in the Lord."

At the cross Jesus bore the sin of the proud and paid for it, and God justifies the ungodly as a
gift. That is why "our boasting" is "excluded" (Romans 3:27, ESV): we are "justified by his grace as a
gift, through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus" (Romans 3:24, ESV). Nothing in your salvation is
your own doing, so pride has nothing left to stand on. And everything you need, you have "in Christ
Jesus" (1 Corinthians 1:30), so you are free to boast in Him: "far be it from me to boast except in
the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ" (Galatians 6:14, ESV).

The practice is humility: humble yourself before the Lord, and He will lift you up (James 4:10). The
fuller study of it, and of Jesus who humbled Himself to the cross, is [In
Humility](../christian-life/humility.md).

## Discussion questions

1. **The language.** *Gaʾon* can mean God's majesty or human pride, depending on who is lifted up.
   What does that tell you about what pride is?
2. **The text in its context.** Uzziah grew proud "when he was strong" (2 Chronicles 26:16), and
   Israel was warned about pride when they were full (Deuteronomy 8:12-14). Why is success more
   dangerous than hardship?
3. **Christ.** Mary sang that God scatters the proud and exalts the humble (Luke 1:51-52). Where did
   Jesus do that in the Gospels?
4. **The hard part.** Isaiah 14 and Ezekiel 28 name human kings, and the church has long read Satan
   behind them. What is gained and what is risked by each reading?
5. **Recognising it.** Which of the six faces of pride in this study are you least likely to see in
   yourself, and why?
6. **Be transformed.** What would it mean for you to "boast in the Lord" (1 Corinthians 1:31) this week
   about something you would normally take credit for?

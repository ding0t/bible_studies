# Pride

Nebuchadnezzar was walking on the roof of his palace, looking out over the greatest city in the world.
"Is not this great Babylon, which I have built by my mighty power as a royal residence and for the
glory of my majesty?" (Daniel 4:30, ESV). The words were still in his mouth when a voice came from
heaven, and he lost his kingdom and his mind.

When his reason came back, he had learned one thing, and he wrote it down for the whole empire:
"those who walk in pride he is able to humble" (Daniel 4:37, ESV).

**In one sentence:** Pride is claiming for yourself the height and glory that belong to God; it comes
out of the heart, it blinds the one who has it, and God sets Himself against it, so the only way down
from it is to humble yourself before Him and boast in the cross of Jesus.

## Key Takeaways

*(This section follows the [Key Takeaways](../about/key-takeaways.md) format — see that page for what each part is for.)*

### Types & Prophecy

- **The day of the LORD.** Isaiah foresees a day when "the lofty pride of men shall be humbled, and
  the LORD alone will be exalted in that day" (Isaiah 2:11, ESV). Every proud thing ends there.
- **Babylon.** Isaiah's taunt over the king of Babylon (Isaiah 14:4-21) and Nebuchadnezzar's own
  fall (Daniel 4) set a pattern Scripture carries to the end: Babylon the great, who says in her
  heart "I sit as a queen" and falls in a single day (Revelation 18:7-8).

### Lessons about Jesus

- **Jesus reverses pride.** Mary sang it before He was born: God "has scattered the proud in the
  thoughts of their hearts; he has brought down the mighty from their thrones and exalted those of
  humble estate" (Luke 1:51-52, ESV).
- **Jesus is the one thing worth boasting in.** "Far be it from me to boast except in the cross of our
  Lord Jesus Christ" (Galatians 6:14, ESV).

### Memory verses

> ✝️ Proverbs 16:18 (ESV)
>
> 18 Pride goes before destruction, and a haughty spirit before a fall.

> ✝️ Jeremiah 9:23-24 (ESV)
>
> 23 Thus says the LORD: "Let not the wise man boast in his wisdom, let not the mighty man boast in
> his might, let not the rich man boast in his riches, 24 but let him who boasts boast in this, that
> he understands and knows me, that I am the LORD who practices steadfast love, justice, and
> righteousness in the earth. For in these things I delight, declares the LORD."

### Be Transformed

- **Think.** Your wisdom, strength and money are all received (1 Corinthians 4:7). Boast in knowing
  the LORD (Jeremiah 9:24).
- **Attitude.** Suspect yourself. Pride deceives the heart it lives in (Obadiah 1:3), so you will be
  the last to see it.
- **Do.** Ask someone close to you where they see pride in you. Thank them, and do not defend
  yourself.

### Prayer

LORD, yours is the majesty. You are the King of heaven; all your works are right and your ways are
just. Forgive us for the glory we have taken for ourselves. Search our hearts, and show us the pride we
cannot see. Humble us under your mighty hand, and teach us to boast only in the cross of your Son
Jesus, who humbled Himself for us.

In Jesus' name. Amen.

## What the words mean

### Majesty in the wrong hands

The commonest Hebrew word for pride is <span dir="rtl">גָּאוֹן</span> (*gaʾon*, gah-OHN, H1347). It
occurs 49 times, and it means two things. Said of God, it is His majesty: Moses sang, "In the
greatness of your majesty you overthrow your adversaries" (Exodus 15:7, ESV). Said of a person, it is
pride: "Pride goes before destruction" (Proverbs 16:18, ESV). It is the same word.

So Scripture's vocabulary defines pride: majesty in the wrong hands. It belongs to God. A proud
person claims it for themselves. Wisdom says of it, "Pride and arrogance and the way of evil and
perverted speech I hate" (Proverbs 8:13, ESV).

Two more words fill out the picture. The second half of Proverbs 16:18 has
<span dir="rtl">גֹּבַהּ רוּחַ</span> (*govah ruach*, H1363), "height of spirit": the proud person has
made themselves tall. And <span dir="rtl">זָדוֹן</span> (*zadon*, H2087) is presumption, the pride
that takes liberties. Obadiah uses it for Edom: "The pride of your heart has deceived you" (Obadiah
1:3, ESV).

### Showing off, smoke and swelling

The New Testament has four pictures:

- **Showing yourself above.** ὑπερήφανος (*hyperēphanos*, G5244) is built from "above" and "to
  appear." Abbott-Smith glosses it "showing oneself above others." The noun ὑπερηφανία
  (*hyperēphania*, G5243) is in Jesus' list of what comes out of the heart (Mark 7:22), its only
  New Testament use.
- **Smoke.** τυφόω (*typhoō*, G5187) comes from the word for smoke. Abbott-Smith: "to wrap in
  smoke," "becloud with pride." The proud person cannot see clearly. It is used of the new convert
  who should not be made an overseer (1 Timothy 3:6).
- **Swelling.** φυσιόω (*physioō*, G5448), "to puff up," from the word for a bellows. "Knowledge puffs
  up, but love builds up" (1 Corinthians 8:1, ESV).
- **Pretension.** ἀλαζονεία (*alazoneia*, G212), boastful self-assurance. James uses it of people who
  plan their year without God (James 4:16). John calls it "pride of life" (1 John 2:16, ESV).

## Where pride comes from

### The heart

Jesus puts pride inside:

> ✝️ Mark 7:21-23 (ESV)
>
> 21 For from within, out of the heart of man, come evil thoughts, sexual immorality, theft, murder,
> adultery, 22 coveting, wickedness, deceit, sensuality, envy, slander, pride, foolishness. 23 All
> these evil things come from within, and they defile a person."

Pride sits in the same list as theft and murder. It comes from the same heart.

### The first temptation

The serpent's offer to Eve was pride in its purest form: "you will be like God, knowing good and
evil" (Genesis 3:5, ESV). Eve was already made in God's image. The temptation was to have God's place
without God. Every later form of pride has that shape.

### Success

Moses warned Israel that pride would come when life was good: "when you have eaten and are full and
have built good houses ... then your heart be lifted up, and you forget the LORD your God"
(Deuteronomy 8:12, 14, ESV). The proud heart says, "My power and the might of my hand have gotten me
this wealth" (Deuteronomy 8:17, ESV).

King Uzziah is the example. God helped him, and he grew strong. "But when he was strong, he grew
proud, to his destruction" (2 Chronicles 26:16, ESV). He walked into the temple to burn incense, a
priest's work, and left it a leper.

### Gifts and knowledge

Spiritual gifts and Bible knowledge are no protection. Corinth was rich in both and was "puffed up"
(1 Corinthians 4:18; 5:2). Paul would not have a new convert made an overseer, "or he may become
puffed up with conceit and fall into the condemnation of the devil" (1 Timothy 3:6, ESV).

## What pride looks like

Pride is hard to see in yourself, because it wraps you in smoke (1 Timothy 3:6). Scripture gives it
faces you can recognise.

- **Comparison.** "God, I thank you that I am not like other men" (Luke 18:11, ESV). Jesus told the
  parable "to some who trusted in themselves that they were righteous, and treated others with
  contempt" (Luke 18:9, ESV). Pride needs someone below it.
- **Planning without God.** "Today or tomorrow we will go into such and such a town ... As it is, you
  boast in your arrogance" (James 4:13, 16, ESV). The fix James gives is one sentence: "If the Lord
  wills" (James 4:15, ESV).
- **Putting yourself first.** John names a church leader, "Diotrephes, who likes to put himself first"
  (3 John 1:9, ESV).
- **Taking God's glory.** The crowd shouted that Herod spoke with "the voice of a god," and he let them.
  "An angel of the Lord struck him down, because he did not give God the glory" (Acts 12:22-23, ESV).
- **Contempt in the eyes.** The first of seven things the LORD hates is "haughty eyes" (Proverbs
  6:16-17, ESV). Pride looks down.
- **Religious show.** Pride can dress as humility. At Colossae, a man practising self-denial was
  "puffed up without reason" (Colossians 2:18, ESV). See [In Humility](../christian-life/humility.md#humility-for-show).

And pride blinds its owner. Edom lived in the high rocks and said, "Who will bring me down to the
ground?" (Obadiah 1:3, ESV). God answered: "from there I will bring you down" (Obadiah 1:4, ESV). The
pride that deceived Edom was the reason Edom could not see what was coming.

## Why God opposes the proud

### His glory, and pride's end

"God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble" (James 4:6, ESV). "Everyone who is arrogant in
heart is an abomination to the LORD" (Proverbs 16:5, ESV).

God opposes pride because pride is a lie. The majesty belongs to Him (Exodus 15:7), and a creature
who claims it is claiming what is not true. So this shows that God guards His glory, and in guarding
it He guards the truth. It also shows His kindness, because pride destroys the one who holds it:
"One's pride will bring him low" (Proverbs 29:23, ESV). The day of the LORD will finish the work:
"the LORD alone will be exalted in that day" (Isaiah 2:11, ESV).

### Mercy to the proud who turn

God opposes the proud, and He also receives them when they come down. Hezekiah's heart grew proud
after God healed him, "But Hezekiah humbled himself for the pride of his heart ... so that the wrath of
the LORD did not come upon them in the days of Hezekiah" (2 Chronicles 32:26, ESV). Nebuchadnezzar
lifted his eyes to heaven, and God gave him back his reason and his kingdom (Daniel 4:34, 36). The
tax collector who beat his breast "went down to his house justified" (Luke 18:14, ESV).

## Did Satan fall through pride?

Many Christians have read two Old Testament poems as the story of Satan's fall: Isaiah 14:12-15 and
Ezekiel 28:12-17. **This reading is contested, and good commentators hold it on both sides.**

### The two passages

Isaiah 14 is "this taunt against the king of Babylon" (Isaiah 14:4, ESV). Its centre is a boast:

> ✝️ Isaiah 14:12-14 (ESV)
>
> 12 "How you are fallen from heaven, O Day Star, son of Dawn! How you are cut down to the ground, you
> who laid the nations low! 13 You said in your heart, 'I will ascend to heaven; above the stars of
> God I will set my throne on high; I will sit on the mount of assembly in the far reaches of the
> north; 14 I will ascend above the heights of the clouds; I will make myself like the Most High.'

"Day Star" is <span dir="rtl">הֵילֵל</span> (*heilel*, H1966), "shining one," found only here. The
Latin Vulgate rendered it *lucifer*, "light-bringer," the old name for the morning star, and the KJV
kept "Lucifer."

Ezekiel 28 has two oracles about Tyre. The first is to "the prince of Tyre," whose heart is proud and
who says, "I am a god" (Ezekiel 28:2, ESV). The second is a lament over "the king of Tyre" (Ezekiel
28:12), who was "in Eden, the garden of God," was "an anointed guardian cherub," and was "blameless
in your ways from the day you were created, till unrighteousness was found in you" (Ezekiel
28:13-15, ESV). Then: "Your heart was proud because of your beauty" (Ezekiel 28:17, ESV). The verb is
<span dir="rtl">גָּבַהּ</span> (*gavah*, H1361), "to be high."

### The case for reading them of Satan

- **The language goes beyond a man.** A human king was not in Eden, was not a cherub, and was not
  blameless from the day he was created. Origen made exactly this argument about Ezekiel 28: "what
  fiery stones can he imagine in the midst of which any man could live?" (*De Principiis* 1.5.4, ANF
  4). He read Isaiah 14 the same way (*De Principiis* 1.5.5).
- **It is old.** Tertullian read Ezekiel 28 of the devil around AD 207: "in the person of the prince of
  Tyre it is said in reference to the devil" (*Against Marcion* 2.10, ANF 3).
- **Jesus used similar words.** "I saw Satan fall like lightning from heaven" (Luke 10:18, ESV). The
  Greek Isaiah 14:12 also has a fall "from heaven" (ἐκ τοῦ οὐρανοῦ). The *ESV Study Bible* points to
  Isaiah 14:12 at Luke 10:18. The shared words are few, and this study's cross-reference tools find no
  quotation, so this is an echo at most.
- **Scripture does join pride and the devil.** The new convert who becomes "puffed up with conceit"
  falls "into the condemnation of the devil" (1 Timothy 3:6, ESV). The most natural reading is the
  condemnation the devil himself fell under, which implies his sin was conceit. (The NIV Biblical
  Theology Study Bible notes the phrase could also mean being handed over to Satan.)

### The case for reading them of human kings

- **Each passage names a man.** Isaiah 14 is addressed to the king of Babylon, and the onlookers say,
  "Is this the man who made the earth tremble?" (Isaiah 14:16, ESV). Ezekiel tells the prince of Tyre,
  "you are but a man, and no god" (Ezekiel 28:2, ESV).
- **The imagery was familiar.** The *NIV Cultural Backgrounds Study Bible* notes that *heilel* is
  probably Venus, the morning star, which appears in Ugaritic myth. The *ESV Study Bible* notes that
  in Canaanite myth the gods met on a mountain in the north (note on Isaiah 14:12-15). Prophets used
  their neighbours' pictures to mock their neighbours' kings.
- **The context never leaves the nations.** The *NIV Biblical Theology Study Bible* says Isaiah 14
  "argues for a continued reference to creaturely pride" (note on Isaiah 14:12-15).

### Where that leaves the reader

The *ESV Study Bible* takes a middle path on both passages: the proud king may be likened, as a poetic
allusion, to the fall of Satan (notes on Isaiah 14:12-15 and Ezekiel 28:11-19). The *NIV Biblical
Theology Study Bible* says that since Satan is a creature, applying these descriptions to him "is not
inappropriate."

This study holds the same. The passages are first about two human kings, and the words are theirs.
The language reaches past them, and the church has read a spiritual power behind them since the
second century. That reading is possible, and it is not established by the texts themselves, so no
doctrine should rest on it. What Scripture does say plainly is enough: the serpent's temptation was
"you will be like God" (Genesis 3:5), conceit is "the condemnation of the devil" (1 Timothy 3:6), and
"that ancient serpent, who is called the devil and Satan" will be thrown down (Revelation 12:9, ESV).
On the most natural reading of 1 Timothy 3:6, pride is the devil's own sin, and the proud person is
walking his road.

## The way down

The answer to pride is a different thing to boast in.

> ✝️ 1 Corinthians 1:28-31 (ESV)
>
> 28 God chose what is low and despised in the world, even things that are not, to bring to nothing
> things that are, 29 so that no human being might boast in the presence of God. 30 And because of
> him you are in Christ Jesus, who became to us wisdom from God, righteousness and sanctification and
> redemption, 31 so that, as it is written, "Let the one who boasts, boast in the Lord."

The gospel leaves pride nothing to stand on. "What becomes of our boasting? It is excluded" (Romans
3:27, ESV), because salvation is received by faith and earned by nobody. Everything you need, you have
"in Christ Jesus" (1 Corinthians 1:30). So you are free to boast in Him, and in His cross (Galatians
6:14).

The practice is humility: humble yourself before the Lord, and He will lift you up (James 4:10). The
fuller study of it, and of Jesus who humbled Himself to the cross, is [In
Humility](../christian-life/humility.md).

## Discussion questions

1. **The language.** The same Hebrew word, *gaʾon*, means God's majesty and human pride. What does
   that tell you about what pride is?
2. **The text in its context.** Uzziah grew proud "when he was strong" (2 Chronicles 26:16), and
   Israel was warned about pride when they were full (Deuteronomy 8:12-14). Why is success more
   dangerous than hardship?
3. **Christ.** Mary sang that God scatters the proud and exalts the humble (Luke 1:51-52). Where did
   Jesus do that in the Gospels?
4. **The hard part.** Isaiah 14 and Ezekiel 28 name human kings, and the church has long read Satan
   behind them. What is gained and what is risked by each reading?
5. **Recognising it.** Which of the six faces of pride in this study are you least likely to see in
   yourself, and why?
6. **Be transformed.** What would it mean for you to "boast in the Lord" (1 Corinthians 1:31) this week
   about something you would normally take credit for?

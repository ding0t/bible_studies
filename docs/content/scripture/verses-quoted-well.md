# Verses Quoted Well

Some verses can be lifted out of their chapters and keep their meaning. A child who learns John 3:16
on its own learns it right. Quoted to open a debate about origins, Genesis 1:1 means there what it
means in Genesis. Hebrews 11:1 is quoted as the Bible's definition of faith, and that is what its author
wrote it to be. Each of these three sentences is the thesis of its own paragraph, which is why it
travels. Reading each back in its paragraph adds depth: the God who made
everything made it through His Son, gave that Son for a world in rebellion, and keeps every promise
faith leans on.

**In one sentence:** Genesis 1:1, John 3:16 and Hebrews 11:1 survive being quoted alone because each
states its paragraph's point in one sentence — God made everything, God gave His only Son for the
world, and faith is the assurance of what God has promised — and read in their paragraphs they show
you more of the same God, so you can trust Him with all three.

### Lessons about Jesus

- **Jesus is the Word through whom God made everything.** John opens his Gospel with Genesis' first
  words: "In the beginning was the Word ... All things were made through him" (John 1:1, 3, ESV).
- **Jesus is the only Son God gave.** μονογενής (*monogenēs*, G3439) marks Him as the one Son of His
  kind (John 1:14, 18; 3:16, 18).
- **Jesus is the one lifted up.** John 3:16 explains the serpent Moses raised: look to Jesus and
  live (John 3:14-15).
- **Jesus finishes the faith He starts.** He is "the founder and perfecter of our faith" (Hebrews
  12:2, ESV).

## Why these travel well

A verse keeps its meaning when quoted alone if it states the point of its own paragraph. A verse
that is a step in an argument, or the second half of a condition, changes meaning when it is cut
loose: John 8:32's "the truth will set you free" needs verse 31's "if you abide in my word."
Each of these three still says what it means when nothing before it is read. Genesis 1:1 is the Bible's opening sentence;
John 3:16 states, in one sentence, why the Son of Man had to be lifted up; Hebrews 11:1 defines the
faith that the rest of the chapter illustrates.

So the question for each is what its context adds. The companion page, [Verses Often
Misquoted](verses-often-misquoted.md), works through six verses that changed meaning when they were
cut loose.

## Genesis 1:1: "In the beginning, God created"

> ✝️ Genesis 1:1-3 (ESV)
>
> 1 In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth. 2 The earth was without form and void,
> and darkness was over the face of the deep. And the Spirit of God was hovering over the face of the
> waters. 3 And God said, "Let there be light," and there was light.

**Why it is quoted well.** It is used for what it is: the claim that God made everything there is.
The [Statement of Faith](../about/statement-of-faith.md) takes it as a historical claim, and the
verse itself puts the whole universe under one Maker.

**The words.** In Hebrew, seven words:

<span dir="rtl">בְּרֵאשִׁית בָּרָא אֱלֹהִים אֵת הַשָּׁמַיִם וְאֵת הָאָרֶץ</span>

*bereshit bara Elohim et hashamayim ve'et ha'arets* — bə-reh-SHEET bah-RAH eh-loh-HEEM et
hah-shah-MAH-yim veh-ET hah-AH-rets.

- <span dir="rtl">בָּרָא</span> (*bara*, H1254, "create"). The *ESV Study Bible* notes that in the
  Old Testament this verb always has God as its subject, and that it stresses His sovereignty and
  power.
- <span dir="rtl">אֱלֹהִים</span> (*Elohim*, H430, "God"). The noun is plural in form and its verb
  *bara* is singular. The *ESV Study Bible* suggests the plural may express majesty, and reads
  the singular verb as marking God as one being. Some Christian readers have also seen a hint of plurality
  within the one God here; that reading is contested, and the verse's own grammar says one God acts.
- "The heavens and the earth" pairs the two extremes to mean everything that exists.

**What the context adds.** Verse 1 can be read as a heading over the whole chapter, or as the first
event, when God brought matter, space and time into being. The *ESV Study Bible* gives both and
favours the second, since the New Testament says creation came from nothing:
"what is seen was not made out of things that are visible" (Hebrews 11:3, ESV), and "you created all
things, and by your will they existed and were created" (Revelation 4:11, ESV). Some translations footnote a third reading, "When God began to create"
(NLT note), which makes verse 1 a clause leading into verse 2. The Hebrew tradition marks
*bereshit* as an absolute noun, which supports the ESV's complete sentence, and that is how this
study reads it. Verse 2 brings in
the Spirit of God, and verse 3 the word God speaks. John's Gospel opens with the same words, "In the
beginning", and names that Word: "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God ...
All things were made through him" (John 1:1, 3, ESV). Psalm 33:6 says it in Hebrew poetry: "By the
word of the LORD the heavens were made, and by the breath of his mouth all their host" (ESV). So the first verse of the Bible shows you a God who
owes His existence to nothing, made everything else, and made it through His Son and by His Spirit.
The world you live in was made on purpose, by Someone who can be known.

**Keywords.** Creation, creation from nothing, Creator, sovereignty of God, the Word, Trinity
(contested in this verse).

[Six Days of History](../last-things/six-days-of-history.md).

## John 3:16: "For God so loved the world"

> ✝️ John 3:14-16 (ESV)
>
> 14 And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up,
> 15 that whoever believes in him may have eternal life. 16 "For God so loved the world, that he gave
> his only Son, that whoever believes in him should not perish but have eternal life.

**Why it is quoted well.** It holds the whole gospel in one sentence: who loves, whom He loves, what
He gave, what you do, and what you receive. The *ESV Study Bible* calls it "the most famous summary
of the gospel in the entire Bible."

**The words.**

- Οὕτως (*houtōs*, G3779, "thus, in this way"). The ESV's "so" can be read as degree ("so much") or
  as manner ("in this way"). Translators divide too: the CSB and LSB footnote "in this way", and the NLT footnotes "so much".
  The *NIV Biblical Theology Study Bible* holds both, "In this way," emphasizing "the intensity of
  God's love." The manner reading ties verse 16 to
  the serpent of verse 14: God loved the world in this way, by lifting up His Son. The degree reading
  hears the size of the love. Both put the giving of the Son at the centre, and the clause that
  follows, ὥστε (*hōste*, G5620) ... ἔδωκεν (*edōken*, G1325), "that he gave", states what the love did.
- κόσμος (*kosmos*, G2889, "world"). John can use it of the created order (John 1:10), but here it
  is humanity turned away from God: "people loved the darkness rather than the light because their
  works were evil" (John 3:19, ESV). The *NIV Cultural Backgrounds Study Bible* notes that Jewish
  teachers stressed God's love for Israel; a love that gives itself for the rebel world goes beyond
  that, though it fits the God of Exodus 34:6-7.
- μονογενής (*monogenēs*, "only"). John's Gospel uses it four times, all of Jesus (John 1:14, 18;
  3:16, 18).

**What the context adds.** John 3:16 opens with "for", explaining the two verses before it. Moses
lifted a bronze serpent on a pole, and every Israelite bitten in the wilderness who looked at it
lived (Numbers 21:8-9). Jesus is lifted up on the cross the same way, and whoever looks to Him in
faith lives. John explains elsewhere what the giving cost: God "sent his only Son into the world"
(1 John 4:9, ESV), "to be the propitiation for our sins" (1 John 4:10, ESV). Propitiation is the Son bearing the wrath your sin deserved, so
that God's love reaches you without His justice being set aside. "God shows his love for us in that
while we were still sinners, Christ died for us" (Romans 5:8, ESV). The verses after it settle the
outcome: "Whoever believes in him is not condemned" (John 3:18, ESV). So John 3:16 shows a God who
loved people in rebellion against Him, paid for that love with His only Son, and gives eternal life
to anyone who believes. That includes you, today.

**Keywords.** Love of God, the gospel, the cross, propitiation, eternal life, faith, whoever.

**Further study on this site:** [As the Snake Was Lifted Up](../jesus/as-the-snake-was-lifted.md),
[Assurance of Salvation](../salvation/assurance-of-salvation.md).

## Hebrews 11:1: "Faith is the assurance"

> ✝️ Hebrews 11:1-2 (ESV)
>
> 1 Now faith is the assurance of things hoped for, the conviction of things not seen. 2 For by it
> the people of old received their commendation.

**Why it is quoted well.** It is used as a definition of faith, and the author wrote it as one. The
*NIV Cultural Backgrounds Study Bible* notes that a definition (verse 1) followed by a thesis (verse 2)
was a fitting way to open an ancient discourse, and that orators drove a point home by repeating an
opening phrase, as the chapter does with "by faith."

**The words.**

- ὑπόστασις (*hypostasis*, G5287, "assurance"). Hebrews uses it twice before this. At 1:3 it is
  God's own being, of which the Son is "the exact imprint" (Hebrews 1:3, ESV); at 3:14 it is the
  believer's "original confidence" held "firm to the end" (Hebrews 3:14, ESV). Translations divide
  the same way here: the ESV reads "assurance", and the LSB footnotes "Or substance." Either way,
  faith gives present reality to what God has promised and you do not yet hold.
- ἔλεγχος (*elenchos*, G1650, "conviction"). The word for proof or evidence that settles a question;
  the LSB footnotes it "Or evidence." Faith is the believer's settled conviction about unseen things,
  resting on the God who spoke them.

**What the context adds.** The verse answers the paragraph before it. "You have need of endurance,
so that when you have done the will of God you may receive what is promised" (Hebrews 10:36, ESV).
Quoting Habakkuk 2:4, it promises that "my righteous one shall live by faith" (Hebrews 10:38, ESV),
and "we are not of those who shrink back and are destroyed, but of those who have faith and preserve
their souls" (Hebrews 10:39, ESV). Hebrews 11:1 then defines that faith, and the chapter shows it
working in Abel, Enoch, Noah, Abraham and the rest. In the *ESV Study Bible*'s words,
biblical faith is "not blind trust in the face of contrary evidence." It rests on God Himself, since
"whoever would draw near to God must believe that he exists and that he rewards those who seek him"
(Hebrews 11:6, ESV). The chapter closes with a surprise: "all these, though commended through their
faith, did not receive what was promised" (Hebrews 11:39, ESV), because "God had provided something better
for us, that apart from us they should not be made perfect" (Hebrews 11:40, ESV). And the next
chapter names the one to look at: "looking to Jesus, the founder
and perfecter of our faith" (Hebrews 12:2, ESV). So Hebrews 11:1 shows a God whose promises are firm
enough to stand on before they arrive, and a Saviour who carries your faith to its end.

**Keywords.** Faith, hope, assurance, endurance, promise, the unseen, Hebrews 11 "hall of faith."

**Further study on this site:** [Faith](../salvation/faith.md), whose Hebrew root *ʾaman*, "to be
firm", is the Old Testament picture behind the same idea.

## Discussion questions

1. **Language.** Genesis 1:1's verb *bara*, "create", only ever has God as its subject. What does it
   tell you about God that Scripture keeps this verb for Him alone?
2. **Context.** John 3:16 begins with "for" and explains the bronze serpent. How does reading it with
   John 3:14-15 change the picture of what believing in Jesus means?
3. **Christ.** All three verses lead to Jesus: the Word of creation, the only Son given, the perfecter
   of faith. Which connection was new to you?
4. **The hard part.** Hebrews 11:39 says the faithful "did not receive what was promised." How do you
   hold Hebrews 11:1's assurance together with promises that are still outstanding in your own life?
5. **You.** Which of the three truths — God made you, God gave His Son for you, God keeps His
   promises to you — do you most need to lean on this week?

# John son of Zebedee

He was nicknamed Son of Thunder and wanted to call down fire on a village. He ended up writing the
letters that define love as the test of whether anyone knows God at all. Between those two points
is the whole argument for what Jesus does to a person.

## Key Takeaways

### Lessons about Jesus

From the cross, in the middle of dying, Jesus arranged his mother's housing. "Woman, behold, your
son!... Behold, your mother!" (John 19:26-27). The scope of his attention did not shrink under
extremity — it went to a specific practical need of one particular person.

### Memory verse

> ✝️ John 19:26-27 (ESV)
>
> 26 When Jesus saw his mother and the disciple whom he loved standing nearby, he said to his mother, "Woman, behold, your son!" 27 Then he said to the disciple, "Behold, your mother!" And from that hour the disciple took her to his own home.

### Be transformed

John's self-description is never "the disciple who loved Jesus." It is always "the disciple whom
Jesus loved" — the love runs the other direction, and he identified himself by being on the
receiving end of it. Examine which way round you tell your own story.

### Prayer

Father, you took a man who wanted to burn a village and made him the one who wrote that anyone who
does not love does not know God. Do that patient work in us. Let us be known not by what we are
willing to destroy for you, but by whom we are willing to love.

## The name

<span lang="grc">Ἰωάννης</span> (*Iōannēs*, ee-o-AN-nace), from Hebrew
<span dir="rtl">יוֹחָנָן</span> *Yochanan* (yo-khah-NAHN), a shortened form of *Yehochanan*:
"Yahweh has been gracious."

## Background

A Galilean fisherman, the younger son of Zebedee and brother of James. The family business employed
hired servants (Mark 1:20). With his brother he received the joint nickname *Boanerges*, "Sons of
Thunder" (Mark 3:17) — and earned it when he proposed incinerating a Samaritan village that had
refused Jesus hospitality (Luke 9:54).

## What Scripture records

John is one of the **inner three** — present at the raising of Jairus's daughter (Mark 5:37), the
transfiguration (Mark 9:2), and Gethsemane (Mark 14:33). He and James together asked for the seats
at Christ's right and left (Mark 10:35-37).

In the Fourth Gospel he never uses his own name. He appears instead as "the disciple whom Jesus
loved" — reclining next to Jesus at the last supper (John 13:23), standing at the cross, and
receiving responsibility for Jesus' mother (John 19:26-27). That the Gospel identifies its own
source this way (John 21:24) while withholding the name is a deliberate authorial choice, not
modesty accidentally preserved.

In Acts he is consistently beside Peter and consistently silent — at the temple gate (Acts 3:1-11),
before the Sanhedrin, where the council notes that both men are "uneducated, common men" (Acts
4:13), and sent to Samaria (Acts 8:14). Paul later names him among the "pillars" of the Jerusalem
church (Galatians 2:9). The man who wanted to destroy a Samaritan village is the one dispatched to
Samaria to pray for the Spirit to come on Samaritan believers.

He is exiled for his testimony:

> ✝️ Revelation 1:9 (ESV)
>
> 9 I, John, your brother and partner in the tribulation and the kingdom and the patient endurance that are in Jesus, was on the island called Patmos on account of the word of God and the testimony of Jesus.

Five New Testament books are traditionally attributed to him: the Gospel, three letters, and
Revelation.

## What tradition says

The consistent early tradition is that John lived to old age at Ephesus, dying a natural death near
the end of the first century — making him, on this account, the only apostle not martyred. Eusebius
preserves the Ephesus connection in the same passage of Origen that assigns the other apostles their
fields: "Asia to John, who, after he had lived some time there, died at Ephesus" (*Church History*
3.1). Irenaeus, writing in the late second century, is the other early witness, and he is often cited
because of his claimed link back through Polycarp, who knew John personally.

That Irenaeus link is worth flagging as unchecked here: *Against Heresies* sits in the Ante-Nicene
Fathers, which this project has not yet obtained — see [Patristic Sources](../resources/patristic-sources.md).
The Ephesus tradition itself does not depend on it.

Note that the authorship of the five "Johannine" books is debated in scholarship, sometimes
distinguishing the apostle from an "elder John" at Ephesus. This study takes the traditional
attribution; readers should know the question exists.

## Takeaway

John is the long answer to whether people actually change. Thunder to love is not a personality
adjustment; it is decades of proximity to Jesus doing what proximity to Jesus does. Nothing in the
record suggests it happened quickly.

# James son of Zebedee

He asked for a throne and was promised a cup. Roughly a decade later he became the first of the
Twelve to die for the name — and the only one whose death the New Testament reports.

## Key Takeaways

### Lessons about Jesus

Jesus answered the ambition of James and John by redefining the request rather than refusing it:
they would indeed share what he was about to go through, just not in the way they meant. He did not
crush their desire for greatness; he told them what greatness costs and where it is actually
located — "whoever would be great among you must be your servant" (Mark 10:43).

### Memory verse

> ✝️ Acts 12:2 (ESV)
>
> 2 He killed James the brother of John with the sword,

### Be transformed

Seven words. No martyrdom account, no last speech, no miracle at the execution — Luke gives James
the same brevity he gives a travel note. Faithfulness does not come with a guarantee of a
significant-looking ending, and Scripture declines to dress this one up.

### Prayer

Lord, we ask for thrones more readily than we ask for cups. Teach us to want what you actually
promise, and give us the courage of a man who asked for the wrong thing and then drank the right
one anyway.

## The name

<span lang="grc">Ἰάκωβος</span> (*Iakōbos*, ee-AK-o-bos) is simply the Greek form of **Jacob** —
<span dir="rtl">יַעֲקֹב</span> *Yaʿaqob* (yah-ah-KOVE), "heel-grabber," hence "supplanter"
(Genesis 25:26). English gets "James" through Latin *Iacomus*, a later variant of *Iacobus*; there
is no separate underlying name. Every "James" in the New Testament is a Jacob.

He is distinguished from the other apostle James by his father: **son of Zebedee**.

## Background

A Galilean fisherman, working his father Zebedee's business with his brother John, in partnership
with Simon and Andrew (Luke 5:10). Zebedee had hired servants (Mark 1:20), which suggests a
business of some substance rather than subsistence fishing. Their mother appears in the narrative
too (Matthew 20:20) and is likely the Salome named at the crucifixion (Mark 15:40).

Jesus gave the brothers a joint nickname:

> ✝️ Mark 3:17 (ESV)
>
> 17 James the son of Zebedee and John the brother of James (to whom he gave the name Boanerges, that is, Sons of Thunder);

<span lang="grc">Βοανηργές</span> (*Boanērges*) is a transliterated Aramaic phrase that Mark
himself glosses as "Sons of Thunder." It reads as a comment on temperament, and the narrative
supplies the evidence: when a Samaritan village refused Jesus hospitality, "James and John... said,
'Lord, do you want us to tell fire to come down from heaven and consume them?'" (Luke 9:54).

## What Scripture records

James belongs to the **inner three**. Peter, James and John alone are taken into the room where
Jairus's daughter is raised (Mark 5:37), up the mountain at the transfiguration (Mark 9:2), and
furthest into Gethsemane (Mark 14:33). Whatever the three groups of four in the apostolic lists
signify, this narrower circle is visible on its own terms.

His one substantial recorded speech is a request for status:

> ✝️ Mark 10:35-37 (ESV)
>
> 35 And James and John, the sons of Zebedee, came up to him and said to him, "Teacher, we want you to do for us whatever we ask of you." 36 And he said to them, "What do you want me to do for you?" 37 And they said to him, "Grant us to sit, one at your right hand and one at your left, in your glory."

Jesus replies that they do not know what they are asking, and asks whether they can drink his cup.
They say they can. He tells them they will — and that the seats are not his to assign (Mark
10:38-40). The other ten are indignant, which is not obviously better.

Then, in Acts, the payment of that promise:

> ✝️ Acts 12:1-2 (ESV)
>
> 1 About that time Herod the king laid violent hands on some who belonged to the church. 2 He killed James the brother of John with the sword,

Herod Agrippa I, around AD 44. Execution by sword indicates beheading. Luke moves straight on to
Peter's arrest — James's death is the setup for someone else's story, told in a subordinate clause.

## What tradition says

Because his death is recorded in Scripture, James needs less tradition than the others, and there
is correspondingly less. A much later Spanish tradition connects him with evangelism in Iberia and
with the shrine at Santiago de Compostela; it has no early support and is hard to reconcile with an
execution in Jerusalem in the mid-40s. Treat it as devotional history rather than evidence.

## Takeaway

James asked for the seat at Christ's right hand and got the sword instead — and Jesus had told him
plainly, years earlier, that this was the shape of the answer. He is the apostle who shows that the
cup is not a metaphor.

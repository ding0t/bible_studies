# Bartholomew (Nathanael)

Bartholomew appears in all four lists of the Twelve and does nothing in any of them. Nathanael has
one of the most memorable first conversations with Jesus in the Gospels and appears in no list at
all. There is a good reason to think these are the same man, and a good reason not to state it as
fact.

## Key Takeaways

*(This section follows the [Key Takeaways](../about/key-takeaways.md) format — see that page for what each part is for.)*

### Lessons about Jesus

Jesus met open scepticism with knowledge and a promise. Nathanael's "Can anything good
come out of Nazareth?" is an insult to Jesus' home town, and Jesus' response is to tell him something only
Jesus could know, and to promise he would see greater things. He did not require the doubt to be
withdrawn first.

### Memory verse

> ✝️ John 1:47 (ESV)
>
> 47 Jesus saw Nathanael coming toward him and said of him, "Behold, an Israelite indeed, in whom there is no deceit!"

### Be transformed

Philip answered Nathanael's objection with "Come and see"
(John 1:46). Some scepticism is defeated by proximity. Consider
whether the person you keep debating needs a better case or an introduction.

### Prayer

Lord, you saw Nathanael under the fig tree before anyone introduced him, and you called him a man
without deceit before he had said a single believing word. Thank you that you know us first. Meet
our honest doubts as you met his. In Jesus' name. Amen.

## The name

<span lang="grc">Βαρθολομαῖος</span> (*Bartholomaios*, bar-thol-o-MY-oss, G918) is Aramaic:
<span dir="rtl">בַּר</span> (*bar*, H1247), "son of," plus **Talmai** (<span dir="rtl">תַּלְמַי</span>,
*Talmai*, H8526). It means "son of Talmai".

It is a **patronymic**: the equivalent of being listed
as "Johnson" with no first name. Bar-Talmai would have had a given name too, and the lists do not
supply it.

**Nathanael**, <span dir="rtl">נְתַנְאֵל</span> (*Netanʾel*, neth-an-EL, H5417), "God has given", is a
given name, and John never pairs it with a patronymic.

## The identification

The case for Bartholomew being Nathanael is cumulative. No verse says it. The
strands:

1. **Bartholomew is a patronymic** and needs a personal name; Nathanael is a personal name with no patronymic attached.
2. **Distribution.** Bartholomew appears in all three Synoptic lists and Acts, never in John. Nathanael appears only in John, never in a list.
3. **Position.** In Matthew, Mark and Luke, Bartholomew is named immediately after **Philip**; in John it is Philip who finds Nathanael and brings him to Jesus (John 1:45).
4. **John 21:2** places Nathanael in a small group of named disciples at a resurrection appearance: "Simon Peter, Thomas (called the Twin), Nathanael of Cana in Galilee, the sons of Zebedee, and two others of his disciples." The company is apostolic.

Taken together that is a strong inference, and it is the traditional identification. Hold it as very probably true;
Scripture nowhere asserts it.

## What Scripture records

If the identification holds, everything we know of him is one scene (John 1:45-51). Philip finds him
and reports that they have found the one Moses wrote about, Jesus of Nazareth. Nathanael's reply is
blunt regional scepticism: "Can anything good come out of Nazareth?" Philip does not defend Nazareth.
He says, "Come and see."

Jesus greets him with a public assessment: "Behold, an Israelite indeed, in whom there is no
deceit!" He says it of a man who has just insulted His home town, and it is possibly a
deliberate contrast with **Jacob**, the Israelite who was full of deceit. Nathanael asks how Jesus
knows him. Jesus says He saw him under the fig tree before Philip called him.

That is enough. Nathanael answers, "Rabbi, you are the Son of God! You are the King of Israel!"
(John 1:49), one of the earliest and fullest confessions in the Gospel. Jesus tells him he will see
"greater things," and speaks of heaven opened and angels ascending and descending on the Son of Man
(John 1:51). That echoes Jacob's ladder (Genesis 28:12), and makes the Jacob contrast
in verse 47 look intentional.

He is named once more, in the boat, after the resurrection (John 21:2). Under either name, he is
otherwise silent.

## What tradition says

Traditions place Bartholomew's later ministry variously in Armenia, Mesopotamia, or India, and he is
commonly said to have been flayed alive, an image that dominates his depiction in Western art. The
sources are late and mutually inconsistent, and the geographic spread of the claims is itself a
reason for caution.

## Takeaway

The man who asked whether anything good could come out of Nazareth made one of the first clear
confessions that Jesus is the Son of God, and did it in the same conversation. Honest doubt stated
out loud turned out to be a short distance from faith.

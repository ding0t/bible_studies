# The Donkey and Her Prophet: How God Turned a Hired Curse into a Star

Balak, king of Moab, pays a famous diviner to curse Israel. On the road the diviner beats his
donkey three times, and the donkey speaks. She has seen what the seer cannot. The LORD opens her mouth, opens the seer's eyes, and fills the
seer's mouth with the blessing He gave Abraham and a promise of a Star.

**In one sentence:** Balak paid a famous diviner to curse the people God had blessed, and because the LORD loved His people He opened a donkey's mouth and a prophet's eyes, turned the curse into Abraham's blessing and promised the Star out of Jacob, His Son Jesus, in whom that blessing now reaches you and no curse can undo it.

### Types & Prophecy

**Prophecy.** Balaam's fourth oracle announces a Star out of Jacob and a Scepter out of Israel
(Numbers 24:17). The Magi saw "His star" and asked for the One born King of the Jews (Matthew 2:2, BSB).
Jesus calls Himself "the bright Morning Star" (Revelation 22:16, BSB). The Star has risen. The Scepter's
rule over the nations, promised to Jesus in Psalm 2:9 and Revelation 2:27, waits for His return.

### Lessons about Jesus

- Jesus is the Star out of Jacob: both the Root and the Offspring of David (Revelation 22:16), the
  Son of David who is also David's Lord (Matthew 22:42-45).
- Jesus is Abraham's blessing in person. He "redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a
  curse for us" so that "the blessing promised to Abraham would come to the Gentiles" (Galatians
  3:13-14, BSB).
- The Star came down. He descended out of heaven into a human family line, born in Bethlehem
  (Micah 5:2; Matthew 2:1).
- Before Bethlehem the Son stood in Balaam's road as the angel of the LORD, the reading this study
  holds (Numbers 22:31-35; Jude 5) and [The Angel of the LORD](../jesus/the-angel-of-the-lord.md)
  argues in full.

## Who Balaam was

Balaam son of Beor lived at Pethor in Aram-naharaim, by the Euphrates (Numbers 22:5; Deuteronomy
23:4), hundreds of miles from Moab. (The Samaritan Pentateuch, Vulgate and Syriac read "Ammonites" at
Numbers 22:5, per the *CSB Ancient Faith Study Bible*'s footnote.) It is the country of Abraham's own
kin: Abraham's servant went to "Nahor's hometown in Aram-naharaim" to find a wife for Isaac (Genesis
24:10, BSB).

**His work was divination.** Joshua calls him "the diviner Balaam son of Beor" (Joshua 13:22, BSB),
<span dir="rtl">קוֹסֵם</span> (*qosem*, H7080). Moab's elders came "with the fees for divination in
hand" (Numbers 22:7, BSB), and the Hebrew is simply "divinations in their hand",
<span dir="rtl">קְסָמִים</span> (*qesamim*, H7081): the fee and the service are one word. He looked for
omens, <span dir="rtl">נְחָשִׁים</span> (*nechashim*, H5173), and only stopped "as on previous
occasions" when he saw that the LORD meant to bless Israel (Numbers 24:1, BSB). He worked by sacrifice
too, seven altars with a bull and a ram on each, before he went off to meet God (Numbers 23:1-4). The
law calls that whole trade "detestable to the LORD" (Deuteronomy 18:10-12, BSB).

**He knew the LORD's name.** His first answer to Moab uses it: "I will give you the answer that the
LORD speaks to me" (Numbers 22:8, BSB), the name <span dir="rtl">יהוה</span> (YHWH). He calls Him "the
LORD my God" (Numbers 22:18, BSB). God came to him by night and spoke with him (Numbers 22:9-12, 20).
Numbers does not say where he learned the name, but his country had heard it. Laban, in Nahor's town,
greeted Abraham's servant as "blessed by the LORD" and said of Rebekah's marriage, "This is from the
LORD" (Genesis 24:31, 50, BSB). The same household kept "household idols" and called them "my gods"
(Genesis 31:19, 30, BSB), and Joshua says Abraham's fathers beyond the Euphrates "worshiped other
gods" (Joshua 24:2, BSB). That is probably Balaam's world: the LORD known by name, and consulted
alongside omens, altars and fees.

His own second oracle names both words of his trade, *nachash* and *qesem*, and says neither works
against Israel: "there is no spell against Jacob and no divination against Israel" (Numbers 23:23,
BSB). This shows that the LORD speaks to whom He chooses, even a hired
diviner far beyond Israel, and that hearing His voice is a different thing from belonging to Him.
Balaam knew the name and went after the wages (2 Peter 2:15).

## Why Balak paid for a curse

Israel is camped on the border of the land at the end of the wilderness years, and Moab's king is
afraid (Numbers 22:1-3). Balak sends for Balaam, and the Moabite elders travel with the fees (Numbers 22:7). A
king who pays for a journey of hundreds of miles believes a curse works, and Balak says so:

> ✝️ Numbers 22:6 (BSB)
>
> 6 So please come now and put a curse on this people, because they are too mighty for me. Perhaps I may be able to defeat them and drive them out of the land; for I know that those you bless are blessed, and those you curse are cursed.

The Hebrew uses one verb three times in that verse, <span dir="rtl">אָרַר</span> (*arar*, H779, "to
curse"): "put a curse", "those you curse", "are cursed". Balak's whole theory of power sits in one
verb and one man. The curser decides. The curse is a weapon, too. Balak wants to "defeat" Israel,
<span dir="rtl">נָכָה</span> (*nakah*, H5221, "to strike"), and drive them out. He has watched
Israel destroy Sihon and Og (Numbers 21:21-35; 22:2), and he has no army that can stand against them.

Balak's belief belongs to his world. A diviner, magician or sorcerer was thought able to move the
gods toward a blessing or a curse on a person or a nation, by sacrifice and incantation (*NIV
Cultural Backgrounds Study Bible*, note on Numbers 22:6). Balaam was such a man.

Numbers tells this as narrative with four poems set inside it, the oracles. The narrator rarely
comments on Balaam; the rest of Scripture gives the verdict (Deuteronomy 23:4-5; 2 Peter 2:15). What
carries over to every age is the answer the story gives: whether a word spoken against God's people lands is God's decision.

## The LORD answers first

Balaam asks God what to do, and God answers before a single curse is spoken:

> ✝️ Numbers 22:12 (BSB)
>
> 12 But God said to Balaam, "Do not go with them. You are not to curse this people, for they are blessed."

"They are blessed" is a verdict already in force. The ground of it is the word God spoke to Abraham:

> ✝️ Genesis 12:3 (BSB)
>
> 3 I will bless those who bless you and curse those who curse you; and all the families of the earth will be blessed through you.

God's word says the blessed are blessed
already, and the curse falls on the one who curses them. Proverbs 26:2 states the same law in a
proverb: "an undeserved curse does not come to rest" (BSB). Balaam says again and again that
the word he speaks is the LORD's to give (Numbers 22:8, 18, 38; 23:3, 12, 26; 24:13). The last is
the plainest: "I must speak whatever the LORD says" (Numbers 24:12-13, BSB).

The second oracle reaches the root of it. "God is not a man, that He should lie, or a son of man,
that He should change His mind. Does He speak and not act? Does He promise and not fulfill? I have
indeed received a command to bless; He has blessed, and I cannot change it" (Numbers 23:19-20,
BSB). The blessing on Israel is a promise God made, and He keeps promises. Moses gives the reason He
kept this one: "the LORD your God turned the curse into a blessing for you, because the LORD your
God loves you" (Deuteronomy 23:5, BSB).

This shows that God binds His own blessing to His love for His people and keeps it against a king's
will, a prophet's greed and the plans of both. It is the covenant with Abraham, held. That blessing
reaches the nations through Israel's Messiah (Genesis 12:3; Galatians 3:14), and those who are in
Jesus share it (Ephesians 1:3).

## The donkey

Balaam had received a clear no (Numbers 22:12). He then asks again, "that I may find out what else
the LORD has to tell me" (Numbers 22:19, BSB). God answers: "get up and go with them, but you must
only do what I tell you" (Numbers 22:20, BSB). The next verse but one says "God's anger was
kindled because Balaam was going along" (Numbers 22:22, BSB). The text sets the permission and the anger side by side and
does not explain the join. Peter supplies the motive: Balaam "loved the wages of wickedness" (2
Peter 2:15, BSB). The angel's verdict agrees: "your way is perverse before me" (Numbers 22:32, BSB), where the LSB
footnotes the word as "reckless".

### What the donkey saw

The angel of the LORD stands in the road "with a drawn sword in his hand" (Numbers 22:23, BSB),
and the donkey sees him. Balaam, the professional seer, does not. She turns into a field. He strikes
her. She presses against a wall and crushes his foot. He strikes her. She lies down under him. He
strikes her with his staff.

The verb is <span dir="rtl">נָכָה</span> (*nakah*, H5221), the verb Balak used for what he hoped
the curse would do to Israel (Numbers 22:6). It occurs six times in Numbers 22: Balak's hope in
<span data-ref="Numbers 22:6">verse 6</span>, and the blows in Numbers 22:23, 25, 27, 28 and 32. English hides the
link. The BSB has Balak hope to "defeat" Israel and Balaam "beat" his donkey; the ULT keeps
"strike" in both places, and this study follows the ULT there. The man hired to strike a people spends
the journey striking the one creature on the road that sees what stands between him and his
errand.

> ✝️ Numbers 22:28-31 (BSB)
>
> 28 Then the LORD opened the donkey's mouth, and she said to Balaam, "What have I done to you that you have beaten me these three times?" 29 Balaam answered the donkey, "You have made a fool of me! If I had a sword in my hand, I would kill you right now!" 30 But the donkey said to Balaam, "Am I not the donkey you have ridden all your life until today? Have I ever treated you this way before?" "No," he replied. 31 Then the LORD opened Balaam's eyes, and he saw the angel of the LORD standing in the road with a drawn sword in his hand. And Balaam bowed low and fell facedown.

### The she-donkey in Scripture

Hebrew has three words for the animal: <span dir="rtl">חֲמוֹר</span> (*chamor*, H2543), the common
word for a donkey; <span dir="rtl">אָתוֹן</span> (*aton*, H860), a she-donkey; and
<span dir="rtl">עַיִר</span> (*ayir*, H5895), a young male, a colt. Balaam's is an *aton* throughout,
and the Hebrew gives her feminine verbs: she saw, she turned, she lay down.

**She was a beast of standing.** She was wealth (Genesis 12:16; Job 1:3), the mount of leaders and
households (Judges 5:10; 2 Kings 4:24), and an animal the law protected even for an enemy (Exodus
23:5); [The King on a Donkey](../jesus/the-king-on-a-donkey.md) follows her through Scripture.
Balaam rode his "all your life" (Numbers 22:30, BSB), with his two servants (Numbers 22:22): a man
of rank on the mount of one. He beat her, faithful for all her years, three times, and the proverb
fits the scene: "a bridle for the donkey, and a rod for the backs of fools" (Proverbs 26:3, BSB).

**Her colt carried the King.** Jacob's blessing on Judah, the tribe of the scepter, has him tie "his colt
to the choicest branch" (Genesis 49:11, BSB), in Hebrew "the son of his she-donkey". Zechariah's King
comes "humble and riding on a donkey, on a colt, the foal of a donkey" (Zechariah 9:9, BSB), the colt
of she-donkeys. When Jesus entered Jerusalem, the disciples found "a donkey tied there, with her colt
beside her" (Matthew 21:2, BSB); the Greek donkey, ὄνος (*onos*, G3688), is female there too. The
animal Balaam beat for turning from the sword is the animal that carried the Star into Jerusalem. The
text draws no line between the two scenes, so read this as an echo: the humble beast of peace, used
by a hireling to ride to a curse, used by the King to ride to the cross.

### Two things the LORD opened

The LORD opened the donkey's mouth, <span dir="rtl">פָּתַח</span>
(*patach*, H6605, "to open"). Then He uncovered Balaam's eyes: the verb in Numbers 22:31 is
<span dir="rtl">גָּלָה</span> (*galah*, H1540, "to uncover"). That verb returns in the oracles.
Balaam introduces two of them as the words of one "falling down with his eyes uncovered" (Numbers
24:4, ESV; also 24:16). The title he claims uses the verb for what the LORD did to him on the road.
A man who read omens for a living needed a donkey to
tell him what stood in front of him.

The angel adds one more fact: "If she had not turned away, then by now I would surely have killed
you and let her live" (Numbers 22:33, BSB). The donkey's refusal to carry him forward saved his life.
Peter names the whole scene in one verse. Balaam "was rebuked for his transgression by a donkey,
otherwise without speech, that spoke with a man's voice and restrained the prophet's madness" (2
Peter 2:16, BSB).

This shows that God speaks through whatever He chooses. He speaks through a donkey, and He gives
sight to a man who has been paid to see. Balaam confesses "I have
sinned" (Numbers 22:34, BSB) and offers to go home, and the angel sends him on (Numbers 22:35). The curse is already beaten. The prophet is the one still to be turned.

### Who stood in the road

The angel of the LORD who stood in Balaam's road is the LORD Himself, appearing, and the New
Testament lets us name Him: God the Son, before Bethlehem. He speaks God's word as His own: "you are
to speak only what I tell you" (Numbers 22:35, BSB), and Balaam then calls it "the word that God
puts in my mouth" (Numbers 22:38, BSB). [The Angel of the LORD](../jesus/the-angel-of-the-lord.md)
follows Him from Hagar to Zechariah, weighs the case against, and holds the reading as the likeliest.

This shows that God the Son came out to meet His people's enemy before He was born. The Star that
Balaam would see "not now" had already met him in the road, with a drawn sword, to stop a curse on
the people He would come from.

## Where it happened

Israel is camped in the plains of Moab, across the Jordan from Jericho (Numbers 22:1; 31:12).
Balak takes Balaam to three high places, each with a view of the camp: Bamoth-baal (Numbers 22:41),
the top of Pisgah (Numbers 23:14) and the top of Peor (Numbers 23:28, where "the wasteland" may be
the place name Jeshimon). Joel Kramer of Expedition Bible walked all three in a 2023 video, and this
study holds his assessment of the ground.

Israel's headquarters was Shittim, Tall el-Hammam on the east side of the Jordan Valley, directly
opposite Jericho: "While Israel was staying in Shittim" (Numbers 25:1, BSB). Beth-peor stands on
the ridge above it, and the text pairs the two: Israel "stayed in the valley opposite Beth-peor"
(Deuteronomy 3:29, BSB), and Moses was buried "in a valley in the land of Moab facing Beth-peor"
(Deuteronomy 34:6, BSB). The mound was an occupied city in the days of Moses and Joshua, and the
Peor ridge carries pottery from the Old Testament period (both as Kramer reports them).

### Three heights over the camp

On that ground the three viewpoints run from south to north, each nearer the camp, and the text's
widening view fits the order. From Bamoth-baal Balaam sees only "the outskirts of the camp"
(Numbers 22:41, BSB), and Balak promises the same limit at the second site: "You will only see the
outskirts of their camp—not all of them" (Numbers 23:13, BSB). From the third, Peor, Balaam "saw
Israel encamped tribe by tribe" (Numbers 24:2, BSB), and Peor is the first of the three with a
clear line down into the valley. The camp was set around the Tent of Meeting (Numbers 2:2). So the
second oracle's line "The LORD their God is with them, and the shout of the King is among them"
(Numbers 23:21, BSB) is Balaam looking toward the LORD's own presence over the Tabernacle, as Kramer
reads it. The King of that sentence is the LORD.

Pisgah is Mount Nebo (Deuteronomy 34:1). Moses saw the promised land from it, and Balaam had looked down on the promised people from it. The area is the last stretch before the Jordan. The people
leave it for Gilgal (Micah 6:5), and the blessing is spoken over them on its edge, before they take
a step into the land.

The road from the east to Jerusalem passed Peor, so the Magi who followed a star may have crossed
the ground where Balaam first named one. Matthew names no road, and Kramer offers this as an
inference. Tall el-Hammam is also the northern candidate for Sodom; for that debate and Kramer's
southern case, see [Cities of the Plain](../scripture/cities-of-the-plain.md).

## Three oracles turn the curse

At each of the three sites seven altars are built at Balaam's word
(Numbers 23:1-2, 14, 29-30). The LORD puts a message in his mouth. He returns to Balak, and Balak hears
a blessing.

### The first and second oracles

The first oracle asks a question Balak cannot answer: "How can I curse those whom God has not
cursed? How can I denounce those whom the LORD has not denounced?" (Numbers 23:8, BSB). It names
Israel "a people dwelling apart, not reckoning themselves among the nations" (Numbers 23:9, BSB),
and it asks, "Who can count the dust of Jacob or number even a fourth of Israel?" (Numbers 23:10,
BSB). That is the promise of Genesis 28:14 in a seer's mouth. Balaam then asks for himself: "Let me
die the death of the righteous" (Numbers 23:10, BSB).

Balak reacts: "I brought you here to curse my enemies, and behold, you have only blessed them!"
(Numbers 23:11, BSB). The second oracle says God has blessed and Balaam cannot change it (Numbers
23:20). It says "there is no spell against Jacob" (Numbers 23:23, BSB).

### The third oracle: Balak's verbs turned on Israel

Before the third oracle "the Spirit of God came upon him" (Numbers 24:2, BSB). Balaam sees "How
lovely are your tents, O Jacob" (Numbers 24:5, BSB), and ends with the blessing Isaac gave Jacob:

> ✝️ Numbers 24:9 (BSB)
>
> 9 He crouches, he lies down like a lion, like a lioness—who dares to rouse him? Blessed are those who bless you and cursed are those who curse you.

Balak's two verbs are back, <span dir="rtl">בָּרַךְ</span> (*barak*, H1288) and
<span dir="rtl">אָרַר</span> (*arar*, H779). In Numbers 22:6 the "you" they turned on was the seer:
those you bless are blessed, those you curse are cursed. In Numbers 24:9 the "you" is Israel. The
power Balak thought a prophet held belongs to God's people under His covenant. The words are
Isaac's over Jacob, "May those who curse you be cursed, and those who bless you be blessed"
(Genesis 27:29, BSB), with the halves turned round; the Hebrew of the two verses uses the same four
words. God's promise to Abraham stands behind both (Genesis 12:3). The lion who "crouches" and "lies
down" echoes Jacob's blessing on Judah, "Like a lion he crouches and lies down; like a lioness, who
dares to rouse him?" (Genesis 49:9, BSB). The same question belongs to the tribe that carries the
scepter (Genesis 49:10), and John hears the lion named in Christ: "the Lion of the tribe of Judah,
the Root of David" (Revelation 5:5, BSB). Balak counts the result: "you have persisted in blessing them these
three times" (Numbers 24:10, BSB). Three blows on the road, three blessings from the mountains.

## The Star and the Scepter

Balak sends Balaam home, and Balaam answers with a fourth oracle, about what Israel will do "in the
days to come" (Numbers 24:14, BSB). Three short oracles on Amalek, the Kenites and the ships of
Cyprus follow it (Numbers 24:20-24).

> ✝️ Numbers 24:17-19 (BSB)
>
> 17 I see him, but not now; I behold him, but not near. A star will come forth from Jacob, and a scepter will arise from Israel. He will crush the skulls of Moab and strike down all the sons of Sheth. 18 Edom will become a possession, as will Seir, his enemy; but Israel will perform with valor. 19 A ruler will come from Jacob and destroy the survivors of the city.

The Hebrew word behind "skulls" is uncertain; the LSB footnotes "corners". The vision is of a
person, seen from far off in time: "not now", "not near". The Star and the Scepter are two names for
one figure, and Numbers 24:19 calls him "a ruler" from Jacob. The Scepter is
settled. It belongs to the tribe of Judah (Genesis 49:10) and means a king. What does Scripture
mean by the Star?

### What Scripture does with a star

Scripture uses a star for a created thing under God, for the family of Abraham and Jacob, for the
servants of heaven, for the counterfeit gods, and for Jesus, "the bright Morning Star" (Revelation
22:16, BSB). [The Bright Morning Star](../jesus/the-bright-morning-star.md) reads every one of
those verses; the two that decide Numbers 24:17 are Joseph's dream, where the stars are Jacob's sons
(Genesis 37:9), and Job 38:7, where the morning stars sing with the sons of God.

### Who the Star is

Put those uses beside Numbers 24:17. A Star "from Jacob" is a person of Jacob's house, and a star is
also the word Scripture uses for those who belong to heaven. The Star of Numbers 24 is a
ruler out of Jacob, and He is also heaven's own. That is how Jesus describes Himself: the *Root* of
David, who came before David, and the *Offspring* of David, who came after him (Revelation 22:16).
It is also His question to the Pharisees in Matthew 22:42-45: David calls his own son "Lord" (Psalm
110:1, BSB). Micah
5:2 says the ruler comes out of Bethlehem and that His "origins are of old, from the days of
eternity" (BSB). Isaiah 9:6 calls the child born to us "Mighty God" (BSB). These texts, together with
Revelation 22:16, carry the Star's deity.

Joel Kramer reads the star in the ancient
world as a sign of deity and the scepter as a sign of a king, and concludes that Numbers 24:17
promises that the LORD Himself would come out of Jacob. Scripture's own vocabulary supports
his conclusion, and it supplies a safeguard. The stars Scripture forbids Israel to worship are
created things and idols (Deuteronomy 4:19; Amos 5:26), and the day star in Isaiah 14 climbs to be
like the Most High. The true Star goes the other way. He comes down out of heaven and takes His place in Jacob's family.

Revelation 2:26-28 joins the scepter and the star again in one promise to the overcomer, an echo of
Numbers 24:17; The Bright Morning Star treats what that promise gives.

**What is settled, and what is contested.** Settled: Numbers 24:17 and 24:19 promise a ruler of
Jacob's line, and Matthew 2:2 and Revelation 22:16 apply the Star to Jesus. Contested: whether the
oracle has a nearer fulfilment first. Many readers see David, who subdued Moab (2 Samuel 8:2).
The words "not now, not near" point past David to someone further off, and the New Testament
names Jesus. The reading held here is that the Star rose at Bethlehem and the Scepter will strike
at His return.

### Read as a king before Bethlehem

Jews read the oracle as a promise of a coming king before the apostles did. One sheet from Cave 4
at Qumran, 4Q175 (the *Testimonia*), copies out four passages in a row: Deuteronomy 5:28-29 and
18:18-19, the promise of a prophet like Moses; Numbers 24:15-17; Deuteronomy 33:8-11, the blessing
on Levi; and Joshua 6:26. Scholars read the sheet as a collection of messianic texts and date its
handwriting to the Hasmonean period, when Jewish kings of the Maccabean family ruled Judea. The
estimates run from the late second century BC into the first. Kramer dates the Cave 4 copy he shows
to 125 BC. Balaam's Star stands beside the prophet like Moses. In the second century AD Rabbi
Akiba hailed Simon bar Kosiba as *Bar Kokhba*, "son of the star", on the strength of this verse
(*NIV Cultural Backgrounds Study Bible*, note on Numbers 24:17). The Magi who came to Jerusalem
asked a question in the same vocabulary:

> ✝️ Matthew 2:1-2 (BSB)
>
> 1 After Jesus was born in Bethlehem in Judea, during the time of King Herod, Magi from the east arrived in Jerusalem, 2 asking, "Where is the One who has been born King of the Jews? We saw His star in the east and have come to worship Him."

A Gentile seer announces the Star in Numbers, and Magi from the east come looking for Him in
Matthew.

## How Scripture remembers Balaam

Balaam is named from Numbers to Revelation. The Old Testament and the New Testament remember him
in two ways, and the two belong together.

[![Chart of how Scripture remembers Balaam. The Old Testament remembers what the LORD did: Deuteronomy 23:4-5, Joshua 24:9-10, Nehemiah 13:1-2 and Micah 6:5. The New Testament warns what Balaam did: the way of Balaam in 2 Peter 2:15-16, the error of Balaam in Jude 11, the teaching of Balaam in Revelation 2:14, and, dashed because it names Peor only, 1 Corinthians 10:8 and 11.](../assets/img/balaam/balaam-remembered.svg)](../assets/img/balaam/balaam-remembered.svg)

### The Old Testament: the blessing remembered

**The Old Testament remembers what the LORD did with his mouth.** Moses tells the next generation
that the LORD "turned the curse into a blessing" (Deuteronomy 23:5, BSB), and Nehemiah's generation
hears it read aloud again (Nehemiah 13:1-2). Joshua gathers Israel at Shechem and speaks "what the
LORD, the God of Israel, says" (Joshua 24:1-2, BSB). In that speech the LORD takes the credit in the
first person:

> ✝️ Joshua 24:9-10 (BSB)
>
> 9 Then Balak son of Zippor, the king of Moab, set out to fight against Israel. He sent for Balaam son of Beor to curse you, 10 but I would not listen to Balaam. So he blessed you again and again, and I delivered you from his hand.

Micah 6:5 gives the lesson to carry: "remember what Balak king of Moab counseled and what Balaam son
of Beor answered. Remember your journey from Shittim to Gilgal, so that you may acknowledge the
righteousness of the LORD" (BSB).

### The New Testament: Balaam's counsel and wages

**The New Testament remembers what Balaam did with his own counsel.** The mouth belonged to the
LORD, and the counsel belonged to Balaam. After the oracles "Balaam arose and returned to his
homeland" (Numbers 24:25, BSB). In Numbers 31 he is among the dead (Numbers 31:8), and Moses names
the reason: "these
women caused the sons of Israel, through the counsel of Balaam, to turn unfaithfully against the
LORD at Peor, so that the plague struck the congregation of the LORD" (Numbers 31:16, BSB). Jesus,
speaking to Pergamum, says some "hold to the teaching of Balaam, who taught Balak to place a
stumbling block before the Israelites so they would eat food sacrificed to idols and commit sexual
immorality" (Revelation 2:14, BSB). The sin of Numbers 25 stands in the same place: "Israel joined
in worshiping Baal of Peor, and the anger of the LORD burned against them" (Numbers 25:3, BSB). Peor
is the ridge from which the third blessing was spoken, and the same hill held the idol.

Peter and Jude draw the line to the church. False teachers "left the straight way" to follow "the
way of Balaam son of Beor" (2 Peter 2:15, BSB), and they
"rushed for profit into the error of Balaam" (Jude 11, BSB). Paul sets Peor among Israel's warnings
(1 Corinthians 10:8), "written down as warnings for us" (1 Corinthians 10:11, BSB). For the
New Testament warning in full, see [Test the Spirits](../christian-life/test-the-spirits.md), and
for his trade, [Sorcery](../sin/sorcery.md).

### The blessing and the warning together

**The lesson across all of it.** God's blessing covers His people against every curse from
outside, and He claims that victory as His own (Joshua 24:10). The blessing gives no licence to join
the feast at Peor. A people no one can curse can still be seduced, and the LORD judges that
(Numbers 25:3). Balaam spoke a prophecy of Christ and went home to plan for the wages. Hold the blessing, and keep close to the One who gave it. For the Star among
Jesus' names, see [Names of Jesus](../jesus/names-of-jesus.md).

## Discussion questions

1. **The language.** In Numbers 22:6 Balak tells Balaam "those you bless are blessed, and those you
   curse are cursed" (BSB). In Numbers 24:9 the same verbs, *barak* and *arar*, speak to Israel:
   "Blessed are those who bless you and cursed are those who curse you" (BSB). Who is the "you" in
   each verse, and what does the change show about where the power sits?
2. **The text in its context.** God says "get up and go with them" (Numbers 22:20, BSB) and is angry
   that Balaam goes (Numbers 22:22). What do Numbers 22:5-19 and 2 Peter 2:15 give you for holding
   the two together?
3. **Christ.** Revelation 22:16 calls Jesus both the Root and the Offspring of David, and Matthew
   22:42-45 asks how David's son is David's Lord. What does Numbers 24:17 say about Him once you hold
   both?
4. **The hard part.** Balaam speaks by the Spirit of God (Numbers 24:2) and later counsels Israel
   into the sin at Peor (Numbers 31:16). How do the texts hold a true word from a man whose motives were
   bad?
5. **The donkey.** Balaam's donkey saw the angel three times before Balaam did (Numbers 22:23-31).
   Who in your life has seen something that you were too sure of yourself to see, and what did it
   take for you to listen?
6. **Be transformed.** Micah 6:5 tells Israel to "remember what Balak king of Moab counseled and what
   Balaam son of Beor answered" (BSB). What was meant against you that the LORD has turned (Deuteronomy
   23:5), and who do you need to tell this week?

## Annex: Balaam's name on a wall at Deir Alla

A wall in the Jordan Valley remembers Balaam by name. In 1967 excavators at Tell Deir Alla found
fragments of ink-written plaster whose first line names "Balaam the son of Beor ... the man who was a
seer of the gods" (as the *ESV Study Bible* note on Numbers 22:1-6 quotes it). The writing is dated to
the ninth or eighth century BC, and Kramer shows the piece in a Jordanian museum. It confirms the man,
a seer the surrounding peoples remembered. The published readings of the fragments do not repeat the
story of Numbers 22-24, and Numbers is what this study stands on. The full entry, with what is debated, is in
[Ancient Texts and Manuscripts](../scripture/ancient-texts-manuscripts.md#12-the-deir-alla-inscription-c-800-bc).

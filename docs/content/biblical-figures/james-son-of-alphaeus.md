# James son of Alphaeus

He occupies position nine in all four lists of the Twelve, at the head of the final group of four —
a fixed structural position no other apostle except Peter and Philip holds so consistently. And that
is the entire biblical record. He never speaks, never acts, and is never described.

## Key Takeaways

### Lessons about Jesus

Jesus chose twelve, and he did not select for prominence. A man about whom Scripture says nothing
beyond his father's name holds the same apostolic office as Peter and will have his name on a
foundation of the city (Revelation 21:14).

### Memory verse

> ✝️ Acts 1:13 (ESV)
>
> 13 And when they had entered, they went up to the upper room, where they were staying, Peter and John and James and Andrew, Philip and Thomas, Bartholomew and Matthew, James the son of Alphaeus and Simon the Zealot and Judas the son of James.

### Be transformed

The instinct to fill this silence — to find him a martyrdom, a mission field, another identity — is
worth resisting and worth examining. If a life is only meaningful once it has a story attached, that
is a standard the New Testament plainly does not apply to a quarter of the apostles.

### Prayer

Lord, you know the names of those whose work no one recorded. Where we are serving without notice,
keep us from needing it. It is enough to be chosen and to be with you.

## The name

<span lang="grc">Ἰάκωβος</span> (*Iakōbos*) is the Greek form of **Jacob**,
<span dir="rtl">יַעֲקֹב</span> *Yaʿaqob*, "heel-grabber, supplanter."

**Alphaeus** — <span lang="grc">Ἁλφαῖος</span> (*Halphaios*, hal-FAH-yoss) — is of uncertain
derivation; Strong's offers only a tentative Hebrew comparison. He is identified by his father
because there were two apostles named James, and no other means of distinguishing them was needed.

## What Scripture records

Four verses. Matthew 10:3, Mark 3:18, Luke 6:15, Acts 1:13 — the four lists.

That is all. He is present at the appointment on the mountain, he is sent out with the others, and
he is in the upper room after the ascension waiting for the Spirit. He does what all twelve did and
none of it separately.

## Three identifications, and why to resist them

Because the silence is uncomfortable, this James has attracted more speculative identification than
almost any other apostle. Each proposal is stated and declined below.

**Is he "James the younger"?** Mark 15:40 names, among the women at the crucifixion, "Mary the
mother of James the younger and of Joses." The Greek is <span lang="grc">Ἰάκωβος ὁ μικρός</span>
(*ho mikros*) — "the small" or "the lesser," which may refer to age or stature. Equating him with
James son of Alphaeus is a traditional guess. Mark does not make the connection, and *mikros* is a
distinguishing tag, not a name.

**Is he James the brother of Jesus?** No. This is the most consequential confusion and the easiest
to dismiss. James the Lord's brother led the Jerusalem church (Acts 15) and is called by Paul "James
the Lord's brother" (Galatians 1:19) — and John 7:5 says plainly that Jesus' brothers did not
believe in him during his ministry. He therefore cannot have been among the Twelve appointed in Mark
3. Two different men.

**Was he Matthew's brother?** Mark 2:14 calls Matthew "Levi the son of Alphaeus," and this James is
also son of an Alphaeus. But no list groups them — they sit in different groups of four — no text
calls them brothers, and Scripture is elsewhere careful to say so when apostles *are* brothers
(Peter and Andrew, James and John, both flagged explicitly). The silence here, against that pattern,
argues against the identification rather than for it.

The honest position: Alphaeus was not a rare name, and coincidence of patronymic is not evidence.

## What tradition says

Later traditions assign him ministry in Syria or Egypt and various martyrdoms, but they are late,
inconsistent, and badly contaminated by the confusion with James the Lord's brother — whose death
*is* independently reported by Josephus, and which has repeatedly been transferred to this James in
retellings. Very little survives scrutiny.

## Takeaway

Nine of the Twelve never speak a recorded word, and this James is the most completely silent of
them. He was chosen, he was there, and his name is on a foundation of the New Jerusalem. Scripture
evidently did not consider the rest necessary to record.

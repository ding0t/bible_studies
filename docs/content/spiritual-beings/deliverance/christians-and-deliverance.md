# Deliverance ministry

Deliverance ministry sets out to free believers from demonic influence. Approach it with caution.
Its methods can lead a believer into the spiritual confusion it promises to resolve.

**In one sentence:** God delivered you from the domain of darkness when He brought you into the
kingdom of His Son Jesus (Colossians 1:13), and the Holy Spirit lives in you, so no demon can indwell
you; it can attack you from outside, and you answer it by submitting to God and resisting the devil
(James 4:7).

## Summary of findings

- Demons are real, and Scripture takes them seriously.
- Demons can possess or control the body of a non-believer.
- A Christian cannot be possessed or indwelt by a demon, because the Holy Spirit already indwells
  them. A Christian can be oppressed or attacked from the outside.
- The Greek word behind "demonized," δαιμονίζομαι, never describes a believer.
- Scripture's word for deliverance, ῥύομαι, never describes an exorcism. A believer's deliverance
  was won at the cross, became theirs when they believed, and will be completed when Christ brings
  them into His kingdom.
- The texts offered for a "demonized" believer describe temptation, deception or affliction from
  outside, and the Epistles answer all of it with one verb: resist.
- Christians who "manifest" in deliverance sessions have usually been led first through
  self-emptying (passivity, opening the mind, surrendering control), which Scripture warns against.
- Some deliverance practices rest on what a demon "said" during a session. Those claims have no
  source in Scripture.

## What deliverance ministry teaches

### The preparation

As commonly practised, deliverance ministry has the believer work toward a freedom Christ has
already given. A typical preparatory process looks like this:

> This is the preparatory work done before deliverance — it is not the deliverance itself:
>
> - Confirm all your confessions are current.
> - Reveal family secrets.
> - Identify any historical addiction or abuse.
> - Identify any occult activity.

### What it adds to the cross

- Proponents affirm Jesus' death on the cross, and describe deliverance as "appropriating the work
  of Jesus on the cross." The concern is what they add on top of the atonement.
- The teaching implies there is "more" to receive from God than Christ's finished work has
  accomplished.
- It takes biblical principles and twists them, and often blends them with therapeutic language:
  inner healing, generational trauma, soul care.

## The key term: "demonized"

The whole case for a Christian coming under demonic control turns on one word:
**δαιμονίζομαι** (*daimonizomai*, dye-mo-NID-zoh-my, G1139). Deliverance teaching treats
"possessed" and "demonized" as two conditions. It concedes the first cannot happen to a believer and
claims the second can. The Greek has no such distinction. Both English words translate one verb,
and the New Testament has no second word for a lesser condition.

### Where the word comes from

In classical Greek δαίμων was a fairly neutral term: a divine or semi-divine spirit that need not be
malevolent. By the time of the Septuagint the word family had taken on a sharply negative sense. The
"gods" of the pagan nations are called δαιμόνια (Deuteronomy 32:17; Psalm 96:5 LXX). Second Temple
Jewish literature (1 Enoch, Jubilees; see [The Zadok Calendar](../../feasts/zadok-calendar.md) for
more on this literature) develops a fuller demonology behind that usage. The verb δαιμονίζομαι itself
has little history outside the New Testament, so its meaning comes from how the New Testament uses
it.

### How the New Testament uses it

The word occurs 13 times in the New Testament (7 in Matthew, 4 in Mark, 1 in Luke, 1 in John), all
of them in the Gospels. Acts and the Epistles discuss spiritual conflict at length in other
vocabulary. Every occurrence but John 10:21 (below):

- describes someone brought to or encountered by Jesus during His earthly, pre-Pentecost ministry
  (the Gadarene demoniac in Matthew 8, Mark 5 and Luke 8, the mute man in Matthew 9:32, the
  blind-and-mute man in Matthew 12:22, the Canaanite woman's daughter in Matthew 15:22, and so on);
- describes someone who is *not* a follower of Jesus.

### Luke's one use: the man "was saved"

Luke uses the word once, and in the same clause he says what happened to the man: "those who had
seen it told them how the demon-possessed man had been healed" (Luke 8:36, ESV). "The
demon-possessed man" is ὁ δαιμονισθείς. "Had been healed" is ἐσώθη (*esōthē*), from σῴζω (*sōzō*,
SOH-zoh, G4982), which means both to heal and to save. It is the verb of Luke 19:10: "the Son of Man
came to seek and to save the lost" (ESV). In Luke the demonized man is a man Jesus rescued, and the
word describes his life before Jesus found him.

### John 10: an onlooker's claim

The one instance outside the healing narratives comes in John 10. At John 10:20 a hostile crowd says
of Jesus Himself, "He has a demon, and is insane; why listen to him?" (ESV; the Greek is δαιμόνιον
ἔχει, "has a demon"). Others reply in John 10:21, "These are not the words of one who is oppressed by
a demon. Can a demon open the eyes of the blind?" (ESV). The verb δαιμονίζομαι is in that reply, as a
participle (δαιμονιζομένου). The crowd's accusation is false. The term is an onlooker's claim about
someone's state, and it carries no clinical category with agreed boundaries.

### Paul's word for a believer harassed

Paul describes a "messenger of Satan" sent to harass him in 2 Corinthians 12:7 (ESV). The verb he
uses is **κολαφίζω** (*kolaphizō*, koh-lah-FEED-zoh, G2852, "to buffet, strike"). It occurs five
times in the New Testament, and the other four describe human blows: Jesus struck at His trial
(Matthew 26:67; Mark 14:65), Paul's own treatment (1 Corinthians 4:11) and a slave beaten (1 Peter
2:20). When Paul described a believer afflicted by a demonic messenger, he chose a word for blows
from outside. One verse suggests this more than it proves it.

### What the word establishes

δαιμονίζομαι is Gospel-era vocabulary. It describes people outside the community of Jesus'
followers, whom He heals as evidence of His authority over the kingdom of darkness: "If it is by the
Spirit of God that I cast out demons, then the kingdom of God has come upon you" (Matthew 12:28,
ESV). Scripture never applies it to a believer. The one time Scripture describes a believer under
demonic harassment, it uses different language. Nothing in the word's usage makes "demonized" a
distinct, lesser condition a believer can be in. That is a redefinition dressed up as a translation
nuance.

## A demon cannot indwell a Christian

Scripture records no case of an evil spirit living in, or controlling, a believer. Deliverance
ministry separates "demonized" from "possessed" in name and treats them alike in practice: an evil
spirit is inside the person and must be expelled. Three things in Scripture rule that out.

### 1. Scripture never casts a demon out of a believer

No passage shows an evil spirit driven out of a believer. The Spirit's gifts point the same way.
They are given to build up the church, and none of them is for exorcising believers.

> ✝️ 1 Corinthians 12:1-11 (ESV)
>
> Now concerning spiritual gifts, brothers, I do not want you to be uninformed... there are varieties of gifts, but the same Spirit... to another the ability to distinguish between spirits... All these are empowered by one and the same Spirit, who apportions to each one individually as he wills.

### 2. You belong to God

A believer is God's own possession.

> ✝️ Ephesians 1:14 (ESV)
>
> 14 who is the guarantee of our inheritance until we acquire possession of it, to the praise of his glory.

> ✝️ 1 Peter 2:9 (ESV)
>
> 9 But you are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for his own possession, that you may proclaim the excellencies of him who called you out of darkness into his marvelous light.

> ✝️ 1 Corinthians 3:23 (ESV)
>
> 23 and you are Christ's, and Christ is God's.

The One who owns you keeps you:

> ✝️ 1 John 5:18 (ESV)
>
> 18 We know that everyone who has been born of God does not keep on sinning, but he who was born of God protects him, and the evil one does not touch him.

The ESV Study Bible and the NIV Biblical Theology Study Bible both read "he who was born of God" as
Jesus (notes on 1 John 5:18). Manuscripts differ on whether He protects "him" or the believer keeps
"himself" (CSB footnote), and the promise at the end of the verse stands on either reading. "Touch"
is ἅπτεται (*haptetai*, HAP-teh-tie, G680), "to lay hold of". The ESV Study Bible limits it to
"causing permanent spiritual loss": a believer can still be attacked, as Paul was, and cannot be
taken.

God has already delivered you:

> ✝️ Colossians 1:12-13 (ESV)
>
> 12 giving thanks to the Father, who has qualified you to share in the inheritance of the saints in light. 13 He has delivered us from the domain of darkness and transferred us to the kingdom of his beloved Son.

God delivered you when He transferred you into His Son's kingdom. It isn't a session to book.
[What Scripture calls deliverance](#what-scripture-calls-deliverance) follows the verb through the
New Testament.

### 3. The Holy Spirit lives in you

The Spirit in you is greater than the spirit of the world, and He has made you new.

> ✝️ 1 John 4:4 (ESV)
>
> 4 Little children, you are from God and have overcome them, for he who is in you is greater than he who is in the world.

> ✝️ 2 Corinthians 5:17-18 (ESV)
>
> 17 Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. The old has passed away; behold, the new has come. 18 All this is from God, who through Christ reconciled us to himself and gave us the ministry of reconciliation.

#### Where could a demon indwell?

*Not the body.*

> ✝️ 1 Corinthians 6:19-20 (ESV)
>
> 19 Or do you not know that your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit within you, whom you have from God? You are not your own, 20 for you were bought with a price. So glorify God in your body.

*Not the spirit.*

> ✝️ 1 Corinthians 6:17 (ESV)
>
> 17 But he who is joined to the Lord becomes one spirit with him.

*Not the soul alone.* A possession of the soul apart from the body has no pattern in Scripture. At
the Gerasene demoniac's deliverance the spirits asked for bodies to enter, and the unclean spirit of
Matthew 12:43 wanders through places.

> ✝️ Mark 5:12 (ESV)
>
> 12 and they begged him, saying, "Send us to the pigs; let us enter them."

#### The empty house

Jesus tells this picture at the end of an argument about houses. Accused of casting out demons by
Beelzebul, He answers that "no city or house divided against itself will stand" (Matthew 12:25, ESV).
Then: "how can someone enter a strong man's house and plunder his goods, unless he first binds the
strong man?" (Matthew 12:29, ESV). His exorcisms are that plundering. The empty house comes last.

> ✝️ Matthew 12:43-44 (ESV)
>
> 43 "When the unclean spirit has gone out of a person, it passes through waterless places seeking rest, but finds none. 44 Then it says, 'I will return to my house from which I came.' And when it comes, it finds the house empty, swept, and put in order."

Matthew 12 gives a house three states: held by the strong man, swept and left empty, or taken by the
One who bound him. Jesus applies the empty house first to a nation: "So also will it be with this
evil generation" (Matthew 12:45, ESV), a generation that watched His exorcisms and would not receive
Him. The picture still describes a person, since "a person" is what the spirit leaves (Matthew
12:43), and the application here draws on that. There is a place in a person made for the Holy
Spirit. Left empty, it is a place a demon can occupy. A believer is the third house: the Spirit
fills it, and the strong man is bound.

So the danger of the swept house falls first on the unbeliever in a deliverance session. A spirit
driven out of a life that is never given to Christ leaves the house empty. For the believer, the
danger is the self-emptying itself. Scripture asks for the opposite posture before the devil: "Be
sober-minded; be watchful" (1 Peter 5:8, ESV), and "be transformed by the renewal of your mind"
(Romans 12:2, ESV). Paul's fear for believers was that their "thoughts will be led astray from a
sincere and pure devotion to Christ" (2 Corinthians 11:3, ESV). A practice that switches the mind off
opens it to that.

## The texts offered for a demonized believer

Deliverance teaching rests its case on a handful of texts. Each deserves a hearing in its context.

### "Give no opportunity to the devil" (Ephesians 4:27)

**The case for.** The Greek word is τόπος (*topos*, TOP-os, G5117), "place", and deliverance
teaching reads it as ground in a believer that a demon can occupy.

**The case against.** "Give place" is a Greek idiom for making room or giving opportunity. Paul uses
the same phrase in Romans 12:19, δότε τόπον τῇ ὀργῇ, which the ESV renders "leave it to the wrath of
God." Luke uses τόπος for an accused man's "opportunity to make his defense" (Acts 25:16, ESV), and
Hebrews says Esau "found no chance to repent" (Hebrews 12:17, ESV). Paul's subject is anger: "Be
angry and do not sin; do not let the sun go down on your anger, and give no opportunity to the
devil" (Ephesians 4:26-27, ESV). His remedy is to settle the anger before sunset. Ephesians 4:27
warns against handing the devil an opportunity through anger left unresolved.

### "Satan filled your heart" (Acts 5:3)

Peter asks, "Ananias, why has Satan filled your heart to lie to the Holy Spirit" (Acts 5:3, ESV).

**The case for.** Ananias stood among "the full number of those who believed" (Acts 4:32, ESV), and
"filled" sounds like occupation.

**The case against.** Whether Ananias was a believer is contested; Luke introduces him as a contrast
to Barnabas (Acts 4:36-5:1) and never calls him one. The phrase itself describes influence on the
heart. John uses the same verb with the same object, πληρόω (*plēroō*, play-RO-oh, G4137) with
καρδία, "heart", when Jesus tells the disciples, "sorrow has filled your heart" (John 16:6, ESV).
Peter's next question makes the act Ananias's own: "Why is it that you have contrived this deed in
your heart?" (Acts 5:4, ESV). Peter casts nothing out. He names a sin, and God judges it.

### "A daughter of Abraham whom Satan bound" (Luke 13:16)

**The case for.** A woman who "glorified God" (Luke 13:13, ESV) had been bound by Satan for eighteen
years (Luke 13:16).

**The case against.** "Daughter of Abraham" names her as an Israelite, as "son of Abraham" names
Zacchaeus (Luke 19:9). She was healed before Pentecost, when "as yet the Spirit had not been given,
because Jesus was not yet glorified" (John 7:39, ESV). And Jesus addresses no spirit. He tells her,
"Woman, you are freed from your disability," and lays His hands on her (Luke 13:12-13, ESV).

### "Get behind me, Satan!" (Matthew 16:23)

Jesus said this to Peter, His disciple, and gave the reason: "you are not setting your mind on the
things of God, but on the things of man" (Matthew 16:23, ESV). Satan worked on Peter through his
thinking, and Jesus answered with a rebuke. Nothing was cast out of Peter.

The one member of the Twelve whom Satan "entered" was Judas (Luke 22:3; John 13:27). Jesus had
already said of him, "one of you is a devil" (John 6:70, ESV).

### "A different spirit" (2 Corinthians 11:4)

Paul warns the Corinthians that they "receive a different spirit from the one you received"
(2 Corinthians 11:4, ESV). The phrase stands between "another Jesus" and "a different gospel" in the
same sentence. The danger is false teaching, and the test is the gospel they first received.

### What the texts show

Each text describes temptation, deception or affliction from outside. Each is answered by a rebuke,
by repentance, by the truth or by Jesus' healing. None shows a spirit cast out of someone the Holy
Spirit indwells.

## What Scripture calls deliverance

"Delivered" in Colossians 1:13 is ῥύομαι (*rhyomai*, HROO-oh-my, G4506), "to rescue". It occurs in
fifteen verses of the New Testament, and none of them describes an exorcism. The Gospels use other
verbs for that: Jesus "cast out" demons (Matthew 12:28) and they "came out" (Luke 8:33). ῥύομαι is
rescue from enemies (Luke 1:74), from death (2 Corinthians 1:10), from the domain of darkness
(Colossians 1:13) and from the wrath to come (1 Thessalonians 1:10). Paul uses it in every tense:

- **Done.** "He has delivered us from the domain of darkness" (Colossians 1:13, ESV), the aorist
  ἐρρύσατο: a rescue completed when God brought you into His Son's kingdom. It was won at the cross,
  where God canceled "the record of debt that stood against us with its legal demands" and
  "disarmed the rulers and authorities" (Colossians 2:14-15, ESV).
- **Continuing.** "Jesus who delivers us from the wrath to come" (1 Thessalonians 1:10, ESV), the
  present participle ῥυόμενον. Jesus taught His disciples to ask for it: "deliver us from evil"
  (Matthew 6:13, ESV).
- **Still to come.** "The Lord will rescue me from every evil deed and bring me safely into his
  heavenly kingdom" (2 Timothy 4:18, ESV), the future ῥύσεται.

One verse holds all three: "He delivered us from such a deadly peril, and he will deliver us. On him
we have set our hope that he will deliver us again" (2 Corinthians 1:10, ESV).

So Scripture answers the question "when is a believer delivered?" The victory was won at the cross.
It became yours when you believed. You pray for it as Jesus taught, and God will complete it when He
brings you into His kingdom. In every tense God is the One who delivers.

## How a believer stands

The Epistles tell believers what to do about the devil, and three apostles use one verb. James:
"Resist the devil, and he will flee from you" (James 4:7, ESV). Peter: "Resist him, firm in your
faith" (1 Peter 5:9, ESV). Paul: "take up the whole armor of God, that you may be able to withstand
in the evil day" (Ephesians 6:13, ESV). Each is ἀνθίστημι (*anthistēmi*, anth-IS-tay-mee, G436), "to
stand against". It is the word for Paul's opposition to Peter at Antioch: "I opposed him to his
face" (Galatians 2:11, ESV). Resistance is face to face, and you do it yourself, in the armour God
gives.

Peter puts it in a posture: "Be sober-minded; be watchful" (1 Peter 5:8, ESV), νήψατε, γρηγορήσατε.
None of the three letters tells a believer to have the devil cast out of them. Each tells the
believer to stand.

## Accounting for manifestations

Christians in deliverance sessions do shake, cough and cry out. The session's own method, its
passivity, suggestion and expectation, is enough to produce these signs. So the question is whether
the signs are demonic or a predictable response to the method. Where a spirit is involved in a
believer's distress, it is oppressing them from outside;
[Deliverance and the Edge of Sorcery](deliverance-and-the-edge-of-sorcery.md#when-a-believer-manifests)
sets out the explanations.

Christians can wander unawares into practices closer to [sorcery](../../sin/sorcery.md) than to
biblical prayer and confession. Some diagnostic techniques, such as naming specific "spirits" by
discernment or tracing generational curses, sit close to the divination Scripture condemns
(Deuteronomy 18:10-11). Sincere Christian intent does not change what the practice is.

## What this shows about God

God does not leave His children half-delivered. He "has delivered us from the domain of darkness and
transferred us to the kingdom of his beloved Son" (Colossians 1:13, ESV), and He has given you the
Holy Spirit as the guarantee of your inheritance (Ephesians 1:13-14). No demon can possess, indwell
or control you.

You can be attacked, harassed and tempted from outside, as Paul was (2 Corinthians 12:7). Submit
yourself to God and resist the devil, and he will flee from you (James 4:7). Your deliverance was
won at the cross, it became yours when you believed, and God will finish it. It belongs to everyone
who is in Christ.

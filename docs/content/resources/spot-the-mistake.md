# Spot the Mistake

**In one sentence:** each answer below reads like a good one and holds exactly one wrong sentence,
and you can find every one of them with your Bible open and the habits in
[Guiding AI in Bible Study](guiding-ai-in-bible-study.md).

!!! warning "Every answer on this page contains a false sentence on purpose"

    Quote nothing from the answers below. The explanations under each one say what the text
    actually reads.

Tap the sentence you think is wrong. Tap a reference to read the verse first: that is the habit the
page is practising. Most of these mistakes were made on this site, by the AI that helps write it, and
corrected before or after publishing.

## 1. The bread of life

**A reader asks:** What did the crowd ask Jesus for after He fed the five thousand?

<div class="spot-answer">
<p><span class="spot-s">The next day the crowd crossed to Capernaum looking for Jesus (John 6:24-25).</span>
<span class="spot-s">He told them they were seeking Him because they "ate their fill of the loaves" (John 6:26, ESV).</span>
<span class="spot-s" data-wrong>They answered, "Lord, give us this bread always" (John 6:34, ESV).</span>
<span class="spot-s">Jesus replied, "I am the bread of life" (John 6:35, ESV).</span></p>
</div>

??? question spot-reveal "The mistake"

    **"Lord, give us this bread always."** The ESV reads "Sir, give us this bread always" (John
    6:34). The Greek Κύριε (*kyrie*, KOO-ree-eh, G2962) can mean either, and the ESV renders it
    "Sir" here. A draft on this site quoted the verse from the assistant's memory, and the wrong word
    sat inside quotation marks with a translation named.

    **The habit:** [make it quote, and name the translation](guiding-ai-in-bible-study.md#make-it-quote-and-name-the-translation).

## 2. Heaven and earth

**A reader asks:** Is Revelation 21:1 picking up Jesus' words in Matthew 24:35?

<div class="spot-answer">
<p><span class="spot-s">Jesus says, "Heaven and earth will pass away, but my words will not pass away" (Matthew 24:35, ESV).</span>
<span class="spot-s">John sees "a new heaven and a new earth, for the first heaven and the first earth had passed away" (Revelation 21:1, ESV).</span>
<span class="spot-s" data-wrong>John uses the same Greek verb as Jesus, parerchomai, so the vision deliberately answers the saying.</span>
<span class="spot-s">Isaiah 65:17 had already promised "new heavens and a new earth" (ESV).</span></p>
</div>

??? question spot-reveal "The mistake"

    **"The same Greek verb."** Jesus' verb is παρέρχομαι (*parerchomai*, par-ER-kho-my, G3928), and
    it occurs nowhere in Revelation. Revelation 21:1 has ἀπῆλθαν, from ἀπέρχομαι (*aperchomai*,
    ap-ER-kho-my, G565). English gives both "pass away", so the link looks real in translation.
    While writing on the Olivet Discourse, the author wanted this link, and a search of the Greek
    came back empty.

    **The habit:** [ask for counts it made](guiding-ai-in-bible-study.md#ask-for-counts-it-made).

## 3. Noah and the rapture

**A reader asks:** Is Noah a picture of the rapture?

<div class="spot-answer">
<p><span class="spot-s">Jesus compares His coming to "the days of Noah" (Matthew 24:37, ESV).</span>
<span class="spot-s">Enoch was taken before the flood: "he was not, for God took him" (Genesis 5:24, ESV).</span>
<span class="spot-s" data-wrong>Noah, too, was taken out of the world before judgment fell, which makes him the classic type of the rapture.</span>
<span class="spot-s">Hebrews 11:5 says Enoch "was taken up so that he should not see death" (ESV).</span></p>
</div>

??? question spot-reveal "The mistake"

    **"Noah, too, was taken out."** Noah went through the flood in the ark: "Only Noah was left, and
    those who were with him in the ark" (Genesis 7:23, ESV). Jesus' point of comparison is that the
    world was unprepared (Matthew 24:38-39). Enoch is the one taken before judgment, and Noah the one
    kept through it. This site's own rapture study once said Noah was removed in one section and
    preserved in four others, and each section passed review on its own.

    **The habit:** [check in a fresh conversation](guiding-ai-in-bible-study.md#check-in-a-fresh-conversation),
    and read the whole answer against itself.

## 4. "I am God"

**A reader asks:** Did Jesus ever claim to be God?

<div class="spot-answer">
<p><span class="spot-s">At the Feast of Dedication, in winter, Jesus was walking in the temple, in the colonnade of Solomon (John 10:22-23).</span>
<span class="spot-s">He said, "I and the Father are one" (John 10:30, ESV).</span>
<span class="spot-s">His hearers picked up stones, "because you, being a man, make yourself God" (John 10:31-33, ESV).</span>
<span class="spot-s" data-wrong>Jesus answered them, "Yes, I am God" (John 10:34).</span></p>
</div>

??? question spot-reveal "The mistake"

    **"Yes, I am God."** John 10:34 reads, "Is it not written in your Law, 'I said, you are gods'?"
    (ESV), quoting Psalm 82:6. The doctrine is true, and John 10:30-33 shows His hearers understood
    the claim. The sentence in quotation marks is in no verse. An assistant that condenses a speech
    can put its own summary in quotation marks, and a true doctrine makes the invented line harder to
    notice. This site's comics carry the rule for it: condense the speaker's words, and never compose
    new ones.

    **The habit:** [make it quote, and name the translation](guiding-ai-in-bible-study.md#make-it-quote-and-name-the-translation).

## 5. Who does the saving

**A reader asks:** What does Ephesians 2 say about who saves us?

<div class="spot-answer">
<p><span class="spot-s">God, "being rich in mercy," made us alive together with Christ (Ephesians 2:4-5, ESV).</span>
<span class="spot-s">"By grace you have been saved through faith" (Ephesians 2:8, ESV).</span>
<span class="spot-s" data-wrong>In Ephesians 2:4-9 every verb has God as its subject.</span>
<span class="spot-s">Salvation is "not a result of works, so that no one may boast" (Ephesians 2:9, ESV).</span></p>
</div>

??? question spot-reveal "The mistake"

    **"Every verb has God as its subject."** "Even when we were dead" (Ephesians 2:5) has "we", and
    "no one may boast" (Ephesians 2:9) has "no one". Each quotation in the answer is right, and the
    conclusion they support is right: salvation is "the gift of God" (Ephesians 2:8, ESV). The
    sentence about them overreaches, and a reader can test it in a minute. This site's study of grace
    made the same claim until a review corrected it.

    **The habit:** [check the claim as well as its parts](guiding-ai-in-bible-study.md#check-the-claim-as-well-as-its-parts).
    Words like "every", "only", "never" and "in order" are the cheapest to write and the first to check.

## 6. A promise for parents

**A reader asks:** Proverbs 22:6 promises my children will never leave the faith if I raise them
right, doesn't it?

<div class="spot-answer">
<p><span class="spot-s">Proverbs 22:6 reads, "Train up a child in the way he should go; even when he is old he will not depart from it" (ESV).</span>
<span class="spot-s" data-wrong>So yes: it is an unconditional promise, and a child raised this way cannot finally walk away.</span>
<span class="spot-s">Parents are to bring children up "in the discipline and instruction of the Lord" (Ephesians 6:4, ESV).</span></p>
</div>

??? question spot-reveal "The mistake"

    **"An unconditional promise."** A proverb states how life generally goes in God's world. The
    book sets two sayings side by side that point opposite ways (Proverbs 26:4-5), so it expects the
    reader to weigh them. And each person answers to God for himself: "The son shall not suffer for
    the iniquity of the father" (Ezekiel 18:20, ESV). The assistant agreed with the question as it
    was asked. The case against was one request away.

    **The habit:** [ask for the case against you](guiding-ai-in-bible-study.md#ask-for-the-case-against-you).

## 7. Twelve months to prepare

**A reader asks:** Did a Jewish bride in Jesus' day have time to get ready for her wedding?

<div class="spot-answer">
<p><span class="spot-s">A betrothal bound the couple before the wedding itself, which is why Joseph planned to divorce Mary quietly (Matthew 1:18-19).</span>
<span class="spot-s" data-wrong>The Mishnah gives the bride twelve months "to prepare herself with clothes and jewelry" (m. Ketubot 5:2).</span>
<span class="spot-s">The Mishnah was written down around AD 200, so it records custom as the rabbis remembered it.</span></p>
</div>

<p class="spot-hint">Hint: one quotation cannot be checked as it stands.</p>

??? question spot-reveal "The mistake"

    **"To prepare herself with clothes and jewelry."** The twelve months are in *m. Ketubot* 5:2.
    The quoted words come from one English translation, the William Davidson Edition, which a
    website served by default; Kulp's translation reads "to prepare herself for marriage". The answer
    names neither, so no reader could check the words. A study on this site quoted the first wording
    until a review re-fetched the passage from a named translation. The wrong edition and the wrong
    words arrived together.

    **The habit:** [name the edition of every other source](guiding-ai-in-bible-study.md#name-the-edition-of-every-other-source).

## 8. Abide in Me

**A reader asks:** What does "abide" mean in John 15?

<div class="spot-answer">
<p><span class="spot-s">Jesus says, "Abide in me, and I in you" (John 15:4, ESV).</span>
<span class="spot-s" data-wrong>Note what this is. It is not a mystical flourish; it is an invitation.</span>
<span class="spot-s">The verb is menō, "remain" or "stay", and it comes ten times in John 15:4-10.</span>
<span class="spot-s">A branch lives by staying joined to the vine, and so do you (John 15:4-5).</span></p>
</div>

??? question spot-reveal "The mistake"

    **"Note what this is."** Nothing in it is false, and nothing in it is needed. It announces a
    point, then sets aside a reading nobody proposed ("a mystical flourish"). Delete it and the
    answer loses no fact, no reference and no step of the argument. A sentence of this shape
    passed a full review of a study on this site, because no automatic check matched it. Its
    neighbours carry the meaning: μένω (*menō*, MEN-oh, G3306) occurs ten times in John 15:4-10.

    **The habit:** [ask for plain sentences](guiding-ai-in-bible-study.md#ask-for-plain-sentences).

## What the eight have in common

Every wrong sentence sat beside true ones and sounded as sure as they did. Each was caught by
opening the verse, asking for the count, naming the source, or asking for the case against. The
[prompt card](guiding-ai-in-bible-study.md#the-prompt-card) asks an assistant to do those things
from the start, and the checking stays yours: the Bereans were "examining the Scriptures daily to see
if these things were so" (Acts 17:11, ESV).

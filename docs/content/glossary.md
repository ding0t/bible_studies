# Glossary

A study will say a word is a *hapax legomenon*, or that MACULA gives the domain as such-and-such,
and carry on. This page is where to find out what that meant without leaving the sentence for long.

**Entries here are deliberately short — one or two sentences, enough to get you moving again.** Where
a term has a page of its own on this site, the entry ends with an arrow to it, and that page, not
this one, is the real explanation. Where no arrow follows, the entry is all there is to say.

## Reading and interpreting

**Apocalyptic**{ #apocalyptic } — A genre, not a subject: visionary literature that discloses God's
settled purpose through symbol, number and cosmic imagery. Daniel, Zechariah and Revelation are its
biblical instances, and reading its symbols as flat description is the commonest way to misread it.
→ [Reading by genre](scripture/how-to-read-the-bible.md#reading-by-genre)

**Antitype**{ #antitype } — The fulfilment that a type points forward to. Melchizedek is the type;
the priesthood of Christ is the antitype. See [typology](#typology).

**Chiasm, chiastic**{ #chiasm } — A passage arranged so its parts mirror around a centre
(A-B-C-B'-A'), which throws the weight onto the middle term. 2 Peter 3:8 is one in Greek — *one day
… a thousand years / a thousand years … one day*.
→ [A day is as a thousand years](last-things/day-is-a-thousand-years.md)

**Dispensationalism**{ #dispensationalism } — Reading Scripture as God dealing with humanity through
successive administrations, and holding that the promises made to Israel are not transferred to the
Church. It is this site's default framework. The name comes from Paul's own word for the present
arrangement, **οἰκονομία** (*oikonomia*, G3622) — the household-management of a thing, its
administration — which Ephesians 3:9 applies to "the plan of the mystery": an age with its own
terms, opened at the cross and not identical to the age before it or the one after.
→ [Israel and the Church](israel-and-church/israel-and-the-church.md#what-scripture-means-by-a-mystery)

**Eisegesis**{ #eisegesis } — Reading a meaning *into* a text that is not in it. Usually the result
of arriving with the conclusion already settled. The opposite failure to [exegesis](#exegesis).

**Eschatology**{ #eschatology } — The study of last things: death, resurrection, judgement, the
return of Christ, the millennium.
→ [Last Things](last-things/index.md)

**Exegesis**{ #exegesis } — Drawing out what a passage meant to the people who first received it,
before asking what it means now. On this site it always comes first.
→ [Ask these questions, in order](scripture/how-to-read-the-bible.md#ask-these-questions-in-order)

**Halakha**{ #halakha } — Jewish legal ruling: the accumulated body of how a commandment is to be
kept in practice, as distinct from the commandment itself.

**Hermeneutics**{ #hermeneutics } — The principles by which a text is interpreted and applied.
[Exegesis](#exegesis) asks what a passage meant; hermeneutics asks what follows from it.

**Inclusio**{ #inclusio } — A unit of text bracketed by the same word or phrase at its start and
end. The bracket marks where the unit begins and ends, and often names its theme.

**Koinonia**{ #koinonia } — Greek κοινωνία (*koinōnia*), "fellowship, sharing, participation" — the
common life of believers and their share in Christ (Acts 2:42).

**Liturgy**{ #liturgy } — The fixed order and wording of public worship. A neutral term: synagogue
and church both have one, whether or not anybody has written it down.

**Midrash**{ #midrash } — Jewish interpretive writing that expounds a biblical text, often by story
and association rather than by argument. Evidence for how a text was heard, not a witness to what it
says. → [Jewish sources](resources/jewish-sources.md)

**Pericope**{ #pericope } — A self-contained unit of text — one story, one saying — as against a
chapter, which is a medieval navigation aid laid over the top. This site's database carries the
Masoretic paragraph markers, so a unit can be read where the text divides it rather than where the
printers did. → [The two databases](about/about-our-datasets.md#the-two-databases-and-what-is-in-them)

**Propitiation**{ #propitiation } — The turning aside of God's wrath by a sacrifice that bears it.
Distinguished from *expiation*, which covers the sin without naming what the sin provoked.

**Protoevangelium**{ #protoevangelium } — "First gospel": Genesis 3:15, the promise that the
woman's offspring will crush the serpent's head, read as the earliest announcement of Christ.

**Seder**{ #seder } — The ordered Passover meal — *seder* is Hebrew for "order" — whose sequence of
cups, bread and telling is the frame the Last Supper accounts assume.
→ [The four cups of Passover](feasts/last-supper-four-cups.md#historical-context-the-four-cups-of-passover)

**Soteriology**{ #soteriology } — The study of salvation: how God saves, on what basis, and what is
secured by it. → [Salvation](salvation/index.md)

**Typology**{ #typology } — Reading a person, institution or event as a divinely intended pattern of
something later: the *type*, fulfilled in its [antitype](#antitype). Sound where Scripture itself
draws the line, guesswork where it does not.
→ [Prophecy essentials](last-things/prophecy-essentials.md)

## Words in the original languages

These are the layers of information attached to each word of the Hebrew and Greek text, and the page
that owns them works Genesis 1:1 through every one.

**Alignment**{ #alignment } — Which original word each English word is rendering. It turns "which
Hebrew word is this?" from a guess into a lookup, and it is the only thing on this list that links a
translation to its source word by word.
→ [Alignment](scripture/original-language-data.md#alignment)

**Construct state**{ #construct-state } — Hebrew's way of saying "X of Y" — the first noun takes a
shortened bound form rather than a preposition. *Bet* ("house") plus *lehem* ("bread") gives
Bethlehem, "house of bread".

**Coreference**{ #coreference } — Annotation recording which word refers back to which earlier one:
who "he" is. Coverage is partial, so a blank means *not annotated*, never *no referent*.

**Gloss**{ #gloss } — A very short English equivalent, for orientation only. A gloss is not a
translation and not a definition — it is a label, so you can tell which word you are looking at.
→ [Gloss](scripture/original-language-data.md#gloss)

**Hapax legomenon**{ #hapax-legomenon } — A word occurring only once in a given body of text. Weight it
carefully: with no second occurrence there is no internal control on what the author meant by it.

**Lemma, lemmatised**{ #lemma } — The lemma is a word's dictionary form, the form you would look up.
A *lemmatised* text is one where somebody has identified that form for every word, which is what
makes a real word study possible — you can ask where else an author uses a word, whatever shape it
was inflected into.
→ [Lemma](scripture/original-language-data.md#lemma-and-what-lemmatised-means)

**Morphology, parsing**{ #morphology } — What grammatical form a particular occurrence is in, written
as a code: `Vqp3ms` is a verb, qal stem, perfect, 3rd person masculine singular. It matters whenever
an argument turns on the form rather than the word.
→ [Morphology](scripture/original-language-data.md#morphology-parsing)

**Semantic domain**{ #semantic-domain } — A code placing a word in a category of meaning, from a
scholarly classification ([Louw-Nida](#louw-nida) for Greek, [SDBH](#sdbh) for Hebrew). It answers
what a dictionary cannot: which other words mean something like this one, including words that share
no letters with it.
→ [Semantic domain](scripture/original-language-data.md#semantic-domain)

**Strong's number**{ #strongs-number } — A number identifying a word in *Strong's Concordance*
(1890), H for Hebrew and G for Greek. Sound as an identifier and unreliable as a definition — its
glosses are Victorian, but its numbers are the common index that lets different resources talk about
the same word. → [Strong's numbers](scripture/original-language-data.md#strongs-numbers)

**Surface form**{ #surface-form } — The word exactly as it stands in the text, inflected, with its
vowel points and accents. What you read, and nearly useless for searching.
→ [Surface form](scripture/original-language-data.md#surface-form)

## Texts, manuscripts and witnesses

**Codex**{ #codex } — A book with leaves bound at a spine, as against a rolled scroll. Christians
took it up early, which is why the oldest complete Bibles are codices.
→ [Codex Sinaiticus](scripture/ancient-texts-manuscripts.md#16-codex-sinaiticus-c-ad-330-360)

**Dead Sea Scrolls**{ #dead-sea-scrolls } — Hebrew biblical manuscripts copied c. 250 BC–AD 68 and
found at Qumran: a thousand years older than the oldest complete Masoretic manuscripts, and the only
pre-Christian Hebrew witnesses this site holds. They are fragmentary, and a reading inside square
brackets is an editor's reconstruction rather than manuscript evidence.
→ [The Dead Sea Scrolls](scripture/ancient-texts-manuscripts.md#1-the-dead-sea-scrolls-c-250-bc-ad-68)

**LXX**{ #lxx } — The standard abbreviation for the [Septuagint](#septuagint), from the seventy
translators of the tradition behind it.

**Masoretic Text, MT**{ #masoretic-text } — The standard Hebrew Bible text, transmitted with vowel
pointing, accents and scribal notes by the Masoretes. This site's copy is the Westminster Leningrad
Codex — a single manuscript dated 1008, not an eclectic reconstruction, so it carries that one
codex's idiosyncrasies with it.
→ [Hebrew Old Testament](scripture/translations.md#hebrew-old-testament)

**NA28**{ #na28 } — Nestle-Aland 28th edition, the standard critical Greek New Testament in
scholarly use. Held on external storage rather than in this repository's own database.

**Peshitta**{ #peshitta } — The standard Syriac Bible: a real, catalogued ancient version, and a
witness in its own right.

**Samaritan Pentateuch**{ #samaritan-pentateuch } — An independent Torah-only text tradition
preserved by the Samaritan community. A second witness to compare on disputed Torah readings, and one
carrying its own theological commitments. → [Hebrew Old Testament](scripture/translations.md#hebrew-old-testament)

**SBLGNT**{ #sblgnt } — The Society of Biblical Literature Greek New Testament: an openly licensed
critical edition, and this site's default Greek New Testament text.
→ [Greek New Testament](scripture/translations.md#greek-new-testament)

**Septuagint**{ #septuagint } — The Greek translation of the Old Testament made from the third
century BC onward. It matters far beyond translation history because the New Testament authors
usually quote it, and it sometimes reads differently from the Hebrew — Hebrews 10:5 quotes "a body
you have prepared for me" where the Masoretic text has "ears".
→ [Septuagint](scripture/translations.md#septuagint-greek-old-testament)

**Targum**{ #targum } — An Aramaic rendering of the Hebrew Scriptures, often expansive enough to be
paraphrase and commentary at once. Evidence for how a passage was understood in the synagogue.

**Textual criticism**{ #textual-criticism } — Working out what a text originally said by comparing
the manuscripts that carry it, and saying openly where they disagree.
→ [Textual criticism](scripture/ancient-texts-manuscripts.md#textual-criticism-how-we-actually-know)

**Uncial**{ #uncial } — Majuscule script written without spaces between words, used in the great
fourth-century codices, and the reason they are sometimes called "the uncials".

**Vorlage**{ #vorlage } — The source text a translator was actually working from — a distinct
question from what the translation says.

**WLC**{ #wlc } — Westminster Leningrad Codex. See [Masoretic Text](#masoretic-text).

## The datasets behind this site

Studies here cite the dataset a claim came out of, so these names turn up in "Sources" sections
constantly. What they are, and which of them a query tool can actually see, is set out in full on
[About our datasets](about/about-our-datasets.md).

**BHSA**{ #bhsa } — Decades of Hebrew linguistic analysis from the Eep Talstra Centre, VU Amsterdam,
with deeper clause hierarchy than anything else held here. Catalogued and licence-checked but not
wired into any query tool, since MACULA already covers what the studies ask for.
→ [BHSA](scripture/original-language-data.md#bhsa-eep-talstra-centre-vu-amsterdam)

**`bible-text.db`**{ #bible-text-db } — The database the studies query: every ingested text,
per-word morphology, cross-references, alignment, Dead Sea Scroll variants. Built from open and
non-commercial sources inside this repository, and rebuilt from scratch rather than hand-curated, so
any claim resting on it can be re-derived.
→ [The two databases](about/about-our-datasets.md#the-two-databases-and-what-is-in-them)

**Licence tiers**{ #licence-tiers } — Every source falls into one of three, and the tier decides how
it may be used: **open** (cited and quoted freely), **restricted non-commercial** (usable while this
site does not monetise, flagged by name), and **quotation-only** (commercially published, cited with
a short attributed quotation and never reproduced at length).
→ [Three tiers, one rule](about/about-our-datasets.md#three-tiers-one-rule)

**Louw-Nida**{ #louw-nida } — The standard semantic-domain classification for New Testament Greek,
by Johannes Louw and Eugene Nida. It is where a Greek word's domain code on this site comes from.

**MACULA**{ #macula } — Open scholarly annotations published by Clear Bible, one set for Hebrew and
one for Greek, and this site's main source for both. It contains no Bible of its own: it carries a
row for every word of one, saying what that word is — lemma, parsing, gloss, semantic domain, clause
syntax. When a study says "MACULA gives the domain as…", this dataset is what it means.
→ [MACULA](scripture/original-language-data.md#macula-clear-bible)

**MARBLE**{ #marble } — The United Bible Societies project supplying the word-sense data MACULA
carries, including the Semantic Dictionary of Biblical Hebrew.

**morphhb**{ #morphhb } — The Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible: the Westminster Leningrad Codex marked up
with lemma and morphology, and the source this site's Hebrew word studies are required to work from.
Text and tagging only — no syntax, no semantic domains.
→ [morphhb](scripture/original-language-data.md#open-scriptures-hebrew-bible-morphhb)

**Scripture links: `quotation-greek`, `inner-biblical`, `allusion-lemma`, `quotation-hebrew`**{ #scripture-links } —
The four kinds of connection this site derives between passages, kept apart and never merged into a
single score, because they are not the same kind of evidence. The first three are textual facts; the
fourth is a judgement.
→ [Four kinds of evidence](about/how-we-cross-reference.md#four-kinds-of-evidence-deliberately-kept-apart)

**SDBH**{ #sdbh } — The Semantic Dictionary of Biblical Hebrew, the Hebrew counterpart to
[Louw-Nida](#louw-nida), and the source of Hebrew domain codes here.

**STEPBible**{ #stepbible } — Datasets from Tyndale House, Cambridge: Strong's-tagged texts,
lexicons, morphology and cross-references. Held as raw files but **not ingested**, so the query
tools cannot see it — check it by hand before concluding that a word is unattested.
→ [STEPBible](scripture/original-language-data.md#stepbible-tyndale-house-cambridge)

**`study-notes.db`**{ #study-notes-db } — The second database, built from commercially published
study Bibles and commentary. Because that material is quotation-only it is built and kept entirely
outside this public repository, and it is used to test a reading already reached from the text,
never to form one. → [The two databases](about/about-our-datasets.md#the-two-databases-and-what-is-in-them)

**TWOT**{ #twot } — The *Theological Wordbook of the Old Testament*. Its bare facts — a Strong's
number pointing to a root, lemma and one-line gloss — are open enough to use freely; its discussion
prose is not, and sits in the quotation-only tier.
→ [TWOT: one source, split across two tiers](about/about-our-datasets.md#twot-one-source-split-across-two-tiers)

**unfoldingWord, ULT**{ #ult } — A translation organisation's Hebrew, Greek and English datasets. The
ULT English translation is aligned to the original texts word by word, which is the one thing here
that no other source supplies.
→ [unfoldingWord](scripture/original-language-data.md#unfoldingword-uhb-ugnt-ult)

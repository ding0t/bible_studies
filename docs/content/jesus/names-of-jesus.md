# The Names of Jesus

Jesus asked His disciples who they thought He was, and the Bible answers with dozens of
names. The prophets gave Him names centuries before He was born. He chose names for Himself. People
with nothing left cried out names to Him, and He answered. Revelation gives Him the names He will
wear when He comes back.

**In one sentence:** Jesus asked "Who do you say that I am?", and every name the Bible gives Him
answers that He is the LORD Himself come as one of us: the names He took claim God's own titles,
the names desperate people cried out to Him were answered on the spot, and the names Revelation
gives Him He will wear when He returns to reign, so you may call on Him by name, and He answers.

This study is the second half of [The Names of God](../god/names-of-god.md), which follows the
names God gave Himself in the Old Testament and ends where this one begins, at the name of Jesus.
The [Names of Jesus word cards](../hebrew/names-of-jesus-cards.md) teach the Old Testament names
in Hebrew, one letter at a time, each beside the verse that gives the name to Jesus. [The Names
of the Holy Spirit](../holy-spirit/names-of-the-holy-spirit.md) is the third study in the series.

## Key Takeaways

### Types & Prophecy

- **Prophecy.** The Old Testament named the coming King before He came: the LORD's Anointed
  (Psalm 2:2), a prophet like Moses (Deuteronomy 18:15), a priest forever after the order of
  Melchizedek (Psalm 110:4), the Branch (Jeremiah 23:5; Zechariah 6:12), the star out of Jacob
  (Numbers 24:17), the one shepherd (Ezekiel 34:23), and the Servant who would be "high and lifted
  up" (Isaiah 52:13).
- **Prophecy.** Daniel saw "one like a son of man" given an everlasting kingdom (Daniel 7:13-14).
  "The Son of Man" became the name Jesus used for Himself more than any other.
- **Type.** Isaiah's Servant goes to death "like a lamb that is led to the slaughter" (Isaiah 53:7,
  ESV). John the Baptist names Jesus with *amnos*, the Septuagint's word for "lamb" in that verse:
  "Behold, the Lamb of God" (John 1:29, ESV).

### Lessons about Jesus

- Jesus takes names that belong to God alone: "I am" (John 8:58), and "the first and the last"
  (Revelation 1:17), which the LORD spoke of Himself in Isaiah 44:6.
- Jesus answers when He is called by name. Blind Bartimaeus cried "Son of David, have mercy on me!"
  and received his sight (Mark 10:47-52). A dying thief said "Jesus, remember me" and was promised
  paradise that day (Luke 23:42-43).
- Jesus is a King who suffers before He reigns. Peter named Him the Christ, and at once Jesus
  taught that "the Son of Man must suffer many things" (Mark 8:29-31, ESV).

### Be Transformed

- **Think.** Each name of Jesus is a promise God made and kept. When you read "Christ", read the
  Anointed King of Psalm 2. When you read "Lamb", read Isaiah 53.
- **Attitude.** Bartimaeus kept shouting when the crowd told him to be quiet (Mark 10:48). Call on
  Jesus the same way, by name and without embarrassment.
- **Do.** Answer His question this week, in words. Write down who you say Jesus is, using the names
  in this study, and pray them to Him.

## "Who do you say that I am?"

Jesus took His disciples north, out of Galilee, to Caesarea Philippi. The town had been called
Paneas after the Greek god Pan, whose cave shrine stood there, and Philip the Tetrarch had renamed it
for Caesar and for himself (*ESV Study Bible*, note on Matthew 16:13). There, among shrines to Pan and to Caesar, Jesus asked who He was.

> ✝️ Matthew 16:13-17 (ESV)
>
> 13 Now when Jesus came into the district of Caesarea Philippi, he asked his disciples, "Who do
> people say that the Son of Man is?" 14 And they said, "Some say John the Baptist, others say
> Elijah, and others Jeremiah or one of the prophets." 15 He said to them, "But who do you say that I
> am?" 16 Simon Peter replied, "You are the Christ, the Son of the living God." 17 And Jesus answered
> him, "Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jonah! For flesh and blood has not revealed this to you, but my
> Father who is in heaven.

The crowds gave Him prophets' names. Peter gave Him two names. <span lang="grc">ὁ χριστός</span>
(*ho christos*, G5547) is "the Anointed", the King God promised. "The Son of the living God" sets Him
beside the God who is alive, in a town full of shrines to gods who were not. Jesus said His Father
had shown Peter this. God makes His Son known, and nobody works it out unaided.

Moses asked God, "What is his name?" (Exodus 3:13), and [The Names of
God](../god/names-of-god.md#what-is-his-name) follows the answer. At Caesarea Philippi the question
turns around: God the Son asks His people what they call Him. [Simon Peter](../biblical-figures/peter.md)
follows the man who answered, and [Take Up Your Cross](../christian-life/take-up-your-cross.md)
follows what Jesus said next. The rest of this study gathers the Bible's answers.

## Names promised before He came

The Old Testament named the King God would send, centuries before He was born. [The Names of
God](../god/names-of-god.md#names-the-prophets-gave-the-coming-king) covers three of those names:
Immanuel, Mighty God, and "The LORD is our righteousness". The others begin with the title that
gathers them, *the Anointed*. Then come the three offices Israel anointed men for (prophet, priest
and king), and three names no anointed man had filled: the Servant, the rejected stone and the son
of man. The Servant and the stone are both rejected by people and raised up by God (Isaiah 52:13;
53:3; Psalm 118:22).

### The Anointed

"The kings of the earth set themselves, and the rulers take counsel together, against the LORD and
against his Anointed" (Psalm 2:2, ESV). The Hebrew is <span dir="rtl">מָשִׁיחַ</span> (*mashiach*,
mah-SHEE-akh, H4899), from the verb for pouring oil on someone's head. It occurs 38 times in the
Hebrew Bible, mostly of anointed kings and priests. The Greek translators wrote it *christos*, and
"Messiah" and "Christ" are the same word in two languages.

Psalm 2 says who this Anointed King is: "The LORD said to me, 'You are my Son; today I have begotten
you'" (Psalm 2:7, ESV). God had promised David a son whose throne would last forever: "I will be to
him a father, and he shall be to me a son" (2 Samuel 7:14, ESV). Hebrews quotes both verses of Jesus
and asks, "to which of the angels did God ever say" them? (Hebrews 1:5, ESV). Daniel's
seventy weeks run "to the coming of an anointed one, a prince", and then "an anointed one shall be
cut off and shall have nothing" (Daniel 9:25-26, ESV); the NLT footnotes "Hebrew reads *a
messiah*". Whether the two verses name one person is debated. This site reads both as the Messiah,
which makes Daniel 9 a prophecy that the Christ would be killed, and [Prophecy Events and
Times](../last-things/prophecy-events-times.md#the-flagship-calculation-daniels-seventy-weeks-to-the-triumphal-entry)
works through the timing.

### A prophet like Moses

> ✝️ Deuteronomy 18:15 (ESV)
>
> 15 "The LORD your God will raise up for you a prophet like me from among you, from your
> brothers—it is to him you shall listen—

A <span dir="rtl">נָבִיא</span> (*navi*, nah-VEE, H5030) speaks God's words: "I will put my words in
his mouth, and he shall speak to them all that I command him" (Deuteronomy 18:18, ESV). Peter told
the crowd at the temple that Jesus was this prophet (Acts 3:22). On the mountain of the
transfiguration the Father said it from the cloud: "This is my beloved Son, with whom I am well
pleased; listen to him" (Matthew 17:5, ESV). "Listen to him" is Deuteronomy 18:15's command. Jesus
is the Prophet whose every word is God's word.

### A priest forever

David wrote a psalm about a King who is also a Priest:

> ✝️ Psalm 110:1, 4 (ESV)
>
> 1 The LORD says to my Lord: "Sit at my right hand, until I make your enemies your footstool."
> 4 The LORD has sworn and will not change his mind, "You are a priest forever after the order of
> Melchizedek."

Jesus put the first verse to the Pharisees as a question: "What do you think about the Christ? Whose
son is he?" They answered, "The son of David." So He asked, "If then David calls him Lord, how is he
his son?" (Matthew 22:42-45, ESV). No one could answer Him (Matthew 22:46). The Christ is David's son
and David's Lord. Hebrews joins the psalm's two oracles: the God who said "You are my Son" also said
"You are a priest forever" (Hebrews 5:5-6). The word is <span dir="rtl">כֹּהֵן</span> (*kohen*,
H3548). Judah's kings were barred from the priest's work, and King Uzziah was struck with leprosy
for burning incense (2 Chronicles 26:16-21). A King who is also a Priest needed a new order. [Jesus, Priest in the Order of Melchizedek](melchizedek-priesthood.md) studies it.

### Isaiah's four names for the King

Isaiah gave the child born to David's house four names: "Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God,
Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace" (Isaiah 9:6, ESV). The Names of God takes *El gibbor*, "Mighty
God". The other three each say what kind of King He is.

- <span dir="rtl">פֶּלֶא יוֹעֵץ</span> (*pele yoʿets*, H6382, H3289), "a wonder of a counsellor".
  His counsel is beyond human wisdom. The Septuagint renders the name
  <span lang="grc">μεγάλης βουλῆς ἄγγελος</span>, "Messenger of great counsel" (Brenton LXX,
  which numbers the verse <span data-ref="Isaiah 9:6">9:5</span>).
- <span dir="rtl">אֲבִי־עַד</span> (*avi-ʿad*, H1, H5703), "father of perpetuity". The phrase names the
  kind of King He is. Isaiah uses "father" for a ruler who cares for his people: Eliakim "shall be a
  father to the inhabitants of Jerusalem" (Isaiah 22:21, ESV). This King cares for His people
  forever.
- <span dir="rtl">שַׂר־שָׁלוֹם</span> (*sar shalom*, H8269, H7965), "Prince of Peace", and "of the
  increase of his government and of peace there will be no end, on the throne of David" (Isaiah 9:7,
  ESV).

### The Branch, the star and the shepherd

#### The Branch

Isaiah saw the royal line cut down to a stump: "There shall come forth a shoot from
the stump of Jesse, and a branch from his roots shall bear fruit" (Isaiah 11:1, ESV). Jeremiah and
Zechariah made "Branch" a name, <span dir="rtl">צֶמַח</span> (*tsemach*, TSEH-makh, H6780). Jeremiah's
"righteous Branch" is named "The LORD is our righteousness" (Jeremiah 23:5-6), which [The Names of
God](../god/names-of-god.md#the-lord-is-our-righteousness) studies. Zechariah's Branch is called
"my servant the Branch" (Zechariah 3:8, ESV), and "the man whose name is the Branch" will build the
temple of the LORD and "sit and rule on his throne. And there shall be a priest on his throne"
(Zechariah 6:12-13, ESV). The King and the Priest are one man again, as in Psalm 110.

#### The star out of Jacob

Balaam, hired to curse Israel, saw its King far off: "I see him, but not
now; I behold him, but not near: a star shall come out of Jacob, and a scepter shall rise out of
Israel" (Numbers 24:17, ESV). The word is <span dir="rtl">כּוֹכָב</span> (*kokhav*, H3556). In the
last chapter of the Bible Jesus takes the name: "I am the root and the descendant of David, the
bright morning star" (Revelation 22:16, ESV).

#### The one shepherd

After the shepherds of Israel had fed themselves and scattered the sheep,
God promised, "I will set up over them one shepherd, my servant David, and he shall feed them"
(Ezekiel 34:23, ESV). The shepherd is <span dir="rtl">רֹעֶה</span> (*roʿeh*, H7473). Ezekiel wrote
four centuries after David died, so "David" here is David's promised son. Jesus said, "I am the good
shepherd" (John 10:11, ESV).

### The Servant, high and lifted up

In the second half of Isaiah God speaks of "my servant", <span dir="rtl">עַבְדִּי</span> (*ʿavdi*,
H5650): "Behold my servant, whom I uphold, my chosen, in whom my soul delights; I have put my Spirit
upon him" (Isaiah 42:1, ESV). Matthew quotes this of Jesus (Matthew 12:17-18).

The fourth of these songs opens, "Behold, my servant shall act wisely; he shall be high and lifted
up, and shall be exalted" (Isaiah 52:13, ESV). "High and lifted up" is <span dir="rtl">יָרוּם
וְנִשָּׂא</span> (*yarum venissa*, H7311, H5375). Elsewhere in Isaiah that pair of verbs belongs to
the LORD: the throne Isaiah saw "high and lifted up" (Isaiah 6:1, ESV), the LORD who says "now I
will lift myself up" (Isaiah 33:10, ESV), and "the One who is high and lifted up, who inhabits
eternity, whose name is Holy" (Isaiah 57:15, ESV). Its other uses are the pride God brings down
(Isaiah 2:12-14; 37:23) and the raising of a signal to the nations (Isaiah 13:2; 49:22). John read Isaiah this way. He quotes Isaiah 53:1 and Isaiah 6:10 together and
explains, "Isaiah said these things because he saw his glory and spoke of him" (John 12:41, ESV).

Then the song goes down into suffering:

> ✝️ Isaiah 53:5-7 (ESV)
>
> 5 But he was pierced for our transgressions; he was crushed for our iniquities; upon him was the
> chastisement that brought us peace, and with his wounds we are healed. 6 All we like sheep have
> gone astray; we have turned—every one—to his own way; and the LORD has laid on him the iniquity
> of us all. 7 He was oppressed, and he was afflicted, yet he opened not his mouth; like a lamb that
> is led to the slaughter, and like a sheep that before its shearers is silent, so he opened not his
> mouth.

An Ethiopian official reading this passage asked, "About whom, I ask you, does the prophet say this,
about himself or about someone else?" Philip, "beginning with this Scripture ... told him the good
news about Jesus" (Acts 8:34-35, ESV). The Servant took the punishment His people deserved. The
church's name for this is substitutionary atonement: "the LORD has laid on him the iniquity of
us all".

### The stone the builders rejected

"The stone that the builders rejected has become the cornerstone" (Psalm 118:22, ESV). Jesus applied
it to Himself in the temple (Matthew 21:42). Before the council that had condemned Him, Peter
applied it to Jesus again and drew the conclusion: "This Jesus is the stone that was rejected by
you, the builders ... And there is salvation in no one else, for there is no other name under heaven
given among men by which we must be saved" (Acts 4:11-12, ESV).

### One like a son of man

The last Old Testament name is the one Jesus used most. Daniel saw four beasts, the kingdoms of the
world, and then this:

> ✝️ Daniel 7:13-14 (ESV)
>
> 13 "I saw in the night visions, and behold, with the clouds of heaven there came one like a son of
> man, and he came to the Ancient of Days and was presented before him. 14 And to him was given
> dominion and glory and a kingdom, that all peoples, nations, and languages should serve him; his
> dominion is an everlasting dominion, which shall not pass away, and his kingdom one that shall not
> be destroyed.

The Aramaic is <span dir="rtl">כְּבַר אֱנָשׁ</span> (*kevar enash*, H1247, H606), "like a son of
man", a human figure. He comes "with the clouds of heaven", and the clouds are where the LORD rides
(Psalm 104:3; Isaiah 19:1). All peoples "serve" Him, and His kingdom will never end.

## Names given at His birth

Around His birth, God named Him through angels.

- **Jesus.** "You shall call his name Jesus, for he will save his people from their sins"
  (Matthew 1:21, ESV). <span lang="grc">Ἰησοῦς</span> (*Iēsous*, G2424) is the Greek form of
  *Yeshua*, "the LORD saves". [The Names of God](../god/names-of-god.md#jesus-the-lord-saves) traces
  it from Joshua. The name occurs 910 times in the SBL Greek New Testament.
- **Immanuel.** Matthew quotes Isaiah 7:14 and translates it: "they shall call his name Immanuel"
  (which means, God with us) (Matthew 1:23, ESV). The Names of God studies [this
  name](../god/names-of-god.md#immanuel).
- **Son of the Most High.** Gabriel told Mary, "He will be great and will be called the Son of the
  Most High. And the Lord God will give to him the throne of his father David, and he will reign
  over the house of Jacob forever" (Luke 1:32-33, ESV). That is 2 Samuel 7 and Psalm 2, promised to
  Mary's son.
- **A Savior, who is Christ the Lord.** The angel told the shepherds, "For unto you is born this day
  in the city of David a Savior, who is Christ the Lord" (Luke 2:11, ESV). The Greek is three names
  in a row, <span lang="grc">σωτὴρ ὅς ἐστιν χριστὸς κύριος</span> (*sōtēr hos estin christos
  kyrios*, G4990, G5547, G2962): Savior, Anointed, Lord.

## The name He chose: the Son of Man

Jesus called Himself "the Son of Man", <span lang="grc">ὁ υἱὸς τοῦ ἀνθρώπου</span> (*ho huios tou
anthrōpou*, G5207, G444), more than by any other name. The phrase occurs 81 times in the four Gospels (SBLGNT:
Matthew 30, Mark 14, Luke 25, John 12), and John 5:27 has it once more without "the". Each time it comes from His own mouth, or from someone
repeating His words: the crowd (John 12:34) and the angels at the tomb (Luke 24:7). Outside the
Gospels the full title, with "the", occurs once, from Stephen at the moment of his death: "I see the heavens opened, and
the Son of Man standing at the right hand of God" (Acts 7:56, ESV).

It sounds humble, and in part it is. "Son of man" in Hebrew and Aramaic means a human being. Jesus
used it for His humility and His suffering: right after Peter's confession He "began to teach them
that the Son of Man must suffer many things and be rejected ... and be killed, and after three days
rise again" (Mark 8:31, ESV). [As the Snake Was Lifted Up](as-the-snake-was-lifted.md) studies the
Son of Man lifted up on the cross (John 3:14).

The name also carries Daniel 7. At His trial the high priest asked Him directly:

> ✝️ Mark 14:61-64 (ESV)
>
> 61 But he remained silent and made no answer. Again the high priest asked him, "Are you the
> Christ, the Son of the Blessed?" 62 And Jesus said, "I am, and you will see the Son of Man seated
> at the right hand of Power, and coming with the clouds of heaven." 63 And the high priest tore his
> garments and said, "What further witnesses do we need? 64 You have heard his blasphemy. What is
> your decision?" And they all condemned him as deserving death.

Jesus answered with Psalm 110:1 ("seated at the right hand") and Daniel 7:13 ("coming with the
clouds of heaven"). The high priest heard a man claim the throne beside God, and called it
blasphemy. So the name Jesus used most for Himself says two things. He is a real man who would
suffer and die. He is also the One to whom God gives the everlasting kingdom. [The Olivet
Discourse](../last-things/olivet-discourse.md) studies His promise to come on the clouds.

## "I am": seven pictures and one name

John's Gospel records seven sayings in which Jesus says "I am", <span lang="grc">ἐγώ εἰμι</span>
(*egō eimi*, G1510), and joins it to a picture. Each answers a need in front of Him and a promise the
Old Testament had made.

| Jesus said | Where | The need and the promise | Studied in |
|---|---|---|---|
| "I am the bread of life" | John 6:35 | A crowd fed the day before, asking for the manna again (John 6:31; Exodus 16) | [Bread of Life](bread-of-life-feeding-the-multitudes.md#i-am-the-bread-of-life) |
| "I am the light of the world" | John 8:12 | Said in the temple treasury (John 8:20), at the close of the Feast of Booths (John 7:37) | [The Lampstand](the-heavenly-pattern/lampstand.md) |
| "I am the door" | John 10:9 | Sheep kept from thieves; "he will be saved" | |
| "I am the good shepherd" | John 10:11 | Ezekiel 34's scattered sheep and one shepherd | [Hurt by the Church](../christian-life/hurt-by-the-church.md) |
| "I am the resurrection and the life" | John 11:25 | Spoken to Martha, with Lazarus four days in the tomb (John 11:17) | |
| "I am the way, and the truth, and the life" | John 14:6 | Thomas: "we do not know where you are going" (John 14:5) | [The Way](the-way.md) |
| "I am the true vine" | John 15:1 | Israel, the vine that bore no good fruit (Isaiah 5:1-7; Psalm 80:8) | |

Each picture is something a person cannot live without: food, light, safety, a shepherd, life
after death, a way home, a source of fruit. Jesus says He is each one. "Apart from me you can do
nothing" (John 15:5, ESV).

He also said "I am" with nothing after it. "Truly, truly, I say to you, before Abraham was, I am"
(John 8:58, ESV). Those are the Septuagint's words at the burning bush, and His hearers picked up
stones. [The Names of God](../god/names-of-god.md#the-son-speaks-the-fathers-names) and [The Woman
at the Well](woman-at-well.md#i-am-the-one-speaking-to-you) study this saying. Every other "I am" in
John rests on it. The One who is the bread and the light is the God who told Moses "I AM WHO I AM".

## Names cried out in need

[The Names of God](../god/names-of-god.md#names-given-in-the-hard-places) shows God giving His names
to people with nothing left. In the Gospels the movement runs the other way. People with nothing
left called Jesus by name, and He answered each one.

### "Son of David", from a blind beggar

Bartimaeus sat by the road out of Jericho. "When he heard
that it was Jesus of Nazareth, he began to cry out and say, 'Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on
me!' And many rebuked him, telling him to be silent. But he cried out all the more" (Mark 10:47-48,
ESV). "Son of David" was the King's name from 2 Samuel 7, and a blind man shouted it in public a few
days before Jesus entered Jerusalem. "Jesus stopped and said, 'Call him'" (Mark 10:49, ESV), and
Bartimaeus "immediately ... recovered his sight and followed him on the way" (Mark 10:52, ESV).

### "Savior of the world", from Samaritans

Jesus spent two days in a Samaritan town because of
one woman's testimony. Then the townspeople said, "we have heard for ourselves, and we know that this
is indeed the Savior of the world" (John 4:42, ESV). Jews and Samaritans did not mix (John 4:9), and
the people whom Israel kept out were the first to give Him this name. [The Woman at the
Well](woman-at-well.md) tells their story.

### "Jesus", from a dying thief

One of the men crucified beside Him said, "Jesus, remember me when
you come into your kingdom." Jesus answered, "Truly, I say to you, today you will be with me in
paradise" (Luke 23:42-43, ESV). The thief had no time left to do anything. He called on the name,
and that was enough.

### "My Lord and my God", from a doubter

Thomas would not believe the others had seen Jesus risen.
A week later Jesus came and showed him His wounds, and Thomas answered, <span lang="grc">ὁ κύριός
μου καὶ ὁ θεός μου</span>, "My Lord and my God!" (John 20:28, ESV). Jesus accepted the words. John
then says why he wrote his Gospel: "so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of
God, and that by believing you may have life in his name" (John 20:31, ESV).

## Names the apostles gave Him

### The Word

"In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God"
(John 1:1, ESV). <span lang="grc">ὁ λόγος</span> (*ho logos*, G3056) is God's speech. God created
by speaking (Genesis 1:3), and the Word "became flesh and dwelt among us" (John 1:14, ESV). Jesus is
what God says, and God has said it in a man.

### The Lamb of God

"Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world!" (John 1:29,
ESV). The word is <span lang="grc">ἀμνός</span> (*amnos*, G286), the Septuagint's word in Isaiah 53:7,
where the Greek has the lamb silent before its shearer. It occurs only four times in the New Testament: John 1:29 and
1:36, Acts 8:32 quoting Isaiah 53:7, and 1 Peter 1:19 ("a lamb without blemish or spot"). John the
Baptist was naming Jesus as Isaiah's Servant. [Passover](../feasts/passover.md#the-lamb-of-god)
studies the Lamb and the feast.

### Lord

<span lang="grc">κύριος</span> (*kyrios*, G2962) can be a polite "sir". It is also the
word the Septuagint uses for the name *YHWH*. Paul gives it to Jesus in the confession "Jesus Christ
is Lord" (Philippians 2:11), quoting the LORD's own oath in Isaiah 45:23. [The Names of
God](../god/names-of-god.md#every-knee-and-everyone-who-calls) studies that passage.

### The great high priest

"Since then we have a great high priest who has passed through the
heavens, Jesus, the Son of God, let us hold fast our confession" (Hebrews 4:14, ESV). He can
"sympathize with our weaknesses", having been "tempted as we are, yet without sin" (Hebrews 4:15,
ESV). So you may "draw near to the throne of grace" (Hebrews 4:16, ESV).

### The last Adam

"The first man Adam became a living being; the last Adam became a life-giving
spirit" (1 Corinthians 15:45, ESV). Adam brought death to everyone who belongs to him. Jesus, the
<span lang="grc">ἔσχατος Ἀδάμ</span> (*eschatos Adam*, G2078), brings resurrection life to everyone
who belongs to Him (1 Corinthians 15:22-23).

## Names He will wear when He returns

### The First and the Last

Through Isaiah the LORD named Himself: "I am the first and I am the
last; besides me there is no god" (Isaiah 44:6, ESV). John fell at the feet of the risen Jesus "as
though dead", and Jesus laid His right hand on him:

> ✝️ Revelation 1:17-18 (ESV)
>
> 17 When I saw him, I fell at his feet as though dead. But he laid his right hand on me, saying,
> "Fear not, I am the first and the last, 18 and the living one. I died, and behold I am alive
> forevermore, and I have the keys of Death and Hades.

<span lang="grc">ὁ πρῶτος καὶ ὁ ἔσχατος</span> (*ho prōtos kai ho eschatos*, G4413, G2078). Only
God can say "besides me there is no god", and Jesus takes the title. He says it again at the end of
the book: "I am the Alpha and the Omega, the first and the last, the beginning and the end"
(Revelation 22:13, ESV). The first thing He says with it is "Fear not."

### The Lion who is a Lamb

In heaven no one could open the sealed scroll, and John wept. An elder
told him, "Weep no more; behold, the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the Root of David, has conquered"
(Revelation 5:5, ESV). John heard of a Lion, and then he "saw a Lamb standing, as though it had been
slain" (Revelation 5:6, ESV). Revelation's word is <span lang="grc">ἀρνίον</span> (*arnion*, G721),
which occurs 29 times in the book, 28 of them of Jesus (the other is the beast with "two
horns like a lamb", Revelation 13:11, ESV). The King of Judah conquered by being killed.

### Faithful and True, the Word of God, King of kings

At the end of the age heaven opens.

> ✝️ Revelation 19:11-13, 16 (ESV)
>
> 11 Then I saw heaven opened, and behold, a white horse! The one sitting on it is called Faithful
> and True, and in righteousness he judges and makes war. 12 His eyes are like a flame of fire, and
> on his head are many diadems, and he has a name written that no one knows but himself. 13 He is
> clothed in a robe dipped in blood, and the name by which he is called is The Word of God. 16 On
> his robe and on his thigh he has a name written, King of kings and Lord of lords.

Four names in one vision. He is Faithful and True: every promise in this study will be kept. He has
a name no one knows: after all these names, there is still more to know of Him. He is the Word of
God, John's first name for Him (John 1:1). He is King of kings and Lord of lords. Revelation 19
puts His return before the thousand-year reign of Revelation 20, and this site reads that order as
the order of events. The Son of Man Daniel saw will come with the clouds, and the kingdom given to
Him will have no end.

## The names answer His question

So the names answer His question. He is the Christ, the Prophet, the Priest and the King, the
Servant who was pierced and the Son of Man who will reign. He is the LORD who says "I am" and "the
first and the last". He heard Bartimaeus and He heard the thief. "Everyone who calls on the name of
the Lord will be saved" (Romans 10:13, ESV). Call on Him by name.

## Discussion questions

1. **The language.** "Christ" is the Greek for the Hebrew *mashiach*, "anointed" (Psalm 2:2). What
   changes when you read "Jesus Christ" as "Jesus the Anointed King"?
2. **The text in its context.** Jesus asked His question at Caesarea Philippi, a town named for
   Caesar and once for the god Pan (Matthew 16:13-16). Why might "the Son of the living God" have
   sounded different there?
3. **Christ.** Isaiah 52:13 calls the Servant "high and lifted up", the words Isaiah uses of the
   LORD on His throne (Isaiah 6:1), and the next chapter has the Servant pierced and crushed
   (Isaiah 53:5). What does it tell you about God that the exalted One is the One who suffers?
4. **The hard part.** At His trial Jesus answered with Psalm 110:1 and Daniel 7:13, and was
   condemned for blasphemy (Mark 14:61-64). Why do you think He chose the name "Son of Man" over
   "Christ" when He spoke of Himself?
5. **Christ.** Jesus calls Himself "the first and the last" (Revelation 1:17), the LORD's own title
   in Isaiah 44:6. What does it mean for you that the first words He says with it are "Fear not"?
6. **Be transformed.** Bartimaeus called Jesus "Son of David", the Samaritans "Savior of the world",
   the thief simply "Jesus", and Thomas "My Lord and my God". Which name would you call Him by today,
   and what would you ask Him for?

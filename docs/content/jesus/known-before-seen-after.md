# Known Before, Seen After: Which Prophecies of Christ Were Understood, and When

A crowd in Jerusalem heard Jesus say that the Son of Man must be lifted up, and they objected from
Scripture: "We have heard from the Law that the Christ will remain forever. So how can You say that
the Son of Man must be lifted up? Who is this Son of Man?" (John 12:34, BSB). They knew one part of
what the prophets said about the Christ. They could not fit the other part beside it.

**In one sentence:** Before Jesus came, Israel read the prophecies of the Messiah's line and throne
as His, and read none of the prophecies of His suffering so; His opponents fulfilled the hidden ones
without knowing it (Acts 13:27), and the risen Christ opened the Scriptures to show that the
sufferings came first and then the glory (Luke 24:26, 45), so the cross was not staged, and you can
read the whole of it now.

### Types & Prophecy

This site's ledger holds 118 Old Testament prophecies of Christ. Before Jesus came, 20 of them were
read of the Messiah by a witness that survives. Six of the eight prophecies of His line were among
them. None of the 26 prophecies of His suffering was, and none of the 19 that His opponents
fulfilled. The risen Jesus named the pattern: "Was it not necessary for the Christ to suffer these
things and then to enter His glory?" (Luke 24:26, BSB).

### Lessons about Jesus

- **He is the son of David whom Israel expected.** Bethlehem, David's line and the Branch were read
  of the Messiah before He came (Matthew 2:4-6; John 7:42).
- **He is the sufferer no one expected.** Peter rebuked Him for saying He must die (Matthew
  16:22).
- **He was handed over by men who did not know what they fulfilled.** "In condemning Him they
  fulfilled the words of the prophets" (Acts 13:27, BSB).
- **He opens the Scriptures.** "Then He opened their minds to understand the Scriptures" (Luke
  24:45, BSB).

## What "known before" means

A prophecy counts as known before when a witness older than Jesus reads it of the Messiah. Four
kinds of witness survive:

- **The Dead Sea Scrolls.** Copied before the community at Qumran was destroyed in AD 68, most of
  them in the first century BC.
- **The Psalms of Solomon.** Written after Pompey took Jerusalem in 63 BC; the second psalm
  describes his death in Egypt in 48 BC (Psalms of Solomon 2:30-31).
- **The Septuagint.** Where its translators, before Christ, turned a prophecy toward a person.
- **The New Testament reporting what was already believed.** The chief priests answering Herod
  (Matthew 2:4-6) and a crowd arguing about the Christ (John 7:42) show a settled reading.

The Targums, the synagogue's Aramaic paraphrases, read many prophecies of the Messiah too, but the
ones that do so (Onkelos, Jonathan, and the Targums of the Writings) were written down after the New
Testament; the Aramaic Job and Leviticus found at Qumran (4Q157; 11Q10; 4Q156) name no Messiah. The
Talmud, completed about AD 500, is later still. The ledger counts them apart, as "read only in
a later Jewish source". Whether the prophecies themselves were written before the events is a
different question, and [What Literary Criticism Declares](../scripture/what-literary-criticism-declares.md)
answers it.

Each of the 118 prophecies falls into one of four groups: read of the Messiah before Jesus (20),
read of Him in part (6), read of Him only in a later Jewish source (12), and read of Him in no Jewish
source, before Jesus or since (80). The chart above sets them out by subject and by who brought each
fulfilment about.

## Known before: the line and the throne

Israel knew where the Messiah would come from. When Herod asked "where the Christ was to be born",
the chief priests and scribes answered at once: "In Bethlehem in Judea... for this is what the
prophet has written" (Matthew 2:4-5, BSB). A crowd at the Feast of Booths knew His line: "Doesn't
the Scripture say that the Christ will come from the line of David and from Bethlehem, the village
where David lived?" (John 7:42, BSB).

They knew who would come before Him. The priests asked John the Baptist, "Are you Elijah?... Are you
the Prophet?" (John 1:21, BSB), and the disciples asked Jesus, "Why do the scribes say that Elijah
must come first?" (Mark 9:11, BSB). Two centuries before Christ, Sirach already called Elijah the
one "written down for the times" to restore the tribes of Jacob: ὁ καταγραφεὶς... εἰς καιρούς
(Sirach 48:10, our translation of the Greek).

The scrolls show the same. One reads Jacob's blessing on Judah (Genesis 49:10) as "until the coming
of the Messiah of righteousness, the Branch of David" (4Q252 v 3-4). Another reads God's promise to
David (2 Samuel 7:11-14) of "the Branch of David who will stand with the Interpreter of the Law"
(4Q174 1-2 i 11). A third sets the prophet like Moses (Deuteronomy 18:18-19) beside Balaam's star
from Jacob (Numbers 24:17) in a list of the figures they awaited (4Q175). The Psalms of Solomon pray,
"raise up for them their king, the son of David" (Psalms of Solomon 17:23, our translation; Swete's
verse numbers, which Rahlfs numbers 17:21), and give him Psalm 2's
rod of iron and the word of his mouth that destroys the lawless, Isaiah 11:4's picture (Psalms of
Solomon 17:26-27).

So the prophecies of His line were the best known of all: six of the eight in the ledger were read
of the Messiah before Jesus. Simeon "was waiting for the consolation of Israel" (Luke 2:25, BSB),
and he was not alone.

## Hidden: the suffering

None of the 26 prophecies of His suffering was read of the Messiah before Jesus. No surviving
witness reads Isaiah 53, Psalm 22 or Zechariah 12:10 of the Christ before the cross.

### The scrolls before the cross

Three texts are offered as exceptions, and each falls short for a reason in its own words:

- **The Great Isaiah Scroll** reads <span dir="rtl">משחתי</span> (*mashachti*, "I anointed") at
  Isaiah 52:14 where the Masoretic Text has <span dir="rtl">מִשְׁחַת</span> (*mishchat*, "marring").
  Some read the one letter as a scribe's messianic reading of the servant. It may be, but the scroll
  is a copy of Isaiah with no comment, and no Qumran writing reads the passage of the Messiah.
- **4Q285** was first read as "they will put to death the Prince of the Congregation, the Branch".
  Its words carry no vowels, so <span dir="rtl">והמיתו</span> (*vehemito*) can be "and they will kill"
  or "and he will kill him". The fragment quotes Isaiah 10:34-11:1, the shoot from Jesse who slays the
  wicked (Isaiah 11:4), and the slain it goes on to name are the Kittim, Israel's enemies (4Q285 7
  1-6). So most now read it as "the Prince of the Congregation, the Branch of David, will kill him".
- **4Q541** describes a figure who "will atone for all the sons of his generation", against whom
  "they will speak many words... and lies" (4Q541 9 i 2, 5-6). He is probably a priest, and the
  fragments name no Messiah.

Two prophecies were read of the last days without the part He fulfilled. The Damascus Document reads
Zechariah 13:7's struck shepherd of the final judgment, with no Messiah struck (CD xix 7-11). Its
column xix survives only in a medieval copy from the Cairo Geniza, so it is a witness before Jesus by
the date of its composition. And 4Q174 reads Psalm 2:1-2 of the nations raging against "the chosen
ones of Israel" in the last days (4Q174 1-2 i 19, where the raging is reconstructed in a gap).

### The disciples

Jesus told them plainly. "From that time on Jesus began to show His disciples that He must go to
Jerusalem and suffer many things... and that He must be killed and on the third day be raised to
life" (Matthew 16:21, BSB). Peter answered, "Far be it from You, Lord!... This shall never happen to
You!" (Matthew 16:22, BSB).

Luke says twice that the meaning was hidden. After the second prediction, "they did not understand
this statement. It was veiled from them so that they could not comprehend it" (Luke 9:45, BSB). The
Greek ἵνα μὴ αἴσθωνται, "so that they would not perceive it", can mark a result, but most read it as
purpose: the veiling is passive (ἦν παρακεκαλυμμένον, "it was concealed"), and Luke 18:34 says again
that "the meaning was hidden from them". The veil had an intent. After the third, "The meaning was hidden from them, and they did not comprehend what He was
saying" (Luke 18:34, BSB). On the road to Emmaus, two of them still said, "we were hoping He was the
One who would redeem Israel" (Luke 24:21, BSB).

### The crowd

The crowd of John 12:34 had read the throne: "the Christ will remain forever". That is probably
the promise to David, that his throne would stand forever (2 Samuel 7:16; Psalm 89:36-37); John does
not say which text they meant. "Lifted up" did not fit
it, so they asked, "Who is this Son of Man?"

### The later synagogue

The Targum of Isaiah, written down in the third century after Christ, shows the same pattern from
the other side. It reads the servant's exaltation as the Messiah's: "Behold, my servant the messiah
shall prosper" (Targum Jonathan on Isaiah 52:13, trans. Pauli, 1871). But it turns the wounds
away from him. Where Isaiah says "He was pierced for our transgressions" (Isaiah 53:5, BSB), the
Targum has the Messiah rebuild the sanctuary "which has been profaned on account of our sins"
(Targum Jonathan on Isaiah 53:5, trans. Pauli). In the Targum, a Messiah who reigns was readable. A
Messiah who is pierced was not.

The Babylonian Talmud, completed about AD 500, does read two of the wounds of a Messiah. One view in
Sukkah 52a applies Zechariah 12:10, "they shall look upon me whom they have pierced", to "the
Messiah son of Joseph, who was killed", a second Messiah beside the son of David; the other view
reads the verse of the evil inclination. And in Sanhedrin 98b the Rabbis give the Messiah's name as
"the leper", from Isaiah 53:4 (Wikisource Talmud Bavli, our translation). Both came centuries after
the cross, and both keep the suffering off the son of David. The ledger counts them as read only in
a later Jewish source.

## Done by those who did not know

His opponents fulfilled 19 of the prophecies in the ledger, and none of the 19 was read of the
Messiah before Jesus. Luke records the apostles saying so twice.

> ✝️ Acts 3:17-18 (BSB)
>
> 17 And now, brothers, I know that you acted in ignorance, as did your leaders. 18 But in this way God has fulfilled what He foretold through all the prophets, saying that His Christ would suffer.

Paul said the same in the synagogue at Pisidian Antioch: "The people of Jerusalem and their rulers
did not recognize Jesus, yet in condemning Him they fulfilled the words of the prophets that are read
every Sabbath" (Acts 13:27, BSB). And to Corinth: "None of the rulers of this age understood it. For
if they had, they would not have crucified the Lord of glory" (1 Corinthians 2:8, BSB).

### ἀγνοέω, used of the rulers and the disciples

"Did not recognize" in Acts 13:27 is ἀγνοήσαντες, from ἀγνοέω (*agnoeō*, G50), "not to know". Luke
used the same verb of the disciples in Luke 9:45: "they did not understand (ἠγνόουν) this statement".
The rulers and the disciples shared the same ignorance of the suffering. What separated them came
after: the disciples' minds were opened.

### Who brought each fulfilment about

Some say the fulfilments were staged: that Jesus, or His followers, arranged events to match the
texts. The ledger records who brought each fulfilment about, and the record answers the charge. The
soldiers who divided His garments did not know Psalm 22:18 (John 19:24). The chief priests who bought
the potter's field did not mean to fulfil Zechariah 11:12-13, which Matthew credits to
Jeremiah (Matthew 27:9-10). The soldiers who left
His legs unbroken and pierced His side did not know Exodus 12:46 or Zechariah 12:10 (John 19:36-37).
No one in Jerusalem read those texts of the Messiah, so no one could have aimed at them.

The honest exceptions are recorded too. Jesus Himself fulfilled 18 prophecies knowingly, and one of
them was known before: the anointed one of Isaiah 61:1-2, whom 4Q521 and 11Q13 already expected. He
sent for the donkey (Matthew 21:2-5), and "to fulfill the Scripture, Jesus said, 'I am thirsty'" (John 19:28,
BSB). His followers fulfilled two: the choice of Matthias to take Judas' office (Psalm 69:25; Psalm 109:8;
Acts 1:20) and His friends standing far off at the cross (Psalm 38:11; Luke 23:49). Those are
the cases a critic can point to. They are the minority, and the acts His enemies did make up the
heart of the passion.

## Seen after

The New Testament says in so many words that some prophecies were understood only afterwards.
"After He was raised from the dead, His disciples remembered that He had said this. Then they
believed the Scripture" (John 2:22, BSB). "At first His disciples did not understand these things,
but after Jesus was glorified they remembered" (John 12:16, BSB). "For they still did not understand
from the Scripture that Jesus had to rise from the dead" (John 20:9, BSB).

### The opened eyes, the opened Scriptures, the opened minds

Luke 24 tells how the understanding came. On the road to Emmaus "their eyes were kept from
recognizing Him" (Luke 24:16, BSB). Jesus rebuked them: "O foolish ones, and slow of heart to believe
all that the prophets have spoken! Was it not necessary for the Christ to suffer these things and
then to enter His glory?" (Luke 24:25-26, BSB).

Then three times Luke uses one verb, διανοίγω (*dianoigō*, G1272), "to open fully". At the table
"their eyes were opened and they recognized Jesus" (Luke 24:31, BSB). They asked, "Were not our hearts
burning within us as He... opened the Scriptures to us?" (Luke 24:32, BSB). And in Jerusalem:

> ✝️ Luke 24:44-46 (BSB)
>
> 44 Jesus said to them, "These are the words I spoke to you while I was still with you: Everything must be fulfilled that is written about Me in the Law of Moses, the Prophets, and the Psalms." 45 Then He opened their minds to understand the Scriptures. 46 And He told them, "This is what is written: The Christ will suffer and rise from the dead on the third day,

The word occurs eight times in the SBLGNT, seven of them in Luke and Acts (the eighth is Mark 7:34). Its last use is Paul
in Thessalonica (Acts 17:3), which [Foretold Long Ago](foretold-long-ago.md#opening-and-setting-before)
follows.

### Sufferings, then glories

The order Jesus gave on the road is the order the prophets saw without understanding. Peter writes
that the prophets "searched and investigated carefully, trying to determine the time and setting to
which the Spirit of Christ in them was pointing when He predicted the sufferings of Christ and the
glories to follow" (1 Peter 1:10-11, BSB). The glories were read. The sufferings came first, and
they were seen after.

The Old Testament pictures of the gospel that were seen only afterwards, the Passover lamb, the
bronze serpent and the Day of Atonement among them, are the subject of [The Gospel in the Old
Testament](../series/the-gospel-in-the-old-testament.md). Hosea 11:1 is another. "Out of Egypt I
called My son" (Hosea 11:1, BSB) remembered the exodus, and no Jewish source reads it of the
Messiah. Matthew saw it fulfilled when the child came out of Egypt (Matthew 2:15). [Weighing the
Prophecies](weighing-the-prophecies.md#out-of-egypt-one-prophecy-weighed) works through it.

## Why God hid it

Paul calls it "the mysterious and hidden wisdom of God, which He destined for our glory before time
began" (1 Corinthians 2:7, BSB). The cross was no accident that the prophets happened to describe. It
was the counsel of God. "He was delivered up by God's set plan and foreknowledge, and you, by the
hands of the lawless, put Him to death" (Acts 2:23, BSB). The church prayed that Herod, Pilate, the
Gentiles and Israel "carried out what Your hand and will had decided beforehand would happen" (Acts
4:28, BSB).

This shows that God is sovereign over the ignorance of men. He wrote the sufferings of His Son into
the Scriptures and kept their meaning veiled until the men who carried them out had done so. That is
the doctrine of providence at the centre of the atonement: the sin of the rulers was their own, and
the plan was His. Their ignorance did not excuse them; Peter called them to "repent, then, and turn
back, so that your sins may be wiped away" (Acts 3:19, BSB). And God's hiding did not excuse the
disciples' slowness. Jesus called them "slow of heart to believe all", because they had believed the
glory and passed over the suffering in the same Scriptures (Luke 24:25).

### The glory still to come

The pattern also teaches you how to read what is still ahead. Three of the sixteen prophecies the ledger files
as still to come were read of the Messiah before He came, among them Psalm 2's rod of iron (Psalms of
Solomon 17:26, Swete). The disciples read the glory and missed the cross. Do not read the cross and miss
the glory still to come. Ask Him, as they had to, to open your mind to all that the prophets have
spoken.

## Discussion questions

1. **The language.** Luke uses ἀγνοέω (*agnoeō*), "not to know", for the disciples (Luke 9:45) and
   for the rulers who condemned Jesus (Acts 13:27). What did the two groups share, and what made
   the difference between them in the end (Luke 24:45)?
2. **The text in its context.** The crowd said, "the Christ will remain forever" (John 12:34). Which
   promises were they reading, and why could they not fit "lifted up" beside them?
3. **Prophecy and Christ.** "Was it not necessary for the Christ to suffer these things and then to
   enter His glory?" (Luke 24:26). Why did the sufferings have to come first?
4. **The hard part.** Luke says the meaning was veiled from the disciples "so that they could not
   comprehend it" (Luke 9:45), and Jesus still called them "slow of heart to believe" (Luke 24:25).
   How can both be true? How does Acts 3:17-19 hold God's plan and the rulers' guilt together?
5. **Be transformed.** The disciples believed the parts of Scripture they wanted and passed over the
   rest. Which part of God's word do you find hardest to believe, and what would it mean to ask Him
   to open it to you?

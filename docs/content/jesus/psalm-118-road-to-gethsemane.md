# Psalm 118 on the Road to Gethsemane

The Last Supper ended in song. "And when they had sung a hymn, they went out to the Mount of Olives"
(Matthew 26:30, ESV). Matthew does not say what they sang. The Passover meal had its own psalms,
and the last of them was Psalm 118.

**In one sentence:** After the Last Supper Jesus very likely sang the close of the Passover Hallel,
ending with Psalm 118; so on His way to be arrested He sang of the stone the builders rejected, of the
one who comes in the name of the LORD, and of the righteous who will not die but live, and the God
whose steadfast love endures forever kept every word of it for Him and keeps it for you.

## Key Takeaways

*(This section follows the [Key Takeaways](../about/key-takeaways.md) format — see that page for what each part is for.)*

### Types & Prophecy

- **Prophecy.** "The stone that the builders rejected has become the cornerstone" (Psalm 118:22,
  ESV). Jesus applied it to Himself (Matthew 21:42), and Peter preached it to the men who rejected
  Him (Acts 4:11).
- **Prophecy, read forward.** "Blessed is he who comes in the name of the LORD" (Psalm 118:26, ESV)
  is the greeting Jesus said Jerusalem would give before it saw Him again (Matthew 23:39).

### Lessons about Jesus

- **He sang before He suffered.** The only time the Gospels say Jesus sang is on the night He was
  betrayed.
- **He knew which psalm was His.** He had claimed its rejected stone that week (Matthew 21:42).

### Be Transformed

- **Think.** Jesus went to Gethsemane with Scripture in His mouth. Learn the psalms well enough to
  sing them when you need them.
- **Attitude.** Thankfulness in the dark. The psalm He sang begins and ends with "Oh give thanks to
  the LORD, for he is good" (Psalm 118:1, 29, ESV).
- **Do.** Read Psalm 118 aloud this week, slowly, as words Jesus sang that night.

## Which hymn?

"Sung a hymn" is one Greek word, ὑμνέω (*hymneō*, G5214). It occurs four times in the New Testament:
Matthew 26:30 and Mark 14:26 of this night, Acts 16:25 of Paul and Silas "praying and singing hymns
to God" in prison (ESV), and Hebrews 2:12, where Hebrews puts Psalm 22:22 in the Son's mouth: "in the
midst of the congregation I will sing your praise" (ESV).

The Passover hymns were the Hallel, Psalms 113-118. The Mishnah, written down around AD 200, records
how they were divided. The school of Hillel sang them before the meal as far as Psalm 114:8, "flint
stone into a water-spring" (Mishnah Pesachim 10:6), and over the fourth cup the leader "finishes
Hallel" (Mishnah Pesachim 10:7, Sefaria Community Translation, CC0). That leaves Psalms 115-118 for
after supper.

**How sure is it?** Fairly, and short of certain. The Mishnah is later than the Gospels, and Matthew
names no psalm. The ESV Study Bible names Psalm 118 as "the last psalm Jesus sang" in its note on the
psalm, and in its note on Matthew 26:30 allows the alternative of Psalm 136, "the last great Hallel
psalm". The Passover order points to Psalms 115-118, and so to Psalm 118 last.

## What He sang

> ✝️ Psalm 118:22-27 (ESV)
>
> 22 The stone that the builders rejected has become the cornerstone. 23 This is the LORD's doing;
> it is marvelous in our eyes. 24 This is the day that the LORD has made; let us rejoice and be glad
> in it. 25 Save us, we pray, O LORD! O LORD, we pray, give us success! 26 Blessed is he who comes in
> the name of the LORD! We bless you from the house of the LORD. 27 The LORD is God, and he has made
> his light to shine upon us. Bind the festal sacrifice with cords, up to the horns of the altar!

### The rejected stone

Earlier that week Jesus had told the chief priests, "Have you never read in the Scriptures: 'The
stone that the builders rejected has become the cornerstone; this was the Lord's doing, and it is
marvelous in our eyes'?" (Matthew 21:42, ESV). Within weeks Peter said it to the Sanhedrin: "This
Jesus is the stone that was rejected by you, the builders, which has become the cornerstone" (Acts
4:11, ESV). The New Testament settles who the stone is. **This shows that God turns His Son's
rejection into the foundation of His house.**

### The one who comes

The crowds had shouted Psalm 118:26 as He rode into Jerusalem: "Blessed is he who comes in the name
of the Lord!" (Matthew 21:9, ESV). Then He told the city, "you will not see me again, until you say,
'Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord'" (Matthew 23:39, ESV). This site reads that as
Israel's welcome when He returns; the ESV Study Bible says more cautiously that Jesus "may imply" the
psalm will be sung again then. At the same table Jesus vowed not to drink the fruit of the vine
"until that day when I drink it new with you in my Father's kingdom" (Matthew 26:29, ESV). Both
"until"s point to the same day. See
["I Will Not Drink Again"](../feasts/last-supper-four-cups.md) for the cup He left undrunk.

### The bound sacrifice

Within hours the soldiers "arrested Jesus and bound him" (John 18:12, ESV). The psalm's festal
sacrifice is a temple offering, and no New Testament writer quotes verse 27 of Jesus. The echo is
there for a reader who notices it; nothing rests on it.

### "I shall not die, but I shall live"

Earlier in the same psalm the singer says, "I shall not die, but I shall live, and recount the deeds
of the LORD" (Psalm 118:17, ESV). Jesus sang it the night before He died. On the third day He rose.

**So you may sing what He sang.** The psalm ends where it began: "Oh give thanks to the LORD, for he
is good; for his steadfast love endures forever!" (Psalm 118:29, ESV).

## Discussion questions

1. **The language.** Hebrews 2:12 uses the same verb for the Son singing God's praise among His
   brothers. What does it add that the Gospels' one record of Jesus singing is on the night He was
   betrayed?
2. **The text in its context.** Jesus quoted Psalm 118:22 in the temple that week (Matthew 21:42).
   What would the disciples have heard when they sang it with Him after supper?
3. **Christ.** Psalm 118:26 is the crowd's cry at the triumphal entry and the greeting of Matthew
   23:39. What does that line say about Jesus' first and second comings?
4. **The hard part.** The Gospels do not name the hymn, and the Mishnah is later. How far should
   this psalm shape the way you picture that night?
5. **Be transformed.** Which line of Psalm 118 do you most need to sing this week?

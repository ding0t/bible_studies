# Priesthood before Sinai: Adam, the firstborn and the Levites

In the second year after the exodus, camped at Sinai, God told Moses who would serve at His
tabernacle. He named one tribe, and He gave the reason:

> ✝️ Numbers 3:12-13 (ESV)
>
> 12 "Behold, I have taken the Levites from among the people of Israel instead of every firstborn
> who opens the womb among the people of Israel. The Levites shall be mine, 13 for all the firstborn
> are mine. On the day that I struck down all the firstborn in the land of Egypt, I consecrated for my
> own all the firstborn in Israel, both of man and of beast. They shall be mine: I am the LORD."

"Instead of" means the office already had holders. Numbers 3 records a handover, the latest
of several.

**In one sentence:** God has always chosen who serves Him as priest — the household heads of
Genesis, then Israel's firstborn, then the Levites in their place, and on a contested reading Adam
before them all — and every holder failed or died, until God appointed His Son Jesus, who "holds his
priesthood permanently, because he continues forever" (Hebrews 7:24, ESV).

This study grew out of [Jesus, Priest in the Order of Melchizedek](melchizedek-priesthood.md), and
traces the line that study's argument assumes.

### Lessons about Jesus

- **God appointed Jesus.** "Christ did not exalt himself to be made a high priest, but was appointed
  by him who said to him, 'You are my Son, today I have begotten you'" (Hebrews 5:5, ESV). Every
  priest before Him was chosen the same way, from Aaron back to Israel's firstborn.
- **Jesus is the last holder of the office.** Adam lost his charge, the firstborn lost theirs, and
  "the former priests were many in number, because they were prevented by death from continuing in
  office" (Hebrews 7:23, ESV). Jesus lives, so His priesthood passes to no one.
- **Jesus makes His people priests.** He "made us a kingdom, priests to his God and Father"
  (Revelation 1:6, ESV).

## Numbers 3 in its setting

Numbers opens at Sinai, "on the first day of the second month, in the second year after they had
come out of the land of Egypt" (Numbers 1:1, ESV). Chapters 1-4 order the camp before it moves: a
census of the fighting men, the tribes arranged around the tabernacle, and then two censuses of Levi.
The *ESV Study Bible* notes that the first Levite census counts every male from a month old so that
their number can be matched against Israel's firstborn males.[^esvsb-num3-census]

Chapter 3 begins with a death. "Nadab and Abihu died before the LORD when they offered unauthorized
fire before the LORD in the wilderness of Sinai, and they had no children" (Numbers 3:4, ESV). Aaron's
two eldest sons held the priesthood and lost it. The chapter about God's chosen servants opens by
recording that the chosen can fail.

Then come the Levites' duties (3:5-10). They minister to Aaron, guard the tent of meeting and its
furnishings, and serve at the tabernacle; Aaron and his sons "shall guard their priesthood," and "if
any outsider comes near, he shall be put to death" (3:10, ESV). The *ESV Study Bible* sums up the two
tasks as guarding the tabernacle from intruders and ministering at it.[^esvsb-num3-duties]

The count closes the substitution. The firstborn males numbered 22,273 (3:43) against 22,000 Levites
(3:39). The 273 left over had no Levite to stand in for them, so each was redeemed for "five shekels
per head" (3:46-47, ESV). God matched His servants to the people they served one for one, and paid the
remainder in silver.

**This shows that God** decides who draws near to Him and on what terms. He says so twice in two
verses: "They shall be mine: I am the LORD."

## Adam in the garden: a contested reading

Many interpreters trace the office back past Sinai to Eden. The reading is contested, so here is the
case for it, the case against it, and where this study stands.

### The case for a priest in Eden

God places the man in the garden "to work it and keep it" (Genesis 2:15, ESV). The Hebrew is
<span dir="rtl">לְעָבְדָהּ וּלְשָׁמְרָהּ</span>, from <span dir="rtl">עָבַד</span> (*avad*, "work,
serve") and <span dir="rtl">שָׁמַר</span> (*shamar*, "keep, guard"). The same two verbs recur
together for the Levites' work at the tabernacle: "they shall keep [<span dir="rtl">שָׁמַר</span>] his
charge... to do the service [<span dir="rtl">עָבַד</span>] of the tabernacle" (Numbers 3:7-8, ASV),
and again at 18:7. ASV's "charge" keeps the sense of the noun <span dir="rtl">מִשְׁמֶרֶת</span>
(*mishmeret*), which the ESV's "keep guard over him" flattens.

The *ESV Study Bible* draws the conclusion directly. Adam's role "is to be not only a gardener but
also a guardian... As a priest, he is to maintain the sanctity of the garden as part of a temple
complex". Its heading for the section names Eden "the Sanctuary of Eden."[^esvsb-eden] The *NIV
Biblical Theology Study Bible* makes the same point and adds where the office goes after Adam. When
the man is driven out, "the task of guarding the garden is given to cherubim" (Genesis 3:24) — and
the verb for what the cherubim do is <span dir="rtl">שָׁמַר</span> again.[^nivbtsb-eden] The *NIV
Cultural Backgrounds Study Bible* adds that Genesis 2 assigns the man his function in priestly terms,
alongside the royal ones of 1:28.[^nivcbsb-eden]

The tabernacle repeats Eden's shape. Eden is guarded on the east by cherubim (Genesis 3:24). The
tabernacle has cherubim worked into its veil (Exodus 26:31) and set over the ark, where God says, "There
I will meet with you" (Exodus 25:22, ESV). And in Numbers 3 the east side of the tabernacle, "toward
the sunrise," is held by Moses, Aaron and his sons, "guarding the sanctuary itself" (3:38, ESV) —
<span dir="rtl">שֹׁמְרִים</span> (*shomerim*), "keepers," from the same verb.

### The case against

Genesis never calls Adam a priest. The word <span dir="rtl">כֹּהֵן</span> (*kohen*, "priest") first
appears in Scripture at Genesis 14:18, of Melchizedek. Eden has no altar and no sacrifice.

The verb <span dir="rtl">עָבַד</span> frames Genesis 2:15 on both sides with farming: before the
garden "there was no man to work the ground" (2:5, ESV), and after it God sent him out "to work the
ground from which he was taken" (3:23, ESV). In both verses the object is <span dir="rtl">הָאֲדָמָה</span>,
"the ground." Read inside Genesis, *avad* and *shamar* describe a gardener. The priestly sense arrives
only when Numbers is read back into Genesis, and a reader who holds that Genesis should be heard first
on its own terms will stop there.

### Where this study stands

The verbal link is in the text, and the cherubim at the east gate make it hard to dismiss as a
coincidence. Calling Adam a priest is still an inference that Genesis leaves unstated, so this study
holds it as a reading and builds nothing on it alone. The line from the household heads to the
Levites stands on explicit texts, and the argument below works without Eden.

If the reading is right, it shows that God made the first man to serve and guard in His presence, and
that the office He gave was taken from Adam when he failed and given to others to guard.

## Household priests: Noah and the patriarchs

Genesis has no separate priestly class. The household head acts as his family's priest: Noah "built
an altar to Yahweh... and offered burnt offerings" with his own hands after the flood (Genesis 8:20,
WEB), and Abraham and Isaac do the same at their own altars (Genesis 12:7-8; 26:25). In this period
priesthood belongs to headship. No one is set apart into it.

## From the firstborn to the Levites

That changes at Sinai, in two steps.

**The firstborn claimed.** After the tenth plague spared Israel's firstborn, God claims them: "Sanctify
to me all the firstborn... It is mine" (Exodus 13:2, WEB). The *ESV Study Bible*'s note on Numbers
3:11-13 draws the implication: Israel's firstborn sons were meant to serve God in the sanctuary, in
place of their Egyptian counterparts who died.[^esvsb-num3] At Sinai God set the calling of the whole
nation even wider: "you shall be to me a kingdom of priests and a holy nation" (Exodus 19:6, ESV).

**The Levites in their place.** That plan did not survive the golden calf. When Moses called "whoever
is on Yahweh's side" after Israel's idolatry, only "the sons of Levi gathered themselves together to
him". They carried out the judgment that followed, and were told, "Consecrate yourselves today to
Yahweh... that he may give you a blessing today" (Exodus 32:26-29, WEB).

Numbers 3:12 then narrates the transfer that loyalty earned. The key word is
<span dir="rtl">תַּחַת</span> (*tachat*, "instead of"): God took the Levites "instead of" every
firstborn. It is restated at 8:16: "instead of all who open the womb, even the firstborn of all the
children of Israel, I have taken them to me" (WEB).

So the priestly service that began as every Israelite firstborn son's birthright passed, because of
Israel's failure and Levi's faithfulness at that failure, to one tribe. Aaron's line within that
tribe is appointed separately (Exodus 28:1), and there the office narrows to a single family — whose
two eldest sons then die in Numbers 3:4.

## What the line sets up for Hebrews

Three things hold at every stage of this history, and Hebrews' case for Jesus depends on the same
three holding of Him.

### God appoints

Adam is placed in the garden (Genesis 2:15). The firstborn are claimed by God's own declaration ("It
is mine," Exodus 13:2, WEB). The Levites are "taken" by God and "wholly given" to Him (Numbers 3:12;
8:16); they proved, at cost, which side they were on, and God did the choosing. Hebrews states the
principle of Aaron and applies it to Jesus: "no one takes this honor for himself, but only when called
by God, just as Aaron was" (Hebrews 5:4, ESV). "So also Christ... was appointed" (5:5, ESV).

### Every holder is replaced

Adam forfeits his charge and it passes to the cherubim — on the reading above. The firstborn's claim
passes to the Levites after the golden calf. Nadab and Abihu die, and Eleazar and Ithamar serve in
their place (Numbers 3:4). Hebrews 7:11 argues that the Levitical priesthood could not bring
perfection and had to be succeeded in turn. Hebrews names the problem in one line: "The former priests
were many in number, because they were prevented by death from continuing in office" (7:23, ESV). And
it names Jesus' answer in the next: "he holds his priesthood permanently, because he continues
forever" (7:24, ESV).

### Melchizedek stands outside the line

Melchizedek belongs to none of these stages. He is outside Adam's garden, Noah's household, Abraham's
family and Israel's firstborn. That is why Hebrews reaches for him. Psalm 110:4 and Hebrews 7 need a
precedent for a lawful priest-king who owes nothing to Israel's repeatedly reassigned priesthood, and
Melchizedek, met by Abraham before anything in Exodus 13-32 has happened, is that precedent. Later
rabbinic tradition sometimes identifies Melchizedek with Shem, Noah's son, which would tie him into
the household line; that identification is post-biblical, and Hebrews 7:3 builds its argument on
Melchizedek having no recorded genealogy.

## The priest who continues forever

The line from Sinai shows that God keeps to Himself the choice of who may come near Him. He chose, He took, and He gave. It also shows that every priest He gave was a sinner or a
mortal, and usually both. Numbers 3 opens with two priests dead at the altar and closes with 273
firstborn bought back for silver because there were not enough Levites to go round.

God's answer to both problems is His Son. Jesus is appointed by the Father (Hebrews 5:5), so His
office carries God's own authority. Jesus lives forever (7:24), so His office never changes hands.
"Consequently, he is able to save to the uttermost those who draw near to God through him, since he
always lives to make intercession for them" (7:25, ESV). The succession that ran from Eden to Aaron
ends in a High Priest who is never succeeded.

The firstborn language reaches the church too. Hebrews calls God's gathered people "the assembly of
the firstborn who are enrolled in heaven" (Hebrews 12:23, ESV). "Firstborn" there is plural,
πρωτοτόκων (*prōtotokōn*), "firstborn ones." The *ESV Study Bible* reads it as Jesus' followers granted an inheritance as though
each were a firstborn son.[^esvsb-heb12] God claimed Israel's firstborn at the exodus; in Jesus He
claims His people the same way, and Jesus "made us a kingdom, priests to his God and Father"
(Revelation 1:6, ESV).

So you may come near to God today, and you come through a priest God appointed, who has never failed
His charge and never will.

## Discussion Questions

1. Genesis 2:15 uses *avad* and *shamar* of Adam, and Numbers 3:7-8 uses them of the Levites. What
   would the reader of Genesis lose if the priestly reading were wrong?
2. Numbers 3 opens with the deaths of Nadab and Abihu (3:4). What does that do to the chapter's
   account of the Levites' appointment?
3. God took the Levites "instead of" the firstborn (Numbers 3:12), and redeemed the 273 extra
   firstborn with silver (3:46-47). What does that exchange show about how God regards the people He
   claims?
4. The Levites earned their place by standing with Moses at the golden calf (Exodus 32:26-29), yet
   Numbers 3:12 says God "took" them. How do you hold those two texts together?
5. Hebrews 7:23-24 contrasts many priests who died with one who "continues forever." How does Jesus'
   permanent priesthood change the way you pray?

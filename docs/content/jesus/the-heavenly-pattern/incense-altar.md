# The Golden Altar of Incense

*The prayers of the saints before the throne of God*

Luke opens his Gospel's story at this altar, and the first thing said there is that a prayer has
been heard. An old priest named Zechariah was chosen by lot to burn the incense (Luke 1:9). Outside,
the people prayed at the same hour:

> ✝️ Luke 1:10-11, 13 (ESV)
>
> 10 And the whole multitude of the people were praying outside at the hour of incense. 11 And there
> appeared to him an angel of the Lord standing on the right side of the altar of incense. ... 13 But
> the angel said to him, "Do not be afraid, Zechariah, for your prayer has been heard, and your wife
> Elizabeth will bear you a son, and you shall call his name John.

The smoke went up inside while the people prayed outside, and the angel who stood by the altar
answered a prayer.

**In one sentence:** God set a golden altar before the veil, facing the mercy seat, where the priest
burned God's own incense every morning and evening as he tended the lamps; Scripture itself says what
that incense is, the prayers of God's people (Psalm 141:2; Revelation 5:8), and in heaven the same
altar receives the saints' prayers and sends fire back to the earth, so the prayers of the saints are
heard before God's throne and answered in what He does.

This is the second furnishing in [The Heavenly Pattern](index.md), after [The
Lampstand](lampstand.md).

### Types & Prophecy

- **Revelation names what the incense is.** The elders hold "golden bowls full of incense, which are
  the prayers of the saints" (Revelation 5:8, ESV). Psalm 141:2 had already prayed, "Let my prayer be
  counted as incense before you."
- **Malachi foresaw incense offered among the nations.** "In every place incense will be offered to my
  name, and a pure offering" (Malachi 1:11, ESV).

### Lessons about Jesus

- **Prayer reaches God through the priest God appoints.** Only Aaron's line could burn this incense,
  and a king who tried was struck (2 Chronicles 26:18-19). Jesus is the High Priest God appointed, who
  "always lives to make intercession" for those who come to God through Him (Hebrews 7:25). Reading
  the altar as a picture of that intercession belongs to later interpreters; the *NIV Biblical
  Theology Study Bible* says it "may prefigure" it (note on Exodus 30:1).
- **Heaven receives the prayers of Jesus' people.** They are kept in golden bowls before the Lamb
  (Revelation 5:8), and the fire of the trumpet judgements comes from the altar where they rose
  (Revelation 8:3-5).

### Memory verses

>

> sacrifice!

> ✝️ Revelation 8:4 (ESV)

### Be Transformed

- **Think.** Your prayers rise before God's throne. Revelation shows them kept there in golden bowls.
- **Attitude.** Come to God on His terms: through Jesus, the priest He appointed.
- **Do.** Pray at a set hour morning and evening this week, as the incense was burned, and write down
  what you ask.

## Made after the pattern

The altar of incense is described apart from the other gold furniture. The ark, the table and the
lampstand come in Exodus 25, the priesthood in Exodus 28-29, and this altar in Exodus 30, after the
priest who will use it:

> ✝️ Exodus 30:1-6 (ESV)
>
> 1 "You shall make an altar on which to burn incense; you shall make it of acacia wood. 2 A cubit
> shall be its length, and a cubit its breadth. It shall be square, and two cubits shall be its
> height. Its horns shall be of one piece with it. 3 You shall overlay it with pure gold, its top and
> around its sides and its horns. And you shall make a molding of gold around it. 4 And you shall make
> two golden rings for it. Under its molding on two opposite sides of it you shall make them, and they
> shall be holders for poles with which to carry it. 5 You shall make the poles of acacia wood and
> overlay them with gold. 6 And you shall put it in front of the veil that is above the ark of the
> testimony, in front of the mercy seat that is above the testimony, where I will meet with you.

The Hebrew of verse 1 reads "an altar, a place of burning of incense":
<span dir="rtl">מִזְבֵּחַ</span> (*mizbeach*, H4196) and <span dir="rtl">מִקְטָר</span> (*miqtar*,
H4729), with <span dir="rtl">קְטֹרֶת</span> (*qetoret*, keh-TOH-ret, H7004), "incense."

### Drawn to scale

Exodus gives this altar's main dimensions, so the plate draws it to scale: a cubit square and two
cubits high, about 45 by 45 by 90 cm, roughly waist height. The text leaves three things unstated:
the size of the horns, the shape of the gold molding and the length of the poles. It is also unclear
about the rings. The ESV, CSB and NASB read two rings in all, one on each side; the NIV reads two on
each side. The Hebrew says "two" and "on its two sides" and allows either.

### In front of the mercy seat

Verse 6 places the altar twice: "in front of the veil" and "in front of the mercy seat." The veil
stood between them, and the altar faced the place God said He would meet with Moses (Exodus 25:22).
The lampstand and the table are placed on the south and north sides (Exodus 26:35); the altar is
placed by the mercy seat.

This shows that God put the place of prayer as close to His presence as a priest could come on an
ordinary day. The priest could not go through the veil, but every morning and evening he stood at the
altar that faced it.

## His incense and His fire

### A recipe reserved for God

God gave the incense its own formula: stacte, onycha, galbanum and frankincense, "of each shall there
be an equal part," blended "as by the perfumer, seasoned with salt, pure and holy" (Exodus 30:34-35,
ESV). The priests made it by that recipe, but no one was to make it for himself: "Whoever makes any
like it to use as perfume shall be cut off from his people" (Exodus 30:37-38, ESV). The
altar was forbidden "unauthorized incense," burnt offerings, grain offerings and drink offerings
(Exodus 30:9). Only God's incense went up from it.

### Nadab and Abihu

Aaron's two eldest sons found out what that meant on the day the priesthood began:

> ✝️ Leviticus 10:1-3 (ESV)
>
> 1 Now Nadab and Abihu, the sons of Aaron, each took his censer and put fire in it and laid incense
> on it and offered unauthorized fire before the LORD, which he had not commanded them. 2 And fire
> came out from before the LORD and consumed them, and they died before the LORD. 3 Then Moses said to
> Aaron, "This is what the LORD has said: 'Among those who are near me I will be sanctified, and before
> all the people I will be glorified.'" And Aaron held his peace.

The text says their fire was "unauthorized" and that God "had not commanded" it. What exactly made it
so is not said. The *NIV Biblical Theology Study Bible* lists the possibilities: coals from somewhere
other than the altar, the wrong incense, the wrong time, or the wrong room (note on Leviticus 10:1).

### Aaron between the dead and the living

After Korah's rebellion a plague broke out among the people, and Moses sent Aaron with incense:

> ✝️ Numbers 16:46-48 (ESV)
>
> 46 And Moses said to Aaron, "Take your censer, and put fire on it from off the altar and lay incense
> on it and carry it quickly to the congregation and make atonement for them, for wrath has gone out
> from the LORD; the plague has begun." 47 So Aaron took it as Moses said and ran into the midst of
> the assembly. And behold, the plague had already begun among the people. And he put on the incense
> and made atonement for the people. 48 And he stood between the dead and the living, and the plague
> was stopped.

Fire from the LORD consumed Nadab and Abihu as they offered incense, and it consumed Korah's 250 men
"offering the incense" in the same chapter (Numbers 16:35, ESV). Now incense saved the nation. This
time the priest God appointed used fire "from off the altar," as Moses told him. Numbers calls it
atonement.

### King Uzziah

Centuries later King Uzziah "grew proud" and went into the temple "to burn incense on the altar of
incense" (2 Chronicles 26:16, ESV). Eighty priests followed him in: "It is not for you, Uzziah, to burn
incense to the LORD, but for the priests, the sons of Aaron, who are consecrated to burn incense"
(2 Chronicles 26:18, ESV). He raged at them with the censer in his hand, and leprosy broke out on his
forehead "by the altar of incense" (2 Chronicles 26:19, ESV). He was a leper until he died.

This shows that God decides how He is approached. The incense was His, the fire was His, and the
priest was His choice. When Aaron came as God directed, incense stopped a plague. When men came on
their own terms, fire consumed them or leprosy struck them.

## Morning and evening, with the lamps

The altar kept the same hours as the lampstand:

> ✝️ Exodus 30:7-8 (ESV)
>
> 7 And Aaron shall burn fragrant incense on it. Every morning when he dresses the lamps he shall burn
> it, 8 and when Aaron sets up the lamps at twilight, he shall burn it, a regular incense offering
> before the LORD throughout your generations.

"Regular incense" is <span dir="rtl">קְטֹרֶת תָּמִיד</span> (*qetoret tamid*, H8548), the same word
that keeps the lamp burning in Exodus 27:20. "At twilight" is literally "between the evenings." Twice a
day the priest trimmed the lamps and burned the incense in one visit. [The Lampstand](lampstand.md)
covers the light; this altar is the other half of that daily service.

### Blood on its horns

Once a year the altar itself was cleansed: "Aaron shall make atonement on its horns once a year. With
the blood of the sin offering of atonement he shall make atonement for it once in the year throughout
your generations. It is most holy to the LORD" (Exodus 30:10, ESV). The *NIV Biblical Theology Study
Bible* ties this to the Day of Atonement of Leviticus 16 (note on Exodus 30:10).

On that day incense also went further than the altar. The high priest took coals and "two handfuls of
sweet incense beaten small" inside the veil, "that the cloud of the incense may cover the mercy seat
that is over the testimony, so that he does not die" (Leviticus 16:12-13, ESV). The one day a man went
into God's presence, he went in behind a cloud of incense.

## Outside the veil, belonging to the inner room

Exodus puts the altar outside the veil (Exodus 30:6; 40:26). Two later texts connect it to the room
behind it.

When Solomon built the temple, 1 Kings describes gold overlay for "the whole altar that belonged to the
inner sanctuary" (1 Kings 6:22, ESV). The Hebrew is "the altar that was to the *debir*," the inner
room.

Hebrews describes the Most Holy Place as "having the golden altar of incense and the ark of the
covenant" (Hebrews 9:4, ESV). Two questions sit in that sentence, and both are contested.

- **"Having."** The Greek ἔχουσα (*echousa*, G2192) can mean "containing." The *ESV Study Bible* argues that
  the writer, who knew the tabernacle, means the altar "belonged to" the Most Holy Place, since its
  smoke drifted in and it served the inner room (note on Hebrews 9:4). The *NIV Cultural Backgrounds
  Study Bible* agrees, pointing to 1 Kings 6:22.
- **"Altar."** The word is θυμιατήριον (*thymiatērion*, thoo-mee-ah-TAY-ree-on, G2369). In the Greek
  Old Testament it names a censer (2 Chronicles 26:19; Ezekiel 8:11), and the *Legacy Standard Bible*
  footnotes Hebrews 9:4 "Or censer." The KJV and NKJV read "the golden censer." Most modern
  translations read "altar." Read that way, Hebrews names every gold furnishing; read as "censer," its
  list leaves the golden altar out.

On either reading of Hebrews, Exodus is clear on where the altar stood: outside the veil (Exodus
30:6; 40:26). 1 Kings and Hebrews both tie it to the inner room it served.

## Incense is prayer

The Old Testament makes the connection before Revelation does. David asks:

> ✝️ Psalm 141:2 (ESV)
>
> 2 Let my prayer be counted as incense before you, and the lifting up of my hands as the evening
> sacrifice!

Luke shows the two happening together. "The whole multitude of the people were praying outside at the
hour of incense" (Luke 1:10, ESV), and the angel answered a prayer at the altar. Malachi, rebuking
priests who despised God's table, looked ahead to a day when "in every place incense will be offered to
my name, and a pure offering. For my name will be great among the nations" (Malachi 1:11, ESV). The
*ESV Study Bible* reads that as the nations brought in to worship the true God (note on Malachi 1:11).

This shows that God has always received the prayers of His people as an offering. The incense was the
priest's; the prayer belonged to everyone standing in the court.
[Prayer as Communion](../../christian-life/prayer-as-communion.md) takes up what that prayer is and how
Jesus and the apostles prayed.

## The golden altar before the throne

Revelation uses the Greek word for "altar," θυσιαστήριον (*thysiastērion*, G2379), eight times. Twice it
is called "the golden altar," the name Exodus gives the incense altar (Revelation 8:3; 9:13; Exodus
40:26).

### Prayers kept

When the Lamb takes the scroll, the elders fall before Him holding "golden bowls full of incense, which
are the prayers of the saints" (Revelation 5:8, ESV). This is Scripture's own interpretation of the
incense, and it is stated as plainly as "the seven lampstands are the seven churches" (Revelation
1:20, ESV). The prayers are in heaven, in gold, before the Lamb.

### The souls under the altar

At the fifth seal John sees "under the altar the souls of those who had been slain for the word of God
and for the witness they had borne," crying, "O Sovereign Lord, holy and true, how long before you will
judge and avenge our blood on those who dwell on the earth?" (Revelation 6:9-10, ESV). Which altar this
is, is contested. The *Cultural Backgrounds Study Bible* reads it as the altar of sacrifice, where blood
was poured at the base (note on Revelation 6:9), and the *ESV Study Bible* describes it as the place
"where sacrificial blood would pool" (note on Revelation 6:9-11). The *NIV Biblical Theology Study
Bible* leaves open either altar (note on Revelation 6:9). The *ESV Study Bible* also argues that heaven
has one altar serving both purposes (note on Revelation 8:3-5). Either way the martyrs' cry is a prayer at an altar
before God. [At Home with the Lord](../../last-things/at-home-with-the-lord.md) covers what the
passage says about believers who have died.

### Prayers offered, fire returned

> ✝️ Revelation 8:3-5 (ESV)
>
> 3 And another angel came and stood at the altar with a golden censer, and he was given much incense
> to offer with the prayers of all the saints on the golden altar before the throne, 4 and the smoke
> of the incense, with the prayers of the saints, rose before God from the hand of the angel. 5 Then
> the angel took the censer and filled it with fire from the altar and threw it on the earth, and
> there were peals of thunder, rumblings, flashes of lightning, and an earthquake.

The censer here, λιβανωτός (*libanōtos*, G3031), appears in the New Testament only in these two
verses. The angel does in heaven what Zechariah did in the temple: he offers incense with the prayers
of the people. Then the same censer carries fire from the altar to the earth, and the seven trumpets
begin. Aaron's censer once turned wrath away (Numbers 16:46-48); this one brings it down. The *ESV
Study Bible* reads the sequence as an answer: "the judgments to follow answer the prayers of the
saints" (note on Revelation 8:3-5). That is a strong inference from the order of the vision, and
Revelation does not state it in so many words.

The altar keeps speaking. At the sixth trumpet a voice comes "from the four horns of the golden altar
before God" (Revelation 9:13, ESV), the horns Aaron touched with blood once a year. An angel "came out
from the altar, the angel who has authority over the fire" (Revelation 14:18, ESV). And when the
bowls are poured out John hears "the altar saying, 'Yes, Lord God the Almighty, true and just are your
judgments!'" (Revelation 16:7, ESV).

This shows that God keeps the prayers of His people and receives them with incense. The martyrs asked
"how long?" The first answer they were given was "rest a little longer" (Revelation 6:11, ESV). The
*ESV Study Bible* sees the answer beginning at the sixth seal (note on Revelation 6:9-11, citing
6:15-17), and the fire from the altar in Revelation 8 appears to carry it further. Revelation presents
their prayers as heard, and as part of how God judges the world.

### No temple in the city

The New Jerusalem has no temple: "its temple is the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb" (Revelation
21:22, ESV). "They will see his face" (Revelation 22:4, ESV). The prayer once offered outside a veil
is spoken face to face. [The New Jerusalem](../../last-things/new-jerusalem.md) covers the
city.

## What the altar shows about God

This table sorts the study's connections by how directly Scripture makes each one.

| Connection | Where | How firm |
|---|---|---|
| The incense in heaven is the prayers of the saints | Revelation 5:8; 8:3-4 | Stated |
| Prayer likened to incense | Psalm 141:2 | Stated, as a prayer |
| Prayer and incense at the same hour | Luke 1:10 | Stated, as narrative |
| The trumpet judgements answer the saints' prayers | Revelation 8:3-5 | A strong inference from the vision's order (*ESV Study Bible*) |
| The altar of Revelation 6:9 is the incense altar | Revelation 6:9 | Contested |
| Hebrews 9:4 means the altar, not a censer | Hebrews 9:4 | Contested; most translations read "altar" |
| The altar pictures Christ's intercession | Hebrews 7:25 | Read in by later interpreters (*NIV Biblical Theology Study Bible*, note on Exodus 30:1: "may prefigure") |

The stated rows carry the doctrine. God set a place of prayer as near to His presence as a priest
could stand on an ordinary day, and He guarded it. Only His incense, His fire and His priest were allowed there, because
"among those who are near me I will be sanctified" (Leviticus 10:3, ESV). When the priest He chose came
with the fire He gave, incense stopped a plague.

Heaven shows what the altar was a copy of. Your prayers go up before the throne. They are kept in gold
before the Lamb, and Revelation shows God acting in the world after they rise, including in
judgement on those who shed His people's blood.

So pray, and pray through Jesus. God appointed Him as your High Priest, and He "always lives to make
intercession" for those who draw near to God through Him (Hebrews 7:25, ESV). Come the way God
opened, and your prayer rises before Him.

## Discussion Questions

1. The altar is placed "in front of the mercy seat" though a veil stood between them (Exodus 30:6).
   What does that placement say about prayer?
2. Nadab and Abihu offered incense and died; Aaron offered incense and a plague stopped (Leviticus
   10:1-2; Numbers 16:46-48). What made the difference?
3. Uzziah was a strong, blessed king when he went in to burn incense (2 Chronicles 26:16). Why is
   success a danger at the altar?
4. The martyrs ask "how long?" (Revelation 6:10), and the fire of Revelation 8:5 comes from the altar
   where the saints' prayers rose. How should that shape the way you pray about injustice?
5. Hebrews 9:4 describes the Most Holy Place as "having" the golden altar, though Exodus puts it
   outside the veil. What does it tell you about prayer that the altar belonged to the room behind
   the veil?
6. The incense was burned "every morning" and "at twilight" (Exodus 30:7-8). What would a set time of
   prayer, morning and evening, change in your week?

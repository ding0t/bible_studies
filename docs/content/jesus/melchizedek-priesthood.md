# Jesus, Priest in the Order of Melchizedek

Genesis mentions him in three verses. The rest of the Old Testament mentions him once more, in a
single line of a psalm. Yet the letter to the Hebrews spends a whole chapter on this Canaanite
king-priest, because he is the pattern for the priesthood of God's own Son. Melchizedek appears
only long enough to bless Abram, receive a tenth of the spoils, and vanish. No birth notice. No
death notice. No father, no mother, no tribe.

**In one sentence:** God swore an oath that Jesus would be a priest forever in the order of Melchizedek, and Jesus holds that priesthood now — by an indestructible life, in the true heavenly sanctuary, through a single sacrifice that never has to be repeated — so you can draw near to God through Him with confidence.

## Key Takeaways

*(This section follows the [Key Takeaways](../about/key-takeaways.md) format — see that page for what each part is for.)*

### Types & Prophecy

**Type.** Melchizedek is a **τύπος** (*typos*, G5179) of Christ's priesthood. Hebrews 7:3 says he
was "made like" (**ἀφωμοιωμένος**) the Son of God. The likeness has two marks: he holds his
priesthood with no inherited claim to it, and Scripture records no end to it. The likeness runs
from the Son back to Melchizedek. The Son is the original, and Genesis drew His outline first.

A second type sits alongside it: the earthly tabernacle, a "copy and shadow" of a heavenly
original (Hebrews 8:5).

**Prophecy.** Psalm 110:4 is a direct, sworn oracle: "The LORD has sworn and will not change his
mind, 'You are a priest forever after the order of Melchizedek'" (ESV). Hebrews quotes it three
times (Hebrews 5:6; 7:17, 21) to press one point: God said this would happen, and it did. God bound
Himself by oath to give His people this priest, and He kept His word in Jesus.

### Lessons about Jesus

- He is prophet, priest and king in one person. Hebrews names all three in its first sentence
  (Hebrews 1:1-3).
- He was appointed by the Father, as every true priest is (Hebrews 5:4-6).
- He holds His priesthood "by the power of an indestructible life" (**ἀκατάλυτος**, Hebrews 7:16).
  His resurrection is His qualification.
- He is the new covenant's guarantor (**ἔγγυος**, Hebrews 7:22). He has staked His own life on it.
- He serves in the true sanctuary, the one "the Lord set up" (Hebrews 8:1-2).
- His sacrifice was "once for all" (**ἐφάπαξ**, Hebrews 7:27; 9:12; 10:10). It is finished.
- He "always lives to make intercession" (Hebrews 7:25). He is praying for His people today.

## What a priest is

### Prophet, priest and king

Hebrews names all three of Jesus' offices in its opening sentence.

> ✝️ Hebrews 1:1-3 (ESV)
>
> 1 Long ago, at many times and in many ways, God spoke to our fathers by the prophets, 2 but in
> these last days he has spoken to us by his Son, whom he appointed the heir of all things, through
> whom also he created the world. 3 He is the radiance of the glory of God and the exact imprint of
> his nature, and he upholds the universe by the word of his power. After making purification for
> sins, he sat down at the right hand of the Majesty on high,

| Office | Which way it faces | What he does | Hebrews 1:1-3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prophet | From God to the people | Speaks God's word to them (Deuteronomy 18:18) | "he has spoken to us by his Son" |
| Priest | From the people to God | Stands for them before God and offers sacrifice for their sins | "after making purification for sins" |
| King | God's rule over the people | Governs and defends them for God | "he sat down at the right hand" |

Israel anointed all three. Moses anointed Aaron (Leviticus 8:12). Samuel anointed David
(1 Samuel 16:13). God told Elijah to anoint Jehu king and Elisha prophet (1 Kings 19:16). The
title Christ, Χριστός (*Christos*, G5547), means "anointed one," so the title itself carries all
three offices. Eusebius made the point in the early fourth century: Israel's anointed kings were "typical
Christs" and its anointed prophets "Christs in type," and all of them pointed to the one "who is the only high priest of all, and the
only King of every creature, and the Father's only supreme prophet of prophets."[^eusebius-1-3]

This study is about the middle office.

### Hebrews' own definition

Hebrews defines a priest before it says a word about Melchizedek.

> ✝️ Hebrews 5:1-4 (ESV)
>
> 1 For every high priest chosen from among men is appointed to act on behalf of men in relation
> to God, to offer gifts and sacrifices for sins. 2 He can deal gently with the ignorant and
> wayward, since he himself is beset with weakness. 3 Because of this he is obligated to offer
> sacrifice for his own sins just as he does for those of the people. 4 And no one takes this honor
> for himself, but only when called by God, just as Aaron was.

That gives four marks:

1. **He is one of us.** He is "chosen from among men," so he can "deal gently" with the people he
   represents (Hebrews 5:2).
2. **God appoints him.** "No one takes this honor for himself" (Hebrews 5:4).
3. **He stands for us before God.** He acts "on behalf of men in relation to God" (Hebrews 5:1).
4. **He brings an offering.** "Every high priest is appointed to offer gifts and sacrifices; thus
   it is necessary for this priest also to have something to offer" (Hebrews 8:3, ESV).

A priest, then, is a man God appoints to stand for His people before Him, carrying an offering for
their sins. Jesus meets all four marks. He was made like His brothers (Hebrews 2:17). The Father called Him (Hebrews 5:5-6). He intercedes for His people (Hebrews 7:25). And
His offering was Himself (Hebrews 7:27).

### What the first readers already knew

Hebrews was written to Jewish believers, most likely while the temple still stood. They had watched priests at
work. The letter never stops to explain the priesthood to them, so a reader today needs seven
things they took for granted.

| What they knew | Where it is written | Where Hebrews leans on it |
|---|---|---|
| Sinful people cannot come near a holy God unaided. At Sinai Israel begged, "do not let God speak to us, lest we die." | Exodus 20:19 | Hebrews 5:1; 12:18-21 |
| Only Aaron's sons could be priests: "if any outsider comes near, he shall be put to death." Written registers proved who qualified. | Numbers 3:10; Ezra 2:62 | Hebrews 7:5, 16 |
| Priests offered sacrifices every day, blessed the people in God's name, and taught the Law. | Exodus 29:38-42; Numbers 6:23-27; Malachi 2:7 | Hebrews 7:27 |
| Once a year the high priest alone entered the Most Holy Place, "and not without taking blood." | Leviticus 16; Hebrews 9:7 | Hebrews 9:6-12, 25 |
| Israel paid its tithes to the tribe of Levi. | Numbers 18:21 | Hebrews 7:5-9 |
| Kings came from Judah and priests from Levi. King Uzziah burned incense in the temple and was struck with leprosy. | 2 Chronicles 26:16-21 | Hebrews 7:13-14 |
| The priesthood and the Law stood together: "under it the people received the law." | Exodus 28-29; Leviticus 8 | Hebrews 7:11-12 |

### The problem: a priest from Judah

Put the second row beside the sixth and the problem appears.

> ✝️ Hebrews 7:13-14 (ESV)
>
> 13 For the one of whom these things are spoken belonged to another tribe, from which no one has
> ever served at the altar. 14 For it is evident that our Lord was descended from Judah, and in
> connection with that tribe Moses said nothing about priests.

Under the Law, Jesus could not serve as a priest at all. "Now if he were on earth, he would not be
a priest at all, since there are priests who offer gifts according to the law" (Hebrews 8:4, ESV).
Yet God swore in Psalm 110:4 that David's Lord would be "a priest forever." The order of
Melchizedek is how both statements are true.

## What "the order of Melchizedek" means

### An order is the basis of a priesthood

**τάξις (*taxis*, G5010, "order")** is a word for a fixed rank or arrangement (Louw-Nida 58.21).
Hebrews uses it six times, every time of priesthood (Hebrews 5:6, 10; 6:20; 7:11 twice; 7:17).
Hebrews 7:11 sets two orders side by side: "the order of Melchizedek" and "the order of Aaron"
(ESV).

An order answers two questions about a priest. What makes him a priest? And how long does he
serve? Aaron's order answers both by birth. A son of Aaron became a priest because of his father,
served until he died, and handed the office to his son. When Aaron died on Mount Hor, Moses took
his garments and put them on Eleazar (Numbers 20:25-28).

Melchizedek's order answers the same two questions with God's appointment and an endless life.
God makes the priest, and the priest serves forever.

### The order's two marks: no inherited claim, no end

Hebrews explains its own phrase. Having said "after the order of Melchizedek," it restates it as
"in the likeness of Melchizedek."

> ✝️ Hebrews 7:15-17 (ESV)
>
> 15 This becomes even more evident when another priest arises in the likeness of Melchizedek,
> 16 who has become a priest, not on the basis of a legal requirement concerning bodily descent,
> but by the power of an indestructible life. 17 For it is witnessed of him, "You are a priest
> forever, after the order of Melchizedek."

"Likeness" is ὁμοιότης (*homoiotēs*, G3665). So a man belongs to Melchizedek's order by resembling
Melchizedek. Hebrews 7:3 says what the resemblance consists of:

> ✝️ Hebrews 7:3 (ESV)
>
> 3 He is without father or mother or genealogy, having neither beginning of days nor end of life,
> but resembling the Son of God he continues a priest forever.

The verse gives the order two marks.

**First, no inherited claim.** "Without genealogy" is ἀγενεαλόγητος (*agenealogētos*, G35),
found nowhere else in the Greek Bible. Hebrews 7:6 shows why it matters: Melchizedek is the one "who
does not have his descent from them" (ESV), μὴ γενεαλογούμενος ἐξ αὐτῶν (*mē genealogoumenos ex
autōn*). "Them" are the sons of Levi of Hebrews 7:5. A Levitical priest's genealogy was his licence.
After the exile, men who "sought their registration among those enrolled in the genealogies, but
they were not found there" were "excluded from the priesthood as unclean" (Ezra 2:62, ESV).
Scripture gives Melchizedek no such register, and he was a priest of God Most High all the same. His priesthood
rested on God alone.

**Second, no end.** Genesis records no death for Melchizedek, so "he continues a priest forever"
(Hebrews 7:3). Hebrews 7:8 makes the same point: Levitical tithes "are received by mortal men,
but in the other case, by one of whom it is testified that he lives" (ESV). In Jesus this mark is
fact. He is a priest "by the power of an indestructible life" (Hebrews 7:16), which is His
resurrection.

### Made like the Son

The verb in Hebrews 7:3 is ἀφωμοιωμένος (*aphōmoiōmenos*, from ἀφομοιόω, G871), "having been made
like." It occurs only here in the New Testament. It runs from the Son back to Melchizedek:
Melchizedek is made like the Son of God.

Hebrews uses the language of likeness for Jesus as well. The Son "had to be made like his brothers in
every respect, so that he might become a merciful and faithful high priest" (Hebrews 2:17, ESV), and
He "in every respect has been tempted as we are, yet without sin" (Hebrews 4:15, ESV). "As we are"
is καθ᾿ ὁμοιότητα, the same likeness-word as Hebrews 7:15. The Son was made like us so that He
could be our priest. Melchizedek was made like the Son so that Scripture could show, centuries
early, what kind of priest the Son would be.

This shows that God planned His Son's priesthood before Levi was born. He drew its outline in
Genesis 14 and swore to it in Psalm 110, and in Jesus you have the priest the outline was drawn from.

## The two texts Hebrews reads

### Where Hebrews gets all this

Every claim Hebrews makes about Melchizedek comes from two Old Testament texts.

| Hebrews 7 | Source |
|---|---|
| King of Salem, priest of God Most High, met Abraham and blessed him (7:1) | Genesis 14:18-19 |
| Abraham gave him a tenth (7:2, 4) | Genesis 14:20 |
| "King of righteousness… king of peace" (7:2) | The Hebrew of his name and his city |
| No father, mother or genealogy, no beginning or end recorded (7:3) | What Genesis 14 leaves out |
| "It is testified that he lives" (7:8) | Genesis 14's silence and Psalm 110:4's "forever" |
| A priest "forever," by oath (7:17, 21) | Psalm 110:4 |

The author learned the gospel from eyewitnesses. He says it "was declared at first by the Lord,
and it was attested to us by those who heard" (Hebrews 2:3, ESV). He reads Scripture as the Holy
Spirit's own speech: "as the Holy Spirit says" (Hebrews 3:7), "the Holy Spirit indicates" (Hebrews
9:8), "the Holy Spirit also bears witness" (Hebrews 10:15, all ESV). His work is to read out what
the Spirit put there. Jesus Himself first read Psalm 110 this way, asking how David's
son could be David's Lord (Matthew 22:41-45), and Peter preached the same psalm at Pentecost
(Acts 2:34-36). Hebrews takes the next step, from Psalm 110:1 to Psalm 110:4. The King at God's
right hand is also the Priest sworn in by oath.

So you can check every step against Genesis and the Psalms. Whether the author also had the
Jewish Melchizedek traditions of his day in view is debated; see
[Did Hebrews know this conversation?](../scripture/melchizedek-in-second-temple-judaism.md#did-hebrews-know-this-conversation).

### Genesis 14: a priest without a genealogy

Genesis 14 is a war story. Four eastern kings carried off the plunder of Sodom, and Lot with it.
Abram armed 318 men of his household, pursued them north past Dan, and brought everything back
(Genesis 14:1-16). Returning through the King's Valley, he was met by two kings: the king of Sodom,
and Melchizedek.

> ✝️ Genesis 14:18-20 (ESV)
>
> 18 And Melchizedek king of Salem brought out bread and wine. (He was priest of God Most High.)
> 19 And he blessed him and said, "Blessed be Abram by God Most High, Possessor of heaven and
> earth; 20 and blessed be God Most High, who has delivered your enemies into your hand!" And
> Abram gave him a tenth of everything.

**The city.** Salem is generally identified with Jerusalem. Psalm 76:2 places God's dwelling "in
Salem," and Egyptian execration texts from around 1900 BC already mention Jerusalem.[^cbsb-gen14]

**King and priest.** Melchizedek combined the roles of priest and king, as many rulers in the
ancient world did.[^cbsb-gen14] Israel's Law later kept the two offices apart, as the table above shows.
Melchizedek holds both, some four centuries before Levi is set apart.

**The name.** Melchizedek, Hebrew <span dir="rtl">מַלְכִּי־צֶדֶק</span> (*malkî-ṣedeq*), joins
<span dir="rtl">מֶלֶךְ</span> (*melek*, H4428, "king") and <span dir="rtl">צֶדֶק</span>
(*ṣedeq*, H6664, "righteousness," TWOT 1879a): "king of righteousness." Salem
(<span dir="rtl">שָׁלֵם</span>, *shalem*, H8004) shares its consonants with
<span dir="rtl">שָׁלוֹם</span> (*shalom*, H7965, "peace"). Hebrews 7:2 gives both meanings "by
translation" for Greek-speaking readers.

**The God he served.** "God Most High" is <span dir="rtl">אֵל עֶלְיוֹן</span> (*El Elyon*). Both
words were used of Canaanite gods, though the compound is not found in the Ugaritic
texts.[^cbsb-gen14] Whether Melchizedek knew Abram's God is contested. Abram makes the
identification two verses later, swearing "to Yahweh, God Most High, possessor of heaven and earth"
(Genesis 14:22, WEB), and Hebrews 7:1 calls Melchizedek "priest of the Most High God" (ESV).

**The silence.** Genesis is built on genealogies; its sections open "these are the generations
of." The one man in it whom it calls a priest of God Most High, <span dir="rtl">כֹּהֵן</span> (*kōhēn*,
H3548), has no father, mother, birth or death on record. Genesis does not say he had none. It leaves them out,
and Hebrews 7:3 builds on what the record leaves out.

So before there was a Law or a Levite, God already had a priest-king blessing Abram in His name.

### Psalm 110: the oath

Melchizedek appears once more in the Hebrew Bible, in a royal psalm of David.

> ✝️ Psalm 110:4 (ESV)
>
> 4 The LORD has sworn and will not change his mind, "You are a priest forever after the order of
> Melchizedek."

The psalm opens with the king: "The LORD says to my Lord: 'Sit at my right hand, until I make your
enemies your footstool'" (Psalm 110:1, ESV). He rules from Zion (Psalm 110:2) and crushes his
enemies (Psalm 110:5-6). Verse 4 then makes that same king a priest, by God's oath, in Melchizedek's
order. Psalm 110:4 promises one who would be both, on the precedent Melchizedek set before either tribe existed.

Zechariah picks up the same hope after the exile. "The Branch" will "build the temple of the LORD,"
"bear royal honor, and shall sit and rule on his throne," and "there shall be a priest on his
throne, and the counsel of peace shall be between them both" (Zechariah 6:12-13, ESV). Whether the
Branch is himself that priest, or sits beside one ("between them both"), is debated. Either way,
Zechariah joins throne and priesthood in Israel's own messianic hope, independently of Hebrews.

God promised one person in whom rule and priesthood meet. In Jesus you have a King to obey and a
Priest who brings you to God.

## Hebrews' argument, traced

### Chapters 2-6: the case prepared

Hebrews introduces Jesus as "a merciful and faithful high priest in the service of God, to make
propitiation for the sins of the people" (Hebrews 2:17, ESV). Propitiation is the priest's work:
the offering that turns away God's wrath against sin.

It returns to the title in Hebrews 4:14 and turns it into an invitation: "Let us then with confidence draw near to the throne of grace, that we may
receive mercy and find grace to help in time of need" (Hebrews 4:16, ESV).

Chapter 5 gives the definition of a priest quoted above, and then names Jesus' appointment by
setting two psalms side by side.

> ✝️ Hebrews 5:5-6 (ESV), quoting Psalm 2:7 and Psalm 110:4
>
> 5 So also Christ did not exalt himself to be made a high priest, but was appointed by him who
> said to him, "You are my Son, today I have begotten you"; 6 as he says also in another place,
> "You are a priest forever, after the order of Melchizedek."

Psalm 2 is about the King, God's Son. Psalm 110:4 makes the King a priest. Hebrews joins them into
one appointment.

**The pause.** Just where the Melchizedek argument should begin, the author stops: "About this we
have much to say, and it is hard to explain, since you have become dull of hearing" (Hebrews 5:11,
ESV). A warning against spiritual immaturity follows, through Hebrews 6:12.

**The hinge.** Hebrews 6:13-20 returns by way of God's oath to Abraham. God "guaranteed it with an
oath, so that by two unchangeable things, in which it is impossible for God to lie, we who have fled
for refuge might have strong encouragement" (Hebrews 6:17-18, ESV). A promise with an oath on top
of it is doubly sure, and chapter 7 will apply that to Psalm 110:4. The section closes on the
sentence chapter 7 expounds: Jesus has gone behind the curtain "as a forerunner on our behalf,
having become a high priest forever after the order of Melchizedek" (Hebrews 6:20, ESV).

### Hebrews 7:1-10: the man, greater than Levi

The first ten verses are about Melchizedek himself. Their question is how great he was.

**He blessed Abraham.** "It is beyond dispute that the inferior is blessed by the superior"
(Hebrews 7:7, ESV). In Genesis 14, Melchizedek blesses and Abraham is blessed.

**He received Abraham's tithe.** The Levites "have a commandment in the law to take tithes from the
people" (Hebrews 7:5, ESV). Melchizedek received tithes from Abraham (Hebrews 7:6).

**So he outranks Levi.** "One might even say that Levi himself, who receives tithes, paid tithes
through Abraham, for he was still in the loins of his ancestor when Melchizedek met him" (Hebrews
7:9-10, ESV). Levi's priesthood, in the person of its ancestor, bowed to Melchizedek's.

Hebrews 7:3 and 7:8 carry the order's two marks, seen above. Everything in these verses comes from
Genesis 14.

### Is Melchizedek still a priest?

Read alone, "he continues a priest forever" (Hebrews 7:3) and "it is testified that he lives"
(Hebrews 7:8) sound as if the man Melchizedek is still serving. Readers have taken the verses four
ways, and the question is contested.

1. **He was a mortal man, and the verses describe the record of him.** This is the majority reading,
   and the *ESV Study Bible*'s: "without genealogy" means the information "is not given anywhere in
   Scripture," and in that sense he continues a priest forever.[^esvsb-heb7-3] The Greek supports
   it. "It is testified" (μαρτυρούμενος, *martyroumenos*, G3140) points to Scripture as the
   witness. Nine verses later the same verb introduces Psalm 110:4: "For it is witnessed of him,
   'You are a priest forever'" (Hebrews 7:17, ESV).
2. **He was the Son of God appearing before the incarnation.** Hebrews 7:3 counts against this:
   Melchizedek is *made like* the Son of God, and the one who resembles is a different person from
   the one he resembles.
3. **He was a heavenly being.** This is how one Qumran scroll (11Q13) reads him; see
   [Melchizedek in Second Temple Judaism](../scripture/melchizedek-in-second-temple-judaism.md).
   Hebrews calls him a man: "See how great this man was" (Hebrews 7:4, ESV).
4. **He was Shem, Noah's son.** This is the reading of Targum Pseudo-Jonathan and the Babylonian
   Talmud (*Nedarim* 32b). But Genesis gives
   Shem a full genealogy, "These are the generations of Shem" (Genesis 11:10, ESV), and a lifespan
   (Genesis 11:11). That is the opposite of *agenealogētos*.

The first reading fits the chapter best, for a reason inside the argument. Hebrews 7:23-24 rests
on one priest holding the office permanently. A Melchizedek still serving would make two, and the
contrast with Levi's "many" would fail. **On this reading the man Melchizedek died, and Genesis
does not record it. In Genesis he only looks deathless. Jesus is deathless in fact, because He
rose, and the order has one living priest.**

### Hebrews 7:11-19: the order, and a change of law

The second part moves from the man to the order, and from Genesis 14 to Psalm 110.

**Why promise another priest?** "Now if perfection had been attainable through the Levitical
priesthood (for under it the people received the law), what further need would there have been for
another priest to arise after the order of Melchizedek, rather than one named after the order of
Aaron?" (Hebrews 7:11, ESV). God made the promise in Psalm 110, centuries after Aaron's line was in
place. The promise itself shows that Aaron's line was never meant to be final.

**Priesthood changed, Law changed.** "For when there is a change in the priesthood, there is
necessarily a change in the law as well" (Hebrews 7:12, ESV). The word for priesthood here is
**ἱερωσύνη (*hierōsynē*, G2420)**, the office itself, as distinct from ἱερεύς (*hiereus*, G2409),
the man who holds it. In the New Testament *hierōsynē* occurs only in Hebrews 7:11, 12 and 24. The
author is arguing about an institution. Because the Law and the Levitical priesthood stood
together, replacing one replaces the other.

**A priest from Judah.** Hebrews 7:13-14 then names the problem stated at the start of this study.

**An indestructible life.** Hebrews 7:16 gives the answer, quoted above. **ἀκατάλυτος (*akatalytos*, G179, "indestructible")** occurs only here in the New Testament.
It joins the negative prefix to καταλύω (*katalyō*, G2647, "to tear down"), the verb Jesus used of
the temple's stones (Matthew 24:2). A genealogy can be checked, and it runs out at death. Jesus'
qualification is His risen life, which death cannot tear down.

**A better hope.** "For on the one hand, a former commandment is set aside because of its weakness
and uselessness (for the law made nothing perfect); but on the other hand, a better hope is
introduced, through which we draw near to God" (Hebrews 7:18-19, ESV). **κρείττων (*kreittōn*,
G2909, "better")** is the letter's structural word. It occurs twelve times in Hebrews (1:4;
7:7, 19, 22; 8:6 twice; 9:23; 10:34; 11:16, 35, 40; 12:24), more than in any other New Testament
book, and with "more" and "greater" the comparisons run to 25.[^esvsb-intro] Each one argues from
the lesser to the greater.

### Hebrews 7:20-28: the oath, and a priest forever

**Sworn in.** "And it was not without an oath. For those who formerly became priests were made such
without an oath, but this one was made a priest with an oath by the one who said to him: 'The Lord
has sworn and will not change his mind, "You are a priest forever"'" (Hebrews 7:20-21, ESV). No
Levitical priest was ever sworn in by God. This one was, and God cannot lie (Hebrews 6:18).

> ✝️ Hebrews 7:22-25 (ESV)
>
> 22 This makes Jesus the guarantor of a better covenant. 23 The former priests were many in
> number, because they were prevented by death from continuing in office, 24 but he holds his
> priesthood permanently, because he continues forever. 25 Consequently, he is able to save to the
> uttermost those who draw near to God through him, since he always lives to make intercession for
> them.

**Guarantor.** **ἔγγυος (*engyos*, G1450)** occurs only in Hebrews 7:22 in the New Testament. It is
a legal and commercial word for someone who stakes himself on another party's obligation. Hebrews
uses a different word, μεσίτης (*mesitēs*, G3316, "mediator"), at 8:6, 9:15 and 12:24 for the one
who brokers the covenant. A guarantor pays if the obligation fails. Jesus has pledged His own life
for the new covenant, and that life cannot fail. Your standing in the covenant rests on Him.

**Permanent.** **ἀπαράβατος (*aparabatos*, G531)**, in Hebrews 7:24, also occurs only here in the
New Testament. Most English versions render it "permanent" or "unchangeable." Lexicographers divide over the
exact sense, and some, from παραβαίνω (*parabainō*, G3845, "to go past"), read "not passing to a
successor." Both senses land on the
point of Hebrews 7:23-24. The One who intercedes for you today is the One who will intercede for you at
the end.

**Once for all.** The chapter closes on a priest who is "holy, innocent, unstained, separated from
sinners, and exalted above the heavens" (Hebrews 7:26, ESV). "He has no need, like those high
priests, to offer sacrifices daily, first for his own sins and then for those of the people, since
he did this once for all when he offered up himself" (Hebrews 7:27, ESV). **ἐφάπαξ (*ephapax*,
G2178, "once for all")** strengthens ἅπαξ (*hapax*, G530, "once"); Hebrews uses it at 7:27, 9:12
and 10:10. The daily repetition was itself the evidence, Hebrews argues, that those sacrifices never
removed sin (Hebrews 10:1-4).

**The oath came later.** "For the law appoints men in their weakness as high priests, but the word
of the oath, which came later than the law, appoints a Son who has been made perfect forever"
(Hebrews 7:28, ESV). Psalm 110 was sworn after Sinai, so God's last word on priesthood is the oath.

### Hebrews 8:1-5: the true sanctuary

**The point.** Chapter 8 names where the argument has been heading. "Now the point in what we are
saying is this: we have such a high priest, one who is seated at the right hand of the throne of the
Majesty in heaven, a minister in the holy places, in the true tent that the Lord set up, not man"
(Hebrews 8:1-2, ESV). The priesthood case and the sanctuary case are one argument.

**A different place.** Christ could not have served in the earthly tabernacle (Hebrews 8:4). His priesthood
belongs to a different order and operates in a different place.

**Copy and shadow.** The earthly tabernacle was a **ὑπόδειγμα καὶ σκιά** (*hypodeigma kai skia*),
"copy and shadow" (Hebrews 8:5), of a heavenly original. Hebrews grounds this in the tabernacle's
own blueprint: "See that you make everything according to the pattern (τύπος) that was shown you on
the mountain" (Hebrews 8:5, ESV, quoting Exodus 25:40). **σκιά (*skia*, G4639, "shadow")** occurs seven
times in the New Testament, twice in Hebrews (8:5; 10:1). Colossians 2:17 uses it of the same
problem: "These are a shadow of the things to come, but the substance belongs to Christ" (ESV).
**ὑπόδειγμα (*hypodeigma*, G5262, "copy")** carries this sense at Hebrews 8:5 and 9:23.

### Hebrews 9:24-10:18: one sacrifice, once for all

**Struck from the die.** Christ "has entered, not into holy places made with hands, which are
copies of the true things, but into heaven itself" (Hebrews 9:24, ESV). "Copies" there is
**ἀντίτυπος (*antitypos*, G499)**, one of two New Testament occurrences (the other is 1 Peter 3:21).
It joins ἀντί (*anti*, G473, "corresponding to") and τύπος (*typos*, "die, pattern"): an *antitypos* is
what is struck from a *typos*, the way a coin is struck from a die. The earthly tabernacle is the
coin, and heaven is the die. Melchizedek works the same way (Hebrews 7:3).

**One sacrifice.** Because Christ's priesthood is Melchizedek's, He serves in the true sanctuary.
Because the sanctuary is the true one, the covenant made there is better: Jeremiah's new covenant,
quoted at Hebrews 8:8-12 and again at 10:16-17 (Jeremiah 31:31-34). And it is sealed with His own
blood, by an offering that "perfected for all time those who are being sanctified" (Hebrews 10:14,
ESV).

This shows that God's purpose was always to bring you into His presence through one finished
sacrifice, and that His Son Jesus has done it. The atonement is complete. Your sanctification rests
on what Jesus "perfected for all time."

## Theological principle

### Confirmed elsewhere in Scripture

None of this rests on Genesis 14's silences alone. Romans 6:10 uses the same
word, ἐφάπαξ, of Christ's death, and 1 Peter 3:18 says He "suffered once for sins" (ESV). And Paul
says of the risen Christ that He "is interceding for us" (Romans 8:34, ESV).

### A dispensational reading

Melchizedek's priesthood predates Sinai by centuries and belongs to a king outside Abraham's line.
Priestly mediation, then, began before the Mosaic covenant and outside Israel. Christ's priesthood
"after the order of Melchizedek" rests on the same ground: God's oath and promise, the terms given
to Abraham four hundred and thirty years before the Law (Galatians 3:17). Hebrews 7:11-12 says that a
change of priesthood requires a change of law, so Christ's priesthood belongs to a different
economy. The Aaronic priesthood served Israel under the Law; the Son's priesthood serves under the
new covenant He guarantees.

### Confidence to draw near, now

Access to God no longer depends on priests who must repeat their work and then die. So you are told
to draw near with confidence (Hebrews 4:16). Jesus' priesthood cannot be transferred or
interrupted, so He is able to save to the uttermost those who draw near through Him (Hebrews 7:25).

This shows that God has provided the priest you need for as long as you need Him. When you pray, you
come to a Father who has accepted the one sacrifice made for you, and you come alongside His Son
Jesus, who is praying for you.

## Discussion questions

1. Hebrews 7:15 restates "the order of Melchizedek" as "the likeness of Melchizedek." What two
   things make up that likeness (Hebrews 7:3, 6, 16), and which of them could no Levitical priest
   share?
2. Under the Law a priest proved his right to serve with a written genealogy (Ezra 2:62). Jesus was
   from Judah (Hebrews 7:14). What does Hebrews put where the genealogy would be, and why can that
   never be lost?
3. Hebrews 7:3 says Melchizedek was "made like" the Son of God, and Hebrews 2:17 says the Son was
   "made like his brothers." What does each likeness accomplish?
4. Hebrews 7:3 says Melchizedek "continues a priest forever." Of the four readings in this study,
   which do you find most convincing, and how does Hebrews 7:23-24 bear on your answer?
5. Hebrews 7:25 says Jesus "always lives to make intercession" for those who draw near. If you took
   that as a description of what is happening right now, what would change in how you pray this
   week?

## Annex: Succession and authorship

### A chain of priests is Levi's pattern

Some teachers describe an "order of Melchizedek" handed down from Adam through Seth and Noah to
Shem, each holder appointed by God and serving until he died. The pieces of that picture are old.
Jubilees has Adam and Enoch burn incense, and the Targum, the Talmud and *Genesis Rabbah* make
Melchizedek Shem. [Before Aaron](priesthood-before-sinai.md#what-later-tradition-added) sets each
one beside what Scripture says.

Scripture itself calls no one a priest before Melchizedek. And the pattern of a priesthood handed
on at each death is the one Hebrews assigns to Levi (Hebrews 7:23-24). A chain of mortal priests,
each valid until death, is the "many" of Hebrews 7:23. Melchizedek's order is defined by the single
priest of Hebrews 7:24.

Scripture does record a priest outside Levi after Melchizedek. Jethro was "the priest of Midian"
(Exodus 3:1, ESV), and he "brought a burnt offering and sacrifices to God," with Aaron and the
elders eating bread "before God" (Exodus 18:12, ESV). Scripture does not say who appointed him,
and Hebrews never mentions him. The order Hebrews describes has no end, and Scripture places two
people in it: Melchizedek in the record, and Jesus in fact.

### Who wrote Hebrews, to whom, and when

**Author.** This study follows the traditional ascription of Hebrews to Paul, writing in Hebrew
with Luke as translator. Clement of Alexandria gives that account (Eusebius, *Church History*
6.14.2-4), and it would explain why the Greek reads closer to Luke-Acts than to Paul's letters.
The ascription is contested, and Hebrews 2:3 is the main reason. The author says the gospel "was
attested to us by those who heard," which places him among those who learned it from eyewitnesses.
Paul says of his gospel, "I did not receive it from any man, nor was I taught it, but I received it
through a revelation of Jesus Christ" (Galatians 1:12, ESV). The church received Hebrews as
Scripture whoever held the pen.

**Audience.** The readers were Jewish believers, probably with some God-fearing Gentiles among
them. They had endured persecution and the "plundering of your property" (Hebrews 10:32-34). Some
had stopped meeting together (Hebrews 10:25). The whole argument implies they were tempted to drift
back to the temple's sacrifices.

**Date.** Hebrews speaks of the sacrifices as still being offered (Hebrews 7:27-28; 8:3-5;
9:7-8, 25; 10:1-3; 13:10-11). An author showing that system's insufficiency would have pointed to
the temple's destruction if it had already happened. So the letter is most likely before AD 70.

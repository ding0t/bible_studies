# The Appointed Times

Leviticus 23 opens with a claim of ownership. "These are the appointed feasts of the LORD that you
shall proclaim as holy convocations; they are my appointed feasts" (Leviticus 23:2, ESV). They are
often called the Jewish feasts. God calls them His.

**In one sentence:** God set seven appointed times in Israel's year to meet with His people, and they
run in order from Passover to Tabernacles; the spring feasts are fulfilled in Jesus' first coming, as
He died as the Passover lamb, rose as the firstfruits and poured out the Holy Spirit at Pentecost,
and the three fall feasts look ahead to His return and kingdom; so you can learn them as shadows whose
substance is Christ, and wait for the appointments still to come.

> ✝️ Leviticus 23:1-4 (ESV)
>
> 1 The LORD spoke to Moses, saying, 2 "Speak to the people of Israel and say to them, These are the
> appointed feasts of the LORD that you shall proclaim as holy convocations; they are my appointed
> feasts. 3 "Six days shall work be done, but on the seventh day is a Sabbath of solemn rest, a holy
> convocation. You shall do no work. It is a Sabbath to the LORD in all your dwelling places. 4 "These
> are the appointed feasts of the LORD, the holy convocations, which you shall proclaim at the time
> appointed for them.

### Types & Prophecy

- **Types the New Testament names.** "Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed" (1 Corinthians
  5:7, ESV). "Christ has been raised from the dead, the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep"
  (1 Corinthians 15:20, ESV). The Holy Spirit came "when the day of Pentecost arrived" (Acts 2:1,
  ESV). Hebrews reads the Day of Atonement as Christ entering the holy places "by means of his own
  blood" (Hebrews 9:12, ESV).
- **Prophecy.** Zechariah says the nations "shall go up year after year to worship the King, the LORD
  of hosts, and to keep the Feast of Booths" (Zechariah 14:16, ESV). Tabernacles will be kept in the
  coming kingdom.

### Lessons about Jesus

- **He kept the feasts.** His parents went up "every year at the Feast of the Passover" (Luke 2:41,
  ESV), and John follows Him to Passover, Booths and Dedication (John 2:13; 7:10; 10:22).
- **He is their substance.** "These are a shadow of the things to come, but the substance belongs to
  Christ" (Colossians 2:17, ESV).
- **He keeps God's timetable.** Passover and Pentecost were fulfilled on their days: He died at Passover
  and the Holy Spirit came at Pentecost. The fall feasts wait for His return.

### Be Transformed

- **Think.** God keeps appointments. Passover and Pentecost were fulfilled on their days, so you can
  trust Him with the ones still to come.
- **Attitude.** Hold the feasts as a gift to learn from. "Let no one pass judgment on you ... with
  regard to a festival" (Colossians 2:16, ESV), and pass no judgment on a brother who keeps one in
  honour of the Lord (Romans 14:6).
- **Do.** Read Leviticus 23 through once, and beside each feast write what the New Testament says
  Jesus did with it.

## What an appointed time is

"Appointed feasts" translates <span dir="rtl" data-strongs="H4150">מוֹעֲדֵי</span>, the plural of
<span dir="rtl">מוֹעֵד</span> (*moʿed*, H4150), an appointed time, place or meeting. It first appears
on the fourth day of creation, when God made the lights "for signs and for seasons" (Genesis 1:14,
ESV); "seasons" is <span dir="rtl" data-strongs="H4150">מוֹעֲדִים</span>. The calendar was set in the sky before it was
given at Sinai.

The same word names the tabernacle. Leviticus opens with the LORD speaking "from the tent of meeting"
(Leviticus 1:1), <span dir="rtl">אֹהֶל מוֹעֵד</span> (*ʾohel moʿed*, H168). The tent was the appointed
place to meet God; the feasts were the appointed times. The ESV Study Bible puts it simply: on the
LORD's appointed feasts, His people are to meet with Him.

Each feast is also a "holy convocation", <span dir="rtl">מִקְרָא קֹדֶשׁ</span> (*miqraʾ qodesh*,
H4744), a summons to a holy gathering. All eleven uses of *miqraʾ* in Leviticus are in this chapter
(Leviticus 23:2-37).

**This shows that God sets the meeting.** Israel did not invent these days to approach God. He named
the time, called the assembly and promised to be there.

## The calendar

Leviticus 23 begins with the weekly Sabbath (Leviticus 23:3), the rest beneath every feast, and then
lists seven appointed times. The year began in spring: "This month shall be for you the beginning of
months" (Exodus 12:2, ESV).

| Feast | When | Leviticus 23 | What Israel remembered or offered |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Passover](passover.md) | 1st month, 14th day | 23:5 | The lamb whose blood spared Israel in Egypt |
| [Unleavened Bread](unleavened-bread.md) | 1st month, 15th-21st | 23:6-8 | The hurried exodus, bread without leaven |
| [Firstfruits](firstfruits.md) | "The day after the Sabbath" (which Sabbath is debated) | 23:9-14 | The first sheaf of the barley harvest |
| [Weeks (Pentecost)](weeks.md) | Fifty days later | 23:15-22 | The wheat harvest, two loaves |
| [Trumpets](trumpets.md) | 7th month, 1st day | 23:23-25 | A day of trumpet blasts and rest |
| [Day of Atonement](day-of-atonement.md) | 7th month, 10th day | 23:26-32 | Atonement for the people; "you shall afflict yourselves" |
| [Booths (Tabernacles)](tabernacles.md) | 7th month, 15th-21st, and an eighth day | 23:33-43 | Living in booths to remember the wilderness |

Three of them were pilgrim feasts: "Three times a year all your males shall appear before the LORD
your God ... at the Feast of Unleavened Bread, at the Feast of Weeks, and at the Feast of Booths"
(Deuteronomy 16:16, ESV). The feasts fall into two clusters: four in spring, around the barley and
wheat harvests, and three in the seventh month, at the end of the agricultural year. Between Weeks
and Trumpets lies the long summer harvest, and Leviticus places one law there, to leave the edges of
the field "for the poor and for the sojourner" (Leviticus 23:22, ESV).

The wheel below sets the seven in Israel's year, beginning with Nisan on the left. Each feast is a
moon, since every month began at the new moon: full for the four spring feasts, which the New
Testament ties to His first coming, half for the Day of Atonement, whose sacrifice is finished and whose national day is
ahead, and outlined for the two still waiting. The next two sections give the case for each.

## The spring feasts and the first coming

The New Testament ties each spring feast to Christ's first coming: three as fulfilment, and
Unleavened Bread as the life that follows from it.

- **Passover.** "Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed" (1 Corinthians 5:7, ESV). John notes
  that no bone of Jesus was broken, "that the Scripture might be fulfilled" (John 19:36, ESV), the
  rule for the Passover lamb (Exodus 12:46). Jesus died at Passover.
  [Passover: When I See the Blood](passover.md) studies the feast, and
  ["I Will Not Drink Again"](last-supper-four-cups.md) the meal He ate that night.
- **Unleavened Bread.** Paul goes straight on: "Let us therefore celebrate the festival, not with the
  old leaven ... but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth" (1 Corinthians 5:8, ESV). He
  applies the feast to the church's life: those whom the Lamb redeemed put away malice and evil.
  [Unleavened Bread: A New Lump](unleavened-bread.md) follows that application.
- **Firstfruits.** "Christ the firstfruits, then at his coming those who belong to Christ" (1
  Corinthians 15:23, ESV). The first sheaf of the harvest was a pledge of the rest; Christ's
  resurrection is the pledge of yours. [Firstfruits: The Sheaf Waved on the Third Day](firstfruits.md)
  works through the statute and its disputed date.
- **Weeks.** "When the day of Pentecost arrived" (Acts 2:1, ESV), the Holy Spirit came on the
  disciples and "there were added that day about three thousand souls" (Acts 2:41, ESV). The harvest feast became the first harvest of the church.
  [Weeks: Fifty Days to Pentecost](weeks.md) traces the count from the sheaf to the loaves.

The New Testament states each of these links, and it dates two of them. **God kept His calendar to
the day:** the Lamb died at Passover, and the Holy Spirit came at Weeks. Jesus rose "after the
Sabbath, toward the dawn of the first day of the week" (Matthew 28:1, ESV). That this was the morning
the first sheaf was waved is a reconstruction; it holds on the
[AD 33 dating](../last-things/chronology-anchors.md#settling-the-crucifixion-year), where both
readings of "the day after the Sabbath" (Leviticus 23:11) fall on that Sunday.

## The fall feasts and the second coming

The three fall feasts are different. The New Testament never says that Trumpets, Atonement or
Tabernacles has been fulfilled on its day. Their fulfilment is read from the prophets and from the
pattern, and this site reads it toward Christ's return and Israel's future, in line with its
[dispensational position](../about/statement-of-faith.md#end-times). The confidence varies, so it is
marked for each.

- **Trumpets: inferred.** No New Testament text names Yom Teruah. Linking it to "the last trumpet"
  of 1 Corinthians 15:52 or to Christ's return is typology from shared vocabulary.
  [Feast of Trumpets: Yom Teruah](trumpets.md) weighs that case.
- **Day of Atonement: the sacrifice fulfilled, the national day ahead.** Hebrews is explicit that the
  high priest went in "but once a year, and not without taking blood", and that Christ "entered once
  for all into the holy places ... by means of his own blood, thus securing an eternal redemption"
  (Hebrews 9:7, 12, ESV). The atoning sacrifice is finished. What remains is Israel's national
  repentance. Zechariah foresees that day: "when they look on me, on him whom they have pierced, they
  shall mourn for him", and "a fountain opened ... to cleanse them from sin" (Zechariah 12:10; 13:1,
  ESV). Paul expects it: "all Israel will be saved ... when I take away their sins" (Romans 11:26-27,
  ESV). Reading that day as the fulfilment of Yom Kippur is an inference from the shared themes of
  mourning and cleansing; the events themselves are prophesied.
  [The Day of Atonement: Once a Year, Once for All](day-of-atonement.md) sets out both halves.
- **Tabernacles: prophesied.** Zechariah 14:16 says the nations will keep the Feast of Booths in the
  kingdom. John says the Word "became flesh and dwelt among us" (John 1:14, ESV), using
  σκηνόω (*skēnoō*, G4637), to pitch a tent. At Booths Jesus stood up and cried, "If anyone thirsts,
  let him come to me and drink" (John 7:37, ESV). The last word belongs to Revelation: "the dwelling
  place of God is with man. He will dwell with them" (Revelation 21:3, ESV). "Dwelling place" is
  σκηνή (*skēnē*, G4633), a tent, and "He will dwell" is *skēnoō* again, the verb of John 1:14.
  [Tabernacles: God Dwelling with His People](tabernacles.md) follows the feast from the wilderness
  to the kingdom.

Some teachers read the summer gap between Weeks and Trumpets as the church age, the long harvest
between Pentecost and the trumpet. It fits the dispensational shape of the calendar, and Scripture
never says it. Hold it as an illustration.

The same teachers often fill the days inside the seventh month. The nine days between Trumpets and
Atonement (Leviticus 23:24, 27), which later Jewish liturgy calls the Days of Awe, become the
tribulation, and the week of a Jewish wedding (Genesis 29:27; Judges 14:12) becomes seven years in
heaven for the church, ending in "the marriage supper of the Lamb" (Revelation 19:9, ESV). The order
these readings give is the order Scripture gives. The tribulation is Daniel's seventieth "week"
(Daniel 9:27, ESV), seven years. Revelation announces that "the marriage of the Lamb has come, and
his Bride has made herself ready" (Revelation 19:7, ESV) before heaven opens on the rider called
Faithful and True (Revelation 19:11). That order comes from Daniel and Revelation, and the calendar
does not carry it: nine days do not measure seven years on any reckoning, Leviticus gives the days
between the feasts no content, and no text sets the marriage supper at a feast. **What the seventh
month does show is the shape God has promised:** a summons, then Israel's cleansing, then God
dwelling with His people in the kingdom.

## Shadow and substance

Exodus calls Passover "a statute forever" (Exodus 12:14, ESV), and some teach that Christians must
keep the feasts. Paul answers for the church: "let no one pass judgment on you ... with regard to a
festival or a new moon or a Sabbath. These are a shadow of the things to come, but the substance
belongs to Christ" (Colossians 2:16-17, ESV). "Shadow" is σκιά (*skia*, G4639); "substance" is
σῶμα (*sōma*, G4983), the body that casts it. Hebrews says the same of the whole law: "a shadow of
the good things to come instead of the true form of these realities" (Hebrews 10:1, ESV).

Two things follow. A Christian who keeps a feast "in honor of the Lord" is free to (Romans 14:6), and
so is one who does not (Romans 14:5). And keeping days as a way to stand right with God is a return
to slavery: "You observe days and months and seasons and years! I am afraid I may have labored over
you in vain" (Galatians 4:10-11, ESV). The feasts were given to Israel, and Zechariah shows them kept
again in the kingdom; the church learns from them and keeps none as law. See
[Hebrew Roots](../israel-and-church/hebrew-roots.md) for the wider question.

**So you may read Leviticus 23 as a portrait of your Saviour, drawn centuries before He came, and
wait for the appointments He has not yet kept.**

## Discussion questions

1. **The language.** The word for the feasts, *moʿed*, also names the "tent of meeting" (Leviticus
   1:1). What does it add to think of a feast as an appointment with God?
2. **The text in its context.** Leviticus 23 lists the weekly Sabbath before any feast (Leviticus
   23:3). Why put it first?
3. **Christ.** The New Testament ties Passover and Pentecost to the days on which Jesus died and sent
   the Holy Spirit. What does that precision tell you about the fall feasts?
4. **The hard part.** Exodus calls Passover "a statute forever" (Exodus 12:14), and Paul calls the
   feasts "a shadow" (Colossians 2:17). How do the two sit together?
5. **Israel and the church.** Zechariah 14:16 has the nations keeping Booths in the kingdom. What
   does that say about God's purposes for Israel?
6. **Be transformed.** Which of the seven feasts do you know least, and what would you most like to
   learn about Jesus from it?

# Tabernacles: God Dwelling with His People

![A booth of branches under a star on an outlined moon: Tabernacles, awaiting His return](../assets/img/feasts/tabernacles.svg){ align=right width="96" }
At the end of the harvest Israel moved out of its houses for a week. "You shall dwell in booths for
seven days ... that your generations may know that I made the people of Israel dwell in booths when
I brought them out of the land of Egypt: I am the LORD your God" (Leviticus 23:42-43, ESV). It was
the most joyful week of the year, and it is the one feast Zechariah sees the nations keeping in the
age to come.

**In one sentence:** The Feast of Booths gathered Israel after the harvest to live in shelters and
rejoice, remembering that God sheltered them in the wilderness; Jesus, the Word who "dwelt among us",
stood up at this feast and offered the living water of the Holy Spirit; and Zechariah sees every
nation keeping it when the LORD is king over all the earth, so you may rejoice in His presence now and
wait for the day God dwells with His people for ever.

> ✝️ Leviticus 23:39-43 (ESV)
>
> 39 "On the fifteenth day of the seventh month, when you have gathered in the produce of the land,
> you shall celebrate the feast of the LORD seven days. On the first day shall be a solemn rest, and
> on the eighth day shall be a solemn rest. 40 And you shall take on the first day the fruit of
> splendid trees, branches of palm trees and boughs of leafy trees and willows of the brook, and you
> shall rejoice before the LORD your God seven days. 41 You shall celebrate it as a feast to the LORD
> for seven days in the year. It is a statute forever throughout your generations; you shall
> celebrate it in the seventh month. 42 You shall dwell in booths for seven days. All native
> Israelites shall dwell in booths, 43 that your generations may know that I made the people of
> Israel dwell in booths when I brought them out of the land of Egypt: I am the LORD your God."

## Key Takeaways

### Types & Prophecy

- **Prophecy.** "Everyone who survives of all the nations that have come against Jerusalem shall go up
  year after year to worship the King, the LORD of hosts, and to keep the Feast of Booths" (Zechariah
  14:16, ESV).
- **Fulfilment, by shared vocabulary.** John writes that the Word "became flesh and dwelt among us"
  (John 1:14, ESV), with σκηνόω (*skēnoō*, G4637), "to pitch a tent", the verb behind the feast's
  Greek name; he does not name the feast there. Revelation uses the same verb: "He will dwell
  with them" (Revelation 21:3, ESV).

### Lessons about Jesus

- **He offered what the feast pictured.** "On the last day of the feast, the great day, Jesus stood
  up and cried out, 'If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink'" (John 7:37, ESV).
- **He is the light of the world.** "Whoever follows me will not walk in darkness, but will have the
  light of life" (John 8:12, ESV).
- **He came to live among us.** The eternal Word pitched His tent with us, "and we have seen his
  glory" (John 1:14, ESV).

### Be Transformed

- **Think.** This life is a booth, and God shelters you in it as He sheltered Israel in the
  wilderness. Your home is where He dwells.
- **Attitude.** Booths was commanded joy: "you shall rejoice before the LORD your God seven days"
  (Leviticus 23:40, ESV). Make room for gladness in what God has given.
- **Do.** If you are thirsty for God this week, take Jesus at His word: come to Him in prayer and
  drink, and read John 7:37-39 aloud as you do.

## The feast

### Names and words

The feast has two names. It is "the Feast of Booths" (Leviticus 23:34, ESV), from
<span dir="rtl">סֻכּוֹת</span> (*sukkot*, H5521), shelters of branches, a word used thirty-one times
in the Hebrew Bible. It is also "the Feast of Ingathering at the end of the year" (Exodus 23:16, ESV),
because it closed the harvest. John calls it ἡ σκηνοπηγία (*skēnopēgia*, G4634), "tent-pitching", a
word used only at John 7:2 in the New Testament.

### What it commanded

Booths began on the fifteenth of the seventh month, five days after the Day of Atonement, and ran for
seven days with an eighth day of "solemn assembly" (Leviticus 23:34-36, ESV). Israel took branches
of palm and leafy trees and willows (Leviticus 23:40), built booths and lived in them.

The offerings were the largest of the year. Numbers sets thirteen bulls on the first day, then one
fewer each day down to seven on the seventh (Numbers 29:13, 32): seventy bulls in all, and one on
the eighth day (Numbers 29:36). The Talmud records a later reading of the number: Rabbi Elazar taught
that the seventy bulls correspond to "the seventy nations of the world", and the single bull of the
eighth day to Israel (Sukkah 55b, William Davidson translation, via Sefaria). That is rabbinic
tradition. Zechariah 14 has the nations keeping this feast.

Deuteronomy gives the feast its mood:

> ✝️ Deuteronomy 16:14-15 (ESV)
>
> 14 You shall rejoice in your feast, you and your son and your daughter, your male servant and your
> female servant, the Levite, the sojourner, the fatherless, and the widow who are within your towns.
> 15 For seven days you shall keep the feast to the LORD your God at the place that the LORD will
> choose, because the LORD your God will bless you in all your produce and in all the work of your
> hands, so that you will be altogether joyful.

Every seventh year the law was read aloud at this feast to "men, women, and little ones, and the
sojourner" (Deuteronomy 31:10-12, ESV).

**This shows that God wanted His people's year to end in joy shared with everyone, and in hearing
His word.**

## Remembered and restored

Booths remembered the wilderness: God "made the people of Israel dwell in booths when I brought them
out of the land of Egypt" (Leviticus 23:43, ESV). For forty years they had no houses and no harvest,
and God sheltered and fed them. Hosea turned the memory into a promise and a warning: "I will again
make you dwell in tents, as in the days of the appointed feast" (Hosea 12:9, ESV).

The feast marks Israel's new beginnings:

- **The temple.** Solomon dedicated the temple when "all the men of Israel assembled ... at the feast
  in the month Ethanim, which is the seventh month" (1 Kings 8:2, ESV).
- **The return.** The returned exiles "kept the Feast of Booths, as it is written" (Ezra 3:4, ESV).
- **The law.** Under Nehemiah the people found the command in the law, built booths on their roofs
  and in the squares, and "from the days of Jeshua the son of Nun to that day the people of Israel had
  not done so. And there was very great rejoicing" (Nehemiah 8:17, ESV). Ezra read from the law every
  day of the feast (Nehemiah 8:18).

## Jesus at the feast

### Living water

John 7 is set at Booths: "Now the Jews' Feast of Booths was at hand" (John 7:2, ESV). Jesus went up
privately and taught in the temple in the middle of the feast (John 7:10, 14). Then:

> ✝️ John 7:37-39 (ESV)
>
> 37 On the last day of the feast, the great day, Jesus stood up and cried out, "If anyone thirsts,
> let him come to me and drink. 38 Whoever believes in me, as the Scripture has said, 'Out of his
> heart will flow rivers of living water.'" 39 Now this he said about the Spirit, whom those who
> believed in him were to receive, for as yet the Spirit had not been given, because Jesus was not yet
> glorified.

The feast had a water ceremony. The Mishnah describes a golden flask filled at the Pool of Shiloah,
carried to the altar with trumpet blasts and poured out there (Mishnah Sukkah 4:9), and says of the
celebration: "he who has not seen the Simchat Bet Hashoevah [the rejoicing at the place of the
water-drawing] has never seen rejoicing in his life" (Mishnah Sukkah 5:1, Mishnah Yomit translation
by Joshua Kulp, CC-BY). The NIV Biblical Theology Study Bible reads Jesus' words against that rite.
The Mishnah was written down around AD 200, more than a century after the temple fell, so it records
the practice as the rabbis remembered it, and John does not mention the ceremony. John does say what
the living water means: the Holy Spirit, given once Jesus was glorified (John 7:39). He does
not say which Scripture Jesus meant. Candidates include "living waters shall flow out from Jerusalem"
(Zechariah 14:8, ESV) and "With joy you will draw water from the wells of salvation" (Isaiah 12:3,
ESV).

### The light of the world

"Again Jesus spoke to them, saying, 'I am the light of the world'" (John 8:12, ESV), and John places
these words "in the treasury, as he taught in the temple" (John 8:20, ESV). The Mishnah describes
great golden lamps lit in the Women's Court during the feast, so that "there was not a courtyard in
Jerusalem that was not illuminated" (Mishnah Sukkah 5:2-3, Kulp). The NIV Cultural Backgrounds
Study Bible reads Jesus' words against that light. John does not say whether the lamps were burning. The claim itself needs no
backdrop: Jesus is the light God promised (Isaiah 9:2), as Matthew says (Matthew 4:14-16).

**This shows that God sent His Son to be what the feast celebrated:** the water that satisfies, the
light that guides, and the presence that shelters.

## The feast still to come

### Zechariah 14

Zechariah's last chapter is set on the day the LORD returns to Jerusalem: "the LORD will be king over
all the earth" (Zechariah 14:9, ESV), and "living waters shall flow out from Jerusalem" (Zechariah
14:8, ESV). Then the nations come:

> ✝️ Zechariah 14:16-17 (ESV)
>
> 16 Then everyone who survives of all the nations that have come against Jerusalem shall go up year
> after year to worship the King, the LORD of hosts, and to keep the Feast of Booths. 17 And if any of
> the families of the earth do not go up to Jerusalem to worship the King, the LORD of hosts, there
> will be no rain on them.

The ESV Study Bible notes that Zechariah broadens Israel's festival worship to those beyond Israel's
borders. How to read the chapter is contested. Many readers take its feast as a picture: the NIV
Biblical Theology Study Bible finds the reality the feast points to "in Christ" and cites Paul, who
calls the feasts "a shadow of the things to come, but the substance belongs to Christ" (Colossians
2:17, ESV). This site reads the passage of the millennial kingdom, Christ's thousand-year reign on
earth (Revelation 20:1-6; see the [statement of faith](../about/statement-of-faith.md#end-times)),
because Zechariah gives the feast a place, Jerusalem, a calendar, "year after year", and a penalty,
no rain, that a picture does not need, and he sets it on the day "the LORD will be king over all the
earth" (Zechariah 14:9, ESV).
Tabernacles is the one feast Scripture names the nations keeping in that age. Ezekiel's temple
vision, which this site also reads of the kingdom, keeps Passover and the seventh-month feast there
too (Ezekiel 45:21-25). What their offerings will mean then is a question this study leaves open.

### Revelation

Revelation's pictures of the end carry the feast's colours:

| In the feast | In Revelation | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Palm branches (Leviticus 23:40) | A multitude "with palm branches in their hands" (Revelation 7:9) | Echo |
| Booths, God's shelter | "He who sits on the throne will shelter them with his presence" (Revelation 7:15), σκηνόω | Echo, in the same verb family |
| Water poured out | "He will guide them to springs of living water" (Revelation 7:17) | Echo |
| God dwelling with Israel | "The dwelling place of God is with man. He will dwell with them" (Revelation 21:3) | The hope stated, in the same verb |

Revelation never names the feast, so all four are echoes. The last states outright what the feast
pictured: God means to live with His people. **So you may live now as one sheltered by God,
and wait for the day when His dwelling is with man, and "God himself will be with them as their God"
(Revelation 21:3, ESV).**

## Discussion questions

1. **The language.** John calls the feast *skēnopēgia*, "tent-pitching" (John 7:2), and says the Word
   "dwelt" among us with the verb for pitching a tent (John 1:14). What does that link say about why Jesus came?
2. **The text in its context.** Booths remembered the wilderness (Leviticus 23:43) and celebrated the
   harvest (Deuteronomy 16:13-15). Why join hardship remembered with plenty enjoyed?
3. **Christ.** Jesus cried out on "the great day" of the feast (John 7:37). What did He claim to
   give, and to whom?
4. **The hard part.** The Mishnah's water and light ceremonies are recorded later than John, and John
   does not mention them. How much should they shape your reading of John 7-8?
5. **The kingdom.** Zechariah 14:16 has the nations keeping Booths. What does that say about God's
   purposes for Israel and for the nations?
6. **Be transformed.** Booths was commanded joy. What would it look like for you to rejoice before
   the LORD this week, and with whom?

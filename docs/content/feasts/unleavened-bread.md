# Unleavened Bread: A New Lump

![A pierced flat loaf on a full gold moon: Unleavened Bread, fulfilled at His first coming](../assets/img/feasts/unleavened-bread.svg){ align=right width="96" }
Israel left Egypt with dough that had no time to rise. "They baked unleavened cakes of the dough that
they had brought out of Egypt, for it was not leavened, because they were thrust out of Egypt and
could not wait" (Exodus 12:39, ESV). God made that night's bread a feast, and kept it for seven days
every year.

**In one sentence:** Unleavened Bread followed Passover for seven days, remembering that God brought
Israel out of Egypt in haste and made them clear the old leaven from their houses; Paul applies it to
the church, which Christ's sacrifice as our Passover has already made unleavened, so you keep the feast
by clearing out the old leaven of malice and evil and living in sincerity and truth.

> ✝️ Leviticus 23:6-8 (ESV)
>
> 6 And on the fifteenth day of the same month is the Feast of Unleavened Bread to the LORD; for
> seven days you shall eat unleavened bread. 7 On the first day you shall have a holy convocation;
> you shall not do any ordinary work. 8 But you shall present a food offering to the LORD for seven
> days. On the seventh day is a holy convocation; you shall not do any ordinary work."

## Key Takeaways

### Types & Prophecy

- **Applied by the New Testament.** Paul reads the feast as the life of the redeemed: "Cleanse out the
  old leaven that you may be a new lump, as you really are unleavened. For Christ, our Passover lamb,
  has been sacrificed" (1 Corinthians 5:7, ESV).
- **Christ the sinless one, inferred.** Many read the unleavened bread as a picture of Christ, who
  "committed no sin" (1 Peter 2:22, ESV). The New Testament never calls Him unleavened bread, so hold
  it as an illustration.

### Lessons about Jesus

- **He warned His disciples about leaven.** "Beware of the leaven of the Pharisees, which is
  hypocrisy" (Luke 12:1, ESV).
- **He is without sin.** He was "tempted as we are, yet without sin" (Hebrews 4:15, ESV), and God
  "made him to be sin who knew no sin, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God" (2
  Corinthians 5:21, ESV).
- **He made His people unleavened.** "You really are unleavened. For Christ, our Passover lamb, has
  been sacrificed" (1 Corinthians 5:7, ESV).

### Memory verses

> ✝️ 1 Corinthians 5:7-8 (ESV)
>
> 7 Cleanse out the old leaven that you may be a new lump, as you really are unleavened. For Christ,
> our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed. 8 Let us therefore celebrate the festival, not with the old
> leaven, the leaven of malice and evil, but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth.

### Be Transformed

- **Think.** In Christ you are already "a new lump" (1 Corinthians 5:7, ESV). Holiness is becoming
  what His sacrifice has made you.
- **Attitude.** Take small sin seriously. "A little leaven leavens the whole lump" (Galatians 5:9,
  ESV).
- **Do.** Israel searched the house and put the leaven out. This week, name one old habit of malice,
  resentment or deceit, confess it to God, and remove what feeds it.

### Prayer

Father, you brought your people out of Egypt in haste and fed them bread without leaven. Thank you
that your Son Jesus, our Passover lamb, was sacrificed for us, and that in Him you have made us a new
lump. Search our hearts as Israel searched their houses. Clear out the old leaven of malice and evil,
and keep us in sincerity and truth, so that the whole lump is yours.

In Jesus' name. Amen.

## The feast

Unleavened Bread began the day after Passover, "on the fifteenth day of the same month", and ran for
seven days (Leviticus 23:6, ESV). The first and seventh days were holy convocations with no ordinary
work, and offerings were made every day (Leviticus 23:7-8). Numbers adds a daily sin offering, "one
male goat for a sin offering, to make atonement for you" (Numbers 28:22, ESV).

The two feasts ran together so closely that the New Testament can use either name for both: "Now the
Feast of Unleavened Bread drew near, which is called the Passover" (Luke 22:1, ESV). Luke dates
Peter's arrest and Paul's sailing by them (Acts 12:3; 20:6).

Three commands shaped the week:

- **Eat unleavened bread.** It was "the bread of affliction—for you came out of the land of Egypt in
  haste" (Deuteronomy 16:3, ESV).
- **Remove the leaven.** "On the first day you shall remove leaven out of your houses ... For seven
  days no leaven is to be found in your houses" (Exodus 12:15, 19, ESV). The penalty for eating it
  was severe: "that person will be cut off from the congregation of Israel, whether he is a sojourner
  or a native of the land" (Exodus 12:19, ESV).
- **Tell your son.** "You shall tell your son on that day, 'It is because of what the LORD did for me
  when I came out of Egypt'" (Exodus 13:8, ESV).

The feast remembered a rescue: "for on this very day I brought your hosts out of the land of Egypt"
(Exodus 12:17, ESV). **This shows that God wanted His redeemed people to remember, every year, that
He had taken them out of slavery, and to live as a people set apart.**

## The words

- <span dir="rtl">מַצָּה</span> (*matstsah*, H4682) is unleavened bread, flat bread baked without
  rising. It occurs fifty-three times.
- <span dir="rtl">שְׂאֹר</span> (*seʾor*, H7603) is leaven itself: a lump of fermented dough kept back
  to raise the next batch. It occurs five times, all in laws (Exodus 12:15, 19; 13:7; Leviticus 2:11;
  Deuteronomy 16:4). The ESV Study Bible notes on 1 Corinthians 5:6 that ancient leaven was fermented
  dough of this kind, carried from batch to batch.
- <span dir="rtl">חָמֵץ</span> (*chamets*, H2557) is anything leavened.
- "Remove" in Exodus 12:15 is <span dir="rtl">תַּשְׁבִּיתוּ</span>, from
  <span dir="rtl">שָׁבַת</span> (*shavat*, H7673), the verb behind "Sabbath": make the leaven cease.
- In Greek, ζύμη (*zymē*, G2219) is leaven, thirteen times in the New Testament, and ἄζυμος (*azymos*,
  G106) is unleavened, nine times.

## What leaven pictures

### Not sin in itself

Leaven is not evil in Scripture by nature. The law banned it from the altar: "No grain offering that
you bring to the LORD shall be made with leaven" (Leviticus 2:11, ESV). Yet the law also required it.
The thanksgiving offering came "with loaves of leavened bread" (Leviticus 7:13, ESV), and at the Feast
of Weeks Israel waved two loaves "baked with leaven, as firstfruits to the LORD" (Leviticus 23:17,
ESV). Jesus Himself said, "The kingdom of heaven is like leaven that a woman took and hid in three
measures of flour, till it was all leavened" (Matthew 13:33, ESV). The ESV Study Bible observes that
leaven usually carries a negative sense in Scripture, and that here Jesus uses it for the kingdom's
hidden, spreading growth.

### What spreads

What leaven does is spread through the whole. That is why the New Testament uses it as a warning:

- **The Pharisees' and Sadducees' teaching.** "He did not tell them to beware of the leaven of bread,
  but of the teaching of the Pharisees and Sadducees" (Matthew 16:12, ESV).
- **Hypocrisy.** "Beware of the leaven of the Pharisees, which is hypocrisy" (Luke 12:1, ESV).
- **Herod.** "Beware of the leaven of the Pharisees and the leaven of Herod" (Mark 8:15, ESV).
- **False teaching in the church.** To the Galatians, pressed to add circumcision to the gospel
  (Galatians 5:2-3):
  "A little leaven leavens the whole lump" (Galatians 5:9, ESV).
- **Tolerated sin in the church.** The same proverb in 1 Corinthians 5:6.

So the picture is a small thing that changes everything it touches. In the feast Israel put it out of
the house entirely for seven days, starting fresh with bread that had none of the old batch in it.

## Paul's festival: 1 Corinthians 5

The Corinthian church was tolerating a man living with his father's wife (1 Corinthians 5:1). "And
you are arrogant!" Paul wrote (1 Corinthians 5:2, ESV). He called for discipline, "Purge the evil
person from among you" (1 Corinthians 5:13, ESV), so that the man's "spirit may be saved in the day
of the Lord" (1 Corinthians 5:5, ESV). To make the case he reached for the feast:

> ✝️ 1 Corinthians 5:6-8 (ESV)
>
> 6 Your boasting is not good. Do you not know that a little leaven leavens the whole lump? 7 Cleanse
> out the old leaven that you may be a new lump, as you really are unleavened. For Christ, our
> Passover lamb, has been sacrificed. 8 Let us therefore celebrate the festival, not with the old
> leaven, the leaven of malice and evil, but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth.

Paul says what they are before he says what to do: "as you really are unleavened"
uses ἐστε, a present indicative, "you are", and the command "cleanse out" rests on it. The reason is the sacrifice:
"For Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed." The same order runs through the calendar.
Passover came first, on the fourteenth, and Unleavened Bread followed on the fifteenth (Leviticus
23:5-6). Israel was redeemed by the lamb's blood and then lived as a people without leaven.

The doctrine is sanctification. Christians are already sanctified in Christ ("you were washed, you were sanctified,
you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ", 1 Corinthians 6:11, ESV), and they are
called to grow into it. **This shows that God does the redeeming, and then calls the redeemed to live
as what He has made them.** Paul describes the whole Christian life as a feast: "Let us therefore
celebrate the festival." Its bread is "sincerity and truth", εἰλικρίνεια (*eilikrineia*, G1505), which
occurs three times in the New Testament, all in Paul's letters to Corinth (1 Corinthians 5:8; 2
Corinthians 1:12; 2:17).

## Christ and the unleavened bread

Many Christian teachers read the unleavened bread as a picture of Christ Himself: sinless, and
buried during the feast without His body seeing corruption (Acts 2:27, quoting Psalm 16:10). The parts of that picture are
true. He "committed no sin, neither was deceit found in his mouth" (1 Peter 2:22, ESV), and He died
at Passover and lay in the tomb during Unleavened Bread. But no New Testament writer calls Jesus the
unleavened bread or links His burial to the feast. Paul's one use of the feast applies it to the
church. Hold the Christ-picture as an illustration, and build on what Paul says.

For where this feast sits among the others, see [The Appointed Times](feasts.md). The feast that
comes before it is [Passover](passover.md), and the sheaf waved "on the day after the Sabbath"
(Leviticus 23:11, ESV) is [Firstfruits](firstfruits.md).

## Discussion questions

1. **The language.** "Remove leaven" in Exodus 12:15 uses the verb behind "Sabbath", to make
   something cease. What does it add to think of the week as a Sabbath for leaven?
2. **The text in its context.** Leaven was banned from the altar (Leviticus 2:11) yet required in the
   loaves of Weeks (Leviticus 23:17). What does that tell you about how to read leaven elsewhere?
3. **Christ.** Paul grounds "cleanse out the old leaven" in "Christ, our Passover lamb, has been
   sacrificed" (1 Corinthians 5:7). Why does the sacrifice come first?
4. **The hard part.** Jesus compared the kingdom to leaven (Matthew 13:33) and warned against the
   leaven of the Pharisees (Matthew 16:6). How do you hold the two together?
5. **The church.** Paul applied the feast to a church tolerating sin (1 Corinthians 5:1-8). What would
   it look like for a church to keep this feast today?
6. **Be transformed.** What is one "little leaven" in your life that you have treated as too small to
   matter (Galatians 5:9)?

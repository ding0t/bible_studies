# Passover: When I See the Blood

![A blood-marked doorway on a full gold moon: Passover, fulfilled at His first coming](../assets/img/feasts/passover.svg){ align=right width="96" }
On the night God judged Egypt, the difference between a house of mourning and a house of safety was
blood on a doorframe. "The blood shall be a sign for you, on the houses where you are. And when I see
the blood, I will pass over you" (Exodus 12:13, ESV).

**In one sentence:** At the first Passover God judged Egypt and spared every household that sheltered
under a lamb's blood; Israel kept that night every year as a memorial, and the New Testament names
Jesus as the Passover lamb, chosen, slain at the feast and without a broken bone; so His blood is the
sign over you, and the judgment you deserve passes over you because it fell on Him.

> ✝️ Exodus 12:12-13 (ESV)
>
> 12 For I will pass through the land of Egypt that night, and I will strike all the firstborn in the
> land of Egypt, both man and beast; and on all the gods of Egypt I will execute judgments: I am the
> LORD. 13 The blood shall be a sign for you, on the houses where you are. And when I see the blood,
> I will pass over you, and no plague will befall you to destroy you, when I strike the land of
> Egypt.

### Types & Prophecy

- **Type, named by the New Testament.** "Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed" (1
  Corinthians 5:7, ESV). John applies the lamb's law, "you shall not break any of its bones" (Exodus
  12:46, ESV), to the cross: "Not one of his bones will be broken" (John 19:36, ESV).
- **Type, by resemblance.** The lamb was "without blemish" (Exodus 12:5, ESV); Peter says you were
  ransomed "with the precious blood of Christ, like that of a lamb without blemish or spot" (1 Peter
  1:19, ESV).

### Lessons about Jesus

- **He is the Lamb God provided.** John the Baptist said it twice: "Behold, the Lamb of God, who
  takes away the sin of the world!" (John 1:29, ESV; John 1:36).
- **He died at the Passover.** John marks the day: "Now it was the day of Preparation of the
  Passover" (John 19:14, ESV).
- **He longed to keep it with His disciples.** "I have earnestly desired to eat this Passover with
  you before I suffer" (Luke 22:15, ESV).

### Be Transformed

- **Think.** You are safe from God's judgment for one reason: the blood of His Son Jesus. "Much more
  shall we be saved by him from the wrath of God" (Romans 5:9, ESV).
- **Attitude.** Live as Israel ate that night, "with your belt fastened, your sandals on your feet"
  (Exodus 12:11, ESV): rescued, and ready to leave the old life.
- **Do.** Answer the question Israel's children asked, "What do you mean by this service?" (Exodus
  12:26, ESV). Tell someone this week, in your own words, what the blood of Jesus has done for you.

## The night in Egypt

Passover came as the tenth plague. Nine times God had struck Egypt and Pharaoh had refused to let
Israel go. The tenth would fall on "all the firstborn in the land of Egypt, both man and beast", and
God named its real target: "on all the gods of Egypt I will execute judgments: I am the LORD" (Exodus
12:12, ESV).

God gave Israel a way through it:

- **A lamb for each house.** On the tenth day of the month every household took "a lamb for a
  household", and a small household shared with its neighbour (Exodus 12:3-4, ESV). The ESV Study
  Bible notes the symmetry: the plague would bring a death into every Egyptian house, and Israel was
  told to bring a lamb's death into every one of theirs.
- **Without blemish.** "Your lamb shall be without blemish, a male a year old" (Exodus 12:5, ESV),
  kept until the fourteenth day and killed "at twilight" (Exodus 12:6, ESV).
- **Blood on the door.** "Take a bunch of hyssop and dip it in the blood that is in the basin, and
  touch the lintel and the two doorposts ..." (Exodus 12:22, ESV). No one went out of the house until
  morning.
- **A meal eaten ready to leave.** The lamb was roasted whole and eaten "with unleavened bread and
  bitter herbs", "with your belt fastened, your sandals on your feet, and your staff in your hand. And
  you shall eat it in haste. It is the LORD's Passover" (Exodus 12:8, 11, ESV).

Every house in the land faced the same judgment, Israel's included. The difference was the blood. **This shows that God's judgment on sin is real, and
that He Himself provides the way to be sheltered from it.**

## What "pass over" means

The feast's name is <span dir="rtl">פֶּסַח</span> (*pesach*, H6453), used forty-nine times in the
Hebrew Bible. The verb behind it, <span dir="rtl">פָּסַח</span> (*pasach*, H6452), "to pass over",
occurs four times in this sense: three times in Exodus 12 (Exodus 12:13, 23, 27) and once in Isaiah.

Isaiah's use shows what the word carries. "Like birds hovering, so the LORD of hosts will protect
Jerusalem; he will protect and deliver it; he will spare and rescue it" (Isaiah 31:5, ESV). "Spare"
is *pasach*, set beside "protect", "deliver" and "rescue". Exodus 12:23 has the same picture: "the
LORD will pass over the door and will not allow the destroyer to enter your houses to strike you"
(ESV). Two readings are held, and the Septuagint, the Greek translation made before Christ, already
carries both: at Exodus 12:23 it has παρελεύσεται (*pareleusetai*, G3928, "he will pass by"), and
at Exodus 12:13 and 27 σκεπάσω (*skepasō*, "I will shelter"). One reading has God passing by the
marked house. The other, from Isaiah 31:5 and Exodus 12:23, has Him standing over the door to guard
it. Both are defensible, and both say the same thing about the blood: it is why God's judgment did not enter.
**God keeps the house that is under the blood.**

## A memorial for every generation

### The children's question

"This day shall be for you a memorial day ... throughout your generations, as a statute forever"
(Exodus 12:14, ESV). God wrote the teaching into the meal:

> ✝️ Exodus 12:26-27 (ESV)
>
> 26 And when your children say to you, 'What do you mean by this service?' 27 you shall say, 'It is
> the sacrifice of the LORD's Passover, for he passed over the houses of the people of Israel in
> Egypt, when he struck the Egyptians but spared our houses.' And the people bowed their heads and
> worshiped.

The firstborn were remembered too. Israel's firstborn had been spared that night, and from then on
each one belonged to the LORD and was redeemed: "the LORD killed all the firstborn in the land of
Egypt ... but all the firstborn of my sons I redeem" (Exodus 13:15, ESV).

### In the land

Once Israel entered the land, the Passover was to be sacrificed only "at the place that the LORD
your God will choose, to make his name dwell in it" (Deuteronomy 16:6, ESV). Israel kept it at Gilgal
on entering Canaan (Joshua 5:10). Hezekiah called all Israel to Jerusalem to keep it (2 Chronicles
30:1), and under Josiah "no such Passover had been kept since the days of the judges" (2 Kings
23:22, ESV).

### Grace written into the law

Two provisions show God's heart in the statute:

- **A second Passover.** Anyone "unclean through touching a dead body, or ... on a long journey" was
  to keep it a month later (Numbers 9:10-11, ESV). God made room for those who could not come on the
  day.
- **The stranger.** No foreigner could eat it, but a stranger who wished to keep it could be
  circumcised and "be as a native of the land. ... There shall be one law for the native and for the
  stranger who sojourns among you" (Exodus 12:48-49, ESV). The door was open to anyone who would come
  under the covenant.

## The Lamb of God

### Named by the New Testament

The New Testament makes the connection itself, six times:

- **John the Baptist:** "Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world!" (John 1:29,
  ESV).
- **Philip:** the Ethiopian official was reading Isaiah 53:7, "Like a sheep he was led to the
  slaughter and like a lamb before its shearer is silent", and "beginning with this Scripture he told
  him the good news about Jesus" (Acts 8:32, 35, ESV).
- **Paul:** "Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed" (1 Corinthians 5:7, ESV).
- **Peter:** "you were ransomed ... with the precious blood of Christ, like that of a lamb without
  blemish or spot" (1 Peter 1:18-19, ESV).
- **John at the cross:** the soldiers broke the legs of the men crucified with Jesus but not His (John 19:32-33),
  "For these things took place that the Scripture might be fulfilled: 'Not one of his bones will be
  broken'" (John 19:36, ESV). The ESV Study Bible traces it to the Passover law (Exodus 12:46;
  Numbers 9:12) and to Psalm 34:20.
- **Revelation:** John sees "a Lamb standing, as though it had been slain", between the throne
  and the four living creatures (Revelation 5:6, ESV).

Hebrews adds that Moses "kept the Passover and sprinkled the blood" by faith (Hebrews 11:28, ESV).
The blood saved because Israel trusted God's word about it.

### His last week

This site's chronology places the crucifixion on Friday 3 April AD 33, Nisan 14; [Chronology
Anchors](../last-things/chronology-anchors.md#verdict-friday-3-april-ad-33) sets out how the year is
settled. On that reckoning the days of Jesus' last
week follow the days of the Passover lamb:

- **The lamb was chosen on the tenth day** (Exodus 12:3). John dates Jesus' arrival at Bethany "six
  days before the Passover" (John 12:1, ESV), and the next day the crowds met Him with palm branches
  (John 12:12-13). On the site's count that entry falls on 10 Nisan. John does not draw the link,
  and it depends on the chronology, so hold it as a correspondence the dates suggest.
- **The lamb was killed on the fourteenth.** John says Jesus stood before Pilate on "the day of
  Preparation of the Passover" (John 19:14, ESV), and that the priests had not yet eaten the
  Passover that morning (John 18:28). On this reckoning He died in the afternoon of Nisan 14, when
  the lambs were being slain.

**What is contested.** Matthew, Mark and Luke call the Last Supper the Passover meal (Luke 22:15),
while John has the priests still to eat theirs the next day. Scholars reconcile the two in several
ways. The ESV Study Bible takes John's "day of Preparation" as the Friday of Passover week and has
all four Gospels agree on a Passover meal on Thursday evening. This site follows the reading in
[Three Days and Three Nights](../jesus/three-days-and-three-nights.md): the meal as Nisan 14 began
on Thursday evening, and the cross on its afternoon. Either way, Jesus died at the Passover, and
the New Testament calls Him its lamb.

One small echo: Israel marked its doors with "a bunch of hyssop" (Exodus 12:22, ESV), and on the
cross the soldiers raised sour wine to Jesus "on a hyssop branch" (John 19:29, ESV). The Greek
Old Testament uses the same word at Exodus 12:22 as John does, ὕσσωπος (*hyssōpos*, G5301). John does
not comment on it.

**This shows that God provided the Lamb He required.** "God shows his love for us in that while we
were still sinners, Christ died for us. Since, therefore, we have now been justified by his blood,
much more shall we be saved by him from the wrath of God" (Romans 5:8-9, ESV). That is the doctrine of
substitution: the judgment due to you fell on Jesus, and His blood is the sign God sees.

## Discussion questions

1. **The language.** Isaiah 31:5 sets "pass over" beside "protect", "deliver" and "rescue". How does
   that change your picture of what God did at the doors in Egypt?
2. **The text in its context.** Every house in Egypt faced the tenth plague (Exodus 12:12-13). What
   made the difference for Israel's houses, and what did not?
3. **Christ.** John applies "not one of his bones will be broken" (John 19:36) to Jesus. Why would
   John point to that detail?
4. **The hard part.** The first three Gospels call the Last Supper a Passover meal (Luke 22:15), and
   John has the priests still to eat theirs the next day (John 18:28). What is at stake in how the
   two are reconciled, and what is not?
5. **Grace in the law.** Numbers 9 made a second Passover for the unclean and the far away, and
   Exodus 12:48 opened the meal to the stranger. What do those provisions show about God?
6. **Be transformed.** Israel's children asked, "What do you mean by this service?" (Exodus 12:26).
   How would you answer that about the Lord's Supper?

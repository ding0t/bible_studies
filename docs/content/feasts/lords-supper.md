# The Lord's Supper: Do This in Remembrance of Me

Paul wrote to a church that met to eat the Lord's Supper and went home worse for it. "When you come
together, it is not the Lord's supper that you eat" (1 Corinthians 11:20, ESV). Then he told them
what the meal is, in the words Jesus spoke over it "on the night when he was betrayed" (1
Corinthians 11:23, ESV).

**In one sentence:** The Lord's Supper is the memorial Jesus gave His church, as the Passover was
Israel's: in it you remember His death, proclaim it until He comes, and share in His body and blood
with the one body He has made, and you wait for the cup He has not yet drunk, which He will drink new
with you in His Father's kingdom; so come to it examined, reconciled to your brothers and sisters,
and with your eyes on Him.

Paul's four verses on the night itself carry all of that, and each section below opens one of them:

> ✝️ 1 Corinthians 11:23-26 (ESV)
>
> 23 For I received from the Lord what I also delivered to you, that the Lord Jesus on the night when
> he was betrayed took bread, 24 and when he had given thanks, he broke it, and said, "This is my
> body, which is for you. Do this in remembrance of me." 25 In the same way also he took the cup,
> after supper, saying, "This cup is the new covenant in my blood. Do this, as often as you drink it,
> in remembrance of me." 26 For as often as you eat this bread and drink the cup, you proclaim the
> Lord's death until he comes.

## Key Takeaways

*(This section follows the [Key Takeaways](../about/key-takeaways.md) format — see that page for what each part is for.)*

### Types & Prophecy

- **Type: the Passover memorial.** God made the Passover "a memorial day ... throughout your
  generations" (Exodus 12:14, ESV). Jesus kept that meal with His disciples and gave it a new object:
  "Do this in remembrance of me" (Luke 22:19, ESV). Paul names the link: "Christ, our Passover lamb,
  has been sacrificed" (1 Corinthians 5:7, ESV).
- **Type: a meal that looks ahead.** Jesus said of the Passover, "I will not eat it until it is
  fulfilled in the kingdom of God" (Luke 22:16, ESV). The Supper is kept "until he comes" (1
  Corinthians 11:26).
- **Prophecy.** "On this mountain the LORD of hosts will make for all peoples a feast of rich food"
  (Isaiah 25:6, ESV). Revelation names it: "Blessed are those who are invited to the marriage supper
  of the Lamb" (Revelation 19:9, ESV).

### Lessons about Jesus

- **He gave Himself for you.** "This is my body, which is given for you" (Luke 22:19, ESV).
- **He made the new covenant in His own blood.** "This cup that is poured out for you is the new
  covenant in my blood" (Luke 22:20, ESV).
- **He is the host.** It is "the Lord's supper" (1 Corinthians 11:20) and "the table of the Lord" (1
  Corinthians 10:21).
- **He is made known at His table.** The two at Emmaus told "how he was known to them in the breaking
  of the bread" (Luke 24:35, ESV).
- **He has promised to finish the meal with you.** "I will not drink again of this fruit of the vine
  until that day when I drink it new with you in my Father's kingdom" (Matthew 26:29, ESV).

### Be Transformed

- **Think.** The Supper keeps you grounded in what is true. "Set your minds on things that are above,
  not on things that are on earth" (Colossians 3:2, ESV).
- **Attitude.** Come examined and reconciled. "Let a person examine himself, then, and so eat"
  (1 Corinthians 11:28, ESV), and "wait for one another" (1 Corinthians 11:33, ESV).
- **Do.** Answer the children's question. At the next Lord's Supper, tell someone, a child if you can,
  what the bread and the cup mean and whom you are waiting for.

us examine ourselves honestly, wait for one another gladly, and tell what this means to those who
ask. And keep us at His table until the day He drinks it new with us in Your kingdom.

## A memorial, as Passover was

### What Israel remembered

The Passover was given to be remembered. "This day shall be for you a memorial day, and you shall
keep it as a feast to the LORD; throughout your generations, as a statute forever" (Exodus 12:14,
ESV). "Memorial" is <span dir="rtl">זִכָּרוֹן</span> (*zikkaron*, H2146), from the verb to remember;
the Septuagint renders it μνημόσυνον (*mnēmosynon*, G3422). The unleavened bread carried the same
purpose, "that all the days of your life you may remember the day when you came out of the land of
Egypt" (Deuteronomy 16:3, ESV).

God built memorials because His people forget. "Take care lest you forget the LORD your God"
(Deuteronomy 8:11, ESV). When Israel crossed the Jordan He had them set up twelve stones, so that
"when your children ask in time to come, 'What do those stones mean to you?'" they would hear what
God had done, and "these stones shall be to the people of Israel a memorial forever" (Joshua 4:6-7,
ESV). The Passover did the same work every spring: a meal, eaten in every house, that said what God
had done.

[Passover: When I See the Blood](passover.md) studies the night in Egypt and the feast itself.

### "Do this in remembrance of me"

Jesus' last meal before the cross was that memorial. "I have earnestly desired to eat this Passover
with you before I suffer" (Luke 22:15, ESV). He kept its form and gave it a new object.

> ✝️ Luke 22:19-20 (ESV)
>
> 19 And he took bread, and when he had given thanks, he broke it and gave it to them, saying, "This
> is my body, which is given for you. Do this in remembrance of me." 20 And likewise the cup after
> they had eaten, saying, "This cup that is poured out for you is the new covenant in my blood.

"Remembrance" is ἀνάμνησις (*anamnēsis*, G364). It occurs four times in the New Testament: here, twice
in Paul's account (1 Corinthians 11:24, 25), and once in Hebrews, of the old sacrifices: "in these
sacrifices there is a reminder of sins every year" (Hebrews 10:3, ESV). The sacrifices offered year after year
brought sin back to mind. The Lord's Supper brings Jesus to mind, and the death that took sin away.

Israel remembered the night God brought them out of Egypt. The church remembers the night Jesus was
betrayed, and the body and blood given "for you." Paul draws the line between the two meals himself:
"Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed. Let us therefore celebrate the festival ... with the
unleavened bread of sincerity and truth" (1 Corinthians 5:7-8, ESV).
["I Will Not Drink Again"](last-supper-four-cups.md) follows the Passover cups through that night.

### "This is my body"

Christians have long divided over these four words, and the question of how Christ is present in the bread and cup is contested between the churches. This
study stays with what Scripture says the meal is.

Scripture names the meal three ways: a remembrance (Luke 22:19), a proclamation (1 Corinthians
11:26) and a participation (1 Corinthians 10:16, below). And it uses "is" for a sign and the thing
signified in the same Passover setting. Of the lamb eaten in haste God said, "It is the LORD's
Passover" (Exodus 12:11, ESV). Paul says of the water from the rock in the wilderness, "the Rock was
Christ" (1 Corinthians 10:4, ESV). Jesus says, "I am the door" (John 10:9, ESV). The disciples heard
"This is my body" at a table where Jesus' own body was in front of them, holding the bread.

**This shows that God gives His people a way to remember.** He gave Israel a meal for the night He
saved them, and He has given you a meal for the death that saved you.

## A meal that teaches

### The children's question

God wrote the teaching into the Passover. "When your children say to you, 'What do you mean by this
service?' you shall say, 'It is the sacrifice of the LORD's Passover'" (Exodus 12:26-27, ESV). "You
shall tell your son on that day, 'It is because of what the LORD did for me when I came out of
Egypt'" (Exodus 13:8, ESV). The feast was "a memorial between your eyes, that the law of the LORD may
be in your mouth" (Exodus 13:9, ESV).

### "You proclaim the Lord's death"

The Supper teaches in the same way. "As often as you eat this bread and drink the cup, you proclaim
the Lord's death until he comes" (1 Corinthians 11:26, ESV). "Proclaim" is καταγγέλλω (*katangellō*,
G2605), Paul's word for preaching the gospel. He uses it of his own preaching at Corinth (1
Corinthians 2:1) and of those who "proclaim the gospel" (1 Corinthians 9:14, ESV). Each time the
church eats the bread and drinks the cup, it preaches the death of Jesus, without a sermon, to
everyone at the table.

The first church kept it alongside the teaching. "They devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching
and the fellowship, to the breaking of bread and the prayers" (Acts 2:42, ESV). At Troas "on the
first day of the week, when we were gathered together to break bread, Paul talked with them" (Acts
20:7, ESV). And the risen Jesus, who had opened the Scriptures to two disciples on the road, "was
known to them in the breaking of the bread" (Luke 24:35, ESV).

### Remembering keeps you grounded

The New Testament treats reminding as part of keeping believers steady. Peter wrote, "I intend always
to remind you of these qualities, though you know them and are established in the truth that you
have. I think it right ... to stir you up by way of reminder" (2 Peter 1:12-13, ESV). Jesus promised
that the Holy Spirit would "bring to your remembrance all that I have said to you" (John 14:26, ESV).

The Supper is a reminder you can taste. It sets your eyes where Hebrews sets them: "looking to Jesus,
the founder and perfecter of our faith, who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross"
(Hebrews 12:2, ESV). **So you may come to the table and be brought back, every time, to what is
true: Jesus died for you, and He is coming back.**

## One loaf, one body

### A sharing in Christ

Before Paul corrects the Corinthians' Supper, he tells them what it is.

> ✝️ 1 Corinthians 10:16-17 (ESV)
>
> 16 The cup of blessing that we bless, is it not a participation in the blood of Christ? The bread
> that we break, is it not a participation in the body of Christ? 17 Because there is one bread, we
> who are many are one body, for we all partake of the one bread.

"Participation" is κοινωνία (*koinōnia*, G2842), sharing, fellowship, having in common. Paul uses it
of being "called into the fellowship of his Son, Jesus Christ our Lord" (1 Corinthians 1:9, ESV). At
the table believers share in what Christ's body and blood have won for them.

### A sharing in one another

Verse 17 draws the second line. One loaf is broken and everyone eats from it, so the many who eat it
are one body. "Now you are the body of Christ and individually members of it" (1 Corinthians 12:27,
ESV). The Supper is never a private meal. You eat it with the people Jesus died for.

### The table of the Lord alone

Paul makes the point to warn them. "Consider the people of Israel: are not those who eat the
sacrifices participants in the altar?" (1 Corinthians 10:18, ESV). A meal joins the one who eats it
to the one it is offered to. So "you cannot drink the cup of the Lord and the cup of demons. You
cannot partake of the table of the Lord and the table of demons" (1 Corinthians 10:21, ESV). The
Lord's table belongs to one Lord, and the one who eats at it belongs to Him.

## What went wrong at Corinth

### "Not the Lord's supper"

> ✝️ 1 Corinthians 11:20-22 (ESV)
>
> 20 When you come together, it is not the Lord's supper that you eat. 21 For in eating, each one goes
> ahead with his own meal. One goes hungry, another gets drunk. 22 What! Do you not have houses to eat
> and drink in? Or do you despise the church of God and humiliate those who have nothing? What shall I
> say to you? Shall I commend you in this? No, I will not.

The meal belonged to the Lord. "Lord's" is κυριακός (*kyriakos*, G2960, "belonging to the Lord"), a
word the New Testament uses only twice: here, and of "the Lord's day" (Revelation 1:10). The
Corinthians had made it "his own meal" (1 Corinthians 11:21). Some ate their fill and drank too much
while others arrived to find nothing left.

### A supper arranged by rank

The churches met in homes (1 Corinthians 16:19), and in a Roman city a dinner was arranged by status.
The *NIV Cultural Backgrounds Study Bible* describes the setting: an average wealthy home could
recline nine to twelve guests on three couches in its dining room, the *triclinium*, with more,
perhaps forty, in the larger *atrium*; the best places and the best food went to those nearest the
host.[^cbsb-banquets] Paul's own words fit that picture. Some "go ahead" with their meal, others
have "nothing", and his remedy is "wait for one another" (1 Corinthians 11:21-22, 33).

![Floor plan of a wealthy Roman house in Corinth, drawn as a reconstruction. On the left, the triclinium: three couches in a U around a table, nine to twelve guests reclining, with the host's food and wine, labelled "each one goes ahead with his own meal" (1 Corinthians 11:21). On the right, the larger atrium around a central pool, up to about forty people, latecomers arriving from work, labelled "one goes hungry" and "humiliate those who have nothing" (11:21-22). Along the bottom, Paul's remedy: "wait for one another" (11:33).](../assets/img/lords-supper-corinth-house.svg)

The layout is a reconstruction; Paul does not describe the room. What he does say is enough. At the
meal that proclaims Christ's death for others, they had put themselves first. **"It is not the Lord's
supper that you eat."**

## Coming in a worthy manner

### "In an unworthy manner"

> ✝️ 1 Corinthians 11:27-29 (ESV)
>
> 27 Whoever, therefore, eats the bread or drinks the cup of the Lord in an unworthy manner will be
> guilty concerning the body and blood of the Lord. 28 Let a person examine himself, then, and so eat
> of the bread and drink of the cup. 29 For anyone who eats and drinks without discerning the body
> eats and drinks judgment on himself.

"In an unworthy manner" is one word, ἀναξίως (*anaxiōs*, G371), found nowhere else in the New
Testament. It is an adverb. It describes how a person eats, and in context it is the way the
Corinthians were eating: each for himself, the poor shamed. No one is worthy of Christ's body given
for them; that is why it was given. Paul asks for a worthy *manner*.

### "Let a person examine himself"

The remedy is self-examination, δοκιμάζω (*dokimazō*, G1381), to test and approve. Paul does not tell
the church to keep people from the table. He tells each person to look at himself, "and so eat"
(1 Corinthians 11:28). The examination ends at the table.

### "Discerning the body"

"Without discerning the body" (1 Corinthians 11:29) is read two ways, and both have support.

1. **The Lord's body given for you.** The bread is "my body, which is for you" (1 Corinthians 11:24),
   so to eat without discerning the body is to eat without recognising what Christ's death means.
   Some manuscripts read "not discerning the Lord's body", which takes it this way.
2. **The church as Christ's body.** Paul has just said "we who are many are one body" (1 Corinthians
   10:17), and he goes on, "you are the body of Christ" (1 Corinthians 12:27). The Greek text behind most modern
   translations reads simply "the body", and the sin Paul is correcting is contempt for fellow believers.

The two readings meet at Corinth. Despising the people Christ died for is a failure to understand
why He died.

### Judged, so as not to be condemned

Paul builds the end of the paragraph on one word family. Discerning is διακρίνω (*diakrinō*, G1252),
and judgment is κρίμα (*krima*, G2917). "If we judged [*diakrinō*] ourselves truly, we would not be
judged. But when we are judged by the Lord, we are disciplined so that we may not be condemned along
with the world" (1 Corinthians 11:31-32, ESV). Some at Corinth had become "weak and ill, and some have
died" (1 Corinthians 11:30, ESV).

The judgment here is the Lord's discipline of His own people, and its purpose is stated: "so that we
may not be condemned along with the world." **This shows that God disciplines His children because
they are His.** Judge yourself at the table, and you will not need His correction.

### "Wait for one another"

Paul ends with a plain instruction. "So then, my brothers, when you come together to eat, wait for one
another — if anyone is hungry, let him eat at home — so that when you come together it will not be for
judgment" (1 Corinthians 11:33-34, ESV). The fix for a selfish supper is a shared one.

## The cup not yet drunk

### "Until it is fulfilled in the kingdom of God"

At the table Jesus spoke of a meal still to come. "I will not eat it until it is fulfilled in the
kingdom of God" (Luke 22:16, ESV). Over the cup: "I will not drink of the fruit of the vine until the
kingdom of God comes" (Luke 22:18, ESV). Matthew records the company He means to share it with: "until
that day when I drink it new with you in my Father's kingdom" (Matthew 26:29, ESV).

So the Lord's Supper is unfinished on purpose. It looks back to the cross and forward to a table in
the kingdom. Jesus promised that table to His disciples in the same evening: "I assign to you, as my
Father assigned to me, a kingdom, that you may eat and drink at my table in my kingdom and sit on
thrones judging the twelve tribes of Israel" (Luke 22:29-30, ESV).

### Three tables

The Passover, the Lord's Supper and the marriage supper of the Lamb stand in a line.

The prophets saw the last of them. "On this mountain the LORD of hosts will make for all peoples a
feast of rich food, a feast of well-aged wine ... He will swallow up death forever; and the Lord GOD
will wipe away tears from all faces" (Isaiah 25:6, 8, ESV). John heard it announced: "the marriage of
the Lamb has come, and his Bride has made herself ready ... Blessed are those who are invited to the
marriage supper of the Lamb" (Revelation 19:7, 9, ESV).

Which cup of the Passover Jesus left undrunk, and why the promise it carried is God taking His people
to Himself, is traced in ["I Will Not Drink Again"](last-supper-four-cups.md#the-fourth-promise-i-will-take-you-to-me-for-a-people).
The wedding supper itself, and who its guests are, is studied in
[The Bride of Christ](../israel-and-church/bride-of-christ.md).

### "Until he comes"

Every Lord's Supper ends with the same two words. The church eats and drinks "until he comes" (1
Corinthians 11:26). The meal is for the time between the cross and His return, and it will not be
needed when He is at the table. **This shows that God finishes what He starts.** The Lamb who was
sacrificed at the Passover will sit down at the marriage supper with the people He bought, and each
time you eat this bread you say that you expect to be there.

## Discussion questions

1. **The language.** "Remembrance" (ἀνάμνησις) is used at the Supper and in Hebrews 10:3 for the
   "reminder of sins" the old sacrifices brought every year. What is the difference between the two
   remembrances?
2. **The text in its context.** Paul says the Corinthians' gathering "is not the Lord's supper"
   (1 Corinthians 11:20). What exactly had they done, and what does his remedy in 11:33 tell you about
   it?
3. **Christ.** Read Exodus 12:14, Luke 22:19 and 1 Corinthians 11:26 together. What did Israel
   remember, what does the church remember, and what does the church announce that Israel could not?
4. **The hard part.** "Without discerning the body" (1 Corinthians 11:29) is read of Christ's body
   given on the cross and of the church as His body. Which reading do you find stronger, and does the
   difference change how you come to the table?
5. **Teaching.** Israel's children were expected to ask, "What do you mean by this service?" (Exodus
   12:26). How would you answer that about the Lord's Supper, in a sentence a child could follow?
6. **Be transformed.** Jesus said He would drink the cup new "with you" in His Father's kingdom
   (Matthew 26:29). How does knowing the meal is unfinished change what you think about while you eat
   it?

# The Names of the Holy Spirit: Hebrew word cards

**In one sentence:** learn the Hebrew alphabet by learning the Old Testament's names for the Spirit
of God, one card at a time, starting with the one word they all share, *ruach*.

> ✝️ Isaiah 11:2 (ESV)
>
> 2 And the Spirit of the LORD shall rest upon him, the Spirit of wisdom and understanding, the
> Spirit of counsel and might, the Spirit of knowledge and the fear of the LORD.

Almost every card begins with <span dir="rtl">רוּחַ</span> (*ruach*, H7307), which means breath,
wind and Spirit. Most of the Spirit's names in Hebrew are "the Spirit of" something, so once you
can read *ruach* you can read the first word of nearly every card. [The Names of the Holy
Spirit](../holy-spirit/names-of-the-holy-spirit.md) is the study behind the cards. The companion
decks are [The Names of God](names-of-god-cards.md), [The Names of Jesus](names-of-jesus-cards.md),
and the [Greek word cards](../greek/names-of-the-holy-spirit-cards.md) for the Spirit's New
Testament names.

## How the deck teaches the letters

The cards are in a deliberate order. The first, *ruach*, has three letters. Each card after it adds
a few more, and the front shows which, with a bar for how many of the 22 you have met. The last
letter, samekh, arrives on David's prayer for "a willing spirit" (Psalm 51:12). The order and the
counts are worked out from the Hebrew on the cards, so they cannot drift from what is printed.

Use the deck alongside [The Hebrew Alphabet](alphabet.md): when a card introduces a letter, find it
in the table, say its sound, then read the word.

## Reading a card

- **The front** has the name as the Hebrew Bible writes it, vowel points included, and beneath it the
  same letters in **Paleo-Hebrew**, the script Israel wrote before the exile.
- **The back** has the reading in syllables with the stressed one underlined, the meaning, a verse
  (ESV), and a line marked **Today** on the word in modern Hebrew. Where the New Testament takes up
  the name, the verse is the New Testament one.
- **The divine name is not sounded out.** On *ruach YHWH* it is read *Adonai*. On *ruach Adonai
  YHWH* (Isaiah 61:1) it is pointed with the vowels of *Elohim* and read that way, so the reader does
  not say *Adonai* twice.
- **Two cards are about the Spirit without being names.** *Eshpokh*, "I will pour out" (Joel 2:28),
  is the verb of the promise, and *ruach nedivah*, "a willing spirit" (Psalm 51:12), stands beside
  David's prayer for the Holy Spirit in the verse before.

## Learning a card

**On screen**, choose **Practise on screen** above the cards. It shows one card at a time: read the
word, show the reading to check yourself, then mark it **Again** or **I read it**. It introduces
three new cards a day, in the deck's order, and brings each card back when it is due. **Hear it**
reads the word in your device's Hebrew voice, where it has one. Your progress is kept in this
browser only.

**On paper**, take one card a day. Read the front aloud, letter by letter, then turn it over to check
the reading. Each time you read it correctly on a later day, tick a circle: **1, 2, 4, 7, 14 and 30
days** after you learn it.

## How to print

1. Choose **Flashcards** for the word on the front and everything else on the back, or **Cards**
   for one-sided cards.
2. Print at **A4, 100 % / actual size**, with **margins set to none** and **headers and footers
   off**. Turn **background graphics** on.
3. For flashcards, print **double-sided, flip on long edge**.
4. Cut on the dashed lines.

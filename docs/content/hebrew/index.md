# Learn Hebrew

**In one sentence:** you can learn to read the Hebrew Bible one step at a time, starting with 22
letters and the names God gave Himself.

God spoke the Old Testament in Hebrew. A translation gives you what He said, and the Hebrew lets you
hear how He said it: the wordplay, the repeated roots, the names that carry a promise. Each step
below builds on the one before, so take them in order.

## The path

1. **[The Hebrew Alphabet](alphabet.md).** The 22 letters, their five final forms and the vowel
   points, with the sound of each. By the end you can sound out Genesis 1:1.
2. **[How Hebrew Is Pronounced](pronunciation.md).** Why you will hear *Shabbat* and *Shabbos*, the
   pronunciation this site uses, and how the name of God is read.
3. **[The Names of God: Word Cards](names-of-god-cards.md).** Twenty-one printable flashcards, in an
   order that adds a few letters at a time. Work through the deck and you will have met all 22
   letters, in the names God revealed Himself by.
4. **[Aleph with Beth](aleph-with-beth.md).** The free video course this site recommends for going
   on to read whole passages.

Alongside these, the [Memory Verse Cards](../resources/memory-verses.md) print verses such as
Genesis 1:1 and Deuteronomy 6:4 in Hebrew, word by word, with the ESV.

## Biblical and modern Hebrew

The letters are the same in both, and so is most of the core vocabulary: *shalom*, *melekh* (king),
*tov* (good) mean what they meant to David. The differences are these:

- **History.** Hebrew was Israel's everyday language through the First Temple period. After the
  exile, Aramaic and then Greek took over daily life, and for about 1,700 years Hebrew was read,
  prayed and written but spoken by no one as a first language. In the late 1800s Eliezer
  Ben-Yehuda and others revived it as a spoken language, and it became the language of the state of
  Israel.
- **Vocabulary.** Modern Hebrew has thousands of words the Bible never needed, often built from
  biblical roots: a *rofe* (doctor) comes from the root of *YHWH rofekha*, "the LORD your healer".
- **Grammar.** Modern Hebrew verbs mark past, present and future. Biblical Hebrew verbs mark
  whether an action is complete or ongoing, and biblical narrative strings events together with a
  form (the *vav*-consecutive) that modern Hebrew does not use.
- **Writing.** Bibles print the vowel points; modern books and newspapers mostly leave them out,
  and handwriting uses a cursive script.

So a modern dictionary or an Israeli friend is a good guide to a word's sound and its everyday
sense today. For what a word meant in Scripture, this site's word studies go to lexicons built on
the biblical text itself, and the cards here mark which is which: the meaning on each card is the
biblical one, and the line marked **Today** is the modern.
